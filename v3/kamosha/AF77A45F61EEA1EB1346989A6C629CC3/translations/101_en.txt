One: Those who appear in litigation (lawsuits) shall not carry long swords or short swords, even if they are direct retainers of the shogun (hatamoto or gokenin).

One: Even if a litigant is a relative, kinsman, or acquaintance of a member of the council of adjudicators (hyōjōshū), the adjudicators shall not handle that person's case at the assembly hall (yoriaiba).

One: Litigation cases brought from distant provinces shall be heard in the order they appear in that day's register. However, matters that cannot be left unheard and urgent cases are exceptions to this rule.

One: If there are any doubts or suspicions regarding a litigation case, the official in charge of that matter shall attend to it. All members of the assembly are also encouraged to freely offer their opinions without reservation.

One: After a verdict (saiky ū) has been rendered in a litigation case, the official in charge shall prepare a written record (shime-tomegaki) of the case. The governors of Izu, Bungo, and Kaga are to have copies made of that day's litigation records.

One: If a litigation case is not resolved on that day, the adjudicators in charge shall convene a meeting the following day. If it remains unresolved, they shall consult with the senior councillors (rōjūchū) and then submit a report to higher authority.
