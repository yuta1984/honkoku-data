Furthermore, they do not wear mourning garments, maintain separate households, and treat their main residence in the same manner as the shrine precincts. Those who are *ujibito* (clan members), though still persons, do wear mourning garments, attend funerals, and become involved in ritual impurity (*kegare*). In all matters, there are significant distinctions established by shrine law.

Item: As you are well aware, those who are *shashi* (shrine officials) have since ancient times been granted the privilege of ascending to the palace (*shōden*) and attending upon the Retired Emperor (*in*). Accordingly, the daughters of *shashi* have been employed as *gerō* (lower-ranking court ladies), while the daughters of *ujibito* have been placed as *nyoju* (female attendants of yet lower rank). This demonstrates the distinction between *shashi* and *ujibito*.

Item: Those who are *shashi* do not undergo the *genpuku* (coming-of-age ceremony), and while in the state of *kodachi* (childhood status), they are given names ending in "*maru*." Those who are *ujibito*, regardless of court rank, undergo *genpuku* at the age of three, and are called "*tayū*" even before being appointed to the *ujibito* position.

Item: Among those who sought the *negi* priesthood this time — persons such as Jingōrō — they belong to the household attendants (*kago*) of the *shashi* families and are of hereditary service (*fudai*); for *ujibito* to serve as *shashi* is utterly without precedent. This is established by shrine law.

Regarding the above articles, we state once again: should even one *ujibito* be appointed as a proper holder of the *negi* position, several hundred *ujibito* would become *shakkan-ke* (shrine official families). [Note in original: "as written; the character 法 (*hō*, 'law') may be missing."] This would cause great confusion. Furthermore, if shrine law (*sha*￮) falls into disorder, the will of the deities (*shinryo*) would be difficult to foresee. As we have stated nothing that is false, we humbly request that matters be handled as they have been heretofore. Thus, as stated above.
