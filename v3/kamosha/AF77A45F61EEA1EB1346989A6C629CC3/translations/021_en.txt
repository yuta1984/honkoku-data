The matter was reported to the Imperial Liaison (Gidensō). In general, from former times, when someone was appointed to a shrine post, it has been the practice to submit such oaths and divine pledges (shinmon) to one's colleagues. The details are as written above: since the time in the Kan'ei era when the shrine officials of four or five houses fell into dispute and arguments arose, the primary concern has been to maintain the peace and tranquility of all parties. That is to say, the "Hoka" (保可) to which the grandfather of the current Okamoto Kunai Yasurei was added when the seven-house system was established in the Kantō (Edo) refers to the divine oath pledged when one first petitioned for a shrine post. For this reason, from that time up to the current Kunai-chika and Yasutaka, there has been no dispute whatsoever. Even in the recent dispute (deiri) that arose from Kyōhō 1 (1716) onward, the seven houses did not collectively take sides. Regarding the original document of the written oath of the Five Houses, the copy of the Hoka divine pledge, and all other matters from the beginning up to the present—concerning the dispute over the Ōraida (通行田, rice fields), a written report was submitted at the command of Lord Shimano-no-Kami on the tenth day of the sixth month of last year.
