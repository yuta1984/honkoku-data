Entoku 2 (1490), 12th month, [day]

Counter-Statement (Shijō) of Shigenori

Kamo Shrine Hōri Shigenori respectfully submits the following:

Regarding the particulars of this matter: concerning the Fukahi and Hakotsukuri-no-shō within the shrine's sacred landholdings (shinryō), the previous Shōhōri had borrowed money and currency, and regarding the sale of the fields within the shrine precincts, the matter of Narajima was due to repeated flooding that caused the farmland to be washed away. Nevertheless, even for the small amount of remaining annual tax (nengu), we borrowed funds to cover it, and from the first month through the twelfth month we performed the shrine rituals as usual. When we stated that matters should be handled in accordance with established precedent, and when we stated that the sacred landholdings should be managed as before, we received no response. When we raised these particulars, the view was expressed—based on the position that the Shōhōri was acting improperly—that after consultation among the shrine officials (shikan) and clan members (ujibito), the matter should be rendered void (muge/null and void), and furthermore the shrine administrator (shamu) twice intervened to say the matter should be rendered void, but we ultimately did not accept this. Therefore, the refusal to perform duties on the 28th day of the 10th month was not a case of negligence (kettai) on Shigenori's part. During the Ōei era (1394–1428), there were numerous precedents of kettai arising from disputes over stipends (jinryō) among shrine administrators and hōri. In particular, the repeated instances of kettai over stipends since the first year of Bunmei (1469) make it all the more outrageous that this is being newly litigated now. Furthermore, regarding the fact that when the Gon-hōri abandoned his shrine duties and both the shrine administrator and Tsuguhira improperly retained many shrine officials under their control—when we took custody of the Gon-hōri in response to a request (wabigoto) that, as the presiding authority of the hōri, we should manage the situation—to now claim that we were hoarding stipends for personal gain is utterly unreasonable and an immature submission that should not be given any credence whatsoever. We respectfully submit these particulars in rough outline, requesting that they be duly conveyed to the Imperial court and that a judgment (seibai) be rendered.
