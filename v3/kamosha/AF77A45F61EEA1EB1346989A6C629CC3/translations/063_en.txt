Item: Regarding the matter of the jinryō (personnel stipends/offerings) for the 28th day of the 10th month — since it was submitted that these should be performed in accordance with established precedents, as stated in my previous submission, if the shrine estates (sharyo) were without irregularity, it would be appropriate for the matter to be handled according to precedent. However, given that the shrine estates are not in the same condition as before, the attempt to lodge a formal complaint (soshō) demanding that, among all the year-round shrine rituals, only the jinryō for the 28th day of the 10th month be handled according to precedent — this was deemed improper for the sake of prayers for the peace of the realm. Shigenori was on the point of submitting a protest to this effect, but after the parties held consultations, orders were issued twice to the effect that the matter should proceed without incident; and although the shrine administration (shamu) repeatedly conveyed this, since the other party ultimately refused to comply, can Shigenori truly be said to have been negligent (kettai) in the performance of the rituals? (Certainly not.)

Furthermore, the claim that he appropriated the annual tribute (nengu) from the sacred estates (jinryō) for private use is a most rash and groundless assertion. The land tribute (dokō/tōkō) from the sacred estates — from the 1st month through the 12th month — should all be accounted for in relation to the shrine rituals, and whether any surplus remains should be verified; there is no question of private appropriation. This is an unsupported petition, and there is nothing to argue about right or wrong in the matter.

Item: Regarding the matter of neglect of shrine rituals (jinki kettai) during the Ōei era — as to whether such things occurred over a period of several hundred years — even if more than a hundred years have passed, how could neglect of shrine duties (shayaku) ever be hidden? Furthermore, as for the claim that there was no neglect during Bunmei 1 (1469): the failure to provide jinryō (personnel stipends) to the shrine administration, the Kifune officials (ryōkan), and the ujibito (shrine people/clan members) was frequent, and each of them is well aware of this.
