One matter: It has been claimed that 120 koku of temple and hermitage lands (ji'an-ryō) out of the total of over 2,570 koku have been unlawfully seized (ōryō). Regarding these temple and hermitage lands, there exist documentary evidence including the vermilion-seal documents (goshuin) and directives (gochishi) of Kōgen'in-dono (Lord Nobunaga Oda), as well as transfer documents (yuzuri-jō) and donation records (kisin-jō) from former masters. These lands were duly registered from the old domain fields, and over 90 koku was received in grant, from which expenses for divine ceremonies and festivals (ishi-kakari) are drawn. Furthermore, various duties are performed each year. Most notably, without the existence of Buddhist establishments, the purity of Shinto practice would be difficult to maintain. For this reason, Head Priest Tsunehisa became a parishioner (dandankata) of Shōdenji Temple, and Ichi-kannushi Otohira established a temple called Jiunan, donating paddy fields and other land within the precincts of this shrine — and this temple, Jiunan, continues to exist to this day. Likewise, Head Priest Kōhei established a temple called Myōkanji and assigned land within the shrine grounds to it, which is still known today as the Myōkanji fields. In this manner, portions of this shrine's domain have been designated as temple lands for several hundred years, and among the ancestors of the very plaintiffs, there are many who were involved in this practice. Despite knowing these circumstances well, to claim that the temple and hermitage lands constitute unlawful seizure is yet another falsehood.
