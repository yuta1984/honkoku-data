In particular, regarding the horses for the kemari-kai [horse racing ceremony] conducted every year as prayers for both the court nobility and the warrior class: the first horse comes from Shitori-no-shō (Mimasaka Province), the second from Kanatsu-no-shō (Kaga Province), the third from Anshi-no-shō (Harima Province), the fourth from Tsuchida-no-shō (Noto Province), and the fifth from Fukuda-no-shō (Izumo Province). All twenty horses continue to retain their designations to this day. Until recently, these horses were procured through the landholdings in those provinces, but as the shrine domains in those provinces have now been lost, we have levied assessments by koku [rice yield] on the stipend fields of the ujibito [shrine-affiliated people], family landholdings, and various officials, distributing payments to the head priests and various officials. Furthermore, the twelfth horse should be procured by the shōhafuri [chief hafuri priest], and the fourteenth horse by the gonnegi [assistant negi priest], but these too are now being provided from among the shrine community. The current shashi [shrine administrators], kannushi [head priests], negi [priests], and hafuri [celebrant priests], while holding a disproportionately large portion of the shrine domains, bear none of the expenses necessary for their official duties, and on the contrary accuse the shrine community of being negligent in the performance of ceremonies — this is a complete falsehood. Furthermore, the divine offerings [goshinkō] of the hafuri group that have been performed for the past hundred years continue to be carried out without fail up to the present year.

Regarding the petition concerning repairs to the shrine buildings: up until the time of the recent reconstruction, there were more than thirty shrine sanctuaries, more than twenty worship halls and auxiliary buildings, and torii gates, bridges, and other structures below these...
