Entoku 3 (1491), 3rd month, [day]

The shrine officials (shashi), functionaries (kannin), and clan members (ujibito) of Kamo Shrine respectfully submit the following once more.
(Marginal note: "Same, 7th day — draft of two-room [copy] (as in the original)")

We earnestly request that the following offenses of Shigenori be promptly conveyed to the Imperial court for adjudication:

Item 1: Regarding the matter that after submitting his petition (jōhyō) to the court, Shigenori has continued to call himself Hōri (Shrine Priest) without having received imperial sanction (chokukyō) — this is clearly contrary to proper conduct, and his offense in this regard is by no means light.

Item 2: Regarding the sacred landholdings (hōri-ryō) of Fukahi and Hakotsukuri-no-shō: although these should be under proper management, between Shigeyoshi (Shigenori's father) and Shigenori himself, the old debts (kyū-shakkusen) have been treated as though they do not exist at all within the shrine as an institution. Furthermore, regarding the matter of Narajima, false claims have been made, citing various pretexts.

Item 3: Regarding the claim that despite intervention (kuchire) from the shrine's side concerning the shrine rituals of the 28th day of the 10th month, Shigenori did not comply — in fact, despite there being fixed deadlines for the rituals in question, he entirely failed to perform them on account of personal affairs (shiyō), and then shifted the blame onto others; having caused the rituals to fall into immediate neglect (kettai), the matter of stipends (jinryō) is no longer within the scope of legitimate grievance.

Item 4: The Ōei era is already nearly one hundred years ago, and it is uncertain whether the ritual precedents cited from that time are even valid. Furthermore, while the first year of Bunmei (1469) was a time of great civil war throughout the realm, the shrine rituals were nevertheless not allowed to lapse. Yet last year's rituals were entirely entrusted to the meeting of the agents (otedai) of the collectors (nōsho), leaving the rituals and stipends unexecuted. To cite ancient precedents without understanding one's own situation is utterly unconscionable — this is clearly a deliberate fabrication (sakui).

Item 5: When the Shōhōri (Chief Shrine Priest) submitted his petition to the court, the Gon-hōri (Provisional Shrine Priest) of the next seat was supposed to assume that office and diligently perform the shrine rituals [in his stead].
