○ The *ujibito* stated: "The claim that the small marginal notation (*kogaki-tsuke*) of Sukehisa was removed is understandable. If that is so, then in the *shashi* side's genealogy, the entry recording that Nobusue was appointed as *ujigami* (*Kamo Shrine's*) *gongohaku* (Provisional Chief Ritualist) should likewise have been removed. In our genealogy, it is clearly recorded as '*ujigami gongohaku*.'"

○ The *shashi* side stated: "Hirohisa, who is presently seeking the position of *shashi*, was an *ujibito* for three generations in the middle [of the lineage]. We respectfully request that you investigate whether there is any precedent for a family, after three generations of interruption, again becoming *shashi*."

○ The *ujibito* stated: "There is no precedent in shrine custom for examining the number of generations. If any such precedent for generational scrutiny exists, we ask that you order it to be produced."

○ Kaan stated: "The office of *gongohaku* was entrusted to Jūsetsu. After Kaan's line was severed (*sekare*), Jūji was appointed *gongohaku* from the 18th year of Keichō [1613] and administered the position until the 1st year of Genna [1615]. Therefore, ours is the legitimate transmission (*shōden*). In light of this, I find it suspicious that Jūsetsu was appointed *gongohaku* in the 19th year of Keichō [1614]."

○ The *ujibito* side presented five documents of *kuzen* (imperial appointment decrees) belonging to Jūsetsu.

○ The matter conveyed by the order of the Kampaku (*Regent*) was an inquiry: "What is the *kuzen* stating that Jūji was appointed *gongohaku* in the 18th year of Keichō?"

○ At this point, Kaan presented the *kuzen* for Jūji's *joshaku* (court rank conferment), but there was no *kuzen* for the office (*shoku*) itself.
