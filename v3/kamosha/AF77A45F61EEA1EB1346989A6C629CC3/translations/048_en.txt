*(Continued from the previous page)* …we find this matter of the granting [of the *kuzen*] to be most troubling. In connection with this, from the eighteenth year of Keichō (1613) through the first year of Genna (1615), Jūji, the son of the *Shōhafuri* (senior *hafuri* priest), had been serving in the position of *Gon-hafuri* (acting *hafuri*). Then, in the nineteenth year of Keichō (1614), Jūsetsu received a *kuzen* (imperial oral decree) for the position of *Gon-hafuri*. This resulted in there being two *Gon-hafuri* simultaneously, which was a separate and irregular development. As such, there is nothing further we can add on that matter. Nevertheless, regarding the current situation in which the *ujibito* are seeking appointment to the position of *shashi*, only the *kuzen* granted to Jūsetsu is being cited as a contemporary precedent; there is absolutely no other precedent for this. Should appointments be made in this manner on this occasion, it would cause great disorder in shrine law (*shahō*), which we find most distressing. We therefore humbly request that instructions be issued in a manner that upholds proper shrine law.

Item: Although we are humbly grateful for the receipt of the *kuzen* for the position of *Gon-hafuri*, [Jūsetsu] does not perform the ritual obeisance (*haiga*) before the gods, does not renew the sacred *ofuda* (amulets/votive tablets), the chief priest and other shrine officials do not fulfill shrine law, and furthermore, he does not carry out the proper and essential divine duties (*shintai no jineki*), does not succeed to the title of the *shashi* household, and moreover becomes entangled in the comings and goings and stipend fields (*kyūden*) of the *ujibito*, performing various duties of the *ujibito*. This conduct is contrary to the proper role of *shashi*.

Regarding the above articles, as we have repeatedly stated, the households of *shashi* and *ujibito* are distinct from one another, with the shrine duties (*shayaku*) of each household defined accordingly. Each has perpetuated its hereditary occupation in accordance with the import of shrine precedents and traditional records, and has faithfully performed *shinji* (divine rites) and *sairei* (festivals) in accordance with the founding traditions (*engi*) and ritual procedures (*hōshiki*) of the shrine. Should *ujibito* now be appointed as *shashi*, shrine law both ancient and modern will fall into disorder, and the several hundred *ujibito* will all become of equal standing—a situation we find most distressing. We therefore humbly request that instructions be issued as they have been heretofore…
