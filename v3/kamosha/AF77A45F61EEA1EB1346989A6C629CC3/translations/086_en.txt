One matter: It has been claimed that the various shrine officials possess no stipend fields (kyūden), but this too is a falsehood. The officials of this shrine include: one Niedono Zenbu (official overseeing food offerings), one Edokoro (painting office official), two musicians (gakujin), forty-two Tone (shrine attendants) [with an additional ninety-odd non-regular members (hishū)], over thirty Jinin (shrine servants), one Ko-mokudai (assistant deputy), one Himono-shi (cypress woodworker), one carpenter (daiku), three pottery makers (kawarake-shi), and two Matsu-gyōji (pine ceremony officials). All of the above hold stipend fields and family landholdings. At the time of the land survey (kenchi), these were duly registered, amounting to over 140 koku in assessed yield, which is currently held and administered by over one hundred persons, each fulfilling their respective duties. To claim that there are no stipend fields whatsoever is an outrageous falsehood. Beyond these, there are also: one Ko-azukari (sub-custodian), one Shinpu (divine servant), three Suitō (accountants/treasurers), five Zushi (draftsmen/map-makers), one Ōmiya ko-zukai (attendant of the Main Shrine), one Koyama ko-zukai, one Nakamura ko-zukai, one Ōhara ko-zukai, and one Yuya hi-taki (bathhouse fire-tender). These officials have been provided with provisions (gegyō) in accordance with long-established custom. Furthermore, serving as high-ranking officials (jōshoku) who perform divine duties within the shrine families' landholdings, there are: one Bettō (superintendent), one Azukari (custodian), one Uta-yaku (poetry official), one Imiko (ritual abstinence child), five Tadokoro (land office officials), one Onmyōryō (divination official), five Ofuku Jōrō (senior ladies-in-waiting for sacred robes), eight Yagotome (shrine maidens) of the Kifune Shrine, and one Sōdai Miko (representative shrine maiden). With officials arranged in this manner across various ranks, the shrine duties throughout the year are carried out without negligence, and the divine ceremonies are duly performed.
