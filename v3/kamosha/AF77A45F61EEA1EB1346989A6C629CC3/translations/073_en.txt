Respectfully submitted to the attention of [the authorities]: Upper Kamo Shrine Officials

Item: Although the total assessed yield (kokudaka) of the shrine's sacred landholdings amounts to over 2,570 koku, since no distinction has been established between funds allocated for ritual ceremonies (神事料) and funds for repairs (修理料), the performance of sacred rites has fallen into decline, offerings to the deities have become neglected, and even minor repairs to the shrine structures cannot be carried out. Given that this is the era of [your] enlightened reign, we would wish to petition for the granting of new stipend lands; however, we find it difficult to presume upon the intentions of the public authorities (幕府). Under such circumstances, within the shrine lands that have fortunately been granted to us, there are those among the shrine clan members (氏人), temple sub-precincts (寺庵), and peasants (百姓) who have unlawfully occupied and appropriated these lands — including in other locations — and we possess documentary evidence (証文) to this effect. We therefore humbly request that an inspection be conducted and that these lands be directed toward shrine use. Furthermore, within the current shrine precinct, there is an exceedingly large amount of uncultivated fallen land (落地) and newly opened fields (新田)...
