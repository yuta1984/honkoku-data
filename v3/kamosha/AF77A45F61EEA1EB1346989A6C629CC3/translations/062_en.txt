Counter-Statement (shigen'jō) of Kamo Shrine Hōri Shigenori
(Marginal note: "Shigenori's counter-statement [of one item?], 25th day of the 3rd month")

Item 1: Regarding the content of the petition claiming that I (as hōri) submitted a jōhyō — this is a matter of which I have absolutely no knowledge or recollection.

Item 2: Regarding the sacred landholdings (shōhōri-ryō) of Fukahi and Hakotsukuri-no-shō: the previous hōri, Enryū, had arranged for annual tribute (nōzei) to be funded through loans (shakkusen), and Shigeyoshi likewise borrowed funds to fulfill his sacred duties (jineki). However, disputes arose between the estate managers (kumon) and peasants (hyakushō) on one side and the shrine on the other, causing the estate lands to fall into wasteland from Bunmei 4 (1472) through Bunmei 6 (1474), making it impossible to undertake repayment of the loans. In Bunmei 7 (1475), when the estate returned to a state of stability, it was hoped that a modest harvest could be secured to provide offerings to the shrine (jinmotsu). However, from the time of Enkyū onward, [Shigenori] fraudulently seized these revenues — temporarily styling himself hōri and collecting the annual tributes. Thereafter, thirty-six men from Izumi Province formed a group (kumi?) and implemented a hanzei (half-seizure) over the temple, shrine, and honjo estate lands, continuing to hold them by force through Bunmei 16 (1484), which made repayment of the loans increasingly impossible, resulting in an ever-growing deficit; the estate remains in a state of paralysis to this day.

Regarding the matter of Narajima: the details are as stated in my previous submission, and there is nothing further to add. If there remains any doubt regarding this matter, an imperial envoy (tsukai) should be dispatched to make inquiries directly.
