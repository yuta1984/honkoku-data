It should be noted that the petition for court ranks at that time followed the same procedure as has always been observed, with no special request involved. Furthermore, regarding the matter of age, since in the fourth year of Kyōhō (1719) there had been an official written directive stating that ujibito could petition for court ranks from the age of fifteen, there should have been no impediment whatsoever. However, the current chief priest, Lord Hayashi Kunai-no-Gon-no-Taifu, having long maintained close and intimate relations with Lord Konoe and being regarded with favor by him, took it upon himself to act as intermediary for a certain man named Tomino Bichūnokami, and together they repeatedly made private inquiries and appearances, interfering in various ways with the affairs of the ujibito and petitioning only for what was convenient for themselves. It appears that for these reasons, matters such as court ranks have been held up. Consequently, even when court ranks were subsequently petitioned for, despite there being indications of favorable intentions, imperial sanction (chokkyō) was not granted. In recent years, after repeated deliberations, official directives were issued, and the shrine had just barely begun to settle into a state of peace and quiet—yet the matter of court ranks, of the utmost importance to shrine families, has been delayed in this manner, causing the greatest inconvenience to all members of the ujibito.
