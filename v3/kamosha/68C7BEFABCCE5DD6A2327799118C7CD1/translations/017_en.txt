In general, among the many varieties of sword construction, there are: divine swords (神大刀), dedicatory swords (奉納大刀), ceremonial swords (御帯刀), yin swords (陰の大刀), yang swords (陽の大刀), swords made for young nobles (公達作り), escort swords (随身大刀), swords for those without rank (無官大刀), and even swords used in Nō performances (能の大刀). Each differs in its specifications, and various traditional rules govern their construction, including gold-worked, silver-worked, exotic-worked, rough-worked styles, scabbards of leopard or tiger fur, and upper scabbards of bear fur, among others. It is said that divine swords and dedicatory swords have neither yin/yang distinction nor fur scabbards. From these downward, all are made in corresponding yin and yang pairs, so it is said. swords, they are made in the "wood-claw" (木爪) style: the menuki (sword handle ornaments) number six on the front and six on the back, totaling twelve, representing the twelve months and the rhythm of major and minor seasonal divisions. The four cut-pieces represent the four seasons. Within the kashira (pommel) fitting, yang numbers are expressed by carving the circular form of the sun disc, to which fittings called "shitodome" are attached, numbering seventy-two in all. At the end of the gold chain hang two pendants, representing the seventy-two seasonal micro-periods (候) paired with day and night. In the fitting known as the "shōgyō" (鈔形), the essence of yin is expressed by using the circular form of the moon disc as a decorative nail cover, which is then applied.
