This is not something that happens every time, and there are numerous precedents. Furthermore, regarding such matters, without having been directed by the court and military authorities and acknowledged by the shrine families (shakke), there has never been any instance of conducting a memorial service (kuyō). During the Kanbun era, an order was issued to the effect that Shinto rites and Buddhist ceremonies should not be mixed together; therefore, although the role of the officiating monks (sōsō) exists at the New Year's御棚会 (Otanakai) ceremony, it has been suspended. On this occasion, as part of a general policy of economy and reduction, the disbursements to shrine treasures and various officials have all been reduced. Nevertheless, if Shinto rites and Buddhist ceremonies were to be intermingled, we feel it would be improper not to report this and seek approval. Therefore, we humbly request that, with the unanimous agreement of the shrine families, it be decreed that the relic offering ceremony (shari kuyō) shall not take place this time, and that matters be settled quietly. We would be most grateful if this could be granted. This is the substance of what was said.
