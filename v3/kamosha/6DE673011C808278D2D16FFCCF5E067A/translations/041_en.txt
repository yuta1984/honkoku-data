[Continuation of the Written Memorandum (Kōjō-oboye):]

"...This is by no means something that occurs on every occasion, and yet there are numerous precedents. Furthermore, in matters such as this, it has been unheard of for a memorial offering ceremony (kuyō) to be conducted without the shrine families (shake) first receiving instructions from the authorities of both the court and the military government (kōbu). During the Kanbun era (1661–1673), an order was issued that ritual Shinto ceremonies (shinji) and Buddhist memorial services (hōji) should not be mixed together. Consequently, although the officiating monks (gusō) had previously held a role in the New Year's Otanaë ceremony, this was discontinued. On this occasion, in the spirit of economization (kanryaku), the divine treasures (go-shimpō) and the stipends (gekyōmono) for all shrine functionaries have been reduced across the board. Under these circumstances, if Shinto rites and Buddhist services were to be intermingled, we feel it would be improper not to bring this to your attention and formally decline. Therefore, we humbly request that, on this occasion, all shrine families be united in declining the Shari-kuyō ceremony, and that we be graciously exempted from holding it. We would be most grateful if this request were granted."

The above was the substance of the statement.

*Note: The precise order of events may differ [from the original], but this is the general gist of what was conveyed.

It was decided that two persons would go out the following day to deliver the written notification of the date and time of the relocation ceremony (sengū) to the following recipients: Lord Nijō Echizen-no-kami, Lord Aki-no-kami, Lord Tango-no-kami, and the four Construction Overseers (Zōgū-bugyō) along with their yoriki (subordinate officers).
