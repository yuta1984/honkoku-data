3rd Day: Shokkyū and Norihisa, acting as miscellaneous administrators (zassho), were dispatched to the residence of Kanō Geki. They were requested to handle the matter of the paintings inserted between the seven sections of the aforementioned engi (founding record).

5th Day: The Middle Counselor (Chūnagon) of Kajūji [it seems] paid a visit to the shrine. Offerings of sacred streamers (heihaku) were presented at the stone altar (sekidan) inside the Rōmon (the two-storied gate). Two hundred hiki were paid to the shrine as the offering fee (heiryō). However, the intermediary for the offerings was Suehisa. The offerings were first placed in the Heiden (offering hall) and then brought out. Generally, [the procession] approaches from the west side of the central gate (chūmon), while in this case it proceeded from the east side. When brought before the deity, [the procession] proceeds as far as the Chinese-style door (karado) and the main raised floor (ōyuka) of the central gate. The ceremonial procedures were as usual.

6th Day: The inspector officials (yokome-shū) of the shogunate (marginal note: one person, Shōsuke-dono) came to pay their respects, and proceeded directly toward Kibune, so Shokkyū and Norihisa followed on horseback to catch up ■. After [the party] stopped by Kurama, sake was offered to them at the worship hall (haiden) of the subsidiary shrine at Kifune (Kibunejinja).

Today, twelve musicians performed music two or three times at the御札屋 (ofudaya, the amulet distribution house). This was because a donor had contributed musical instruments for use during the shrine's sacred festivals (shinji), and [instruments including] drums (taiko), shō (mouth organ), hichiriki (double-reed flute), kakko (small barrel drum), and others had been received...
