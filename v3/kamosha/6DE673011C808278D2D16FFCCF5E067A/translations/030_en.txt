(Continued from previous page) It is said to be the same as freshly woven cloth of today.

The decorative curtain stand (mikichō): the upper portion is white, and the lower two-tenths (two bu) is dyed in sappanwood red (suō-zome), forming the hem section (susoko).
　On the front are two-width panels (futa-no), and two-width panels at the back; the side panels each lack about two or three sun if made from a single width, so two widths of fabric were used and overlapped inward. The cords feature root-bamboo fastenings (neshino) and spiral knot ties (minawamushubi), among others.
　There appears to be more than four shaku of bright green (moegi-iro) silk, with hanging cords attached at various points.

Inside the mikichō curtain stand: a tatami mat, on top of which a cushioned seat (oshitone), and on top of that a hanging corner fitting (kake-kado) [possibly brass (shinchū)].

A sacred mirror (mikagami) [the hanging corner fitting is □ at the front, and the mirror is at the back]. Apart from these, nothing else was found. Outside, there was also a newly made box of approximately one shaku two sun in height and one shaku in width, constructed of molded board (momiban), designed like a carrying chest (tansu) with a lock at its core, openable by inserting a key from above, with a thin cloth serving as its cover. It was wondered whether this box certainly contained a sacred pillow (o-makura) or perhaps a reliquary (shāritō); the matter remains unclear.

The gohei sticks (御幣串), large jars (ōkame), tatami mats, and the like were placed in the Takakura storehouse at the time of the temporary enshrinement ceremony (kari-sengū).
