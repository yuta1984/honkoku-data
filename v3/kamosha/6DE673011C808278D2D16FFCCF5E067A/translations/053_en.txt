This time, the aoi-maru (hollyhock roundel) design was adopted.

Regarding the御茵 (oshitone, a cushion/mat for divine use): During the Tenshō and Kōji eras, small hollyhock (ko-aoi) woven fabric was used as the border, with the center featuring a white twill weave (shiroaya) with a ground pattern of saki-aoi (split hollyhock), and a shiki-maru (layered roundel) as the upper pattern. The "ko-aoi" refers to the small hollyhock pattern used at the Imperial Court, possibly depicting flowering brambles within a diamond-lattice design. In the fifth year of Kan'ei, this design was applied to the sword bag and bow bag. This time, the ko-aoi pattern unique to this shrine was used, with the upper pattern rendered in red thread as a tomoe-no-maru (comma-swirl roundel).

Kifune Shrine has used the tomoe-maru design since ancient times. At our shrine, so as not to change the design of the sacred seat, either the shiki-hana-maru (layered flower roundel) or the aoi [lacuna] design has been used — this occasion marks the first time [the new design has been employed].

御茶碗 (sacred tea bowl): silver-plated, with a matching silver-plated stand. 御盃 (sacred cup): silver-plated, with a gold-plated stand carved with peony designs in the horse-head stand (batō-dai) style.
御箸 (sacred chopsticks): silver-plated stand.
The御屏風 (folding screen),御衣架 (clothing rack), large platform (daishōji), willow box (yanai-bako), water pitcher (hanzō), and square washbasin (tsuno-darai) are all the same as before.

Today's items are for the Main Shrine only.

The fabric covers (yudan) of the karabitsu (Chinese-style chests) are all made of Yamato brocade with a five-color aoi (hollyhock) crest pattern.
