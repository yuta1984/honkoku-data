Next, (Ujinori) said that the carving of a "baku" (貘, a mythical beast) attached to the object called "o-makura" (御枕, sacred pillow) in the inner sanctum (御内陣) of this shrine is a great error. Perhaps someone was misled by the term "pillow" (枕) and added it, or perhaps it was done deliberately to conceal a secret. The fundamental nature (of this object) relates to the Three Sacred Regalia (三種神器), and the "pillow" represents the jewel (玉, the curved jewel/magatama). Among all the shrines throughout the land, this shrine alone is said to enshrine a complete set of the Three Sacred Regalia. In ancient times, this sacred mirror [red ink annotation: o-makura, kaketsuno o-makura] — the three types combined — had a stand beneath them, a stand in the shape of the kana character "ku" (く). It appears that a "hire" (領布, a type of ceremonial scarf) was attached. As for the dimensions, each successive master of shrine-moving ceremonies (遷宮師) measured by placing their elbow on the tatami and—

The 7th day: A sudden storm (夕立) occurred before dawn, causing a great flood; damage from flooding was reported in various places. The shrine precinct was unharmed. It is said that two sake merchant workers in Kyoto were swept away and drowned. As a result, the shrine attendants (神人) from Nishikamo did not come. The letter delivered to Genbansuke at the Taguchi meeting hall (田口会所) reads as follows: "Given these circumstances, it would be impossible for the shrine attendants to cross the river. Please wait for a short while. Should they absolutely fail to attend, we shall consult on the matter and determine an alternative course of action."
