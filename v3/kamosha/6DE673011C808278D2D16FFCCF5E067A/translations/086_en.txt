It was privately communicated to the Tensō (court intermediary) that it was the deepest wish of the uji-bito (shrine-affiliated persons) of Junior Third Rank (Shōsanmi) to be promoted to Senior Fourth Rank Upper Grade (Jushiinojō) as a reward for their service in the current construction project. However, the reply received was that such promotion could not be granted to those without established precedent.

The old shrine building (furumikoto) of the inner sanctuary (oku-yashiro) of Kibune Shrine was demolished today. The overseers (bugyō) were Minbunojō Kōken and Kurando-no-kami Hōken, the two of them, assisted by two carpenters (daiku) and six laborers (genin).

11th: Carpentry work was carried out beneath the roof of the timber storage building (kiya).

12th (Rain fell after nightfall): At a gathering (yoriai), it was decided that surplus items — including small eight-legged stands (ko-hakkya) and large eight-legged stands (ō-hakkya) decorated with aogai (blue shell inlay) — would be disposed of by sealed bidding (irefuda). The bidding was held today. Eight aogai-decorated stands (hakkya) went for twelve monme, won by the kannagi (shrine priest). Another lot went for seven monme two bu, won by Ujitoku. The eight aogai-decorated stands for Kibune Shrine went for six monme, won by Norihisa. The large eight-legged stands went for six monme eight bu.

Kiho came and brought a bound volume (issatsu) of written records concerning the matters of the lower and upper Kamo shrines' military encampment protocols (jin-gi kakitate). A copy was made and submitted as a separate volume.

It was decided that remnant pieces of the interior sanctuary's御几帳 (mikichō, hanging curtains/screens) would be distributed, even if only in small amounts, through the jitte (administrative relay network).
