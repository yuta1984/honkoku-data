[Continued from the previous page: Regarding the imperial decree (senjī) for the land registers (fūki/honogi) of Kifune Shrine and Kataoka Shrine]

During the year, [the decree] was sent via the Kanmu (Office of Shrine Affairs) to the chief priest's residence (kannushi-tei), to be held against the day of the shrine's relocation ceremony (sengū). However, it was simply set aside without purpose. [It was decided that] when the imperial decree messenger (senjī-tsukai) came on a future occasion, it should then be duly presented.

28th Day: Clear weather. At the Hour of the Sheep (approximately 2:00 p.m.), the imperial decree messenger (senjī-tsukai) Yamana Kazue Miyoshi-no-suke-kane arrived, with Deputy Messenger (fukushi) Aoki Tanomo Toshiyuki, and the carrier of the inspection box (ranbako-mochi) being a junior retainer (aozamurai) of the Kanmu.

The ceremonial protocol for the arrival (sankō) was the same as before. However, today, concerning the [imperial decrees] for Kifune Shrine and Kataoka Shrine, [the ritual priest] Shukkyū appeared before the Shingū Shrine in full court dress (sokutai) and received [the decrees] while standing. The ceremonial procedure was as follows: the imperial decree messenger entered through the gate and faced west while positioned on the eastern side. Shukkyū faced east, with a distance of approximately one ken (approx. 1.8 m) between them, and both parties bowed simultaneously (ichiyu). Next, the carrier of the inspection box (ranbako) passed it to the decree messenger, who then passed it to Shukkyū; Shukkyū received it together with attendants dressed in lacquered caps (eboshi) and plain silk robes (hōi) (these were hired men from Kyoto). While standing, Shukkyū received and inspected (haiken/hiken) the contents, after which both parties bowed to each other once again and withdrew facing one another.

Next, [the procession] went out through the Tama Bridge (Tamabashi), paid respects at Suwa Shrine, and waited at the upper end of the bridge hall (hashidono) — at the foot of the bridge (hashimoto) — facing Kataoka Shrine. Then, the ritual priest (hafuri-shoku) Shukkyū of Kataoka [came forward] wearing full court dress (sokutai) and inner robes (shitagasane), coming from behind the two-storied gate (rōmon) via the Tama Bridge. [He maintained] a distance of approximately one ken from the decree messenger, and both parties bowed simultaneously once.
