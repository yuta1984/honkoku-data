The aforementioned items were placed in the御殿 (shrine hall) and distributed to the administrators (沙汰人).

A barrel (five-shō capacity) was sent by the magistrates (奉行衆) to Shōjin-ji temple, and the ridgepole barrel was distributed among the carpenters and others as follows:
(One load to the shrine families [社家中]; one load to the merchants [買人]; one load to the carpenters; one load as a gratuity to the magistrates.)

The ritual offerings and stipends (御料) were distributed in the same manner.

At the time of the ridge-raising ceremony for the Gon-sha (auxiliary shrine), four kanmon in coins were hung, but in lieu of this, eighteen monme in silver was handed to the carpenters. Of this, nine monme was taken by the deputy head carpenter (権大工), and the remaining nine monme was administered and taken by the chief of the deputy master carpenter (権棟梁長). The same arrangement applies this time as well. The coins hung on the ridgepole are to be returned to the shrine community.

According to the account of Uemon: In the Kan'ei era, of the two ladles (銚子), one was to be taken by the chief carpenter (正大工) and one by the master carpenter (棟梁). As various people of the time intervened to mediate due to disputes over this, a written pledge was obtained from them before the items were bestowed.

Today, the shimenawa (sacred rope) pulled down from the ridgepole was cut up and taken piece by piece by miscellaneous workers (雑人) to use as amulets, and everything was stolen before it could reach the chief carpenter. It would be advisable to assign a magistrate to prevent this from happening.

Sixth Day: Sand was spread in the courtyard of the lecture hall (講所), and the ceremony to determine the layout for the foundation-purification ritual (鎮壇) was held.

Seventh Day: Rainy weather. Those in attendance at the meeting: Shrine priest Hoka, Korehisa, Shokuhisa.
