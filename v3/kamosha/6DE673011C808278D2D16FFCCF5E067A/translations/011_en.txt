(Continued from the previous page) There was a desire to arrange things in accordance with the old records, and once the certificate of transmission (一札) had been issued, the relationship with the Mori family was already beyond repair. First, even when the messenger bearing the imperial proclamation (宣旨使) for the provisional shrine (権社) came during the tenure as Provisional Negi (権祢宜), the daily schedule proclamation (日時の宣旨) was not received. Moreover, during the provisional shrine transfer ceremony (仮遷宮), although all matters of the inner sanctum (内陣) rites were supposed to be under their administration, they were obstructed by the head priest (神主), causing immeasurable frustration and resentment, so it is said. Furthermore, as for the head priest's own thinking: since there had already been a transmission of traditions (伝授) in the line since Yasunori (保矩), and given that the sacred treasures and other matters were largely known, there was no reason to formally issue a certificate. Moreover, if it were merely a matter of such a certificate, anything beyond one silver coin as a courtesy fee would be unseemly.

This is entirely an unnecessary expense. In any case, I had been passing time thinking I wished to hear all matters as they unfolded, but it has turned into an unexpected quarrel and breach (of relations). It is a truly absurd situation, and difficult to put into words.

The 5th day: Clear; a summer shower fell before dawn.
The 6th day: I was on duty. Ujinori said that the records of Morinaga (維久) of the Mori family were written on dandoshi (high-quality paper) with yellow dye (黄檗歟) applied; there is virtually no worm damage, and most of it is written in katakana. The contents are largely written out, with diagrams in certain places, and beneath the pasted-over sections, everything is annotated, with marks of acknowledgment (合点) inserted to correspond with the written entries on the right.

[In red ink] Kiyoshige states: It is not that yellow dye (黄檗) was applied to the dandoshi; rather, it is simply paper that was yellow in color for that era. Also, it is not katakana but regular kana (hiragana).
