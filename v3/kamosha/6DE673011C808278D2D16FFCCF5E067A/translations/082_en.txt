This key should remain in the custody of Hōka, sealed and placed in the key box. Furthermore, the imperial decrees (sōji), messenger edicts (senmyō), and related documents issued on each occasion should be stored in the shrine's repository. However, on this occasion, the fact that they were kept by the chief priest (kannagi) as well as the households of both the Kibune shrine priests (negi) is an improper matter.

Regarding the imperial decrees of the Fonogi (land survey records), the portions belonging to Ōta Shrine and Shingū Shrine were kept at the two residences of Nobunao and Suenori — all of this is likewise improper.

On the 27th day of the 9th month, the officials assigned to the Kataoka Shrine's formal relocation ceremony (Shō-sengū) were as follows:

Seikanji Ben'ki served as the designated lodging host, and Nuinono-no-gonnosuke Dōshun performed the same duty.

The Shōshi, Shishō, two Sayu Kanzō officials, and six Kamon-ryō officials —
　lodging was arranged by Nobunao's son, Wakasa.

The Ōkura-shō, Mokuryo, two Mikura officials, and the Shusuishi — six officials in total —
　lodged at the residence of Sakon-shōgen Ujimichi.

Two attendants (meshitsukai), two deputy envoys (fukushi), two Tonomore-ryō officials, and two Shibu — eight persons in total — lodged at the residence of Daizen-no-gonnosuke Nagahisa. As the hosts and entertainers within the shrine precincts, Kuranonosuke and Hayato-no-shō, two persons, were assigned to accompany them.

On the 23rd of the previous month, during the formal relocation ceremony of Kibune Shrine, all of these officials had likewise attended. Additionally, the Tensō-kata's Chōkei (a senior court official serving as liaison) also made a visit to Kibune.
