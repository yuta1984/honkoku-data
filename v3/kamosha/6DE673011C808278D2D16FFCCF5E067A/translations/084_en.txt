However, since the stipend rice (kajōmai) from the bakufu (shogunate) was excessive, this time the matter became a negotiation with the Kuraryo (Imperial Storehouse Bureau), and by the adjudication of the various districts, it was ordered that the main shrine's portion be prepared by the Accounting Office (Suitō) and the Ministry of Treasury (Ōkurashō), while the Kibune portion be prepared by the Kuraryo. ◦Today, Izuguard (Izunokami) Ujihiro died of illness. He was fifty-eight years old.

3rd Day: In the early hours of this morning, the wife of Hyōbujo, a woman named Koman, died of illness. She was fifty-five years old.

4th Day: Today, a private written message (kōjōgaki) was submitted to the bakufu.

5th Day: At the evening meal, a farewell banquet was held for those departing for Edo.

7th Day: A meeting (yoriai) was held, and the response letter from Tonomorikamis (Tonorikamis) was submitted.

8th Day (Clear): Four people departed for Edo: Korehisa, Narihisa, Yasumori, and Hirokazu. Of these, Hirokazu's journey was privately requested in connection with a lawsuit over the horse-racing territory (keiba-ryō), while the other three were sent to offer thanks for the recent shrine reconstruction (gozōei). They departed at noon.

9th Day: Today, regarding the dispute between Chikurin ■ and Seishō, they were summoned by the bakufu and attended. It was determined that the matter did not require a formal face-to-face hearing (taiketsu), and it was ordered that Seishō be appointed as kanbō (temple superintendent), making Seishō the winner. Ultimately, this was in accordance with the bakufu's intention that a temple should not be allowed to flourish within shrine precincts, and thus documents such as the kanbō appointment letter could not be recognized...
