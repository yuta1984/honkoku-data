Today, a person involved in a land dispute with Furu Genbanokami, a commoner (hyakushō) of Shimogamo, appeared at the deliberation office (hyōgisho). A six-month magistrate appointment was made (with strikethrough in original), and a departing person said that recently, Okihisa, Shokhisa, Korehisa, and Narihisa had been gathering at Junpei's residence, reviewing the old records of the Torii-Ōji family day and night. It was said that this was a scheme to have the current shrine superintendent removed from office. In detail: since those individuals had earnestly requested that the shrine superintendent privately transmit the procedures for the Mori family's Sengū ceremony and other related matters, Korehisa had been acting as an intermediary in various matters and had broadly reached an agreement. However, the most important matters were not spoken of openly; they had expected to receive the transmission when the provisional Sengū (Kari-Sengū) drew near, but when the Mori family indicated that this would be difficult, they largely understood the situation. As for Yasuka, it was said that while claiming there should be a transmission of a particular tradition (ichiryū), in practice there was no sign of any such transmission having taken place.

Furthermore, the instructions from the Mori family went unheeded, and after the provisional Sengū, relations became strained. It was said that there was a scheme to investigate internal failings and this time submit a formal complaint. In sum, the Mori family's position was that—while they knew nothing of times before Kan'ei, Tenshō, and Kōji—at least since the third year of Kagen, the Mori family had performed the Sengū on every occasion, and therefore the detailed ceremonial procedures had been recorded and passed down through generations. This is the first Sengū since the seven families were formally designated as such. While the seven families have been making use of the Mori family's old records and coordinating accordingly, people from other families find it difficult to properly carry out the Sengū, and the Mori family wishes to preserve their transmitted traditions for all generations to come.
