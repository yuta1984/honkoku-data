Among the shrine officials (shashi), the acting head priest (nadai shōhafuri), Shigeyoshi, had his aunt pass away around the 7th of the month, and due to mourning obligations, a servant was summoned from the Konoe residence to serve as substitute (Uneme and Shigeyoshi designated as proxies).

The substitute for the Shingū priest (shingū hafuri) is his younger brother, Norichisa. The ranked groups of uji-bito (hereditary clan members) are as follows:
- 1st group: Daikenmotsu, Ujikane
- 2nd group: Orибe no Shō, Yasuyoshi
- 3rd group: Ukyō no Suke, Yasuta
- 4th group: Umanotsuke, Kiyonori
- 5th group: Hatsuichishō, Fukuhisa
- 6th group: Nuidono no Suke, Sueyuki
- 7th group: Daizennoshin, Motoaki
- 8th group: Kōzuke no Kami, Ujiharu
- 9th group: Sakon-shōgen, Ujimichi
- 10th group: Moku-gonnosuke, Suemichi

On the day of the ceremony, the shrine officials (shashi) are to wear formal court dress (sokutai), while the lower-ranking uji-bito are to wear red-collared large hitatare (ōkazuki).

Note: The wearing of sokutai by shrine officials is at discretion (zuii), following the instructions of the Tendai-so (transmission official). During the Kan'ei era, the dress was informal court dress (ikan) and a plain white robe (chihaya); this time as well, the five-official roles (gokan-yaku) are to wear chihaya.

11th day, rainy. All those in sacred retreat (sanrō-shū) perform daily shrine visits without exception, each making one circuit of the shrine precincts. This is in preparation for the Main Shrine Relocation Ceremony (shō-sengū) to take place on the coming 16th.

12th day, clear. As the sacred treasures (go-shimbō) will be displayed for public viewing at the Ofuda-ya (amulet hall) tomorrow, today the treasures are moved from the head priest's residence into the Shinpō-ya (treasure hall), as well as into the Chō-ya (administrative hall) and Takakura (elevated storehouse). The transmission of the sengū (relocation ceremony) rites was conveyed to both the Gon-hafuri Shigeyoshi (重栄) and the Ujigami-hafuri Sakunao (作直).

13th day, clear. The sacred treasures were displayed for viewing at the Ofuda-ya.
