These are: a directive (下知状) from the Ōei era; a bequest document from the master; an exchange document with Umanosuke; writings of the senior monk Ei (永首座); and a letter from Yamamoto Kanemon. This Yamamoto Kanemon resided in Iwakura, and his wife was reportedly the aunt of Seishō. For this reason, (the Yamamoto family) had been Seishō's family's patrons (檀那) since his father's generation. According to the written response from Seishō: at the time of arbitration before the Edo magistrate of temples and shrines (寺社奉行), it was stated that (Chikurinji) was not listed in Tōfukuji's register of sub-temples. When the current head monk Sekizōsu entered the temple, three members of Sekizōsu's group—who were Kyoto townspeople—drew up a caretaker's document (看坊状) and handed it to Seishō, but the crucial seal/signature (判形) of Sekizōsu was absent, and it is also said that the seal of the father known as Den'emon was likewise missing. Yet it is written as if such a seal exists.
