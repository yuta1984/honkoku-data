A sea bream stand (tai-dai) was placed in the center, followed by cups (made of earthenware), then the offerings (goryō), and after mochi and shitogi rice cakes were cut and three rounds of sacred sake were offered, everyone withdrew. However, before the sake, the construction overseers (zōei bugyō) distributed folded paper (origami, likely containing gratuities) to the various functionaries from atop the southern stairs, as was done at the time of the Gonden (temporary shrine). The carpenters sat beneath the cut-board area of the administrative building (chōya) and received their sake.

Today, as I was on duty, I performed the provisional role of the senior officiant during the offering ceremony. The cloth drawn from the ridge of the roof was one hiki (a unit of cloth). Additionally, two tan of rough cloth (arabuno) were used for knee padding, and the head carpenter's knee-padding cloth appears to have been a remnant piece.

　　Regarding the above offerings (goryō):
One portion (kata-gozen) goes to the head priest (kannushi); one portion to the rotating duty officer (rinban); one portion is divided in two, going to the shrine officials (shashi) and clan members (ujibito); one portion goes to the overseers (bugyō-shū); and one further portion is divided in two, to be received by the tōne and the jinin (shrine servants).

Regarding the bow-string cloth for the ridge ceremony: one tan each (the bows and arrows being made of barrel wood, four-sun square timber). It is said that two lengths of cloth for the strings of two bows should go to the head carpenter (shō-daiku). For the head master carpenter (shō-tōryō), it is said that he should receive half of all the bow-and-arrow cloth.
