(Continued from previous page) ...Regarding the divine rites, when the Shōhafuri position is vacant, the shrine officials of the Seven Houses (shichike no shaji) shall confer among themselves and perform the duties, and for the replacement, the eldest legitimate son (chakudan) among the children of the shrine officials of the Seven Houses shall be appointed to perform these duties. This was duly decreed.

**Kyōhō 2nd Year (From the 1st Day of the 1st Month to the 29th Day of the 3rd Month)**
Index of Key Documents

**Kyōhō 2nd Year (1717), 6th Day of the 1st Month**
Item: It was decreed that documents to be submitted by the shrine (Kamigamo Shrine), the Five Houses (goke), the Hayashi House, and the ujibito community shall each be prepared as separate volumes and duly submitted.

**17th Day of the Same Month**
Item: Since, as had been decreed at the time of the minor festival (shōsai) in the previous winter, the existing practice (sharei) could not be maintained and the lineages and standing of the ujibito would be brought to ruin, a written statement of oral representation (kōjō-gaki) was submitted.

**22nd Day of the 2nd Month**
Item: The shrine families (shake) of Shimogamo were summoned to the Office of the Imperial Household (gosho-shi / Tensō's office), where inquiries were made regarding the ranks and standing of the Shimogamo shrine officials and ujibito.

**13th Day of the 3rd Month**
Item: Representatives (sōdai) of Shingū and below were summoned and appeared, whereupon inquiries were made regarding: (1) a matter stating that among the festival duties listed in the festival register (gosai-moku), those pertaining to Kifune (Kibune Shrine) and above shall be performed by the Seven Houses; and (2) among the records of precedents (kan-rei) previously submitted, inquiries were made regarding the daikan role and matters pertaining to mutual consultation (katawai). It was (decreed that) a written document should be submitted in response.

**14th Day of the Same Month**
Item: Regarding the inquiries of the previous day, one copy each of a written document was submitted to the Tensō Bugyō (Commissioner of Imperial Transmissions).

Item: The genealogy derived from a single individual of the extant lineage of the Kamo clan makes no distinction between the main line (chaku) and collateral lines (sho) within a single document.
