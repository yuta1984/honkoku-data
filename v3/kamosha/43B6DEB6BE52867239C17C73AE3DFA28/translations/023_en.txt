**Extract from the response document sent in reply to an inquiry from the Magistrate's Office (go-bugyōsho) on the same day:**

One matter: The officials known as *Tone* are those who stand above the *jinin* (shrine attendants) in rank. They are responsible for handling the sacred food offerings (*shinku*) and for serving at the table on behalf of the shrine priests (*shashi*). These *Tone* wear what is called *hakue* ("white robe"), a type of garment in the category of *hakuchō* (coarse white hemp clothing).

One matter: Those called *jinin* are the lowest-ranking among the official personnel. They serve as bearers (*tantō*), carrying the sacred palanquins and offerings. Beyond this, they perform all manner of humble duties. However, as they are people of ancient origin, they have been entrusted with carrying sacred *sakaki* branches and the like. The character *jinin* (神人, "shrine person") has long been in use, though it is possible that in ancient times it may have been written as *chijin* (地人, "earth person") — this has yet to be verified. In all matters, they perform their duties in the courtyard (*niwae*) and on the ground (*chijō*). For this reason, these *jinin* have traditionally worn yellow robes (*kige*), taking the color of earth as their symbol.

**12th Month, 7th Day**

One matter: The representatives of the Shingū and the shrines below it were summoned, and the representatives Taiken and Kiyoshige appeared before the Transmission Officials (*tensō*) and requested an audience. There was an imperial inquiry (*chokumon*) and an imperial response (*chokutō*), and a written statement regarding matters to be inquired of the Five Houses was submitted.
