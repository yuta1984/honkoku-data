**Extract from the response document submitted in reply to an inquiry from the Magistrate's Office on the same day:**

One matter: The official known as the *tōne* is a functionary who stands above the *jinnin* (shrine people). This person oversees the handling of divine offerings (shinku) and performs the service of arranging and presenting food offerings on behalf of the shrine priests (shashi). These *tōne* wear what is called *hakui* ("white garments"), a type of clothing in the category of *hakuchō* (plain white vestments).

One matter: Those referred to as *jinnin* are the lowest-ranking among the functionaries. They are bearers who carry the divine palanquin and offerings. Beyond this, they perform all manner of lowly duties. Nevertheless, as they are persons of ancient lineage, they have traditionally been entrusted with carrying sacred *sakaki* branches and the like. The written character used for *jinnin* (神人) has been in use for a long time; however, it has not yet been examined whether in earlier ages the term may have been written as *chijin* (地人, "people of the earth"). In all things, they perform duties on the grounds and upon the earth. For this reason, these *jinnin* have traditionally worn yellow robes, taking yellow as the color symbolic of earth.

**12th Month, 7th Day**

One matter: The representatives of the Shingū and other [shrines], summoned by [imperial] command, appeared — namely the in総代 (general representative), Taiken, and Kiyoshige. Regarding the matter of petitioning for an audience with the Tenshō [imperial liaison officer / Tennō's messenger], there were an imperial inquiry (chokumon) and an imperial response (chokutō). A written document was submitted concerning the matter inquired of the members of the Five Houses.
