…However, among them, those who have been appointed to shrine posts are referred to as "shashi" (shrine officials), while those who have not yet been appointed are customarily called "ujibito." Even those referred to as "hereditary shrine officials" (sōden no shashi) in the adjudication document (gosaikyojō) of the fourth year of Kanbun are exclusively persons who have been formally appointed to shrine posts. As long as they have not yet received such appointments, the eldest sons of hereditary shrine officials and all others below them are, without exception, non-office-holding ujibito (hishoku no ujibito). We therefore humbly petition that an order be issued permitting all ujibito who have been granted court ranks to wear the robe (hō) equally and without distinction.

**One matter:** Regarding the question of court ranks for non-office-holding ujibito — how is this matter to be handled? We were instructed by the Court Transmitter (Gidensō), Lord Nakamikado Sukehiro, that persons under the age of twenty must not submit such petitions, which caused us great distress. Nevertheless, since petitions had been submitted on behalf of those of young age since earlier times, we had been restraining ourselves and waiting for an opportune moment to raise the matter again. In the meantime, when the young, non-office-holding eldest sons of the Seven Houses petitioned for the bestowal of court rank, and as we all submitted our petitions together, imperial sanction (chokukyо̄) was granted, for which we are most humbly grateful. Yet, as stated above, there appears to be an intention to treat this matter as an exceptional case applicable only to certain parties, which again causes us great distress.

The rank of Senior Third Rank (Shō Sanmi) for the Head Priests (kannushi) of this shrine, and Junior Third Rank (Ju Sanmi) for the Hafuri priests, are matters for which there is no direct precedent, yet when we petitioned on the basis of analogous precedents, imperial sanction was graciously granted in recognition of reverence for the deities, and we humbly regard this as an honor befitting the dignity of so great a shrine. Furthermore, regarding the matter of ujibito receiving imperial sanction for court ranks while still in childhood, there exist precedents going back to ages of seven and eight years, and this is even recorded in the shrine's own record of ranks (kurai-den).
