…Among them, those appointed to shrine posts are referred to as "shashi" (shrine officials), while those not yet appointed are customarily called "ujibito." Even those referred to as "hereditary shashi" (sōden no shashi) in the judicial decree (gosakyojō) of the fourth year of Kanbun [1664] are persons who have already been appointed to shrine posts. Until such appointment is made, the eldest sons of hereditary shashi and all others below them are non-office-holding ujibito. We therefore respectfully petition that an order be issued permitting all ujibito who have been granted court ranks to wear the rank robe without exception.

One matter: Regarding the question of court ranks for non-office-holding ujibito — we were instructed, during the tenure of Court Transmitter (gidensō) Lord Nakamikado Sukehiro, that rank petitions were not to be submitted on behalf of those under twenty years of age. This caused us great distress. However, since petitions had previously been submitted on behalf of young children from of old, we held back, waiting for an appropriate moment to make our request. In the meantime, as the young, non-office-holding eldest sons of the Seven Houses petitioned for the bestowal of noble rank (joshaku), we submitted the petition collectively on their behalf, whereupon imperial sanction (chokkyō) was graciously granted, for which we are most grateful.

With all due respect, as stated above, since there are those intending to treat the matter as exceptional, we find this, too, to be most troubling. The rank of Shōsanmi (Senior Third Rank) for the head priest (kannagi) and Jūsanmi (Junior Third Rank) for the negi (ritualist) of this shrine have no direct precedents, but when we petitioned on the basis of analogous examples (hōrei), imperial sanction was graciously granted in recognition of piety toward the gods (keishin), and the standing of a great shrine was thereby established, for which we are deeply grateful. Moreover, regarding the granting of imperial sanction for court ranks to ujibito while still young children, there are precedents involving children of seven and eight years of age, and these have been recorded in this shrine's own registry of ranks (kurai-den)… *(continued on the next page)*
