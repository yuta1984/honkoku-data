**25th Day of the Fourth Month**

One matter: For this year's *o-matsuri* (Kamo Festival), the *Konoe* messenger did not attend; instead, the *Giso* (Imperial Liaison Officer) and the *bugyō* attended in his place.

---

**6th Day of the Fifth Month** *(Extracted entry ○)*

One matter: The *bugyō* sent word that the general representatives (*sōdai*) of the combined shrine (*issha*), as well as the representatives of the Five Houses — Hayashi and Okamoto — should appear promptly. Each duly appeared, whereupon five questions were put to the combined shrine representatives. As for the Five Houses: the inquiry was that, regarding the contents of the written directive (*o-kakitsuke*) issued last winter, a formal acknowledgment (*o-uke*) had not yet been submitted. As for Hayashi and Okamoto: the inquiry was whether "the formal acknowledgment submitted last winter still stands as stated."

---

**1st Day [of the Same Month]** *(Marked ○)*

One matter: The *Ashizoroe* ceremony proceeded as in previous years. On this day, it is the established custom (*sharei*) of the shrine that the head priest (*kannushi*), when taking his seat, wears the patterned *sashinuki* (formal divided skirt) even if he does not hold Third Rank. However, today Tsuguhisa, when taking his seat, did not wear the patterned *sashinuki*, but instead wore a plain *sashinuki* of pale blue (*asagi*) silk.

---

**4th Day** *(Marked ○)*

One matter: The riders (*norijiri-shū*) of the horse race (*keiba*) paid a visit to Shimogamo Shrine today. However, since this year the *kaichō* (periodic public display of sacred objects) at Kurama Temple had drawn large crowds of people, there was discussion as to whether it was appropriate that they attended without the proper formal procession (*shikishiki no gūbu*). It was therefore decided that for this year, [the riders] would perform a distant worship (*yōhai*) from Nagaregi Shrine.

---

**8th Day of the Same Month**

One matter: Pursuant to the directive issued yesterday, from Shingū and below, abbreviated genealogies (*ryaku-keizu*) of the Sixteen Streams (*jūroku-ryū*), along with genealogical documents (*keizu-kakitsuke*) of the Torii, Ōji, Matsushita, Umetsuj, Mori, Tomino, Hayashi, Okamoto, and other families, were submitted.

One matter: Regarding the Kamo clan, it shall be completed by preparing a written document (*kakitsuke*) showing that, based on a single genealogy, the branches and descendants flourished from a single person named Arimi (*Zanejitsu*) in the middle ages, and [this shall be] submitted.
