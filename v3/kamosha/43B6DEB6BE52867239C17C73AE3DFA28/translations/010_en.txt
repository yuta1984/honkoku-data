There were no longer any public goods to fulfill the votive wishes for the shrine ceremonies. Although the shrine families held stipend fields (kyūden), they could ultimately do no more than stave off hunger and cold; and despite the solemn nature of the divine rites, the shrine families could not bear to witness their decline. They drew upon their stipend fields, contributing a levy of rice per tan (段別の役米), maintaining at least the outward form of the ceremonies. But beyond this there was no other means of support. At this point, the domain holdings (shokuryō) that were to fund the administration of all twenty-one positions — from the chief priest down to the ujigami hafuri — were likewise cut off, and the shrine offices themselves fell into decline. Nevertheless, those currently managing the remaining shrine estates number a mere three or four persons. These individuals have continued to claim the shrine offices in recent times. As for the vacant positions (kekkushoku) without domain holdings, deputies (daikan) were installed,

and the ancient ceremonies associated with those vacant posts were carried out without deviation from the old procedures. Thereafter, although there were no domain holdings, persons occasionally came forward lamenting the decline of shrine custom and seeking to claim shrine offices. However, those already serving at the time refused to allow others to be appointed, claiming these to be hereditary positions (hudai no shoku), and disputes arose repeatedly. These disputes ultimately reached the ears of the Emperor (叡聞) during the Kan'ei era (1624–1644), imperial inquiries were made on multiple occasions, and a full deliberation (御僉議) was conducted among the various noble houses. The conclusion was that an imperial rescript was issued to the various houses declaring: "The Kamo Agatanushi are of a single lineage, with no distinction between main and collateral lines; the imperial ordinance that shrine offices be filled by selecting those of suitable character and transferring them progressively through the ranks is beyond dispute."
