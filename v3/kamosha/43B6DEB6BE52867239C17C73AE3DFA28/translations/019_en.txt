**[Continued from the 29th day of the 1st month, Kyōhō 3 (1718)]**

Item: Matsushita Tsunehisa's son, Yukimaru (Teruhisa), performed his coming-of-age ceremony (genpuku) today. At ten years of age, he visited the shrine dressed in court cap and layered robes (ikan-kasane). The details of the coming-of-age ceremony are recorded separately.

**[6th day of the 2nd month]**

Item: Regarding Matsushita Teruhisa's recent visit to the shrine wearing court cap and robes (ikan): Although there was a precedent from Genroku 10 (1697) in which non-office-holding ujibito (shrine clan members not holding official shrine posts) were instructed that wearing court cap and robes was unnecessary, the matter of Teruhisa's visit in court dress was brought up for discussion, and the question was raised as to whether it would be appropriate for all members to collectively petition for the right to wear court dress. The group unanimously agreed that this would indeed be appropriate.

**[14th day of the same month]**

Item: Regarding the wearing of the court robe (hō) and the time limits for rank conferral, Hogawari and Kiyomo appeared in person to submit an oral statement (kōjō-gaki).

**[Extracted Document (nukigaki)]**

Item: A memorandum of a humble petition, respectfully submitted.

Item: Yukimaru (Great Officer Teruhisa), the son of Acting Negi Matsushita Minbu Tsunehisa and a non-office-holding ujibito, recently underwent his coming-of-age ceremony and visited the shrine wearing court cap and robes. It goes without saying that once a court rank has been bestowed, wearing the corresponding rank robe (ihō) is only natural. However, in Genroku 10 (1697), a non-office-holding ujibito by the name of Yamamoto Hyūga Toshikazu, following established precedent, wore court dress and paid worship at the shrine after receiving the Fourth Rank by imperial sanction. At that time, the shrine officials of the Seven Houses objected, stating that there was no precedent for those not appointed to shrine posts to receive and wear the robe.
