**Matsushita Tsunehisa's son, Yukimaru (Teruhisa), came of age ceremony (genpuku) today at ten years old, attended the shrine in court dress (ikan). Details of the coming-of-age ceremony are recorded separately.**

**6th day of the 2nd month:**

Regarding Matsushita Teruhisa's wearing of court dress (ikan) and attendance at the shrine on this occasion: Although in Genroku 10 (1697) it had been decreed that non-officeholding ujibito (clan members without shrine posts) should not wear court dress, the matter of Teruhisa attending the shrine in court dress was brought up for discussion. The question of whether it would be appropriate to collectively petition for the right to wear court dress was deliberated, and all agreed it was a reasonable course of action.

**14th day of the same month:**

Regarding the age requirements for wearing the ceremonial robe (chakuhō) and receiving court rank, Hokaeshige (a person's name, likely "保替清") also appeared in person to submit a written oral statement.

A respectful petition in the form of an oral statement:

The son of Gon-negi (Associate Shrine Priest) Matsushita Mimbu Tsunehisa, the non-officeholding ujibito Yukimaru, known as Tayū Teruhisa, recently completed his coming-of-age ceremony and attended the shrine wearing court dress. We believe that once a court rank has been bestowed, wearing the ceremonial court robe (ibō) appropriate to that rank is a matter of course. However, in Genroku 10, when a non-officeholding ujibito named Yamamoto Hyūga Toshikazu attended shrine worship wearing court dress following precedent after receiving imperial permission for the Fourth Rank, the shrine officials (shashi) of the Seven Houses argued that there was no precedent for those not appointed to shrine posts wearing the ceremonial robe.
