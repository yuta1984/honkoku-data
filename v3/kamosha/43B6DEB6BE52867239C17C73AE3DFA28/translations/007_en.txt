Both parties submitted their claims to the court nobles (kuge), and after repeated deliberations and imperial inquiries, it was determined that "the claims of the ujibito are clearly substantiated by old records and documents." Accordingly, during the reign of Emperor Gosai'in (Go-Sai), several ujibito were appointed as shrine officials in accordance with ancient custom.

Item: Thereafter, in the fourth year of Kanbun (1664), when representatives (sōdai) of the ujibito traveled down to the Kantō (Edo) to submit a petition for the repair of the shrine, those who had previously opposed the ujibito's appointment as shrine officials also traveled down afterward and made the following supplication: "Following the deliberations of the court nobles, several ujibito have been appointed to shrine offices. However, should this be the case, the four or five families who have until now been fulfilling the shrine offices will be permanently relegated to obscurity, and it is impossible to predict when, if ever, they might have the opportunity to become shrine officials. We humbly request that some provision be made for these individuals as well."

This petition was heard and deemed to have merit. The ruling was then made that "henceforth, specific families shall be designated, and the aforementioned persons shall be ordered to fulfill the roles of Negi (Senior Priest) and Hafuri (Minor Officiant) from the head shrine's Kannagi (Chief Priest) down to the Kibune Shrine." Upon hearing this, the ujibito in turn submitted their own supplication, stating: "The Kamo clan stems from a single genealogical line, constituting one surname and one unified lineage, governed by the ancient custom of successive transfer of office. If the families of those persons are permanently designated, then the other ujibito will effectively be treated as separate and distinct persons, which is a matter of extreme inconvenience and distress." This argument too was heard and deemed to have merit. Accordingly, the ujibito who had traveled down as the representative of the ujibito at that time were also added, and for the first time seven families were designated and established.
