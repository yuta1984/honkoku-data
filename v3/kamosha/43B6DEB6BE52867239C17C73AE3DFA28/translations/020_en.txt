**[Extracted Document (nukigaki), continued]**

…A written report was submitted to the Office of the Court Transmitter (Gidensō) and to the Magistrate's Office of the military government (bakufu). However, as objections were raised, it was decreed that while not holding a shrine post, the robe (hō) must not be worn. This caused great distress to all, and we have since been waiting for an opportune moment to make our petition. Given these circumstances, while we have continued to wear the robe for duties directly pertaining to shrine service (shayaku), we have, in all other matters, refrained as a group from wearing the robe while not holding official shrine posts.

Last spring, when the eldest son of Shinto Priest Okamoto Sanmi — known as Kamechiyodayū Hokō — underwent his coming-of-age ceremony, he visited the shrine wearing only a plain white robe (jōe). Yet in the case of Matsushita, despite having previously prevailed in this dispute, he did not obtain permission this time, nor did he consult with the community of shrine families (shake nakama). Instead, the members of five of the Seven Houses colluded amongst themselves and willfully wore court cap and robes (ikan).

Their apparent intention seems to be as follows: by forcing the issue of wearing court dress on this occasion, they seek to establish a precedent whereby the eldest sons of shrine officials (shashi) of the Seven hereditary Houses may henceforth wear court dress as a matter of course even while not holding shrine posts, while making it all the more difficult for other ujibito to do so — effectively creating a distinction between the two groups.

Should matters proceed in such a fashion, no consistent ruling or governance (oshioki) can be applied to all non-office-holding ujibito alike, and this is a source of the utmost distress to us. After all, if wearing the rank robe (ihō) is made impossible, there is no benefit whatsoever in having a court rank bestowed, and we find it deeply lamentable to be in such a situation under so great a shrine. Therefore, we humbly and most gratefully request that an order be issued permitting all of us to wear court robes henceforth without distinction.
