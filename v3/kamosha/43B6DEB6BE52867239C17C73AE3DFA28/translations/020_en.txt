…the matter was reported in writing to both the Court Transmitter's office and the shogunal magistrate's office, but it caused complications, and an order was issued stating that those who do not hold shrine posts should not wear the court robe (hō) while in that non-office-holding status. We found this exceedingly troublesome, and have been waiting for an appropriate moment to submit a petition. For this reason, while we have continued to wear the robe when doing so is required by shrine duties (sharō), we have otherwise refrained from wearing it as a group while in non-office-holding status. Even last spring, when Kamechiyodayū Hokō, the eldest son of Shrine Chief Okamoto Sanmi, underwent his coming-of-age ceremony, he attended the shrine in plain white robes (jōe) rather than court dress.

Nevertheless, in the present case of Matsushita (Teruhisa): despite the complications that arose from the previous precedent in Genroku, he neither obtained permission nor consulted with the community of shrine families, and members of five of the Seven Houses conspired together and presumptuously wore court cap and robes of their own accord. Their apparent intention seems to be: by forcing the issue this time and establishing this as a precedent, to arrange going forward that the eldest sons of the hereditary shrine officials (shasiof the Seven Houses could wear court dress even while in non-office-holding status as a matter of course, while making it even more difficult for other ujibito to do so — thus creating a distinction between the two groups. If matters proceed in this way, there will be no way to maintain proper discipline among all non-office-holding ujibito, and we find this exceedingly troublesome. In short, if wearing the rank robe (ihō) is to be rendered impossible, then there is no point in having been granted a court rank at all, and we consider it a most lamentable state of affairs to exist under this great shrine. We would be most grateful and honored if you would issue an order permitting all of us to wear court dress going forward.
