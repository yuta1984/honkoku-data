It is clear that all are descendants of Arizane, and there is no distinction among their lineages. As the principal evidence that shrine officials (ujibito) have been appointed to shrine positions since ancient times, the genealogy from the time of Otoko-yuka onward is unambiguous. Furthermore, the Kōnin Rinji-kyaku (Provisional Regulations of the Kōnin era) states: "Henceforth, from among that clan (the Kamo clan), those who are pure, upright, and worthy of appointment as chief priests (kannagi) shall be selected and rotated every six years." Indeed, at this shrine, for approximately twenty-one positions — from the chief priest (kannushi) to the negi and hafuri of the tutelary shrines of the Kataoka, Kifune, Shingū, Ōta, Wakanara, Sawada, and other clans — it has been the practice since ancient times to select capable individuals from among the ujibito, first appoint them as hafuri of their clan shrine, and then advance them through successive appointments according to imperial decree, passing through the positions of negi and hafuri at eight shrines before finally being elevated to the position of chief priest.

Furthermore, regarding the conferral of court ranks: at this shrine, one first submits a petition for rank, and all those who have reached the age of sixteen and are engaged in divine service are recorded in a box before the deity, and are made to devote themselves exclusively to shrine duties. This is entirely due to the benevolent reverence shown to the gods by the holy sovereign (the Emperor), the grace of which has extended to the shrine families, and which together demonstrates the grandeur of this great shrine.
