(Regarding the descendants of Arizane)
All the current shrine families (shake) are descended from Arizane, and there are no other separate lineages. The fundamental proof that shrine positions have from ancient times been filled by ujibito (clan members) is the clearly preserved genealogy from the time of Otoko. Furthermore, the Kōnin Extraordinary Regulations (弘仁臨時格) state: "Henceforth, select from among the clan members those who are pure and upright and suitable to serve as chief priest (kannagi), appointing them for a term of six years before rotation." Indeed, at this shrine, for all twenty-one positions — from the chief priest down to the negi and hafuri of the ujigami (clan deities) of Kataoka, Kibune, Shingū, Ōta, Waka-nara, and Sawada — since ancient times, the practice ordained by imperial decree has been to select suitable candidates from among the ujibito, first appoint them as hafuri of the ujigami, then transfer them progressively through the ranks, and only after serving as negi and hafuri at eight shrines would they finally be nominated as chief priest.

Moreover, regarding the granting of court ranks (叙位): at this shrine, rank is first requested before the deity and then conferred, corresponding to one's divine duties. All those up to the age of sixteen have their names recorded in a box placed before the deity, and they are made to diligently serve their shrine duties. This is entirely due to the gracious overflow of the sacred sovereign's reverence for the gods, which has extended to the shrine families, and it demonstrates the scale and organization of this great shrine.
