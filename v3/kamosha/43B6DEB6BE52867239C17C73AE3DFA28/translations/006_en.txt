Item: The Kamo shrine families (shake) revere Taketsunomi-no-Mikoto as their ancestral deity, and their genealogy stems from a single line. The descendants of that deity, during the reign of Emperor Murakami, branched off from a single individual known as the Negi (Shrine Priest) Zaijitsu, and today number several hundred persons. All members of the Kamo clan are called "ujibito" (clan members). This is the same usage as when one speaks of the "ujibito of the Minamoto clan" or the "ujibito of the Fujiwara clan." Among these, those who have been appointed to shrine offices (shashoku) are called "shrine officials" (shashi), and their number is twenty-one. As can be seen in the Kōnin Rinji-kyaku (Supplementary Regulations of the Kōnin era), these persons were selected from among the ujibito who had rendered meritorious service to the shrine and who possessed the necessary abilities and talents, and were then recommended and appointed. According to the old custom, one was first appointed as the Hafuri (Minor Ritual Officiant) of the clan deity's shrine, and from there was gradually transferred through successive posts, with the ultimate goal being promotion to the rank of Kannagi (Chief Priest).

Item: From the time of the age of civil wars (the Sengoku period), the shrine's landholdings (shinryō) in other provinces were entirely lost, and since there were no longer any stipends (ryō) for the shrine offices, more than half of the twenty-one shrine official posts fell into extinction. Only the Five Office roles (gokanyaku) of the main shrine survived, while the subordinate shrines' positions remained vacant (ketsushoku). Consequently, the ujibito holding the five ryo daikan (deputy administrator) posts, which had been established within the shrine from long ago, continued to perform the annual divine rites. The families who had been fulfilling these Five Office roles were the four (or five) families of Matsushita, Mori, Torii, Ōji, and Hayashi, who had inherited shrine landholdings in other provinces and had thus been able to maintain their offices. The two families of Umetsuji and Tomino were branches of the Matsushita and Mori families and were shrine officials appointed in more recent times. Indeed, the great-great-grandfather of the current Umetsuji Bingo-no-Kami, a man known as Umetsuji Shikibu Yoshihisa, spent his entire life as an ujibito who also served as a daikan officer.

Item: Thereafter, when peaceful governance was restored, around the Kan'ei era (1624–1644), ujibito sought to obtain the shrine official positions that had fallen into extinction through the loss of their hereditary holders.
