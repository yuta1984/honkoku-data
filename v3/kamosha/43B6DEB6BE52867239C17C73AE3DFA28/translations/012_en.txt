(Continued from previous page) ...the remaining persons were designated as those to serve as negi (祢宜, senior priests) and hafuri (祝, ritualists) of the Shingū, Ōta, Wakamiya, Nara, Sawada, and Ujigami shrines. In the final analysis, it was because the lack of職領 (office stipends/shrine lands) had led to vacant posts (闕職), which had in turn given rise to this dispute. Therefore, on this occasion, the stipend lands (職領) for twenty-one offices, ranging from the chief priest (神主) down to the Ujigami-hafuri, were assigned (寄附). It was declared that henceforth, using these stipend lands as their support, those holding shrine offices should perform their duties without abandonment or withdrawal.

That restrictions (限制) were established at this time regarding shrine offices was not in accordance with ancient custom; however, in compliance with the directive of the military government (武命), a reconciliation was achieved, and all matters pertaining to the shrine were settled through unified consultation among all shrine families without distinction of rank, resulting in a ruling (裁判).

The twenty-first day of the same month.

Article: In response to a summons from the Tensō (Court Liaison), Ujibito Muneakira (旨顕清) also appeared before them, whereupon the following matters were raised: regarding the office of Daikan-yaku (代官役, proxy/deputy official), that the purport of the old records concerning the arrangements established from ancient times for the purpose of filling vacant posts should be reported.

Article: Whether there had been, from ancient times, any consultations (語合) conducted by means of the clansman serving as Daikan-yaku.

Article: Whether, on occasions when duties pertaining to the inner sanctuary (御内陣) of the main shrine arose, the shrine officers (社司) of Kataoka and Kibune served in rotation in place of those normally responsible.
