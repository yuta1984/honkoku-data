(Continued from the previous page) — Since the foundation stones (mi-ishizue) had been taken and work was carried out directly without ceremony, the jichin (ground-purification rite) was deemed unnecessary and improper to include. Furthermore, since the kiyoharae (purification ritual) falls under the jurisdiction of the Onmyōryō (Bureau of Divination/Yin-Yang), the matter was to be referred back to the Naizen (Palace Table Office). Given that during the Tenshō era, Toshihisa had already presided over the kiyoharae, it does not appear to be strictly a duty of the Onmyōryō, and the ujibito (clan members affiliated with the shrine) found this to be a puzzling matter.

One: On the same day, at the Moridokoro (grove administrative office), the Nuinodono-no-suke and the Ōinokami consulted regarding the celebratory rites (shūgi) for the carpenters' pillar-raising, as well as matters concerning ceremonial dress (shōzoku). It was determined that since in ancient times both the pillar-raising and the ridge-pole raising sometimes took place on the same day, and since ceremonial dress was required, it would be appropriate for ceremonial dress to be worn on this occasion of the pillar-raising as well.

One: On the same morning, the carpenters completed the written specifications (chūmon) for the pillar-raising and ridge-pole raising ceremonies.
