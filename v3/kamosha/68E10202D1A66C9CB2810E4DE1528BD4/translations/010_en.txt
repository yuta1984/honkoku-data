The people of Kibune Valley were going down to Edo to pursue a lawsuit, and it was therefore deemed appropriate that a representative from this location (Kamo Shrine) should also travel down to Edo.

- It was decided that the persons to be sent down to Edo should be Mori Samanosuke (Ujitaka) and Uemon-no-tayū (Shūken), along with a third person, making three in total.

- Around the 8th day of the 3rd month, in preparation for the journey down to Edo, ten silver pieces (ginsu) were sent from the collective (sōchū) to Mori, with three pieces allotted to Samanosuke. The administrative officer (zōshō) was not given silver coins as he was considered a functionary, but instead was provided with formal attire (kamishimo) and a horse.

- On the 11th day of the 3rd month, Mori Samanosuke, Uemon-no-tayū, and the other members of the group appeared before Lord Gomi. It was confirmed that these three were the ones to travel to Edo, and all matters were handled in a private, informal manner.

- On the 13th day of the same month, as Lord Gomi was departing for Edo, the collective (sōchū) sent him farewell gifts including a ritual purification wand (o-harae), a barrel (of sake), and various other items.

- On the 27th day of the same month, at the Hour of the Snake (approximately 10:00 a.m.), Mori Samanosuke, Uemon-no-tayū, and the three men departed for Edo. The details of their journey and their activities in Edo are recorded on a separate document.

- From the day of departure to Edo until the day of return to Kyoto, it was decided that the Nakatomi Harae (a Shinto purification ritual) should be recited before the deity for the safety of the magistrate (go-bugyō) and the three men on their journey. Beginning from the 28th day, the head of ritual fasting (shōjingashira) and others were invited to recite the prayers. This request was made by Sabyōe-no-suke. During this purification ritual, offerings of rice and sacred sake were made.
