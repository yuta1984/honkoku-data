Item: On the same day, it was determined that [inserting a foundation beneath the Junior Shrine (gon-sha)] was unnecessary.

Item: On the same day, the shrine officials notified the carpenters that, since the sacred treasures (shinpō) of the Junior Shrine were to be removed and temporarily stored, a temporary treasure house (shinpō-ya) should be constructed and enclosed around them. However, as this was a sudden development, there were no boards (lumber) available.

Item: On the same day, a meeting was convened at the residence of Mori Jibu-no-taifu. In attendance were: Zusho-no-kami, Kawachi-no-kami, Ōi-no-kami, Bungo-no-kami, Nagato-no-kami, Mimasaka-no-kami, and two shrine administrators (zasshō) along with Ujiyuki—nine persons in total—who gathered to deliberate on matters concerning the sacred treasures. The written document (kakitate) was then shown directly to the two persons serving as magistrates (bugyō) under Lord Gomi: Kyūi Sabyōe and Enami Shichirōemon. Since the matter of the implements was understood, it was stated that [the document] should next be shown directly to Kinemon.

Item: On the same day, the Naizen [the official in charge of provisions/culinary affairs] submitted one document (kanmon) calculating the auspicious date for the relocation (hiki-ya) [of the shrine building]. Details are noted in a separate sheet.

Item: On the same day, regarding the Ōfusa and others performing the "Uke-ryō" ritual, this is to be performed beneath the shōbu-gi [a sacred/ceremonial tree associated with competitions].

Item: The twenty-fourth day, fine weather, with rain in the evening. A meeting was held with assigned supervisors (sashisoe-yoriai). It was deliberated that, since the old Junior Shrine (gon-sha) is to be relocated on the twenty-sixth day, the various officials (shoyakunin) should observe a period of ritual abstinence (shōjin) in preparation for the sacred rites. Since postponing until the sixth of the following month would cause an excessive delay, it was deemed best to proceed with the relocation as soon as possible. This work (sakuji) was carried out following instructions conveyed to the shrine administrators (zasshō) of Lord Kyūi and others. Accordingly, even though the days of ritual abstinence (shōjin) were insufficient in number, it was communicated by the shrine precinct members (shatō-shū) that the various officials—the Bettō-tayū, the Azukari-tayū, the Shoshi-tayū, the Mokudai, and five or six members of the shrine precinct (shatō-shū)—should undertake the period of abstinence.
