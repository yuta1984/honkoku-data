Although the jinin's formal white robes (jōe) might be easily confused with those of the ujibito, causing the magistrates (bugyō) to think they were among those doing the hauling, this had been arranged by assigning them to a role called "kuma muyaku" (a supervisory or ceremonial bystander role). The ujibito gathered at elevated ground to the east, west, and north, standing alongside Gomi-dono's representative (gedai) and Kyūi, as well as Itashūshū's representative Sakuma Sei'emon and others, and directed the general peasants (sō-hyakushō). The head carpenters (sō-daiku) stood between the ropes of the capstans on both sides, overseeing everything. Among them, the Senior Carpenter (shō-daiku) and the Deputy Carpenter (gon-daiku) both wore formal courtier caps (samurai eboshi) and upper-and-lower garments (kamishimo): the Deputy Carpenter stationed himself atop the northern capstan, while the Senior Carpenter stood upon the balustrade (railing) of the temporary shrine (gon-sha), and together they directed the head carpenters and peasants. In this manner, the hauling was completed within half an hour (hantoki), and by the lower quarter of the Hour of the Snake [around 10:00–11:00 AM], the shrine had been hauled and placed approximately two ken (approx. 3.6 meters) further north than its own height indicated as the proper distance.

One entry: On the same day, the foundation stones at the former site of the temporary shrine were swept and cleaned, sakaki branches were erected at the four cardinal directions, and shimenawa (sacred rope) was strung around the area.

On the last day of the month (misoka), instructions were received from Gomi-dono to write up and submit an estimate of the silk cloth for the sacred treasures (shinpō) and a record of the various craftsmen's work. There was one matter for the shrine officials (shashi) and ujibito to deliberate upon, and accordingly all shrine officials gathered at Mori's place (with the exception of the Kataoka negi and the Kataoka hafuri, who did not attend). Among the ujibito, the following ten individuals went there for deliberation: Izumo-no-kami, Bungo-no-kami, Ōi-no-kami, Kawachi-no-kami, Shūzen-no-shō, Nuino-suke, Shume-no-suke, Ujiyuki, Hogaku, and Uemon-no-tayū. From the Hour of the Snake [9:00–11:00 AM] through the lower quarter of the Hour of the Ram [around 2:00–3:00 PM], they managed to complete roughly half of it.
