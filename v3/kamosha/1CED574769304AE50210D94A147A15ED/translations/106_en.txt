Regarding this matter: according to precedent, the naorai (communal feast) was held in the seated area of the chōya (administrative hall). However, following a petition from the tōne group in the previous winter, it was determined that for the subsidiary shrines (sessha), this arrangement (holding the naorai in the haiden) would henceforth be followed for each divine rite. With that explanation concluded, they withdrew.

After entering between the First and Second Torii without putting on their shoes, they ascended through the western entrance of the shrine grounds and returned the sacred key (omikagi) to the Azukari Dayū. Passing through the Second and Third Torii, they arrived before the Nara Shrine, put on their shoes, and performed a single bow. They then ascended from the haiden (worship hall) to the chōya, where the hafuri group took their seats in the upper-western, south-facing position. At this time, they did not lower their trailing robes. After the shrine officials (shashi) and shōjin-gashira (fasting heads) had assembled, the tōne completed the presentation of the shinku (sacred offerings), tōso (spiced sake), and all three rounds of sake offerings. When the tōne announced that the offerings had been duly presented, everyone rose from their seats. Both the negi and hafuri descended from the southern veranda and put on their shoes directly, then proceeded through the Third Torii, the Nara Stone Bridge, and other landmarks to arrive in front of the Second Torii. There, on the grass to the east and west, twenty-one shrine officials conducted the Shihōhai ceremony (ritual obeisance to the four directions). The negi group was positioned to the west and the hafuri group to the east; on both sides, three tatami mats were spread out, and each person lowered their trailing robes and took their seat. Since the current year was a kinoe (甲) year, the hafuri group performed two bows first, followed by the negi group performing two bows. The shoe-bow and seated bow were performed in the customary manner. After this concluded, they dispersed. Exiting through the Nara Bridge toward the Tomoe-gaki (巴垣 / crescent-shaped fence), they made circuit worship visits up to the Ōta Shrine. Their withdrawal was at around the midpoint of the mi (巳) hour (approximately 10 a.m.).

The Third Day (Hinoto-u / 丁卯). Clear skies. The U-no-tsue (卯杖) divine rite. The time is the exact u (卯) hour (6 a.m.). Due to a vacancy in the post (ketsushoku), the ujigami hafuri's deputy official, Keisen, was attending in service. He bathed before dawn, donned his court dress (ikan), and proceeded to the shrine before daybreak. Inquiring with the guide, he immediately departed through the gate, bringing one attendant (wearing a sword) who was ordered to carry his shoes. Note that, according to precedent, today's visit to the shrine occurs only once. From the privacy screen (kiribyōbu) of the bathhouse, he passed through the Third Torii, put on his shoes at the Second Torii, and proceeded along the eave of the Hosodono, through the Kusunoki Bridge (Shōkyō), the Sakura Gate, the Middle Gate, and entered the Naorai-dokoro (直会所, communal feast hall) through the side door (tsumado). Once the shrine officials at the shrine grounds had assembled, the Head Priest (kannagi), Lord Nobuhira, inquired whether the shōji officials and lower-ranking attendants had assembled. The reply was given that they had. Next, each person performed ritual hand-washing (temizu) at the north entrance of the naorai-dokoro, put on their shoes, advanced before the deity, performed a single bow at the appropriate place for each person's seat, took their seats, and carried out the shoe-bow, seated bow, and protocols for ascending and descending in the customary manner. At that time, the shōji official came inside the Chinese-style doors (karado) and asked, "If the hafuri group is assembled, shall we erect the u-no-tsue?" The hafuri, Lord Shigeoki, replied that they were assembled. Next, the Onmyō Dayū (Master of Yin-Yang) came dressed in court attire and performed purification (harae) on each shrine official one by one. The hafuri then advanced, opened the sacred doors, and each person shifted their seated position and prostrated themselves. Then, as per precedent, two persons at a time lined up and performed a single bow at the doi (earthen platform), and each took their positions above and below the steps.
