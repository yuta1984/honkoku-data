One entry: Heishi (ritual sake flasks) are sent one by one to the shrine buildings via lower-ranking attendants (shimobe) to collect them. Regarding the small shrine's o-hitsu (rice containers): Kawakamigō receives five gō worth, while the remaining villages (gōkata) receive four gō each. These are requested from the o-kudasho (offering preparation area) and fetched from the Niedono (a hall for ritual food offerings).

　　　　Items received are recorded below:

One entry: Fifty-two booklets of susuki (pampas grass decorations) — broken down as: twenty-five red, twelve blue, and fifteen yellow booklets each. These are received by the bettō-dayū (chief administrator) from the o-fuku-sho (clothing/supply office) and delivered to the daikan-chū (group of deputy officials).

One entry: Ten cuts of o-oi-kinu (covering silk) per set, two cuts of shiki-kinu (laying silk), and four cuts of mizuhiki (decorative cord). These are provided by the kannagi (shrine priest), received by the bettō (chief administrator), brought to the chōya (administrative office), and delivered to the daikan-chū.
　　Once the above is completed, one booklet of susuki and one booklet of miscellaneous paper (zōshi) are sent to be collected, and at this time these items are forwarded.

One entry: One large nusa-gushi (large ritual wand skewer) per set, ten small nusa-gushi, heishi-magaki (a woven enclosure for sake flasks), one corresponding ridgepole piece, and ten pieces of small and large timber are brought by the Ishishō craftsmen (master carpenters) and placed upon [the altar]. Regarding the ■ matter (illegible character) of the o-tana (sacred shelves) of the five villages: note that the standing timber is to be brought to the daikan's seat.

One entry: Two large ori (folded vessels/containers) per set and one small ladle (ko-bishaku) per set are prepared and supplied by the himonoshi (craftsmen specializing in cypress-wood vessels). These are received at the Gon-Kawaguchi residence by the toneri (attendants) who carry the go-hei (sacred wands) of the five villages, then brought to the chōya and placed upon the o-tana (sacred shelves).

One entry: One tori-tsuke-ki (bird-mounting stick/perch) per set and cords of the same (two strands) are brought by the toneri who carry the sacred wands of the five villages and placed upon the o-tana. Furthermore, the four strands of old bamboo cord are used by the toneri when shouldering/carrying the o-tana, [the text continues on the next page].
