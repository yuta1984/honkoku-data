Beyond the above, the various major and minor Shinto rites that have been conducted since ancient times as affairs of this shrine numbered over seventy occasions; however, following the decline of the shrine's landholdings, they have gradually been reduced to over thirty occasions, and in truth we are now merely maintaining the outward forms of these ceremonies.

　　　　　　　On the Matter of Shrine Landholdings (Shinryō)

In former times, by means of official documents (kanpu), the four villages of Atago District in this province (Yamashiro), as well as estate lands (shōen) and imperial kitchen establishments (mikuriya-dokoro) in various provinces, were donated to the shrine, amounting to several dozen locations — all administered with great rigor and stability. However, following the military conflicts of the Bunmei and Ōnin eras, these all fell into ruin. What little remained in the nearby villages was gathered together, and at the time of Toyotaiko (Toyotomi Hideyoshi)'s land survey (kenchi) during the Tenshō era, a yield of 541 koku was officially transferred at Shimogamo village — this constitutes the current extent of the shrine's landholdings.
