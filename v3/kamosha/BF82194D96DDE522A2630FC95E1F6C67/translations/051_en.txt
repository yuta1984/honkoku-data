A copy of the document submitted in October of Meiji 2 (己巳, 1869) in response to an inquiry from the Jingikan (Department of Divinities) regarding the enshrinement, history, and festivals of this shrine.
[In red ink] On the 18th day of the 11th month, this was submitted again to the Jingikan; it was presented directly to Lord Hagiwara, Junior Assistant of the Jingikan, Kazumitsu.

Regarding the Imperial Grand Shrine: Since ancient times, the reverence shown by the Imperial Court has been incomparable to that afforded to other shrines, and the solemn festivals have been conducted without a single day of neglect. This is made abundantly clear in the national histories, the procedures set forth in the Engishiki, and in the old records of various noble houses — so much so that it would be impossible to enumerate each instance individually. As for the year of the enshrinement, the era is so remote and predates written records that the precise origins cannot be determined in detail; however, at this shrine it is understood that the enshrinement took place during the reign of Emperor Jinmu. Nevertheless, works such as the *Honchō Gatsurei* and *Nenjū Gyōji Hishō* record that in the second month of the sixth year of Emperor Tenmu's reign, "the Kamo Shrine was established in this small-moon land (Komon-koku)." Furthermore, it is recorded that already during the reign of Emperor Kinmei, a swift horse was offered and dedicated to this shrine on an auspicious day in the fourth month, for the purposes of peace throughout the realm and an abundant harvest of the five grains —
