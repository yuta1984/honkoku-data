Around the Hour of the Goat (approximately 2 PM), attendees of the large meeting:

1. A small meeting was held earlier, and there was general discussion regarding the rain prayer ceremony. As to how it should be conducted, the council found it appropriate. Since prayers are currently being offered, it was decided that a Shinto ritual should be performed, and the ceremony was formally set for the day after tomorrow, the 27th.

2. During the meeting, farmers from Ōno appeared and made a petition, stating: "We wish to direct water to our paddies and begin planting, and while most of the landowners above us have consented, one person — Okabe Saburōbei of Minami-zushi-machi — is obstructing us, and the matter has already come to a dispute. We humbly request that you order him to stop interfering and allow the water to flow down to us." Accordingly, the meeting hall promptly summoned Saburōbei and instructed that he be reprimanded on this matter.

3. Regarding the petition of tenant farmers of the lower paddies in the Ōno district for water rights. This appears to have been mixed up with the diary entry of the 23rd; that entry should be consulted.

4. The water officials from Hongō district came to report that the planting of all paddies in the Hongō district had been completed by today. Accordingly, they requested that they be allowed to withdraw and rest from tonight through the morning, that water watch duties be suspended from tonight through tomorrow day and night, and that watch duties resume from the Hour of the Hare (approximately 6 AM) on the 27th, the day after tomorrow.

5. The junior officials of the Jitte corps were summoned, and they were informed of the execution of the rain prayer ceremony on the coming 27th, as well as the suspension of water watch duties from tonight through tomorrow day and night.

6. A letter arrived at Naokata's residence from Hamaji Shūzen of the Hirohashi household, stating that he wished to consult on a matter and requesting an immediate visit. Accordingly, Naokata visited at once. In his absence, the acting scribe was Kunikiyo.

7. Naokata had fully explained yesterday's circumstances to Hamaji, who indicated that he would appear early today and report to the Lord Dainagon.

8. Early the following morning, after breakfast, a small meeting should be convened to deliberate on the return of the water grant. Since it would be difficult to reach a decision without the attendance of Yasushige, Kuniuji, and others, the officials decided to visit Yasushige that evening to request that he attend tomorrow no matter what other commitments he might have. One or two members took it upon themselves to do so, and accordingly Mikiyo went to Yasushige's residence to make the request. However, Yasushige initially gave a reply that he would not attend regardless. After explaining matters at length with reasoned arguments, Yasushige relented, saying "Very well, I will attend." However, he said he had something to tell them, and proceeded to relay the following: In recent days, a person in his employ had come to him and reported that Miyaka Tayū had left his seat and called upon various prominent members of the council, lamenting about the punishment ordered this time, saying "I have never heard of such a thing before — this is truly distressing. Through what deliberations was such a ruling handed down?" In response, those eminent persons reportedly said, "We tried in various ways to have the matter resolved lightly, but since Kunai-no-taifu alone refused to agree and continued pressing his position, it ended up as it did." Yasushige said he had received this account and had been advised not to speak too much about matters. Yasushige then stated: "This matter is not something you [i.e., the intermediary] readily understands. Everything is decided by collective deliberation — even if I alone propose something, it cannot be decided without the agreement of the assembly. Conversely, even if the assembly agrees but I am not convinced, I will continue to put forward my views. All decisions are made collectively, and this is not simply a matter within your understanding." He said he conveyed this message to the person. As for himself, he had not gone to the riverbank at the time and did not know what state Miyaka Tayū had been in. Nevertheless, since the assembly as a whole deemed that further disciplinary authority would be difficult to maintain, he had relayed the message as above. He found it truly regrettable to be regarded with such lack of trust in recent times, and felt resentment at heart. He also stated that it was with some reluctance that he attended meetings, because even when he attended and did not voice a single opinion, the assembly still questioned his motives. He said that to receive such censure was truly incomprehensible to him. Even an ordinary person would find such suspicion inappropriate, let alone the eminent members who meet with him morning and night, day and evening, and consult on all matters together — it was truly something difficult to hear.
