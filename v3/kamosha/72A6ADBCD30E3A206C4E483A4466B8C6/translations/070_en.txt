**Record of the Written Reply from the Sixth Group's Young Officers**

The retained copy of the written reply is as follows:

―――Memorandum of Oral Statement―――

We have received and understood the substance of the deliberations held by the elders and young officers (rōjaku) in their entirety regarding the repeated petitions from Koyama-gō requesting an end to the fixed-rate tax system (do-men) and a switch to crop-inspection taxation (tachige-midori) from this year. After thorough deliberation among all members of the Sixth Group's young officers, without exception, we concluded that implementing crop-inspection taxation on an individual basis would be entirely beyond our means. We therefore humbly request that, despite the shrine community's many pressing affairs and burdens, you would be so good as to handle this matter collectively as a community tax obligation (sha-nō). Since this period entails many expenses, we propose that the miscellaneous administrative costs (zatsujiyō) associated with this tax collection be covered by each member contributing rice according to their names on the register (uchigome). We humbly request your kind consideration in this matter as well. Respectfully submitted.

　　Sixth Month, Tenth Day, Year of the Boar　　The Young Officers of the Sixth Group
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　To the Presiding Officers

**Dispute over Miscellaneous Expenses for the Water Officials**
Kunishi reported the following: Buzen-no-kami had said: "Regarding the miscellaneous silver expenses (zatsuyo-gin) for the water dispute (mizu-deiri) of the three villages (sango) petitioned by the water officials, for which the shrine community is considering providing funds — what does Hokaku think? There is precedent to consider: I understand that during the Nozaka dispute around Kyōhō 12 (1727), no such funds were provided by the shrine community. It seems this matter should be borne by the peasants [in connection with some crop matter, possibly wheat]. If silver is provided and the community is ridiculed for it afterward, that would be unfortunate." Kunishi then asked: "Did you not explain this reasoning at the recent meeting? Did you consult the diary records from Kyōhō 12?" To which the response was: "The diary was not consulted. I did express my general view, but the ruling was that the funds should be provided, and even Harima-no-kami seemed to go along with it, though apparently not fully convinced."

Accordingly, Kunishi thought it best to investigate the matter more carefully so as not to invite ridicule afterward, and spoke of this to Hōdo. As it happened, the field administration elders (takata-toshiyori) were also present in the meeting room, so the matter was discussed with them. Their opinion was: "The matter concerning [the crop/wheat] pertains to attached land rights (shitaji), which falls within what the peasants are responsible for. The attached lands that the peasants provide are entirely the property of the peasants, not of the proprietor (honjo). In our case, the portion that each member holds as attached land all belongs to their respective peasants. Since those peasants are unable to cultivate the land themselves, they have entrusted it to tenant farmers (shita-saku). There is no reason to have those tenant farmers bear these expenses. Even if we are laughed at for it afterward, it matters little. If it is a matter of compassionate consideration (go-reimin), then even if the proprietor were to provide the funds, it would not be so greatly ridiculed."

However, at the previous meeting, Hokaku had not expressed such a view, but had instead said in private that "it is difficult to say this cannot be done; somehow it should be provided under a different pretext." Speaking in such a manner privately is most inappropriate of late.
