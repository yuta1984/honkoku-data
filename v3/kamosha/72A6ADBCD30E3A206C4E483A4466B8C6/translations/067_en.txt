The order should be issued by the professional shrine staff (*shoku-naka*) and dispatched accordingly.

1. On the 7th, as is the custom each year, the peasants of the eastern and western districts paid their house tax (*munabetsu*), leaving a surplus of over nine *koku* and three *to* of wheat (*mugi-barai*). Divided among the thirty-four members of the assembly, this amounts to over two *to*, seven *shō*, and four *gō* per person. The question was raised as to whether distribution should proceed as usual, or whether the matter of converting to silver should be discussed. — *It was determined that distribution should proceed as in previous years, and that the silver conversion rate should be set in accordance with last year's precedent — that is, thirty-five *mon* in coin per two *to*, seven *shō*, and four *gō*.*

2. On the same day, the notification of leave of absence and mourning (*hima/fuku*) for Tori Tōdayu Tadasane was submitted in accordance with customary procedure.

3. On the same day, the village elders of Oyama Village — Kyūzaemon, Gonzaemon, and Jinuemon — together with five representatives of the general peasantry (*sō-hyakushō sōdai*) came forward and reported that, having consulted within the village regarding the instructions previously conveyed, the villagers were unanimously and resolutely refusing the matter of the fixed land tax assessment (*do-men*), as they had previously petitioned, and that they were humbly requesting, once again and in the strongest terms, that the matter be handled by visual inspection (*mi-tori*). They brought the original petition document once more.

4. The repair department (*go-shūri-kata*) submitted a request: the thatched-roof storehouse within the repair shed, in which bamboo had been stored, is leaking exceptionally badly, and the bamboo inside has begun to rot. A request was made that orders be given for re-thatching, which would cost approximately one *kan* and five or six hundred *mon*. — *Since allowing the stored bamboo to continue to rot would result in further loss, it was determined that re-thatching should be ordered.*

5. The shrine professional staff submitted a notice: the supply of bamboo torches (*taimatsu*) used and carried over to the paddy-field work this summer has been reduced to very little. Should an order be placed? — *For the sake of safety and precaution, an order should be placed. The matter should be reviewed when firewood is arranged at the deliberation hall in the coming days, and the order issued at that time. The matter was filed with the monthly duty officers (*tsuki-ban-naka*).*

6. Among those remaining at the assembly, the monthly duty officers (*tsuki-ban*) and the six officials (*roku-yaku*), along with Mikawa-no-kami, Yamato-no-kami, and Chikuzen-no-kami, were asked to remain, as there was a matter for consultation.

   **Small assembly (*ko-yoriai*)** — Attending priests: Masahisa, Yasutomo, Hirohisa, Tsunekiyo, Suenaga, Suemoto, Fumiari, Furitoshi, Yasukaku, Yasuto, Dainaoyasu, and three members of the shrine professional staff. As there was a consultation matter today, the *sōmen* noodles sent earlier by Ryōkuchiya Noto were ordered out; the meal was taken, and the consultation followed. Gakunori made the announcements.

   **Regarding consultation on the procurement of silver (*gin-ko chōtatsu*):**

   - 1. Over 570 *me*: repayment of interest to Suekage and Eikiyo
   - 1. Over 270 *monme*: arrears from the fifth month
   - 1. Over 790 *monme*: excess balance from the *Aoi* messenger (*Aoitsukai*) professional staff accounts
   - 1. Over 57 *monme*: excess for the two *Aoi* messengers
   - 1. Over 520 *monme*: repayment to Fujiya Kahei
   - 1. Over 520 *monme*: repayment to Hiragahara Shichirōbei
   - 1. 600 *me*: funds required by the repair department
   - 1. 1 *kan* 180 *monme*: repayment to Marūya Zenbei

   The total of the above amounts to approximately four *kan* and five hundred *monme*. Moreover, there are no funds at all available for payment this month or next. It was proposed that approximately five or six *kan* be borrowed. As it was understood that Chikuzen-no-kami had access to a moneylender (*gin-nushi*), it was determined that he should be asked to approach them. Accordingly, the request was made, and he immediately gave his assent, went directly to make enquiries, and presently returned, reporting that five or six *kan* could be made available. The interest rate is eight percent (*hachibu*), and as the interest is low, this is most welcome. However, the moneylender's condition was that in the future, when timber from the mountains — such as the Kinofune-yama or the current mountain — is to be sold or clear-cut, he wished to be given the right of tender, either at the assessed price or at a one-percent premium above it. In essence, this lending is motivated by the desire to secure that right. It was also decided that the name on the promissory note (*tegata*) should be written in the name of Chikuzen-no-kami's intermediary.
