On the same day, the River Magistrate (Kawa-bugyō), Sakyō-no-gonnosuke Kiyotoshi, came to visit and submitted a notice requesting permission to conduct fishing for divine offerings (jingu-gyoryō) on the Seki River on the 2nd of next month, if there were no objections. Permission was granted.

On the same day, the timber mountain officials (kiyama-bugyō) — Awa-no-kami Sueharu, Buzen-no-suke Yasumune, Daizenndayu Hirohisa, Nakatsukasa Narishisa, Kii-no-kami Shigesome, Suruga-no-suke Tetsuki, Yamato-no-suke Noriharu, Utanosuke Yasubuno, Izumo-no-suke Tomotoshi, and Bungo-no-suke Nobuyasu — submitted the timber mountain accounts (kiyama-kanjō). The total silver amount came to over ten kan and five hundred monme. Of this, nine kan monme was deducted as a deposit from Hatae Chōshirō, with the remaining one kan and five hundred monme in cash being paid into the shrine treasury.

On the same day, a program for a Noh performance was submitted by Kawakatshu Gonroku. A letter had arrived stating that Gonroku had intended to bring it in person, but as he was ill and unable to walk, he had planned to have his disciple carry it up; however, as that too proved difficult, he was sending notice by letter as a matter of simplicity.

Today, a report was received from the repair officials (goshūri-kata) stating that the Noh stage required an outlay of 150 monme and repairs to the Kifune [duty office] required 128 monme to be disbursed. As regards the stage, as in previous years, the disbursement would be: the complete stage fixture at 85 monme, the shrine-use partition at one kan mon, and additional items such as nails and bamboo baskets to be purchased for approximately 140 to 150 monme. It was decided that the funds would be disbursed.

A sachet bag (kaōri bukuro) as a mid-summer (doyō) gift for Lord Matsudaira Tominnosuke was completed. The price was reduced by five bu per bag compared to previous years, making it five monme per bag. Five varieties (five bags) came to twenty-five monme total. Each bag was placed in a paulownia wood box (kiribako) tied with a single cord of flat purple braid, at six monme and five bu per cord. The box (lid and body combined) came to approximately forty monme. It was agreed that the letter of accompaniment should be written out today, and that delivery should be made tomorrow. A small hand towel (kote nugui) was also to be included.

A letter arrived from Chikuzen-no-suke, the duty official at Kifune, reporting that upon examining the records at the additional duty office (kaban-sho), it was found that in the eighth month of Kyōhō 10 (1725), two hundred cedar trees had been planted as a donation from Jōjuin. The duty officials at the time were Ukyo-no-jō Sueharu and Kamon-no-kami Ujimotoi. Furthermore, according to Genzaemon, two hundred trees were also donated and planted by Jōjuin in Kyōhō 8 (1723); however, as the records from Kyōhō 8 are not held at Kifune, the matter should be investigated on this side (at the meeting hall). This was the content of the communication.

Regarding the wooden pillar at the location where a votive picture (ema) is to be hung, as arranged by Aki-no-kami Ujióki — it had been previously decided by deliberation that the master carpenter should be asked to estimate how to prevent excessive damage to the pillar — however, at that time the master carpenter had gone up the mountain to Kifune. As he is currently residing at home, it was proposed that someone from among the monthly duty officers who had been in attendance at that time should go to the site, indicate the location, and, after the master carpenter completes his estimate, determine whether metal fittings etc. should be commissioned. This was so resolved.

Kawatateyuki Daizen-no-gon-tayu Kiho submitted a report stating that the wooden horses (kiba) that had been used previously are currently left within the precincts of Hijiri Shrine (Hijiri Jinja), and since the Noh performance season was approaching and people from Kyoto would be coming in, he inquired whether they should be cleared away. He also asked whether the drifting timber (nagashi-ki) suspended in the river should be hauled out. It was decided that the drifting timber in the river should indeed be pulled out, but that the wooden horses could be left as they were without difficulty.
