During the small gathering (shōkikai), Gozaburō of Umegatsujichō submitted a written petition stating the following: A quarrel over a folding fan broke out between the son of Kichijirō of the same ward (aged five) and the son of Gozaburō (aged four). As a result, Kichijirō severely beat Gozaburō's wife, causing her bodily pain and great hardship. Accordingly, a neighborhood meeting was convened and someone was sent to summon Kichijirō; however, Kichijirō's father, Kichiemon, appeared in his stead and again hurled abusive language and appeared about to enter the mosquito net where Gozaburō's wife lay ill, whereupon neighbors physically restrained him. This person is entirely lawless, and even the slightest show of force by him leaves both the victim and the entire neighborhood unable to feel at ease. Therefore, we humbly petition that Kichijirō be summoned and firmly admonished. If this is done, not only the victim herself but the entire neighborhood will be most grateful. The petition bore the official seals (yakuban) of the ward official (gyōji) and the elders (toshiyori). Accordingly, immediately thereafter, the petitioner Gozaburō, the elder Denbei, the ward official Sōgorō, and the other party Kichijirō were summoned, and both sides were examined. Kichijirō's testimony was greatly at odds with the facts, and he attempted various evasions, but since the petition was made by the entire neighborhood and bore the seals of the ward officials, Kichijirō alone could not make his excuses stand. Consequently, Kichijirō's cousin, Yojirōemon of Takenachō, was summoned and placed under custody. This was not strictly a prohibition on going out, but since the person in question was lawless,

and there was concern that he might again go to the neighborhood and resort to force in some manner, he was instructed to take great care and ensure that no falsehoods were told. Further instructions would be issued in due course. As for Jirosaku of Takenachō, the son-in-law of Kichiemon, he had also been summoned but was currently away from home, and it was expected that when he returned and came forward, he would receive the same instructions as Yojirōemon. Kichijirō mentioned that he himself had matters he wished to petition about, and so he was told that any petition he wished to make could be submitted in due course. However, it must be submitted with the official seals of the elders and ward officials — a petition from one individual alone would not be accepted. This was the instruction conveyed.
