According to Chomei's Hojoki: Around the 2nd year of Genpeki (1185), there was a great earthquake. Its nature was unlike anything ordinary — mountains crumbled and filled rivers, the sea tilted and flooded the land. The earth split and water gushed up, rocks broke apart and tumbled into valleys. Ships offshore were tossed about by waves, and horses traveling the roads were bewildered as to where to step. Still more so in the surroundings of the capital, where in every direction not a single hall, pagoda, or temple remained undamaged. (omitted) Though the violently shaking ceased after a time, the aftershocks continued for a while without stopping. There was not a day without twenty or thirty earthquakes of a scale that would ordinarily cause alarm. As ten days and then twenty days passed, they gradually became more infrequent — sometimes four or five times, two or three times, or perhaps every other day, or once every two or three days. In general, those aftershocks continued for about three months, it seems.

According to the Tenmon Koryo: In the 5th month of the year Kanmon-Jintora (the 2nd year of Kanbun, 1662), the Kinai region was greatly shaken. Northern Omi (Hokukou) was the most severely affected. Aftershocks occurred repeatedly and continued until the end of the year.

According to the Honcho Tenmon Shi: On the 29th day of the 2nd month of the 1st year of Horeki, Kanoto-Hitsuji (1751), there was a great earthquake. Various halls and buildings were destroyed, and the aftershocks continued until the 6th and 7th months before finally ceasing.
