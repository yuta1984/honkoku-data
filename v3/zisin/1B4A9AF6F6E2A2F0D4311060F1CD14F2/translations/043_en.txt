On the 18th day of the 9th month, fire broke out at Aka-sawa, and on the 26th day at Chiri-sawa. Even now, the fires at these two streams—Aka-sawa and Chiri-sawa—continue to burn fiercely. In accordance with the orders previously received from the main government office, officials from the prefecture were sent to question the island officials. The questions and answers are as follows:

Question One: What is the current state of the fires on the island, and which village is most severely affected? Answer: The sacred crater (御洞) measures half a ri east to west and two ri and sixty steps north to south. Its interior is utterly dark and obscure, and even those islanders who are accustomed to traversing dangerous terrain cannot fathom its depth. On the island, the crater is revered and called Mihara Daimyōjin, and it is regarded as the principal shrine of the island. It is transmitted in oral tradition that in the Tenna 4th year, Kinoe-ne, fire broke out within the crater and, though it varied in intensity, did not extinguish for seven years, finally dying out in Genroku 3rd year, Kanoe-uma. However, last year on the 29th day of the 7th month, fire broke out once again within the crater. Since then, the fire has again varied in intensity and has not yet been extinguished to this day. The fire at Nakano-sawa has already been extinguished, but both Aka-sawa and Chiri-sawa continue to burn, and smoke from the crater still drifts without ceasing. The fall of sand and ash has not been severe enough to reach the villages. In general, the nearest fire outbreak location is nine ri from any inhabited dwelling, and the farthest is eighteen ri away.

Question Two: Since Nakano-sawa and Aka-sawa have already erupted and Chiri-sawa is also in flames, with three separate fires each burning fiercely, is it not the case that communication between villages is completely severed and they are unable to assist one another? Answer: On the 22nd day of the 3rd month of this year, the northwest slope of the crater peak collapsed and burned, and the fire descended into Naka-sawa, stopping just east of Izuzu village. This is a road used for collecting firewood, located three ri from the nearest dwellings. On the 18th day of the 9th month, the southwestern hill of the crater collapsed and burned, and the fire descended into Aka-sawa. This stream lies midway between the two villages of Nomashi and Sashikiji, about six ri from each. The fire in the stream has somewhat subsided, so the road from below the stream to the seashore is now passable, and the islanders are able to assist one another. On the 27th day, fire from the northeastern hill of the crater descended into Chiri-sawa and reached the rocky shore, with scorched stones buried in the sea over an area measuring sixty steps in length and six hundred steps in width, rising five or six steps above the sea's surface.
