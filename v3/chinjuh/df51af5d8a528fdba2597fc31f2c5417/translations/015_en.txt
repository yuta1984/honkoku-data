(Continued from previous page) ...there remains an old utensil described as a wasabi grater and daikon grater, said to have been used by [Aoto Saemon]. Upon inspection, it is an utterly inconvenient object, and who in this day and age would use such an impractical thing? One should reflect upon the customs of that era. Nevertheless, Fujitsuna was a man of wisdom who abhorred wasteful expenditure throughout the realm. If so, perhaps through his own ingenuity, he devised this daikon grater as an invention that required no money. In any case, it must be called a rare and unfamiliar utensil.

　　　Diagram of Aoto Saemon's Daikon Grater (Daikon-oroshi)

Made of sugi (Japanese cedar) board, four-bu thick; height two sun nine bu, length seven sun five bu, width four sun three bu.

This is made by taking thick pieces of bamboo, fashioning them into shapes like saw teeth, fitting them into cuts made on the left and right sides, and then driving bamboo edges from the top to prevent movement. The sharp edges and corners of the bamboo are arranged so that one can grate either daikon or wasabi [other sources add: "and shave them off"]. 

[It is made so that one] transfers [the grated material] from the opening [other sources: "from this opening"] below.
