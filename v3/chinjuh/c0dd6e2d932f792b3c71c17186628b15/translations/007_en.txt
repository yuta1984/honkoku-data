### Section on Beasts (Kemono)

The *Myōshō* states: "Beast" (*kemono*). The commentary to the *Wenxuan* states: a group of fur-bearing creatures is called a "beast." The commentary to the *Erya* states: a four-legged creature with fur is called a "beast" (pronounced *shū*; Japanese reading: *kemono*).

"Domestic animal" (*kedamono*). According to the examination by Yewang [Gu Yewang], the "six domestic animals" (pronounced *chū*, alternate pronunciation *kyū*; colloquially called *chikusho*, as in the two-syllable pronunciation of *jikusho*; Japanese reading: *kedamono*) are: cattle, horses, sheep, dogs, chickens, and pigs.

"Female animal" (*me-kemono*). The *Shuowen* states: a female animal (pronounced *bin*; Japanese reading: *me-kemono*) is the mother of domestic animals.

"Male animal" (*o-kemono*). The *Shuowen* states: a male animal (pronounced *bō*; Japanese reading: *o-kemono*) is the father of domestic animals. And so on.

It is further stated that "domestic animal" (*chiku*) refers to those that are raised and kept. Fresh meat from such animals is called *sen* (fresh), and dried meat is called *kō*. The common usage today of the term *chikusho* presumably derives from Buddhist doctrine.
