(Continued from previous page — description of the *Raijū*)

It has fur, and its whiskers are dark gray mixed with chestnut-colored hairs, with patches of jet black, and are long. Its eyes are round and sharp, its ears are small and erect, resembling those of a rat. Its claws are pointed and curve inward at the tip, and its tail is exceptionally long. The toes of the four limbs number four on the front feet, with one webbed toe on each hind foot. From head to tail it measures over two *shaku* (approximately 60 cm) in length, with the tail accounting for more than half of this. When stroked, it emits a most foul odor. This creature appears to have no voice, for no one has ever been recorded as having heard it cry. During the Bunka era (1804–1818), a certain hunter once succeeded in shooting and capturing one with a firearm. It is also said that the *raijū* inhabits Mount Fuji and the high mountains and outstanding peaks of the seven districts, though in all cases they are not particularly large.

**Kappa (River Child)**

According to accounts, it lives in the Tomoe River in Ihara District. The villagers call it *kappa*. Its form resembles that of a child of five or six years of age; its entire body has a raw, fishy smell and is slimy like a catfish. Its eyes are round, its pupils are sharp and gleaming, and its fingers and toes are webbed, resembling fins. It ordinarily lurks at the bottom of the water and does not show itself, but on the rare occasions when it comes ashore and confronts a person, it is very strong; if one runs and chases it, it moves so swiftly that it is difficult to catch. If someone manages to grapple with it and win, that person will afterward suffer a fever and fall ill. If someone is harmed by a *kappa*, their entrails will invariably be pulled out through the anus, and there is no escaping death. The accounts of *kappa* are much the same everywhere.
