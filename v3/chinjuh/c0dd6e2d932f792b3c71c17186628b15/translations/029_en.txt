**Tiger**
According to the *Sunpu Seijiroku* (Records of Suruga Government Affairs), on the first day of the ninth month of Keichō 19 (1614), a Dutchman named Yayousu (Jan Joosten) appeared before the lord and brought two tiger cubs. One of them had fur growing above the base of its tail in the shape of the character for "wind" (風). The world considered this a curiosity, and it was reported that the cubs should be presented to the young lords of the Edo shogunate.

**Chinese Dogs (Tōken)**
According to the *Sunpu Seijiroku*, on the third day of the second month of Keichō 17 (1612), a hunt was held at Sakaigawa and Futagawayama in Tōtōmi Province, with an entourage of five to six thousand men carrying bows and firearms. Sixty to seventy Chinese dogs were let loose to chase prey in all directions, and twenty to thirty wild boars were caught. The hunt was called off when heavy rain began to fall. According to one account, this area had been maintained as a falconry ground where dogs had long been kept and trained.
	On reflection, the dogs used for hunting are all females, with white coats tinged with red, and those with short legs are preferred. These are distinct from Chinese dogs (tōken) proper. Females have a keen sense of smell and can detect where birds are hiding. That is why they are taken along. Males have a poor sense of smell and are not fit for the purpose.

**Mysterious Creature**
This creature appeared in the mountains of Oshika Village in Udo District. According to the villagers, a mysterious creature appeared on Mount Oshika near the village. Its face resembled a cat, its hands and feet were like those of a monkey, it was about the size of a dog in stature, and it had two wings measuring over three shaku (approximately 90 cm) in span. On the seventh day of the second month of Bunsei 9 (1826), unable to endure the deep snow in the mountains, it descended into the village, where it was caught by a farmer. What manner of beast this might be remains unknown, as no one has yet been able to identify it.
