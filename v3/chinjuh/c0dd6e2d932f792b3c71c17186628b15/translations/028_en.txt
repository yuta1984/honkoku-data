**Large Rat (Ōnezumi)**

It is said that when a heated metal implement is applied to the spot where [a large rat's] tail has been cut off, the wound heals immediately.

According to the memoranda of the castle guard of Sunpu Castle: "There are large rats in the castle that destroy property." In the barracks of the current additional guards (kaban-shū), there are particularly many old large rats, which scurry about day and night without showing the slightest fear of people. Their size is roughly that of a small cat.

**River Otter (Kawauso)**

According to tradition, river otters are found in great numbers in the Tomoe River of Ihara District, and in the moats of Fuchū Castle in Abe District. Their fur is black, or sometimes mottled. The otter catches fish before dawn, drags them into its burrow as food for the day, and then rests. If it fails to catch fish at dawn, it forgets to return to its burrow and wanders along the riverbed. At such times, mud naturally rises up from the water. Upon seeing this, if one repeatedly strikes the water and drives the otter, it will ultimately be unable to escape or hide and will come up onto the land. The otter is inherently very slow at running on land, and so it can be quickly caught at this moment.

**Hihi (Giant Ape-like Creature)**

According to the *Junson-ki* (Record of Village Inspection Tours), this creature lives deep in the mountains at the headwaters of the Ōi River in Shida District. It stands over one *jō* (approximately 3 meters) tall, has long lips that curl upward, and its head hair is long and hangs down. Its movements are swift, like a gale. It likes to eat animals and also eats *shino* (dwarf bamboo), and accordingly its droppings contain only animal fur and dwarf bamboo.

Hunters say: "When a traveler encounters this creature, he must hide himself and must not raise his voice. If the creature learns of a person's presence, the harm it inflicts is severe."

Some say: "This is not a *hihi* but a *Yamahara-ha* (mountain giant)."

The author's note: Deep within remote mountains and valleys, why should only the *Yamahara-ha* dwell? The *hihi* too must surely exist there.
