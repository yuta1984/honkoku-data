**[Records Concerning Deer (Continued)]**

The place called Hara produces deer; Asouyi produces deer hides. Yamahara pays tribute in deer hides; in Eno Maki of Abe District, deer come from the mountains; Kogare-sato also produces deer. Nakugura of Fuji District pays tribute in deer hides; Yazume of Suruga District pays tribute in deer hides; Marikome pays tribute in deer hides and skins; Komatsu pays tribute in deer hides and skins. Nishito of Mashizu District pays tribute in deer hides; Akunami-i pays tribute in deer. Iwata of Toda District pays tribute in deer skins; Iwata-yama also pays tribute in deer skins (and so forth). There are also references to seaweed, tide-grasses, and Ojika-oka, all of which are said to refer to Ojika Village and Ojika-hara in Udo District. Shizuhata-yama in Abe District is also counted among the famous places.

A certain Sakuraya, a maker of kemari balls (鞠師), states that the deer hides used in making kemari balls come from four places: Suruga, Izu, Yoshino in Yamato Province, and Kumano in Kii Province. The hides from these places are used because the deep mountain and coastal environments give them superior quality (and so forth).

**[The Three-Horned White Deer]** — As recorded in *Ruijū Kokushi*:

In the winter of the 25th year of Emperor Kinmei's reign (564 CE), the year of Kinoe-Saru (甲申), Soga no Iname was ordered to hunt in Sugiyama in Rohara District of Suruga Province. A three-horned white deer was captured and presented to the court. At the end of that year, there was a great flood disaster (and so forth). The name Sugiyama still survives as a village name today.
