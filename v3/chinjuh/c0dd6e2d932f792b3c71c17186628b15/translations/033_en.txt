(Continued from previous page — description of the *Tengu*)

It captures people and makes them its servants, compelling them to serve it. When they no longer please it, it returns them to their home village. At other times, in the dark of night, it tears apart great trees and hurls massive rocks, yet when one looks the next morning, there is no sign that anything has happened. It also performs elegant court music (*gagaku*) in the forest, allowing listeners in the darkness to hear the sounds of a banquet. It possesses divine powers freely at its disposal. The *Nihon Shoki* records it as "*amatsu kitsune*" (Heavenly Fox), and it appears in the section on Emperor Jomei. The *Kogo Shūi* refers to it as "*furukusotobi*" (Old Dung-Kite), which is also a reference to the *tengu*. Some say that the *tengu* is a kind of earthly immortal (*chisen*) rather than a supernatural monster.

**Mōryō (罔両)**

It is found in Ikaumi Village, Shida District. According to accounts: on a stormy, rainy night of a certain year, when people were escorting a deceased person to be buried at a burial ground (*zanmai*), a monstrous creature appeared from the clouds and attempted to seize the coffin. The people fled in terror. After a while the rain cleared and the moon shone bright, and when they returned to look, the coffin was lying nearby — but its interior was entirely empty. An elder of the village said that he had heard of this happening on rare occasions. The people of this province call this supernatural occurrence *kuwasha* (罔両), and some call it *yasha* [夜叉, *yaksha*]. It is said to be the work of the *kinkaneko* (金花猫, a supernatural cat), and not the doing of demons and evil spirits (*chimimōryō*). However, this may not necessarily be so. The *Yamato Honzō* records, citing Li Shizhen's *Bencao*: "The *mōryō*, also written *mōryō* [魍魎], is fond of eating the livers of the dead," and this is recorded in the section on birds and beasts. How then can one say it is merely the work of a monstrous cat? Surely there must indeed be such a thing as the *mōryō*.
