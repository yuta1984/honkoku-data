**[Ansei Tōnankai Earthquake, the Great Ansei Earthquake, and Ansei Nankai Earthquake]**
Record of events from the 4th to the 10th day of the 11th month of Kaei 7 (1854). Omissions will be added after further investigation.

*"A Comprehensive Record of the Great Earthquakes and Tsunamis Across the Provinces"*

**The Great Tsunami in Osaka**

From around 2:00 PM on the 5th day of the 11th month (Seven-tsu, or "seventh bell"), a great earthquake struck, and from around 3:00 PM (Seven-and-a-half bells), the sea offshore began to roar, producing a fearsome sound like a conch shell being blown, which echoed throughout the city. People wondered with great puzzlement what was happening, and then from around 6:00 PM (Kure-Mutsu, "evening sixth bell"), waves of approximately two jō (roughly 6 meters, or about 20 feet) in height came crashing in with extraordinary force. Large and small boats numbering in the hundreds were pushed up into the Dōtonbori River—the total number was beyond count. About three hundred large boats alone were swept in, and the smaller boats were (too numerous to count). Many boats were crushed and broken by the large vessels; some had their masts snapped; the whereabouts of some ship captains and crew members became unknown; and there were very many dead and injured.

Moreover, the boats demolished the bridges, and crowds of people on the bridges fell together with them into the river—a truly terrifying and pitiful scene. On the Dōtonbori River, Yoshibashi, Karakanebashi, Sachi-bashi, and Sumiyoshi-bashi all fell, overwhelmed by the large boats. The middle section of Kanaya-bashi also broke and caved in. Large and small boats were pushed as far as Daikoku-bashi.

Furthermore, the townspeople, already frightened by the earthquake, had arranged teaboats and large cargo vessels to escape with the elderly, women, and children, who were aboard in great numbers. When the tsunami-driven vessels crashed into these boats, smashing them with their many passengers, the cries of "Please help us!" and the clasped hands of those praying for rescue were utterly heartrending. Of course, rescuers wished to help, but the force of the water was so violent that it was impossible to get close, and there was no choice but to watch helplessly as people perished—a truly lamentable state of affairs.

On the Horie River, Kurogane-bashi and Mizuwake-bashi suffered the same fate as the Dōtonbori. On the Nagahori Canal, the high bridge (Takabashi) partly survived on both banks but collapsed in the middle. Twenty to thirty boats were swept into the Tateuribori canal. Awa-bori suffered similarly. In the Zakoba area, many boats were destroyed and a number of people were killed. In the Terajima area, a great number of bodies were washed ashore on the beach. The Ajikawa River suffered likewise. Kameibashi collapsed. Damage to boats was relatively minor. No one at Tenpozan was harmed. New reclaimed lands and the Kizu River suffered great damage throughout. The total number of dead is quite impossible to estimate. The injured were extremely numerous. This great upheaval was beyond all words to describe—a catastrophe of the highest order.

**Shima Province (Ise-Shima) · Toba**

From around 8:00 AM on the 4th day of the 11th month (Five-tsu), a great earthquake caused collapses in various places. On the 5th day, around 3:00 PM (seven-and-a-half bells), another great earthquake struck, followed by a great tsunami. About sixty percent of the samurai residences were swept away; about eighty percent of the townspeople's areas collapsed or were swept away; the remaining twenty percent suffered damage. Across the entire province of Shima, there was not a single place left unscathed—truly Japan's greatest devastation. The death toll was estimated at over ten thousand, though this could not be confirmed; the number of missing was also beyond count. The magnitude of shaking was approximately two shaku (about 60 cm).

---

**Kii Province (Kishū)**

On the same day and at the same time, a great earthquake and great tsunami caused the river mouth to be swept away; about 300 or more people perished. In the areas of Kuroe, Hidaka, and Fujishiro, tidal water rose about three shaku (roughly 90 cm) above the floors, killing around 150 people. About 300 houses collapsed. All surviving structures were damaged. The number of collapsed areas within the town districts is beyond count; other details are too numerous to record with a brush.

---

**Earthquake Shaking Record**

Shaking occurred: 4th day morning at five-tsu (8:00 AM) and seven-and-a-half tsu (5:00 AM?); same night at four-tsu (10:00 PM) and eight-tsu (2:00 AM); 6th day at five-tsu (8:00 AM) and four-tsu (10:00 PM). The magnitude of shaking was approximately one shaku five sun (about 45 cm).

---

**Ise Province (Seishū)**

On the same day and at the same time, a great earthquake struck Matsusaka, Tsu, Shiroko, Kōbe, and Yamada, causing approximately half of the buildings to collapse or sustain damage—too many to count. It is reported that approximately forty to fifty people perished in each area. Many injured.

---

**Yokkaichi**

From the morning of the 11th month at five-tsu (8:00 AM), a major earthquake struck, with approximately fifty or more buildings affected. On the 5th day, around 4:00 PM (seven-tsu), another great tremor caused the earth to crack open; eighty warehouses were damaged. About 200 people were killed; the number of injured is unknown. Twelve villages in the surrounding area suffered half-collapses; deaths were relatively few. Shaking: one shaku five sun (about 45 cm).

---

**Kuwana**

On the same day and at the same time, following a great earthquake, a great tsunami swept over the coastal areas completely. Great panic as far as Ōtsu (?); many injured.

---

**Harima Province (Banshū)**

From the morning of the 4th day of the 11th month at five-tsu (8:00 AM), a great earthquake struck. More than half of Himeji Castle's castle town collapsed; what remained was also damaged. About one hundred or more people died. Many injured. Shaking: approximately eight sun (about 24 cm).

---

**Inner Harima Province (Oku-Banshū)**

In the Katō District, Kurishino area, a great earthquake caused four-tenths of the buildings to collapse; the number of damaged houses is unknown. Other villages were in the same situation as Osaka.

---

**Izumi Province (Senshi), Sakai**

On the morning of the 4th day of the 11th month at five-tsu (8:00 AM), a great earthquake struck, causing collapses in various places. On the 5th day, from around 4:00 PM (seven-tsu), another great earthquake and great tsunami struck, causing great damage in Shinchi, Kuwaya-chō, Kojima, rice markets, and other spots by the large tsunami. Various points along the main road collapsed. The number of damaged buildings is beyond count. About 60 people killed; many injured. In fifteen neighboring villages, collapses in various places and great damage; few deaths but many injuries. Shaking: approximately six sun (about 18 cm).

---

**Names of Bridges That Collapsed**

Azuma Bridge, Sakae Bridge, Ryūjin Bridge, Sumiyoshi Bridge, Isami Bridge, Aioi Bridge, Shin-Sakae Bridge, Shin-Aioi Bridge—all collapsed.

Note: Provinces not mentioned here suffered similarly to Osaka in general. From the morning of the 4th day of the 11th month through the night of the 8th day, there were eighty tremors in total.
