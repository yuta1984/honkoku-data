In manufacturing this medicinal liquid, the vessels used for the process corrode before anything else can be accomplished. For this reason, the production process is exceedingly troublesome. Glass (bīdoro) alone does not corrode. However, since the process requires intense heat, glass too cannot withstand the fierce flames. Therefore, the method begins by constructing a double-layered furnace (sōro). Sand is packed into this furnace, and a long-necked glass vessel containing the liquid is buried within the sand. A strong, active fire (katsuhi) is then placed beneath the lower level of the furnace and lit. The intense heat passes through the sand, and as the heat of the sand penetrates the glass, the liquid turns to vapor (dew) and rises, dripping down from the long neck of the glass vessel. This is what is called "sutereki waatoru." It should be noted that grape wine (budōshu) is used in the production of this liquid. If a wine made from grains is used instead, the potency of the medicine becomes dull and inferior. The apparatus used to produce the medicinal liquid is illustrated below.
