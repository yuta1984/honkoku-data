**Nowosayō (Nux Vomica / Bezoar Pills of Indian Origin)**

Nowosayō is a medicinal substance of Indian (天竺, Tenjiku) manufacture. Its form is round, varying in size with no fixed dimension. It is coated on the outside with gold leaf, and when broken open, an aromatic fragrance strikes the nose. Its internal structure is similar to that of *sakutō* (bezoard) or *goō* (cattle bezoar pills): it is wound in many successive layers, and each individual layer is likewise coated with gold leaf. Its therapeutic efficacy in restoring *shin'gen* — the fundamental vital energy of the human body — surpasses ginseng by a hundredfold, it is said. For this reason, it is used for all manner of acute conditions: shave off two or three *bu* (a small measure) and wash it down with plain hot water (白湯). It is something that the Kōmō-jin (the Dutch, "Red-Haired People") only rarely bring over, and it is the foremost medicine of priceless and extraordinary rarity.

**Bankōka (Saffron / 蛮紅花)**

Bankōka (saffron) is something that foreign traders (Nanban people) frequently bring over, and it is a medicine for the blood (血薬) known broadly to people of all stations — refined and common alike. Even a small amount of this medicine, if exposed to the wind (i.e., to open air), will dry out, and its medicinal quality and flavor will grow thin and deteriorate. There is, however, a method of preservation: place it together with an egg (鶏子) in a glass (びいとろ) vessel and seal it tightly, and it is wonderfully effective at preventing the loss of the medicine's potency. However, if it is left for too long, the medicinal vapors may cause the egg to hatch. In that case, the egg should be replaced.
