**Shiridaka-kai (Shiridaka Shell)**

This shellfish is found in Nagasaki. The largest specimens are about the size of a fist, while the smallest resemble cod roe (*tarako*) in size. They are eaten boiled. The shells can be pierced with a hole and are suitable for use as ornamental accessories worn at the sash (*obi*). [Illustration included.]

**Kama-kai (Kama Shell)**

The *kama-kai* comes from the seas around Amakusa. It is about the size of a fist, and its flavor is exceptionally fine. However, it lives at the bottom of the sea at depths of several *jō*. The local people there, intending to collect seaweed to use as fertilizer for their rice paddies, attach hooks to long poles and probe the seafloor to gather the weed by entangling it — and this shell is one that comes up clinging to the seaweed as it is hauled to the surface. **Mekabu (Wakame Root Frond)**

Mekabu comes from the coastal waters near Nagasaki around the second month (approximately March by the solar calendar). What appears in the third month is not as tender. The portion attached to the rock is cut away and discarded, and when hot water is poured over what remains, it becomes soft. When this is thoroughly pounded, it becomes sticky, like glue or lacquer. It can serve as a substitute for *tororo* (grated yam), and indeed surpasses it. It may also be seasoned with soy sauce and sake blended together, and mixed into cooked rice to eat. Alternatively, it can be dried and stored, then when needed, hot water should be poured over it and it should be pounded. One may also grind the dried form into powder and mix it into soup. Furthermore, frying the dried form in oil to eat is also an excellent preparation.
