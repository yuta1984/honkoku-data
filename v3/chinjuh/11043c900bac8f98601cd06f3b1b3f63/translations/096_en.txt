**Koruttsu (Cork)**

*Koruttsu* (cork) is a soft wood (bark) that the Dutch bring over for use as stoppers for the mouths of glass bottles (*biidoro*) and similar containers. When it absorbs moisture, it swells further and prevents water from leaking, making it an unsurpassed material for the mouths of flasks (*tokkuri*) and similar vessels. To cut it, one requires something as sharp as a razor; otherwise, it splinters and is difficult to cut cleanly. Alternatively, one may smooth it with a whetstone, shape it to the right size, and then fit it to the mouth of a container.

**Banguri (Horse Chestnut / Exotic Nut)**

Among the Dutch, there is something called a "barbarian chestnut" (*banguri*). This is a remarkable curiosity; it is about the size of a chicken's egg, and its entire surface is covered with beautiful patterns. Moreover, these patterns are raised in relief, resembling expertly executed carvings. Its color is lacquer-like and beautiful, and its shell is hard and solid. A hole is bored through it for threading a cord, and from that hole a small amount of the inner flesh is extracted; using this flesh to polish the shell brings out an ever-greater luster. It is decorated with rings of gold or silver and used as an *obimono* (an ornament worn at the sash/belt).
