Natsu-kusa Fuyu-mushi (Cordyceps sinensis — "Summer Grass, Winter Worm")

Natsu-kusa Fuyu-mushi is a medicinal substance that Chinese traders (Tōjin) have been bringing over in recent times. They regard it as a medicine for the kidneys (jin-yaku) and treat it with great care. However, the people of this country (Japan) are largely unfamiliar with it, and because they tend to undervalue it or assess it carelessly, the Chinese traders generally end up taking it back with them. This medicine does not appear at all in the Honzō (Materia Medica) or other classical texts. Its efficacy, too, was unclear — until, by chance, I came across a book of the Qing dynasty called the Shūin Sōsetsu (書隠叢説), a compilation of various matters, in which it was described. I shall now transcribe the relevant passage below for reference. The people of Nagasaki today all refer to it as "Tōyu Kasō (冬由夏草)," but in that book it is recorded as "Kasō Tōchū (夏草冬虫)."

【Passage transcribed from the Shūin Sōsetsu (rendered in modern prose)】
Once, a friend who had come from afar presented me with a certain object. Its name was Xiàcǎo Dōngchóng ("Summer Grass, Winter Worm"), and it was said to come from the borderlands of Shaanxi. In summer it becomes a plant; in winter it becomes an insect — hence the name. If steeped in wine and consumed, it is said to ward off illness and prolong life. When I observed it, it appeared to be nothing more than a dried grass root. However, upon cutting it crosswise, the form and color of the two halves differed: the half that was somewhat green barely resembled a plant in shape, while the half that was somewhat black was rather thick and coarse, and gave the impression of something writhing and about to move. As I found no record of it in any traditional texts, I set down this account here to await future investigation.
