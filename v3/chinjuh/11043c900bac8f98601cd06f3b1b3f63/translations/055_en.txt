## Hōōjō (鳳凰城 — "Phoenix Castle" Ginseng)

Hōōjō is the finest grade of ginseng. In Joseon (Korea), there is a mountain called Ryūtōzan (竜頭山 — "Dragon's Head Mountain"), and it is on the southern slopes of this mountain that this particular ginseng grows. To the north of this mountain lies Chinese territory, and ginseng produced in the northern areas is universally considered to be of inferior grade.

## Yōkaku Ninjin (羊角人参 — "Ram's Horn Ginseng")

This ginseng is also among the goods brought over by Chinese traders, and it is a high-quality (fine-grade) variety. Its flesh is clear and translucent in appearance, resembling amber. The region from which it originates is not known.

## Tama Ninjin (玉人参 — "Jewel Ginseng")

Jewel Ginseng is round in shape like a musket ball, yellow in color, clear and translucent. When cut, it is found to contain much moisture, and in the texture of its flesh it resembles Yōkaku Ninjin. It is a high-quality ginseng. In recent times it is no longer brought over. Only rarely does one still encounter pieces imported in earlier days.

## Kōchi Nikkei (交趾肉桂 — Cochinchina Cinnamon)

As for the finest grade of cinnamon (nikkei), it is a rarity even in China. What I have personally seen is of middle grade. It is stored in a tin vessel, sealed shut with paper and paste. Even at middle grade, it commands a price of approximately five hundred monme per kin.
