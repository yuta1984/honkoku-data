【On Sterk Water (すてれきわあとる)】

"Sutereki" means "strong" or "fierce," and "waatoru" means "water." This is the name of a liquid medicine brought over by the Dutch. This liquid is extraordinarily potent: when one tries dipping a piece of paper into it and pulls it out, smoke immediately rises from it. When a drop is placed on copper or iron, it corrodes and dissolves everything. When Dutch people engrave copper plates, they first pour wax over the plate, then carve fine illustrations or characters into the surface of the wax. They then pour this liquid into the grooves carved through the wax, leave it overnight, and wash away the wax — whereupon, without any further effort, all manner of engravings appear in clear and complete form. It is truly the most powerful corrosive liquid (kusari-gusuri) of all. It is so rarely brought over that it becomes an article purchased by the government (the Shogunate). For this reason, it is never available as a commercial medicine. Upon investigating its composition, it turns out that at its root it is derived from "tanban" (blue vitriol / copper sulfate)...
