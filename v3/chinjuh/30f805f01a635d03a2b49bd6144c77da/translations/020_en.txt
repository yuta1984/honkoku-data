Bears also know that in places with overhanging cover, snow does not accumulate beneath, and so some dig earthen dens to hibernate. However, even in such places, three to five feet of snow tends to drift and pile up. In the snow above a bear's den, there is always a small hole, like a tube or pipe. This is a hole melted by the bear's breath. When a hunter spots this, he digs through the snow to expose the den, then inserts branches and brushwood into the opening. The bear grabs these and pulls them inside. After this is repeated several times, the den becomes blocked, and when the bear comes to the mouth of the den, the hunter strikes with his spear. The moment he thrusts, several fierce dogs leap upon the bear at once and bite it. The dogs rely on the man, and the man relies on the dogs to kill the bear. This technique is also used when a bear has taken refuge inside a hollow tree.

○ The White Bear

That bears are black, just as snow is white, is the natural order of things—yet Heaven has turned its wheel and brought forth a white bear into the world.

○ In the spring of the third year of Tenpō (the Year of the Dragon, 1832), a woodcutter from Ōkura village, in the Uchiura and Sajuku area of Uonuma District—where I reside—entered Mount Hakkai and somehow captured a white bear cub alive. Considered a rarity in the world, it was kept as a pet, until a *kōgushi* (※ an old-fashioned term for the kind of showman called a *misemono-shi* in Edo) purchased it and exhibited it at markets, festivals, and wherever crowds gathered. I myself saw it at one such place: it was about the size of a dog, its form entirely that of a bear, its white fur so dazzling it seemed to rival the snow itself, with a lustrous sheen like velvet, and its eyes and claws were crimson. It was well accustomed to people and was an exceedingly endearing creature. It was taken about here and there, but I do not know what became of it in the end. The white tortoise that prompted a change of era name, the sacred omen of the white bird, the doves of Hachiman, the white banner of the Minamoto clan—all white things are auspicious symbols of our imperial land. Thus, Heaven's production of a white bear must likewise be a propitious omen heralding an age of everlasting peace.

　　According to the talk of mountain-dwelling people, when two or three bears are killed, or even when a single old bear is slain, the mountain where this occurs will inevitably become wild and troubled. The mountain people call this *kuma-are* ("bear-fury"). For this reason, it is said that farmers in mountain villages do not willingly go out to hunt bears. That bears possess a spiritual power is attested to in ancient writings as well.
