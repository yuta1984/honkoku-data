(Continued from previous page) There was a girl in the village who was exceptionally skilled at weaving, and the wholesaler had requested crepe cloth from her by name. She was at her loom by the window, where patches of snow still lingered, when she looked up to see something standing outside — it resembled a monkey, yet its face was not red; its head hair was long and hung down, and the figure was larger than a person, peering in at her. At that moment, all the members of the household had gone out for mountain work, and the girl was alone, so she was particularly frightened and tried to flee, but the threads of the loom were wound about her waist and she could not move freely. Before she could do anything, the creature departed. Presently, it appeared near the hearth and kept pointing at the rice tub, evidently desiring food. As the girl had already heard of this strange beast, she formed two or three rice balls and gave them to it, whereupon the creature took them away looking delighted. After that, whenever no one else was home, it would come from time to time to beg for rice, and eventually she grew accustomed to it and fed it without any sense of fear.

Now, this girl had been in the middle of weaving an urgent order of crepe cloth, but it happened that her monthly course began, and she was therefore unable to enter the sacred weaving room. (The customs of the sacred weaving room are described in detail in the first volume.) If she stopped work, the deadline would be missed. The girl, as well as her parents, worried and lamented over this. On the third day of her indisposition, at dusk, perhaps knowing that the household had not yet returned from their farmwork, the creature came for the first time in a while. Speaking to it as one would to a person, the girl told it of her distress over her monthly course, and formed a rice ball of chestnut rice and gave it to the creature. This time, unlike usual, the creature did not immediately leave; it stood for a while with an air of deep thought, and then departed. That very night, the girl's monthly course stopped completely. Finding this strange, she nonetheless purified herself, finished weaving the cloth, and her father took it to the wholesaler. Around the time it was thought to have been delivered, the girl's cycle suddenly returned of its own accord. "Surely," she said, "the creature heard my lament and came to my aid" — and all who heard the tale were filled with wonder, so it was told.

In those days, there were occasionally those who caught sight of the creature in the mountains, but it would not show itself when even one other person was present alongside the observer.

Furthermore, a retainer of the Takada Domain, having entered Mt. Kurohime on timber business with a party of woodcutters, built a shelter and spent his days on the mountain. One night, a creature resembling a monkey yet not a monkey entered the shelter and warmed itself by the fire. It stood about six shaku tall, had reddish hair, was bare-skinned, and its entire body was ash-colored…
