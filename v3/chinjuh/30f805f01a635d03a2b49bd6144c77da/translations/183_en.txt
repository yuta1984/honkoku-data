## The Fire of Jigoku Valley (Hell Valley)

In the first volume of this book, in the section titled "Fire in the Snow," I wrote about fire burning from underground in the mountains to the west of Muikamachi (Uonuma District). However, I omitted the matter of the fire at Jigoku Valley, so I record it here.

Among the most famous wonders of our Echigo Province, counted as one of the "Seven Mysteries," is the fire burning from underground in the home of a peasant named Sō-uemon (note: there are also earth fires in the homes of Shichibe and Magoroku) in Nyōhōji Village, Kanbara District — a phenomenon widely known among people. However, even more impressive than that fire is the one at Jigoku Valley, a settlement near Ojiya in Uonuma District. In China, this is called a "fire well" (火井, kasei). In recent years, a building has been constructed at this Jigoku Valley where the earth fire is used to heat water, and guests are invited to bathe there. From summer through the beginning of autumn, there are many leisure visitors. These fire wells are unheard of in other provinces; they are found only in Echigo.

Some years ago, in a certain household in Kanbara District, when a well was dug, a doctor came that same night and, having heard about the well being dug, lowered his paper lantern into the well to look inside on his way home. After he left, fire suddenly burst forth from the well and blazed up vigorously. The neighbors rushed over crying "Fire!" and, upon seeing the flames rising from the well, declared that it was because the well had been dug that the fire had appeared. The villagers one after another cursed and blamed the owner of the house. Frightened by the fire, the owner had the well filled in, it is said.

This earth fire is also called "yin fire" (陰火, inka). The yin fire of Nyōhōji Village also requires that a lit tinder stick be held near the slight drift of gas issuing from the ground; it will then ignite in response to the air current. It cannot burn without obtaining a "yang fire" (陽火, yōka — an actual flame). It is said that back in the Kanbun era, the fire began burning from the time when Sō-uemon used a bellows in his garden in Nyōhōji Village. The fire in the well mentioned earlier also must have ignited because the doctor lowered his lantern (a yang fire) into the well.

Now, furthermore, on the coast of Kubiki District, there is a post town called Nōshō-juku, which lies on the Hokuriku Highway, the official road. About two leagues (ri) inland from this post town is a village called Maseguchi. It is said that earth fires appear in the farmhouses here in the same manner as the earth fires of Nyōhōji Village. In this area, water is scarce, and during droughts people sometimes go into the mountains and dig horizontal wells to obtain water. On one occasion, when workers were digging such a horizontal tunnel and had reached a point deep inside, they used a torch to illuminate the darkness of the tunnel. Upon obtaining this yang fire, the yin fire suddenly blazed up, and a person was burned to death as a result.
