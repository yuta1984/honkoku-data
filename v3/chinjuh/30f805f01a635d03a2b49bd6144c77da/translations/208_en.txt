○ Strange Beast

From Horinouchi in Uonuma District to Tōkamachi is a distance of over seven ri, and although there are villages along the way, it is a mountain path through the interior. Now, at the beginning of summer in a certain year, the crepe-cloth wholesaler of Tōkamachi sent word to the wholesaler in Horinouchi, urging them to dispatch some white crepe cloth as quickly as possible. And so, shortly past midday that same day, they chose a powerfully built man named Takesuke, loaded him with the goods, and sent him on his way. When he had reached roughly the midpoint of the road, the sun was approaching the seventh hour (around four o'clock in the afternoon). Takesuke sat down on a roadside stone to rest a while and was eating his grilled rice when something came pushing through the dwarf bamboo in the valley below. Drawing closer to look, he saw that it resembled a monkey yet was not a monkey. The hair on its head was long, hanging down over its back, and half of it was white. Its stature was taller than an ordinary person, its face resembled a monkey's but was not red, and its eyes were large and gleaming. Takesuke was a man of stout heart, and so he drew the mountain knife he carried for protection, bracing himself to cut the creature down should it approach. But the creature showed no sign of aggression; instead, it appeared to be pointing at the grilled rice Takesuke had placed on the stone, as if begging for it. Understanding its intent, Takesuke tossed it some, and the creature ate with evident delight. Thus reassured, Takesuke gave it more, and the creature drew closer to eat. Takesuke then said, "I am traveling from Horinouchi to Tōkamachi. I shall pass this way again tomorrow on my return. I'll bring you more grilled rice then. But this is an urgent errand, so I must be off," and he moved to shoulder the load he had set down. At that, the creature took up the load, slung it lightly over its own shoulder, and set off ahead of him. Takesuke thought, "So it means to help me in return for the rice," and followed along behind, whereupon the creature moved as though the load weighed nothing at all. Thanks to its help, Takesuke passed over the rugged mountain road with ease, and when they had covered roughly one and a half ri of mountain path and approached the village of Ikedani, the creature set the load down and scrambled up the mountainside — swift as the wind, it was said. Takesuke related this in full detail to the wholesaler in Tōkamachi, and the story has been passed down to this day. This occurred some forty or fifty years before the time of writing. In those days, people who worked in the mountains occasionally reported having seen this strange beast.
