It appeared to be some kind of funeral cremation ceremony procession, but it was not. The monks came into the hall and repeatedly demanded, "It is against the rules for a layperson to be here — come out!" They berated him in every way, but the samurai had made up his mind: "No matter what happens tonight, I will not leave." He stopped responding and, steeling himself with cautious composure, endured their harassment — whereupon these figures, too, simply vanished.

At last the night began to give way, and when it seemed to be around the hour of the seventh drum-beat (just before dawn), a young novice monk of about sixteen or seventeen years of age entered the inner sanctuary through the rear gate and lit the hanging lamp, then began to pray. Then the novice's form began to change in various ways: sometimes the face grew long, sometimes short; sometimes it turned red, sometimes white; the figure grew so tall the ceiling seemed to lower, and the face spread so wide the hall itself seemed to narrow. Yet the samurai was not in the least bit intimidated. He gripped the hilt of his sword so tightly it seemed his knuckles might split, and he kept his gaze fixed upon the monster's face — until, having no other recourse, this apparition too vanished.

After it disappeared, he was suddenly jolted back to his senses, and as he was calming his shaken heart, the dawn-announcing roosters began to crow one after another, joined by the sound of the temple bell, and the sky of early morning began to brighten. He waited for his men to come and fetch him, and presently one person arrived leading a horse. "What of the rest?" he asked. The servant replied: "They will all be along shortly. As for myself, I was so worried about your welfare that, presumptuous as it was, I came on horseback." So the samurai mounted the horse, and the servant asked, "Was there anything unusual last night, my lord?" "Indeed there was," he replied. "Ghosts came three times. The first two visits did not trouble me much, but the third time, when the young novice came and changed his face in all manner of ways — that was both fascinating and terrifying." At this, the groom said, "Did the face look something like this?" — and when the samurai looked, he saw that terrible face…
