[The story] closely resembles the vendetta of the [Soga] brothers. Long ago, in a place called Nakagusuku in the Ryukyu Kingdom, there was an *aji* (local lord) named Mō Kokutei, a loyal and brave man who governed his domain well. At that time, the *aji* of Katsuren, a man called Akō, rose to the position of *gunba* at a young age and enjoyed great favor with the king, indulging himself in extravagant luxury to the utmost. However, harboring secret resentment toward Mō Kokutei, he cleverly used his eloquence to fabricate a slanderous accusation before the king, reporting that Mō Kokutei was plotting rebellion. The king was both startled and enraged, and without conducting any inquiry whatsoever, immediately granted Akō soldiers and ordered him to attack and destroy Mō Kokutei. Though Mō protested his innocence and lamented the unjust accusation, Akō refused to listen at all. Resigning himself to his fate, Mō ultimately took his own life. Mō had two sons: the elder was called Tsuru (Crane), aged thirteen, and the younger was called Kame (Turtle), aged twelve. Both children were exceptionally clever. Their father Mō had always used two treasured swords to teach them swordsmanship, training them until, young as they were, their skill was equal to that of grown men. At this time, the two boys were staying with their mother at the home of a relative named Sa Kokukichi in Sannan, when they heard that their father Mō had been forced to die a dishonored death at the hands of attackers, all due to Akō's slander. They looked up to the heavens and prostrated themselves on the ground, weeping bitterly. But wiping away their tears, they appealed to their mother: "Father's passing is something no amount of grief can undo. Since our faces are not known to the enemy, we intend to steal close to Akō, kill him, and avenge our father. We beg you to grant us the two treasured swords that Father so dearly prized." They pleaded with the utmost earnestness. The mother, forgetting her own sorrow, said: "What brave and admirable sons you are! Come then—I shall give you the two swords you desire," and took them out and divided them between the brothers. The brothers joyfully took their leave, girded themselves with their father's treasured swords as mementos, disguised themselves, and traveled to Katsuren, where they watched ceaselessly for a chance to take their revenge on their father's killer. Now, Akō, having eliminated Mō Kokutei—the man he had long despised—felt that he could now enjoy himself freely without concern for anyone. He set out with his retinue to admire the spring countryside. The brothers quickly learned of this, concealed their treasured swords in their robes, and lay in wait for even the slightest opportunity. Akō, not dreaming in the least that the two small boys were Mō's sons, said, "My, what charming little pages—come here and pour my wine," and beckoned them close to his knee. Enchanted by the brothers' beautiful faces, he drank cup after cup of wine, and in his drunken merriment, stripped off the garment he was wearing and distributed it between the brothers. Still feeling it was not enough, he even presented his own sword to Tsuru. Tsuru, sensing that the moment had come, gave his brother a meaningful glance. Without a moment's hesitation, he drew the sword and threw himself upon Akō, crying: "Do you know who we are? We are the two sons of Mō Kokutei, who took his own life because of your slander.
