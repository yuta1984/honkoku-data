(Continuation of the Imperial Edict)
[The Ryukyu envoys] respectfully observed the imperial calendar, sincerely submitted a memorial, and returned the old imperial edict and seal. We find this most praiseworthy. Therefore, We specially dispatch the Chief Envoy, Vice Official of the Bureau of Military Affairs Zhang Xue-li, and the Deputy Envoy, Messenger of the Bureau of Messengers Wang Gai, bearing the imperial edict and seal, to proceed [to Ryukyu] and invest [the ruler] as the King of Zhongshan of the Ryukyu Kingdom. In addition, We bestow upon him textiles and other goods. May the officials of your kingdom and your common people support your king, uphold the proper conduct befitting a vassal state, unite in loyal sincerity, diligently fulfill your duties, thereby accumulating blessings to endure for generations. Therefore, this edict is hereby proclaimed so that all may hear and know. Bestowed upon the King: one seal, thirty bolts of satin; upon the Queen Consort: twenty bolts of satin.

In the 3rd year [of the Shunzhi reign, 1646], the King dispatched his ministers Wu Guo-yong and Jin Zheng-chun to present a memorial of gratitude and offer tribute. They also submitted a petition stating that, upon reading the imperial edict, [the King learned] that many of his envoys had died, that they had been detained in Fujian for a long time, and that the chief and deputy envoys along with the provincial governors and other officials had been punished. However, since all within and outside [the empire] were subjects of the throne, the King, having himself received Heaven's blessings, was unable to render even the slightest service on behalf of these officials, and had instead added to their troubles. "What manner of man am I," [he wrote], "that I could rest peacefully through the night?"

The Emperor commanded that Zhang Xue-li and the others be restored to their original posts, and bestowed upon the King of Ryukyu: two bolts of mang-patterned satin, four bolts of colored satin, two bolts of blue satin, two bolts of plain satin, two bolts of shimmering satin, two bolts of brocade, two bolts of pongee silk, two bolts of gauze-weave silk, and two bolts of thin silk. To the King's maternal uncle: four bolts each of colored satin (outer and lining), four bolts of gauze-weave silk, one pair of boots, and three bolts of colored satin. To the Purple-Gold Grand Master: four bolts each of colored satin (outer and lining), three bolts of gauze-weave silk, and one pair of boots. To the envoys: two bolts each of colored satin (outer and lining) and four lengths of paper-equivalent cloth. To the interpreters and attendants: satin and cloth in graded amounts according to rank.

In the 4th year [of the Shunzhi reign, 1647], the King of Zhongshan dispatched envoys to offer incense [at imperial temples] and to present congratulations on the Emperor's ascension to the throne, along with tribute. Among the tribute goods, some were lost when ships encountered storms and capsized at Meihua Harbor. By imperial decree, [the King] was exempted from making supplemental tribute to replace them.
