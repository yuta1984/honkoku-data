The Ming Emperor admonished the king, saying: "Recently, your envoy Chikaru and others came to the capital, and We treated them with great hospitality. Upon their return, when they reached Fujian, they unlawfully seized sea vessels, violently killed government soldiers, beat and wounded eunuch officials, and stole their clothing and belongings. Chikaru, as the chief offender, was punished by the great penalty of decapitation. The remaining sixty-seven men, including Abomakessē and others, shared in this wrongdoing and their crimes likewise warranted death. However, out of regard for the king's loyalty, they were specially pardoned and sent home. Henceforth, when dispatching envoys, you should properly caution and restrain them so that they do not violate the laws of the court."

In this year (1416), the King of Chūzan destroyed Hokuzan. King Hanachi of Hokuzan was arrogant and tyrannical, relying on his own bravery. His retainer Hirabaru was physically powerful, his troops were quite fierce and vigorous, and his castle and moat were steep and difficult to approach. Ever since receiving the Ming enfeoffment, his arrogance and willfulness grew increasingly severe, and he plotted with Hirabaru to destroy Chūzan. Day by day he organized and drilled his troops. The Haneji-setsushi Anji and Nago Anji surrendered to Chūzan and reported that the King of Hokuzan was reviewing his troops, requesting a swift punitive expedition against him. Thereupon, the King of Chūzan commanded his heir apparent Hashi to urgently mobilize troops and launch an expedition against Hokuzan. Hashi then led the six anji of Urasoe, Gushikawa, Yomitanzan, Nago, Haneji, and Kunigami, departing first and arriving at the port of Kanjina. Hanachi plotted with Hirabaru and joined forces to defend against them. Hashi advanced to attack the castle. Arrows rained down from the castle walls like rain, and it was impossible to advance.
