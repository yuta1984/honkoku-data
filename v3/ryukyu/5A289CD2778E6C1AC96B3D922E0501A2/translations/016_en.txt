Tō Iesue was about to shoot Ietada. Tametomo stopped him and said, "He is a peerless
man of the nation. How could I bring myself to kill him? If we gain victory, he shall serve
our purpose another day." The enemy forces, riding the momentum of victory, pressed forward in competition. Tametomo said to Iesue,
"The enemy troops are numerous. Even though our men can each take on a hundred, we ultimately cannot
win. I will shoot their commander and strip away their fighting spirit." Iesue said,
"Can you be sure not to miss?" Tametomo said, "Brother, watch what I do."
He then shot, piercing the central ornament of Yoshitomo's helmet and then piercing through the gate door behind it. Yoshitomo, greatly startled,
called out, "Hachirō's shot is not yet precise!" Tametomo replied, "It is not that I cannot,
I simply choose not to. If permitted, I would pierce through armor and helmet alike. I merely follow
my elder brother's commands." Then he nocked an arrow. Fukaso Kiyokuni stepped forward to shield Yoshitomo.

He fell at the release of the bowstring. The warriors led by Yoshitomo were all fierce and brave men of the eastern provinces,
and these elite soldiers pressed forward in turns. Tametomo shot without a single wasted arrow. The enemy's dead and wounded were
beyond counting. Tametomo too lost twenty-three horsemen, yet still held his position firmly. Yoshitomo then set fire
to the windward side. The wind was fierce and the fire blazed intensely, throwing the palace into great disorder. The Retired Emperor
left the palace. Tame-yoshi led his warriors and followed. The enemy pursued and attacked. Tametomo repeatedly turned back to
fight. The enemy soldiers were afraid and dared not advance. Tametomo passed through the Shōgon Gate, with
one arrow remaining in his quiver. He drew the arrow, shot it through the gate pillar, and said, "Let those who come after
know the strength of my sinews." He then followed the Retired Emperor toward Mount Nyoi.
