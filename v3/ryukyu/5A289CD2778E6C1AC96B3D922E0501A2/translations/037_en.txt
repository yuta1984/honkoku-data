In the fourth year of Shōō, kanoto-u (1291), the Yuan ruler Kublai Khan dispatched sixty soldiers and ordered his minister Yang Xiang to come and attack. In the course of the voyage, they engaged in battle with troops at one island, the army's fighting spirit was broken, and they withdrew. In this same year, the Yuan ruler changed the name "Ryūkyū" (流求) to "Ruqiu" (瑠球). In the fourth year of Einin, hinoto-saru (1296), the Yuan ruler Temür again ordered his general Zhang Hao and others to come and invade. At this time, King Eiso's benevolence and authority were widely felt, and the spirit among the subjects to give their lives for the country and die in the face of adversity was strong. Finding in the end that their mission could not succeed, the invaders abducted one hundred and thirty fishermen and departed. Eiso had received abdication and remained on the throne for forty years; he passed away on the fifth day of the eighth month of the first year of Shōan, tsuchinoto-i (1299). He was seventy-one years old. The people mourned and grieved as though they had lost their own parents.

King Taisei was Eiso's son, born in the first year of Hōji, hinoto-hitsuji (1247). In the second year of Shōan, kanoe-ne (1300), at the age of fifty-four, he succeeded his father Eiso and became king. By nature he was gentle, respectful, and frugal. There was no anxiety among those above and below, and the common people enjoyed their work. He reigned for nine years and passed away on the eighth day of the twelfth month of the first year of Enkyō, tsuchinoe-saru (1308). He was sixty-two years old.

King Eiji was the son of King Taisei, born in the fifth year of Bun'ei, tsuchinoe-tatsu (1268). In the second year of Enkyō, tsuchinoto-tori (1309), he succeeded his father King Taisei and became king. He reigned for five years and passed away on the twentieth day of the ninth month of the second year of Shōwa, mizunoto-ushi (1313). He was forty-six years old.

King Tamagusku was the fourth son of King Eiji, born in the fourth year of Einin, hinoto-saru (1296). In the first year of Tokuji, hinoto-uma (1306), he governed Tamagusku and was called Prince Tamagusku. In the third year of Shōwa, kinoe-tora (1314), he succeeded his father and became king. He was given to drinking and womanizing, fond of hunting, and neglected affairs of state. The various aji lords ceased attending court, and the common people harbored resentment.
