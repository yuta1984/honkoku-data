...and we were so informed. We were also instructed to submit an estimate for the appropriate compensation (yamashiro-ryō) for the forest, but at that time, the villagers collectively stated that they did not need to receive the forest compensation, and instead wished to be appointed collectively as forest wardens, as had been the custom, and to receive the undergrowth, fallen leaves, and dead wood in common. Thus, at that time, we refrained from receiving the forest compensation.

For a year or two, the villagers collectively gathered the undergrowth. However, thereafter, we received an order from the authorities stating that no one other than the four designated forest wardens—Takehyōe, Shōbei, Tarōbei, and Takeemon—was permitted to set foot in the official forest (o-hayashi). This was communicated to the villagers by those four individuals. From that point on, only those four men were free to enter the forest, and not a single other person was permitted to do so, causing great hardship for everyone. Since we had petitioned to serve collectively as forest wardens, we had also refrained from accepting the forest compensation.

However, this forest is exceptionally overgrown with large trees, making it a difficult and rugged mountain, and the paulownia (kiri) trees that were planted there have not grown in the least. Under these circumstances, we humbly submit that this situation cannot be said to serve the interests of the authorities. Even so, the hardship for the villagers is without limit.

Furthermore, at the time of the paulownia planting, the Kaisan Office (Development Office) sent a large quantity of rice bran (konuka) and other materials, which were dug into the forest floor. As a result, the undergrowth has grown exceptionally well. However, those four men have cut down all of it, and since others are forbidden from entering the forest, the villagers are in great distress.

Therefore, on this occasion, we humbly present this petition. We earnestly request that you carefully consider these circumstances and, with exceptional compassion, return the forest to us, allowing it once again to be held collectively by the villagers as it has been since ancient times. We most sincerely beseech you to issue such an order.

Respectfully submitted.
