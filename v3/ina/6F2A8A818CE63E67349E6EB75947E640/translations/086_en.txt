The 25th day

Item: At Edo, Lord Fuon'in had been ill, and on the 21st of last month, the御将太夫 (a senior official) paid a visit on behalf of [the domain]. Accordingly, [a messenger] went to inquire after his health and reported the matter to the Senior Councilors (okaro-jū). Respectfully submitted.

Item: From Jūzō-dono, regarding the matter of Lord Fuon'in's mourning rites (or related ceremonial observance) to be performed on the 15th of the following day — the fish weir supervisors (omae), the inner staff (oku insū), and the close-attendant inspectors (okinjū metsuke) all submitted their reports. It was ordered that until the 11th of next month, such matters need not be raised, and that [the information] be retained until it must be reported. Furthermore, at the fish weir, when two or three [fish] of approximately eighty momme value are caught on a given occasion, there is occasion to present [them]. Moreover, it was ordered that [this be handled] in the manner of the weir-master.

The 27th day

Item: During the night, [work was conducted] at the fish weir — firewood handling, the "ushi-kirikomi" [a weir-cutting operation], and clearing/diverting — and from around the hour of the ninth watch (approximately midnight), from the inner room, the following official items were retrieved. Jūzō-dono was reported to accordingly during the night. Jūzō-dono himself was present and took care of the matter. Therefore, the fish weir need not be held in custody [by this office].

Item: On the 28th and 29th days, [the messenger] did not arrive. Around midday on the 29th, the fish weir's upper section was completely cut off [by high water]; the firewood section said [the water] was being dammed up.
