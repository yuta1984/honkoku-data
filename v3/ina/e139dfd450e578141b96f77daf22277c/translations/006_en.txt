(Continuing from previous page) …When a death has occurred (among a member's family), (the association members) shall attend to the affairs with sincere and thorough care. When (members) are present within the household (of the bereaved), since the chief mourner's household ■■ (illegible) will have concluded (certain rites), and furthermore as various matters will be in a state of confusion and disorder, there will be a tendency for things to fall short of what is ideal. Without waiting until such a moment (of need), not only the monthly official (月行事) but all members of the association shall confer together and ensure that no delays occur in any and all matters ■■ (illegible), and shall attend to affairs without coldness or indifference. Members shall on no account voice any complaint or dissatisfaction toward the chief mourner (施主). As this is a matter of mutual benefit and reciprocal obligation, members should take great care and attend to all things with warmth and sincerity. Naturally, even if the sake and food offerings at the grave should be modest or plain, one shall not, upon attending, voice any complaint of dissatisfaction. This particular matter shall be especially noted and recorded in the three copies (三室), and all members of the association shall solemnly agree to abide by it.

At the biannual gatherings (二季の寄合) and the like, (members) shall come together in harmony within their respective means and station, observe proper conduct, and disperse (after the meeting) in accordance with what has been agreed upon. Monthly dues (月寄) shall also be submitted without delay, so that those on duty (当番) shall suffer no inconvenience.
