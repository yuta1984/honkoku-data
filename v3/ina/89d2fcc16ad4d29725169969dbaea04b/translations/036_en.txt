Memorandum of Oral Statement

Item: The plot facing east along the second row of the grave site has been shared as one section (*hitokawa*) by both the Heibei and Kyūemon households. The one section of sixteen stone grave markers (*sekitō*) facing west at the rear of that same site has been in the possession of my ancestors from generation to generation, of which I have proof. However, in the sixth month of the 7th year of Kaei, the Year of the Tiger (1854), Heibei's household took the liberty of engraving their ancestors' posthumous Buddhist names (*hōmyō*) on my family's stone markers, treating them as though they were their own property. While I was in the process of remonstrating over this matter, in the eighth month Heibei's daughter passed away. For that reason, I held off [on pursuing the matter], and in the meantime Heibei himself also passed away. Feeling sympathy for their circumstances, I left the situation as it was. As a result, I believe the current Heibei [i.e., the successor] may have come to regard that grave site as his own land. The petition submitted by Heibei on this occasion requests that when members of Kyūemon's household pass away, they not be allowed to inter [their remains] in the said grave site, and that should such a situation arise, [the authorities] be asked to prohibit it.

Item: Although I had wished to file a petition concerning the unauthorized use of my ancestors' stone markers by Heibei's household, I refrained from doing so out of reluctance to trouble the authorities (*kami-sama*). Since Heibei's household has now submitted its own petition on this occasion, I hereby submit this written account of the circumstances surrounding the grave site.
