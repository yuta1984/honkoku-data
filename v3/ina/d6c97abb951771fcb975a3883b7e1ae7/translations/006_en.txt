[Continued from previous page] …it has become difficult for townspeople to make their living, which we find deeply lamentable.

Item: Regarding salt, tea, and dried seafood (isaba) and the like — from former years these goods were handled by the wholesale merchants (toiya) of the town, who received their customary commission (kuchi-sen) and sold the goods accordingly. However, in recent years, merchants from the surrounding villages have taken to purchasing these goods directly from other places, and as a result sell them at prices lower by exactly the amount of the wholesaler's commission. This in turn makes the townspeople's goods appear to be overpriced by comparison, which we report with great trepidation. Furthermore…

Item: In recent years, due to poor harvests (ishaku), transportation costs for goods from various provinces have risen sharply. In particular, the cost of packhorse transport from the Iida area to this place (Takato) was until quite recently approximately 1,500 mon per three horse-loads, but at present it has risen to around 20 monme per three horse-loads, and at times even as high as approximately 30 monme. As a result, the prices of all manner of goods have risen accordingly,
