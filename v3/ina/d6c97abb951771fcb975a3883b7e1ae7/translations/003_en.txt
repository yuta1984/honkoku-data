## Memorandum of Oral Statement (Translation, continued)

*(Continued from previous page)* ...there are no more than two establishments doing any substantial brewing to speak of — a situation we find deeply lamentable.

Item: In former years, there were wealthy and prosperous merchants in the town, and so from the Three Metropolises (Edo, Kyoto, and Osaka), as well as from various provinces, large quantities of goods were procured and wholesaled to villages in the surrounding area. In particular, regarding cotton and woven cloth (*futomono*), vast quantities were shipped in from Osaka, distributed even to small shops throughout the town, and payment was collected in two installments per year (twice-yearly settlements). This arrangement helped maintain financial circulation among all residents. However, at present things have come to such a pass that small amounts of cotton and woven cloth are purchased with cash from Suwa, Kōshū (Kai Province), or from Iida and Matsumoto. Whereas in former years we used to wholesale goods from this place to the villages of the surrounding domains and territories (*go-henryō*), the merchants have gradually grown weaker in means — a situation we find deeply lamentable.

*(Interlinear note)* Item: At present, in the rural villages (*zaichū*), there are those who are energetically engaged in various trades, and in particular... a great many have come to do so...
