As a result, the supply of grain flowing into the town has naturally run short, and all [townspeople] are suffering hardship…

[Left folio]

…and are in a state of financial inflexibility and difficulty. For this reason, there is nothing that can be procured from the townspeople for the domain's corvée laborers (shihaibe), and that is how matters stand. We find it distressing even to have to report this, and humbly beg your understanding.

As stated above in the various items we have presented, the town is declining day by day, and those currently [living there] are barely managing to eke out a livelihood. Those who remain are merely getting by in much the same way as before, suffering nothing but hardship. In former times, as the castle town was flourishing, even when tasks and duties were assigned to us by the authorities — with all due deference — we were fully able to carry out and supply what was required. However, at the present time, even when the most minor duties are assigned to us, they create a strain on our capacity to fulfill them, which we find most distressing and humbling. Gathering together on this matter, we have deliberated with various concerns, but we are utterly unable to arrive at any good plan, and each of us is burdened with nothing but anguish.

[Boxed section]
We humbly wish that, beyond this point, generous measures [and consideration] be extended to us, so that the town may be restored to the state it was in in former years.
[End of boxed section]

Moreover, as the townspeople have been petitioning one after another, as stated in the foregoing text, at the present time those who engage in various trades in the rural villages each possess agricultural land (rice paddies and fields), and moreover conduct trade as a supplementary activity. Therefore, even in the event of a poor harvest, they face no serious difficulties, and are naturally able to make a firm and stable living. In the town, however, those who own agricultural land are rare, and in seasons when the aforementioned grain supply falls short, having to purchase everything for daily sustenance means that, even if a considerable profit [is made in trade], when compared to [rural] livelihoods, [the townspeople] tend to come up short. It is in this way that the castle town has gradually come to its present state. As for the villages, they were originally [engaged in their] primary occupation [of farming] — once [farming] needs are attended to, they devote themselves exclusively to cultivation and agricultural work, and conduct trade only as a secondary activity. Moreover, second and third sons [of farming families] having been sent [to the town] at the time [of its founding] □, [they were expected to] engage in commerce and [help] the town be populated with many people… [continues on next page]
