### Humbly Submitted as a Written Response

Item: Regarding the matter of investment funds for the joint sake brewing operation, which has been in effect since the ninth month of Kaei 2 (1849), we are deeply humbled that Wata'ya Kūzō of Niēkawa-juku in Kiso submitted a petition to the honorable government office through a representative.

Item: On the twenty-sixth day of the ninth month of this year (the Year of the Horse), three people arrived at my residence: Chūshichi, acting as representative for Kūzō of Niēkawa-juku; one retainer of Rokuemon of Shimo-Yokogawa village. They lodged at my home and conducted negotiations in good faith over the course of several sessions. Concerning the loan from Hachiemon and Shoemon, various matters regarding a three-part (三分) interest rate were discussed, and given that there was a prior relationship with Kūzō, through Hachiemon a proposal for a joint sake brewing operation was made. It was arranged for both parties to invest two hundred ryō each as planned capital. Regarding this money, due to internal rules of Hachiemon, it was to be held until the twenty-eighth, and after various arrangements, fifty-five ryō were duly received and acknowledged, and they departed.

Item: On the second day of the tenth month, my son Kikuhachi was sent to Shoemon's residence in Shimo-Yokogawa village to negotiate. Concerning the loan (on'kin), it was requested that fifty-five ryō be deducted from the principal, and that the remaining sum be repaid in installments over twenty years; otherwise, since there remained considerable amounts under approximately five items, after reviewing the account books, we wished to arrange for a two-part (20%) settlement. As there was clear evidence for the fifty-five ryō, and it was also written in the original contract document as an agreed matter, we requested to review the original contract at some point, but were not shown the documents. When the two-part profit-sharing arrangement for the joint operation was raised, we were told that the entire sake brewing operation would be left entirely in our hands, and what had been invested funds were now called loans. Regarding the calculation of interest, up until now, no interest was to be charged until the Year of the Hare (Uzudoshi), and the two-part profit was to be [settled] in the Year of the Tiger (Toradoshi) with three parties present…
