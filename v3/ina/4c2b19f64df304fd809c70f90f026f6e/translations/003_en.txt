(Continued from previous page) We had agreed that we wished to review the evidence for the fifty ryō as a balance from the account ledger, but the evidence was not shown to us. When we said we would repay the money, they complained that the original document would need to be rewritten, and stated that this was because Kūzō had passed away, and so on. We maintained that there should be reliable receipt evidence as recorded in the original document, and continued to request that such evidence be presented.

Item: On the morning of the twenty-third day of the eleventh month, Chūshichi of Niikawa-juku and Shoemon of Shimoyokogawa village came to Sehachi's residence, saying they intended to file a petition at the Takatō magistrate's office regarding the matter of Denjirō's money. We refused their request and stated that we had no recollection of having added our seal to any borrowing document from Niikawa-juku, whereupon they appeared extremely displeased and left after paying their respects to Lord Matsu.
　Regarding the three ryō that was paid over separately:

　　　　　　　2 bu 2 shu　　　One satin sash
　　　　　　　2 shu　　　　　　One woman's cape collar
　The above sum was paid as the purchase price for the aforementioned goods.

Item: On the twenty-eighth day of the eleventh month, we received a summons, and as we had been lodging at Kisoya in good faith, following their petition regarding the matter of money from Niikawa-juku in which sake brewing equipment and business licenses had been listed as collateral, we were instructed to reach a settlement. Through Matsudaya Rokurōemon, we came on the morning of the twenty-ninth to present our case fairly and request a settlement. Although arrangements had been made on the twenty-fifth, the other party stated that they could not agree to a settlement unless it was based on the full principal and interest of 260 ryō, and that in any case, they could not agree to a settlement if 55 ryō were to be deducted from the principal. This was conveyed to us by Rokurōemon.
