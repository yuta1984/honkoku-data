(Continued from previous page) The matter of detaining the horse and dispatching the arresting party with said horses.

One: On the second day of the eleventh month, the pardon decree (oyurushijō) was issued, and officials from various villages and townships appeared. Regarding Arama Township (Sessha-gō), the village headmen (nanushi) were each informed that there was official business requiring their presence and were ordered to remain in lodging. They were instructed to stay until the fifth day, depart together on the morning of the sixth day, and serve as guides along the route. The six individuals so appointed were:
　　Iwadare Kichibei, Osobu Heiemon,
　　Nishi-Arama Shichirobei, Onozawa Saburōbei,
　　Hari'o Heisaku, Furumi Rokurōemon — six persons in total.

One: The plan was now firmly set. On the morning of the fifth day of the tenth month, instructions were issued to the various units. With Karasawa Okizaemon of the advance guard unit (o-sakite-gumi) serving as overall coordinator, individual meetings were held at the township office, the articles of the regulations were read aloud to each person, and small instruction slips (kogiri-gami) and unit identification tags (kumiai-fuda) were distributed. They were given detailed instructions to depart from near their respective homes at the half of the fifth hour (approximately 9:00 a.m.) on the morning of the following sixth day, and to assemble at Miyaki and at Shiojiri Pass.

One: In preparation at Miyaki-juku, Ōta Montayū, Odagiri Kinbei, and Yajima Yagokichi departed on the fifth day. They carried gandō lanterns, snake-eye-patterned lanterns, and candles; purchased straw sandals (waraji) at Miyaki; and set out charged with arranging rest lodgings for the various personnel and overseeing the provision of horses and porters.

One: Hirasawa Hikoji and Kurotani Chūji, who departed on the fifth day, proceeded to Kamishima Village. Treating their post as a checkpoint (sekisho), and in anticipation of the arresting party being dispatched from this side to apprehend persons in Arama Township, they set out with instructions that should they spot anyone resembling a courier (hikyaku), they were to detain that person and prevent passage until the arresting party arrived.

One: Misawa Yozō and one other person, who departed on the fifth day, proceeded to Minamisawa Village. Treating their post as a checkpoint, they were to act in the same manner as those at Kamishima.
