Messages were repeatedly sent stating that [we] must not enter [the mountain], and so we appeared in person to inquire about the reasoning. However, without any valid justification whatsoever, we were told to cease access from the central boundary (nakami) upward. We attempted to resolve this quietly through private negotiation, but the other party showed no willingness to accept reason or come to a reasonable agreement. We therefore had no choice but to bring this matter before the authorities.

Item: Regarding the communal mountain, there is no record of acreage (chōbu), and access has been conducted using the plot names, character names, and general names applicable at the time. There are no written boundary records based on the Fujikawa cutoff, ridgeline paths, or village boundary lines in general, but the evidence that we have [been coming to this mountain] is——

[Left page]

——that the places from which grasses and timber are brought down from the mountain plots are called "arashi" (cleared/harvested areas), and there are limitless such areas that suffice for our daily needs. If, in your benevolence, you would be pleased to conduct an inspection, the places to which we have had access up to the ridgeline path will be made abundantly clear, and we humbly trust that this will reach your wise and careful consideration.

Item: As for our village, beyond the repairs needed for the current flood damage, we have fallen into the depths of poverty, and have no means by which to restore ourselves within the domain. We suffer to the utmost degree. Furthermore, even in the fields that remain after the flooding, the soil is a mixture of sandy subsoil (mane tsuchi) and fine sand, making them poor agricultural land——
