(Continued from the previous folio) — they shall submit a petition to this office accompanied by a covering letter (添翰) from their supervising authority.

1. When Shinto priests (神官), Buddhist monks (僧徒), mountain ascetics (修験者), novice monks (所化僧), blind guild masters (検校), farmers (百姓), townspeople (町人), and others residing in small-precinct areas throughout the provinces travel on official business or on business directed by their domain lord, they shall petition their respective supervising authority and borrow the seal of that authority for passage.

1. Regarding the procurement of porters (人足) in Kyoto: whereas until now this has been handled through individual private contracts (相対雇い入れ), when traveling on official business, those among the court nobility (宮・堂上家) who hold their own domains have hitherto used their own domain subjects (領民) for this purpose. However, since auxiliary transport villages (助郷) are now being assigned and rotated among the various post stations, henceforth porters shall also be stationed at the post-horse offices (伝馬所). For those traveling on official business for whom this office issues its seal, porters shall be dispatched at the fixed wage rate. The auxiliary transport villages attached to the post-horse offices shall be organized and mobilized when pressed into service beyond their regular routes.

In recent times, as commodity prices have risen sharply and general exhaustion has spread throughout the surrounding villages, the rates for human and horse transport along all highways shall, for a period of one year from the fifth month of this Year of the Dragon (辰年, 1866) to the fifth month of the following year, have the existing supplementary surcharges (割増) removed, and shall be set at a 65% increase (six-and-a-half tenths above the base rate, i.e., 50% above the base) — (text continues on next folio)
