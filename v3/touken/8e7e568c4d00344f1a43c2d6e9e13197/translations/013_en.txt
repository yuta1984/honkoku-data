One: Munechika (annotated split text: jō-jō / highest ranking), Reign of Emperor Ichijō — Around the Eien era (987–989) — Sanjō Kokaji

The tachi's shape is elegant and slender (kare-tari), with a somewhat compact kissaki (ko-kissaki). The mune is iori-style, and the forging pattern (kitae) is masame-hada (straight grain), exceedingly fine in texture. The ji (surface) is dark, tinged with purple, and lustrous. However, early works have a somewhat whitish ji color. From the middle period onward, the forging became more refined, and the blade shimmers with a bluish gleam. After that period, the ji color, while still bluish, becomes somewhat subdued and murky, with a ne-yaki (base-tempered) coloration. The shinogi (ridge line) of the tachi is somewhat wide. The hamon generally displays a flame-like activity (hi wo kaku). The blade is forged with a slender suguha (straight temper line) with ko-ashi (small foot-like formations) present. There are also ko-midare-ba (small irregular temper) with tare-midare (drooping irregular temper), and toward the base (koshi-moto), the temper is rendered in a slender midare-ba (irregular pattern), while overall some have suguha (straight temper). Nie is abundant. The nakago (tang) tapers toward the tip. The yasurime (file marks) are sujikai (diagonal). The tip of the nakago is sometimes cut in a form resembling a ken-gashira (sword pommel shape — a clipped angular form), and sometimes rounded.
