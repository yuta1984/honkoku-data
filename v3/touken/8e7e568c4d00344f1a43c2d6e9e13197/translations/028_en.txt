One entry: Kunikiyo (国清) [interlinear note: Upper-middle Imperial Treasure (chūjō go-mono)] — active during the same reign. Around the Genji era (interlinear correction visible: "Gen" crossed out, replaced by "Ken"ji era). Also known as Atsutōshirō (厚藤四郎).

The sugata (form) of the tachi resembles that of Kuniyasu. The iori-mine (ridged spine) is steep (kyū). The kitae is masame (straight grain), and the ji (surface) color is dark. It is tempered in a narrow sugu-ha (straight hamon). There are kinsuji (gold-colored lines) in the jihada (surface texture), and it resembles the work of Kuniyoshi. Both the tachi and the katana have a yakitsume tip (hamon running to the very tip). The katana has a mitsu-mine (three-ridged) spine.

One entry: Arikuni (有国) [interlinear note: Upper-middle Imperial Treasure (chūjō go-mono)] — active during the same reign, the same period.

The sugata of the tachi is slender and long; the shinogi (ridge line) is narrow and high.

It resembles Kuniyasu's work. The kitae grain is not so clearly discernible and is fine. However, it is masame. Like Bichū (備中) work, there is a clear, refined hada in the ji. The ji color is dark. It is tempered in a narrow sugu-ha. There is niē, the hamon is tight and has a bluish quality. The sori (curvature) of the tachi is pronounced, and the iori-mine is steep (kyū).
