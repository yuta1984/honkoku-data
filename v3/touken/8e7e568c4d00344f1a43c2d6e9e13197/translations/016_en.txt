One: Kuniyoshi (annotated split text: ge-jō / second-lowest upper ranking) — Reign of Emperor Kameyama — Around the Shōgen era (1259–1260)
He is the father of Rai Tarō Kuniyuki. The founder (progenitor) of the Rai school.

The tachi's shape has a high shinogi (ridge) and a slightly thin mune (back edge). The iori-mune (ridged back) is of medium depth, and the kissaki (point) is compact and gently rounded (tsutsumayaka). The forging is masame-hada (straight grain). The ji color at the base (soko) is vivid and lively (kyō nite), with a reddish (warm) tone there, while the upper portion (toward the tip) is whitish. The hamon color is slightly dark and white. The nie (crystalline martensite) appears in scattered clumps (mura-mura). Where the ashi (branching lines into the hamon) are fine and closely nie-laden, it is beautiful and genuine. The nakago (tang) tapers toward the tip. The tip is rounded. The yasurime (file marks) are horizontal, running slightly diagonal from lower to upper. It is far inferior to Kuniyuki, but has an exceptionally aged, venerable character.

One: Kuniyuki (annotated split text: chū-jō / middle upper ranking — Go-mono / Imperial collection piece) — Reign of Emperor Fushimi — Around the Shōō era (1288–1293) — Rai Tarō

The tachi's shape has a high curvature (sori). For the most part, a hi (groove) is carved. The iori-mune is of medium depth, and the kissaki is also medium. The forging is masame-hada and fine (komayaka). The ji color is darkish and appears bluish toward the base, with a reddish hue toward the upper portion. The hamon is of a broom-like (hahaki) shape, fanning out broadly from the base, with approximately one shaku (≈30 cm) from the koshi-moto (base of the blade) rendered in midare-ba (irregular temper). Moving upward, the hamon transitions to a yellowish straight temper (ki-sugu-ha) for about one to two sun (≈3–6 cm), and then, at intervals of about five bu (≈1.5 cm), ashi (feet/branching lines) are burned in.
