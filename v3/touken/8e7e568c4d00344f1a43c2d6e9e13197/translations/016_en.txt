One: Kuniyoshi (annotated split text: ge-jō / second-lowest upper ranking) — Reign of Emperor Kameyama — Around the Shōgen era (1259–1260)
　　　　　　Father of Rai Tarō Kuniyuki; the founding ancestor of the Rai school

The tachi's shape has a high shinogi (ridge line) and a somewhat thin mune (back edge). The iori-mune (ridged spine) is of medium depth, and the kissaki (point) is compact and gently rounded (tsutsumayaka). The forging is masame-hada (straight grain) and fine. The ji color at the base (ne/moto) has a yellowish hue, while toward the tip it is whitish. The hamon color is slightly dark yet also white. The nie (crystalline martensite particles) appears in uneven clusters (mura-mura), and where the ashi (short lines descending from the hamon) appear with fine, delicate nie, it is beautiful and upright in character. The nakago (tang) narrows toward the tip, which is rounded. The yasuri-me (file marks on the tang) are横 (horizontal/yoko), angling slightly diagonally upward from below. Although far inferior to Kuniyuki, it has an extraordinarily aged and venerable quality.

One: Kuniyuki (annotated split text: chū-jō-jō mono / upper-middle, finest grade) — Reign of Emperor Fushimi — Around the Shōō era (1288–1293) — Rai Tarō

The tachi's shape has a high sori (curvature). Most examples have a carved hi (groove). The iori-mune is of medium depth, and the kissaki is also of medium length. The forging is masame-hada, fine and close. The ji color at the base appears darkish and bluish, with a reddish tint appearing toward the tip. The hamon is of the haki-dashi type (sweeping outward from the base), with broad yakidashi at the moto. From the base area, approximately one shaku (approx. 30 cm) is tempered in midare-ba (irregular pattern), then transitioning upward into a yellowish suguha (straight hamon), with ashi (feet) tempered in at intervals of approximately one to two sun (3–6 cm) and again at about five bu (approx. 1.5 cm) apart.
