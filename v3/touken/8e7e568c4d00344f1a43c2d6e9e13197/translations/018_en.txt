(Continued from previous page) ...there are also examples tempered with chōji (clove pattern) in the upper section. In the upper portion, the chōji forms large clusters (ō-fusa). In the suguha (straight hamon) with tari-midare (gentle irregular undulations), nie is abundant. The tachi with chōji-ba resemble those of Bizen Ichimonji and similar schools, but without nie; the kitae (forging) is masame (straight grain), the ji-iro (surface color) is dark, the upper color is reddish and has a glittering quality — it is a well-forged jigane (blade steel). The two-character signature "Kunitoshi" represents a highly skilled maker. Yubashiri (subsidiary tempered areas) are also tempered along the middle of the iori-mine (ridged spine). The interior of the kissaki is straight, with a slight tendency toward irregularity (secret teaching).

A note states: The period in which straight hamon were being tempered corresponds to Shōwa, Bunpō, and Gen'ō eras (approx. 1312–1321).

The tachi's shape has a wide and high shinogi (ridge). The iori-mine (ridged spine) is also slightly steep. Along the edge of the suguha, there are spots slightly set back from the edge where the nie-lined hamon is about as thick as a heavy thread. These are found roughly around the monouchi (striking area). Since it is suguha, the koshi-moto (lower portion near the base) is tempered in a yellowish tone for about four to five sun (approx. 12–15 cm). The kaeri (turnback at the kissaki) is not particularly long. Tantō (short swords) signed with the two-character "Kunitoshi" are rare. One exists in a private collection (shisen). The ji-iro (surface color) resembles that of Yoshimitsu. The kaeri is the same as that of the standard Kunitoshi. The nakago (tang) of the tachi closely resembles that of Kuniyuki.
