In the areas of drooping and irregular hamon (tare-midare) with suguha, nie is abundant. The chōji-ba tachi resembles those of Bizen Ichimonji and similar schools, but without nie; the kitae (forging pattern) is masame (straight grain), the ji-color (surface color) is dark, the upper area has a reddish hue, with a bright glittering quality — it is a well-forged jihada. The two-character signed "Kunitoshi" is a master craftsman. Yubashiri (subsidiary tempered zones) are also tempered along the center of the iori-mune (peaked spine). The interior of the kissaki is straight but carries a slight suggestion of irregularity — this is a secret teaching (hiji).

An addendum states: The period in which suguha was tempered was around the Shōwa, Kubo, and Gen'ō eras (approximately 1312–1321).

The tachi form has a wide and high shinogi (ridge). The iori-mune is also somewhat steep. At a slight distance from the edge of the suguha hamon, there are nie as thick as a thread of yarn. This occurs approximately around the mono-uchi (striking zone). Because the hamon is suguha, the koshi-moto (lower base area) is tempered with a yellowish hue for about four to five sun. The kaeri (turnback of the hamon at the kissaki) is not particularly long. Tachi bearing the two-character signature "Kunitoshi" are rare. In my personal view, the ji-color resembles that of Yoshimitsu. The kaeri is the same as that of the ordinary Kunitoshi. The nakago (tang) of the tachi closely resembles that of Kuniyuki.
