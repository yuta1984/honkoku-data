Ko-midare (small irregular hamon) and also notarika (gently undulating) hamon are tempered [with a character that is unclear in the original]. Among katana, there are some tempered in the manner of the kaeri (turnback) of Bizen-style points. (This swordsmith) was previously a disciple of Sakon-shōgen Nagamitsu. He appears to have been a highly skilled craftsman. The time when he received transmission from Kunitoshi should have been during the Bunpō era (1317–1319). As for tachi, none have yet been seen. It is recorded that Chūdō-rai (works of the Chūdō school) consists of katana only. This is a secret matter, and oral transmission exists.

One entry: Ryōkai [interlinear note: middle (rank)] — active during the reign of Kōmyō-in (Emperor Kōmyō), in the Ryakuō era (1338–1342), and is the son of Kunitoshi.

In general, the form is somewhat slender, the shinogi (ridge line) is high, and ko-ashi (small feet) are tempered into a fine suguha (straight hamon). The kitae (forging) is masame (straight wood-grain pattern), producing a fine and refined hada (surface texture). The mine (back ridge) is of the iori-mine style, and within the ji (surface) there is a tendency toward yubashiri (a flowing activity in the ji). This is not a trait limited to this smith's works, nor is it an entirely defining characteristic. The hamon at the koshi-moto (base area near the habaki) is tempered in ko-midare (small irregular pattern), resembling the works of Kagemitsu of Bizen and others. However, toward the upper portion (toward the tip), the hamon shifts to suguha (straight). The ji color is dark, and the hamon color is bluish with a languid (toromeita), subdued quality. Tachi with hi (grooves) carved into them are numerous, displaying niē (hard martensite crystals) and a fine texture.
