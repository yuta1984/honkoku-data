1. Tomomitsu 《Marginal note: Lower-Upper [rank]》 Same reign, same period

The shape of the tachi is withered/austere in character, with a wide and high shinogi. The forging is masame-hada (straight grain pattern), extremely fine. The ground color is dark. It has a ko-midare-ba (small irregular temper pattern). From the base (koshimoto), it is tempered out in a narrow, straight line, and from around the monouchi (striking area) it broadens into a wide temper line (hiro-ha). The narrow straight base is dominant, while the rank/quality of the hamon is slightly inferior. The color reflection of the hamon is skillfully done. Oral tradition exists.

1. Kunitsugu　From the reign of Emperor Go-Daigo, around the Gentoku era (1329–1331), child of the younger sister of Kunigo

The shape of the tachi appears whitish-yellow, the left side of the kissaki has a deep iori (ridge). There is generally a thick hi (groove), and the overall appearance resembles that of Kuniyuki. The forging is itame-hada (wood grain pattern). In this work as well, midare-ba (irregular temper pattern) is tempered at the base, and toward the tip it transitions into a yellowish suguha (straight temper line) with ashi (extensions) tempered in.
