There are many points that differ depending on the region (school). First, the method of examining the higher-ranked (superior) works is recorded here.

One: Norinaga (annotated split text: lower/upper) — Shikkake — Reign of Emperor Gonijo — Around the Kagen era (1303–1306)

The tachi's shape is slender and long, the shinogi (ridge line) is wide and extends far, and the kissaki (tip) is somewhat compact. It has an iori-mune (peaked back ridge) and is of an old style. Straight hamon (suguha) is preferred in the tempering, though irregular hamon (midareba) is also common. Regarding the nature of nie (crystalline martensite), there is a secret oral tradition. In the midareba, there are ashi (short lines extending from the hamon).

In the narrow areas between sections, the nie is fine and dense, while in the wider areas of the hamon, the nie is coarser. There are two generations of Shikkake smiths, sharing the same signature. The senior generation (grandfather) was highly skilled, while the grandson was inferior. The first two generations signed as "Yamato Norinaga" without including the character for the province name separately. The grandson signed as "Yamato no ju Norinaga" (Residing in Yamato, Norinaga). This is a secret matter. The tip of the nakago (tang) is shaped like a ken-kashira (sword pommel), with the blade side filed in an old manner, cutting from the shinogi line toward the mune (back) side of the blade edge.
