...the image remained open (to veneration), yet the figure was left empty to one side, and the forty-eight marks (of the Buddha) remained, yet could no longer be directly beheld. It was at this point that his subtle words spread far and wide, rescuing all sentient beings sunk in the Three Evil Paths (hell, hungry ghosts, and animals), and his bequeathed teachings were proclaimed afar, guiding all living beings through the Ten Stages (of bodhisattva practice). And yet, the true teaching was difficult to look up to, and there was none who could unify its essential meaning into one; deviant learning was easy to follow, and it distorted what was correct. Accordingly, the discourse on Emptiness and Existence (the debate between śūnyatā and *vijñaptimātratā*) was debated as right or wrong according to the customs of one's school, and the Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna vehicles rose and fell in turn with the flow of time.
