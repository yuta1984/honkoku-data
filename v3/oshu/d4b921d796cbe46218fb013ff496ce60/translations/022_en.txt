How could anyone compare him with Tang or Wu for superiority, or with Yao or Shun for sagely virtue? (Indeed, he surpassed even them.) The Dharma Master Xuanzang, from his youth, harbored lofty aspirations and an unbounded will. His spirit was pure and simple, his mind clear and serene. Even in his childhood years, he possessed a character that stood apart from the superficiality of the world. He concentrated his mind in the stillness of the meditation hall, concealing himself among secluded rocky crags. Resting in the three dhyānas, he wandered through the ten stages (of bodhisattvahood), transcending the realm of the six dusts, walking alone in Kapilavastu, grasping the essence of the One Vehicle teaching, and transforming sentient beings according to their capacities.
