...(the Dharma Master Xuanzang) was the foremost leader among the gates of the Dharma. From childhood, he harbored a pure and keen (spirit), and early on awakened to the mind of the Three Emptinesses (emptiness of self, emptiness of phenomena, and emptiness of both). As he grew, he entered into deep accord with divine understanding, and from the outset had already encompassed within himself the practice of the Four Kinds of Forbearance. The wind through the pines and the moon reflected on water are insufficient to compare with the purity and brilliance of his virtue; how could the dew of immortals or luminous pearls possibly match the clarity and radiance of his character? Thus, his wisdom penetrated all things without obstruction, his spirit perceived what had not yet taken form; he transcended far beyond the Six Dusts (the six sense objects: form, sound, smell, taste, touch, and dharmas) and stood alone through the ages without equal. Concentrating his mind upon the inner realm, grieving at the (decline of) the True Dharma...
