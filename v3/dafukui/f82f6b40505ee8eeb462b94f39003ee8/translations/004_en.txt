Item: On the third day of the First Month, to celebrate the New Year's first utai (nō chanting ceremony), a messenger was sent to present, in accordance with custom, a ceremonial sake cup stand (sakazuki-dai) and sake money (o-sakadai).

Item: On the seventh day of the same month, to celebrate the Festival of Young Herbs (wakana), a messenger was sent to present fresh sea bream (sen-tai) in accordance with custom.

Item: On the twenty-eighth day of the same month, as there was official business to attend to, the Senior Councilor (chūrō) Shimada Seizaemon (Shigekuni) was dispatched to the home domain.
　　Note: Seizaemon arrived at the home domain on the eleventh day of the Second Month.

Item: On the twenty-ninth day of the same month, a man named Sahei of Kida Nishi-Yashiki-machi had a mother who had reached the age of one hundred. She had previously been granted a stipend (fuchi), but passed away from illness this spring. In recognition of Sahei's exceptional filial piety toward his elderly mother, he was granted three bales of rice.

Item: On the eleventh day of the Second Month, at Reigan-ji Temple in Fukagawa, a memorial service (hōji) was held for the seventieth anniversary of the death of Lady Jōjō-in, from the evening of the tenth through the morning of that day. A similar service was also held at Un'shō-ji Temple.
　　Note: Regarding the memorial services for Lady Jōjō-in, these had previously been conducted with a simplified format (sakuryaku), distinct from the full memorial services held for successive lords. However, on this occasion, by special decree, it was ordered that the services be conducted in the same manner as those for successive lords.

Item: On the fourteenth day of the same month, a circular notice (mawashijō) arrived from the Grand Inspector (ō-metsuke) Lord Inaba Dewa-no-kami (Masauji), stating that, as the population registers (ninbetsu-aratame) for all provinces were to be submitted this year of the Rat, the procedures for recording religious affiliation checks (hōji-aratame) and documentation methods were to follow previous practice, and that the population register books (ninbetsu-chō) should be submitted by around the Eighth or Ninth Month of this year.
