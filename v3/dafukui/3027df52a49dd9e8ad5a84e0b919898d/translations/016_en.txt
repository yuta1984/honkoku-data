[Right folio]

　　As an escort for [Yoshiaki's] journey, Maenami Fujiemon Kagesada attended with five hundred cavalry, and [the party] arrived at the port of Tsuruga. From there, Asakura Nakatsukasa-no-taifu Kagetsune joined Maenami with over three hundred cavalry, and they proceeded to Odani-no-shō in northern Ōmi Province. Azai Bitchū-no-kami Nagamasa came out to receive [Yoshiaki] and had him installed in Odani Castle. From Gifu, Fuwa Kawachi-no-kami and Murai Minbu-no-shō were dispatched as guides, and the two envoys of the Asakura house returned from Odani. Azai [Nagamasa] also escorted [Yoshiaki] as far as the lodgings at Sekigahara. On the twenty-fifth of the same month, Yoshiaki entered the lodgings at Gifu.

[Marginal note in vermillion, upper margin:]
Nobunaga presses Jōtei.

　　Three envoys were sent from Yoshiaki to Jōtei at Mitsukuri: Niki Iga-no-kami Yoshimasa, Iikawa Yamashiro-no-kami Nobukane, and Isshiki Shikibu-no-shō Fujinaga. Together with Nobunaga's mounted guards, totaling over fifteen hundred men, they accompanied the three envoys and departed Gifu on the eighth day of the eighth month, arriving at Sawayama in Ōmi Province on the tenth. The chief envoy, the head priest of Zuiryūji Temple, and Takei Sekian were sent to Kannonji Castle.

[Left folio]

　　Jōtei refused to comply. Nobunaga, greatly enraged, was about to begin his return journey [on the eighteenth], when a man named Nomura Etchū-no-kami Takakatsu, a retainer of the Sasaki clan, who bore a grudge against Mitsukuri-nyūdō [Jōtei], deserted and came over to Nobunaga's side. Furthermore, Gamō Chūzaburō Ujisato, residing at Hino in Ōmi Province, secretly sent a messenger [to Nobunaga].

[Marginal note in vermillion, upper margin:]
Castles of Ōmi Province

　　The castles held by Nyūdō Bakkansai Jōtei's faction were: first, Kannonji Castle, the main stronghold and residence of the supreme commander Sasaki Shuri-no-taifu Yoshihide; and in addition, Mitsukuri, Wadayama, Hachimanyama, Tsuda, Nagahara, Yamamoto, Odani, Yokoyama, Kashiwabara, Namazue, Kanisaka, Kōka, Ogoto, Katata, Wani, Tanaka, and Shinjo—eighteen places in total. (Among others including Aoji Iyo-no-kami Hidetsugu, there was also Nomura Higo-no-kami.)
