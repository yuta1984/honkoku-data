　　(Isono Tanba-no-kami Hidemasa and Sakai Ukon) Shigeyasu fought, and Isono, gaining the upper hand, routed Sakai's forces. Ikeda, Kinoshita, and Shibata also appeared to be in peril, when on the western flank, the three thousand troops from Tōtōmi [under Ieyasu] began to engage the seven thousand Asakura forces. At first the Echizen forces appeared to have the advantage, but later the Tōtōmi forces gained the upper hand, and over two hundred men—including Maenami, Kobayashi, Uozumi, Kurosaka, Tomita, and Magara—were killed. As a result, on the Azai side too, Imamura, Kano, Yuge, Endō, and others were slain, and [the Azai forces] withdrew to Mount Ōyori and returned to Odani. Nobunaga fell back as far as Kozekino [near his encampment], but because Lord Tokugawa had established his main camp on the heights of the Ane River, the Mino forces recovered their composure. Thereafter, Yokoyama Castle was stormed and taken; Yokoyama and Ōnogi castles surrendered; and Kinoshita Tōkichirō was installed as castellan of Yokoyama, while Niwa Gorōzaemon was placed as castellan of Sawayama. Nobunaga then returned to Mino Province, and Lord Tokugawa also returned to Tōtōmi.

Battle of Moriguchi

　　[Noda, Fukushima village] On the fourteenth day of the ninth month, [the Ikkō forces] sallied forth from the Osaka temple precincts intending to carry out a harvest raid on the surrounding area. Shimo Azechi (Azetsuki) Hō-

[Left folio]

　　— Bridge Yorijū was made commander, and they advanced toward the area of Moriguchi. The Mino forces—Hayashi Shinzaburō, Mōri Kawachi-no-kami, Sassa Kuranosuke, Fukutomi Heizaemon, and Nomura Etchū-no-kami, among others—rode out to meet them, crossed the river, and attacked from the flank. Shimo rallied his troops, opened fire with arquebuses, and over two hundred of the Gifu forces were killed. Nomura Etchū Takakatsu and Shimo Yoshirō crossed spears, and Takakatsu was slain.

[Marginal note in vermillion, upper margin:]
Yoshikage Advances to Shiga

　　Because the Osaka Monzeki—the Most Reverend Kennyo—requested a rear attack on the Osaka front, Yoshikage, declaring that the bonds of kinship could not be ignored, led a great army along the western Ōmi road, advanced into Shiga District, and resolved to storm the positions of Mori Sanzaemon, who was garrisoned at Usayama, and Aochi Suruga-no-kami, who held a castle at Katata—and in particular, since Oda Kurō [Nobunaga's younger brother] was the overall commander, [Yoshikage planned] to destroy them all, advance on Osaka, and, attacking from both sides simultaneously, achieve his long-cherished ambition.
