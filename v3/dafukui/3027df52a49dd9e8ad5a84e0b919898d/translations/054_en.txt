...for his distinguished service, Nobunaga was so greatly impressed that he had him change his name and style, granting him the title of Katsurада Harima-no-kami Nagatoshi, appointing him provisional military governor (*karino shugo-dai*) of the entire province of Echizen, and stationing him at Ichijōdani.

Thereafter, Lord Nobunaga declared, "I will not linger in such a fierce and turbulent country," and on the twenty-fourth day of the eighth month of the same year (Tenshō 1 [1573]), he departed from Fuchū in Echizen, passed through Itadori, Nakakawachi, and Tsubakizaka, emerged at Yanagase, visited the Jizō-dō hall at Kinomoto, and established his camp at Amenomori, near Odani. *(Interlinear note: The fall of the Asai clan is omitted.)*

In the latter part of the second month of Tenshō 2 (1574), Maeba Sōkichirō was sent from Ichijōdani in Echizen to Gifu to report to Nobunaga that an uprising (*ikki*) had occurred in defiance of Nobunaga's directives. The local people had been speaking ill of Nobunaga. The man who had taken the name Katsurada Harima-no-kami was Maeba Kurōbei Yoshitsugu, the younger brother of Maeba Tōemon Kagesada, a hereditary retainer of the Asakura clan. Tonda Magokurorō Nagahide had entered Nobunaga's service at the same time as Yoshitsugu two years prior, but whereas Maeba had been promoted and rewarded, Tonda had received no reward whatsoever. Harboring resentment over this, Magokurō conspired with former colleagues Uozumi Bingo-no-kami and Kobayashi Saburōjirō, and on the twentieth day of the previous month attacked Katsurada (Yoshitsugu)'s castle at Ichijōdani. As a result, Maeba Genba and others including

**[Left folio]**

Kojima and Uebayashi fell in battle, and Katsurada (Nagatoshi) together with his eldest son Shinshichirō both committed suicide. Tonda also killed Uozumi and, from the castle at the provincial capital (*fu*), schemed to seize control of Echizen. The followers of the Honganji temple, rallying their affiliated parishes, sought to avenge the death of Yoshikage. Furthermore, Asakura Magosaburō Kagekane had fortified a stronghold on Mount Chōsenji and was holding it, but Tonda Magokurō Nagahide, upon hearing of this, advanced on Chōsenji on the eighteenth of the previous month and engaged Kagekane in battle; however, Nagahide was struck by a stray arrow and killed — such was the report presented to Nobunaga.

Upon hearing this, Nobunaga asked, "And what has become of Shikibu-no-taifu Kagekiyo?" Sōkichirō responded: "As Kagekiyo is shunned by all, he had taken his wife and children, enlisted the warrior-monks of Heisenji, and had been sheltering in Bodairin. However, plans are afoot to destroy him: Terao no Oya-zaemon, Amagata Uma-no-jō, and Mizumaki Shinzaburō have been appointed as leaders of the uprising (*ikki*), and they have constructed a fortification at Katsuyama in Muraoka and are pressing their attack. Kagekiyo and Heisenji alike should fall within the near future." Nobunaga deemed that this was likely to be so.
