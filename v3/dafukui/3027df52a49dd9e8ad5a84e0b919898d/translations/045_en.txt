Aochi (Suruga-no-kami Yasusuke) had been a retainer of the Sasaki clan, serving them as an administrator, but had transferred his allegiance to Nobunaga and taken up residence in the new castle at Katata; perhaps feeling shame at his former loyalties, he fell in battle before the formation of Asai Bizen-no-kami (Nagamasa). The Echizen forces, emboldened, pressed forward and surged toward the castle of Usayama. Mori Yoshinari's retainers—Mutō Gorōuemon, Hida Genba, and Hida Hikouemon—defended with a mere two hundred or so horsemen against the vastly superior force, but because their commander had been slain, they were ultimately overwhelmed; some died in battle, some fled in defeat, and so that day came to an end.

Asakura Yoshikage relocated his encampment to Kami-Sakamoto and won over the support of Sanmon (Enryaku-ji Temple). Asakura Shikibu-no-taifu Kagekage ascended the mountain with over a thousand horsemen, mustered more than five thousand of the temple's warrior-monks, lit beacon fires on Ōdake peak, and established encampments at Hayao no Ue, Hachigamine, Aoyama, Tsubokasayama, and Hachiman. Allying with the Ikkō sect adherents in the areas of Kusatsu, Noji, and Tanaka in Ōmi Province, as well as the remnants of the Sasaki clan, they demolished the bridge at Seta and blocked Nobunaga's return to his home territory. The Asakura forces deliberated and resolved to launch an assault on Sesshū (Settsu Province), with Wani Echigo-no-kami, Shinjo Ise-no-kami, and Tago Sakon-no-hyōe serving as guides.

**[Left folio]**

They advanced as far as the areas of Ōtsu, Hinookayama, Yamashina, and Daigo. When it was reported that Oda Nobunaga would enter the capital from Sesshū on the twenty-third day of the same month, the Asakura vanguard turned back from Daigo, and detachments were stationed at Totsu, Hieitsuji, Sakamoto, Kinukawa, Ōgi, and various other locations throughout western Ōmi. The warrior-monks of Sanmon fortified the Akazanguchi and Nyoigoe passes and awaited orders from Yoshikage at Higashi-Sakamoto. Nobunaga established his main camp at Miidera Temple. His advance forces extended their positions as far as Ōtsu, Matsumoto, Shiga, and Karasaki. Yoshiaki (the Shogun) established his camp at Shōgunzuka, while Hayashi Sado-no-kami deployed with more than three thousand horsemen at Kitashirakawa. Enemies and allies alike sent out foot soldiers, and the months passed in a succession of skirmishes.
