Beginning with this, Asakura Gondō, Kitanoshō Tosa-no-kami, Takumi Echigo-no-kami, Kawai Aki-no-kami, Asakura Izumo-no-kami, Asakura Kamon-no-suke, Aoki Hayato, and others turned back and fought at various points, dying in battle.

Yoshikage had considered taking refuge in Kanasaki Castle, held by Asakura Gondō Michikage, but as Michikage had also been killed, and since the castle at Hikidan held by Tsuga Saburōuma — as well as the fortifications at Yamanaka and Tateishi — had all been abandoned, it was decided that the only remaining option was to take shelter in the castles of Konome and Hachibuse, held by Inemaki Tamba-no-kami. Yoshikage therefore withdrew as far as Konome Pass. However, after the rout at Toneyama, the men had all fled and dispersed, and on top of this, word came that Nobunaga had arrived and encamped at Tsuruga. Yoshikage accordingly abandoned Konome, passed through Imagō, and arrived at Fuchū with a force that had dwindled to a mere five hundred men, whereupon he hastily returned to his mansion at Ichijōdani.

were said to be deeply loyal. Asakura Shikibu-no-taifu Kagekiyo offered: "Please allow me to move you to my castle at Iyama in Ōno." It was ultimately settled that Yoshikage would move to Ōno. Under Kagekiyo's management, the mansion at Ichijōdani was burned by its own inhabitants, and on the night of the seventeenth, Yoshikage arrived at Iyama Castle in Ōno. By this time, the number of those accompanying Yoshikage — high and low alike — had dwindled to just over fifty.

Lord Nobunaga ordered Maeba Kurōbei to make secret contact with Kagekiyo through Reisenji Temple at Shinono. Kagekiyo had a change of heart, and declared that he would kill Yoshikage and surrender. Kagekiyo's retainers, Hirano Genzaemon and Chūdōji Han'emon, both remonstrated with him, but their counsel was not heeded. Luring Yoshikage out from Iyama to Rokubō Kenshōji, on the twentieth day at the Hour of the Sheep (around 2 p.m.), Kagekiyo surrounded Rokubō. Yoshikage took his own life. Katō Shinzaburō served as his second and then killed himself as well. Torii Hyōgo Kagenori and Takahashi Shinsuke Kagemasu had fought to hold back the enemy, but upon hearing that their lord Yoshikage had taken his life, they too cut open their bellies to repay their lord's kindness. Yoshikage was forty-one years of age at the time of his death. Kagekiyo took Yoshikage's head and presented it to Nobunaga.
