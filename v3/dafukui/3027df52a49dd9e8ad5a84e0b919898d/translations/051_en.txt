Abe Awaji-no-kami and Asami Tsushima-no-kami came over to Nobunaga's side and offered their loyal service. Nobunaga was greatly pleased. [Having utterly destroyed the enemies of western Ōmi,] he advanced from there toward Odani, and boarding more than ten newly constructed large atakebune (warships), he had them rowed to the shores of Shirahige Myōjin Shrine. Kido Castle's lord Tago Sakon'bei Ujihisa, Shinshō Ise-no-kami Sadamitsu, Tanaka Castle's lord Takashima Etchū-no-kami Takayasu, and Isono Tanba-no-kami Hidemasa, upon seeing the great army, apparently decided they could not prevail — and so, their lives having been spared, they opened their castles, surrendered, and fled toward the western mountains. Western Ōmi was entirely pacified; thereafter, both Kido and Tanaka castles in Takashima District were granted to Akechi Hyūga-no-kami (Mitsuhide). Added to his previous fief in Shiga District, he received a total of one hundred thousand koku. His landholdings in Anbatsu District of Mino Province were surrendered at this time.

By Nobunaga's gracious consideration, the senior retainer of Yodo — Suwa Hida-no-kami's lieutenant Ōi-no-suke — and Yamamoto Tsushima-no-kami, who was at Shizuhara, were placed in Akechi's service, and these three men proceeded to Sakamoto Castle.

Asakura Yoshikage, hearing that his vassals in western Ōmi had been destroyed by Nobunaga's forces, sought to mobilize his troops to mount a rescue. However, the men of the domain, having in recent years repeatedly made long, arduous expeditions only to return without result,

**[Left folio]**

had seemingly grown weary of it: Asakura Shikibu-no-tayū of Ōno, Asakura Magosaburō of Oi, Mizoe Ōi-no-suke of Kanazu, Asakura Genbannojō of Fukasaka, Sakurai Shinzaemon of Mikuni, Asakura Hyōgo-no-suke of Oda, and others — including Kobayashi Saburōjirō and Uozumi Bingo-no-kami and numerous other commanders, each commanding one or two hundred horsemen, numbering several dozen captains in all — feigned illness and failed to respond to the call to arms.

Yoshikage was powerless to compel them. Yamazaki Nagato-no-kami Yoshiie, Takumi Echigo-no-kami Yukitada, and Kawai Aki-no-kami Munekiyo, with some three thousand cavalry, advanced ahead and arrived at Hikiita Castle on the third day of the eighth month. Yoshikage himself, placing at the vanguard the Tosa-no-kami of Kitanoshō, Asakura Kamon-no-suke, Shōrenga Ōmi-no-kami, Tōgō Shimōsa-no-kami, Toba Uma-no-suke, and Asakura Izumo-no-kami, led over five thousand cavalry and arrived at Tsuruga on the fourth day of the same month. Upon hearing that the castles of Kido, Tanaka, Ōmizo, and Shinshō had already fallen, they concluded: "It is because the rear force arrived too late. There is nothing to be gained by pressing forward now. In that case, let us instead go to the support of the Azai father and son in northern Ōmi." Setting out from Hikiita, they crossed Tone Mountain, arrived at the post town of Yanase, and advanced their vanguard as far as the vicinity of Tagami (Tagami Mountain).
