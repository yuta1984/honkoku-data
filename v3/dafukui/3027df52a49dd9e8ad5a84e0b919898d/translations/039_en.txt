**[Right folio]**

Nobunaga ordered fire to be set to the Kehi Shrine, which stood close to the enemy castle, burning it to the ground, and then sent in fresh troops to relieve the attackers. At last, Terada Uneme could hold out no longer, and the castle garrison of over four hundred men fought to the last without retreating a single step, all dying in battle. At that moment, a spy who had been stationed in Ōmi Province came galloping to Tsuruga with a report: "In connection with your advance on Tsuruga, remnants of the Sasaki clan, Asai Shimotsuke-no-kami of Odani, Ahe Awaji-no-kami of Yamamoto, and Isono Tamba-no-kami of Nagahama have allied with Echizen, and a great force is heading this way—by land, crossing Yanase and Tonē, and by water, crossing the lake from Nagahama and Maibara." Since reports indicated that to the front Asakura held firmly to strongly fortified positions with a large army, and to the rear the warriors of North Ōmi were rising in revolt, it was judged: "Even if we take one of Tezutsuyama's outworks, it counts for little, and if our rear is encircled, we shall be in grave difficulty." Accordingly, with Kutsuki Kunai-no-tayū as guide, and Shindō Yamashiro-no-kami, Shibata Shuri-no-suke, and Sakuma Emon-no-jō leading the vanguard, Nobunaga departed Tsuruga, forced his way through the stronghold at Hikida, passed through the seven-and-a-half-ri mountain interior and through Kaizu, and from the post town of Imazu turned to the right—

**[Left folio]**

—and so came at last to Kutsuki, barely managing to return to his camp. Lord Tokugawa had been accompanying him, but was abandoned by Nobunaga and, left with no other recourse, with Akechi Jūbē and Niwa Gorōzaemon in the lead, he turned off from Kaneyama post town onto the Wakasa road, entered Saburidani, and reached the residence of Mutō Kōzuke-no-suke at Ishiyama. However, since Mutō and the surrounding area were in turmoil, he took Kōzuke-no-suke's mother as a hostage and, crossing Nagasaka, returned to Kyoto. Kinoshita Tōkichirō, Iga(-no-kami), and Hayashi Sado-no-kami, who were serving as the rear guard, were intercepted by the Asakura forces; over seven hundred men were killed, and they ultimately managed to return to Kyoto. Mitsukuri Nyūdō Shōtei, who was living in confinement in Kōka in Ōmi Province, conspired with the warriors of North Ōmi and was devising a plot to retake Ōmi Province. In the meantime, men from Yasu District, without even waiting for a signal to engage, swarmed against Inaba Nyūdō Ittetsu while he was at the post town of Moriyama and set fire to the town entrance. Ittetsu and his son divided their force of over eight hundred men into three units, launched a counterattack, routed the enemy thoroughly, killed over one hundred and fifty in pursuit, and sent their heads to Kyoto.
