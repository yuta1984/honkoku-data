A letter of commendation was bestowed upon Inaba, and in order to secure the defense of the surrounding area, Sakuma Emon-no-jō Nobumori was stationed at Nagahara, Shibata Shuri-no-suke Katsuie at Chōkōji, and Mori Sanzaemon, Aochi Suruga-no-kami, Yamaoka Mimasaka-no-kami, and others were each ordered to guard their strongholds firmly. On the nineteenth day of the fifth month, Nobunaga set out from the capital heading down to Mino Province, but upon hearing that a party from Ōmi had blocked the road, he returned to Gifu by way of the Chigusa mountain crossing.

[Sixth month, fourth day] Sasaki Shōtei and his son were defeated by Shibata and Sakuma and, unable to sustain themselves in Ōmi, fled toward Kawachi and Settsu, where they allied with the Miyoshi. Nobunaga, expressing his satisfaction, declared: "During the recent advance on Tsuruga, the Azai father and son of North Ōmi allied with the Asakura of Echizen and furthermore communicated with the people of Iga and Kōka—this cannot be left unaddressed. Since Azai Bizen-no-kami Nagamasa is a kinsman of Nobunaga, he ought properly to be on our side, yet his conduct has been utterly outrageous. We shall first subdue North Ōmi." Accordingly, on the nineteenth day of the sixth month, leading tens of thousands of horsemen, he advanced toward Azai District, contained Yokoyama Castle, which was being defended by Yokoyama Sado-no-kami Takanaga,

**[Left folio]**

and on the twenty-first day of the same month, launched an assault on Odani. Odani Castle was the most renowned stronghold in all of Ōmi Province, and moreover its lord, the son of Azai Shimotsuke-no-kami Sukemasa, Bizen-no-kami Nagamasa, was a commander who combined both bravery and strategic acumen. Contrary to expectations, Nobunaga could not advance even to the castle's vicinity. Nobunaga, observing the situation, declared: "Let us first withdraw from this front and attack Yokoyama Castle," and ordered Yanada Saemon-no-tarō, Sassa Kuranosuke, and Chūjō Shōgen to command the rearguard. As they conducted a staged withdrawal, the castle troops sallied forth and gave pursuit, and so Yanada, Sassa, and Chūjō exerted every effort and barely managed to fall back to Suijō. Nobunaga then turned to attack Yokoyama, pressing the assault day and night, but within the castle the commander and his officers—including Ōnogi Tosa-no-kami, Mitamura Saemon-no-jō, Nomura Higo-no-kami, and Nomura Hyōgo-no-suke—were holding out with a force of thirty-five hundred men, making the castle extremely difficult to take. When Azai heard of this, he wished to relieve Yokoyama, but reasoning that "Nobunaga has a large force while we are few, so we must rely on the Asakura," he requested reinforcements.
