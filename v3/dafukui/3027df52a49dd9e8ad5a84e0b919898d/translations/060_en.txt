On the ninth day of the first month of Tenshō 7 (1579), in a small study called the "Shūbu no Ma" at Azuchi, Hyūga no Kami Mitsuhide summoned Nagaoka Hyōbu no Tayū Fujitaka. Mitsuhide's third daughter, then sixteen years of age, was given in marriage to Fujitaka's eldest son, Yoichirō Tadaoki. The fourth daughter was given in marriage to Oda Shichibei Nobuzumi, the nephew of Nobunaga. Mitsuhide had seven children in all. The eldest daughter, in the winter of Eiroku 12 (1569), married Akechi Samanosuke Mitsuharu at age sixteen — now twenty-six years old. The second daughter, in the summer of Genki 1 (1570), was wed to Akechi Jiuemon Mitsutada at age fifteen — now twenty-four. The fifth child was a son, Koretō Jūbei Yoshimitsu (Mitsuyoshi), aged eleven, stationed at Kameyama Castle in Tamba. The sixth son, Jūjirō, became the adopted son of Tsutsui Junkei. The seventh son, Otojumaru, being only five years old, remained at Sakamoto Castle. All were of the same mother — the daughter of Mogi Kageyuzaemon Norihiro, a retainer of the Toki clan of Mino Province, who was apparently

Around that time, Shibata Genazemon Katsusada, a resident retainer of Ago in Echizen, following the dismissal and disgrace of his lord Katsuie, came to Sakamoto in Ōmi Province with his wife and children and an escort of over a hundred horsemen. He called upon Akechi Chikanosai and his son Jūrozaemon, seeking to become a retainer of Hyūga no Kami Mitsuhide. This Genazemon had taken as his wife the sister of Akechi Samanosuke, and therefore stood in a relationship of "fellow sons-in-law" (ai-muko) with Jūrozaemon. Since Chikanosai, the father of Mitsuchika, was an uncle of Mitsuhide, he acted as an intermediary on Katsusada's behalf.

Mitsuhide met with Genazemon and said: "In recent years, the power and influence of Lord Shibata has flourished and the northern provinces have been largely pacified. With the aim of shortening the route to Gifu and Azuchi, I am told that during the Year of the Tiger just past (1578), a new road was built from Itadori through Nakakaichi and Tsubakizaka in this province (Echizen) to run directly to the post town of Yanagase; that the castle complex at Kitanoshō was expanded; that a great bridge was rebuilt over the Asuwa River; and that, for the first time, a pontoon bridge was constructed over the Kuzuryū River to facilitate travel to and from the lower provinces — all for the benefit and welfare of the people.
