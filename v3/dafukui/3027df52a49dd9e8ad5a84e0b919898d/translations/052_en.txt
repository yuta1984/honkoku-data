Having subdued western Ōmi, Nobunaga entered Nagahama Castle on the eighth day of the eighth month, intending to advance into northern Ōmi. Shortly thereafter, Abe Awaji-no-kami, lord of Yamamoto Castle, who had secretly communicated his intention to defect, came to Nagahama on that same day and paid his respects. Nobunaga questioned him in detail about the enemy's dispositions and movements. He then stationed Oda Saburōgorō, Saitō Shingo, and Hayashi Sado-no-kami in three locations — Shizugatake, Kaizan, and Naka Castle — to contain the Odani front. Nobunaga himself advanced and encamped facing Tagami Mountain, where Yoshikage had established his headquarters.

Nobunaga ordered Akechi Hyūga-no-kami (Mitsuhide) to pacify [western Ōmi,] Takashima District, and directed that Akechi Samanosuke be sent as a guide into Echizen Province, with additional troops assigned to him. Accordingly, with Akechi Samanosuke Mitsuharu at the vanguard, he led over three hundred horsemen — including Miyake Shikibu, Oki Gorōbei, Okuda Saemon, and Imamine Tanomo — and took up position in front of and behind Nobunaga's main force.

Meanwhile, Asami Tsushima-no-kami, a resident warrior of Yakio to the east of Ōdzuku on Mount Odani, had already secretly pledged allegiance to Nobunaga, and on the night of the tenth of the same month, he led Nobunaga's forces into the stronghold and joined Nobunaga's side. As the troops from Mino Province were about to attack the outlying fortifications, the garrison soldiers at the branch castles each abandoned their posts and gathered at Tagami Mountain, the forward encampment of the Asakura vanguard. Azai Bizen-no-kami Nagamasa, for his part, continued to firmly defend Odani Castle itself.

The Asakura forces had taken up positions at [the post town of] Yanagase, and were deliberating over whether they should attempt to rescue the Azai, when Abe Awaji-no-kami's betrayal cut off the road between Yanagase and Odani. Unable to determine a course of action, opinions were divided. The lord of Kitanoshō Castle, Asakura Tosa-no-kami Kageyuki (a senior retainer), and Yamazaki Nagato-no-kami Yoshiie, lord of Fuchū Castle, declared: "Let us first withdraw to Tsuruga and assess the enemy's movements." Asakura Izumo-no-kami of Awata District, Kawai Aki-no-kami of Somayama, and Asakura Kamon-no-suke of Ikeda, along with the others, all agreed to this proposal. In a mad scramble to be the first to withdraw to the port of Tsuruga, they rushed pell-mell over Tone Mountain. Nobunaga, watching the situation closely, gave chase with over a thousand horsemen of his main force.
