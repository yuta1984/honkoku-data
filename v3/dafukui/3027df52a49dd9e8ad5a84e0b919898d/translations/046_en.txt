Upon hearing this, Nobunaga appointed Sakai Ukon as commander, attached to him Andō Uemon, Mutō Mimasaka-no-kami, and Kuwahara Heibei among others, and on the night of the twenty-fifth day of the eleventh month, they embarked on more than ten vessels with over a thousand horsemen and entered Katata castle by a covert infiltration route. The Echizen forces had been paying local fishermen at a place called Nishi no Kiri in Katata with gold and silver to monitor activity on the lake, and when these fishermen reported the arrival of these reinforcements, Yoshikage, upon hearing this, declared they must attack immediately. He sent the Lord of Kitanoshō, Tosa-no-kami, as commander, along with Asakura Genba, Shōrenka Ōmi-no-kami, Sakurai Shinzaemon, Nojiri Jibu-no-zaemon, and others totaling over three thousand horsemen, who pressed their attack. From the waterborne force, Abe Awaji-no-kami attacked with more than seven hundred men. The reinforcements inside the castle had entered only the previous night and had not yet established their defensive positions, leaving the castle in disarray. As the attacking forces stormed in from all four sides, Ikai Jinsuke, Baba Magojirō, Isobe Matajirō, Urano Genpachi, and others sallied forth and fought with incomparable valor, but fell in battle at various points. Sakai Ukon Shigeyasu also engaged the enemy with Andō and Kuwahara at his side, refused to retreat a single step, and died in battle. Katata castle was thus taken by storm.

**[Left folio]**

With the fall of Katata castle, Sakai Ukon and over four hundred men were slain, throwing the Gifu forces into alarm. At this time, Niwa Gorōzaemon Nagahide and Kinoshita Tōkichirō Hideyoshi, who were stationed at the two castles of Sawayama and Yokoyama, were startled upon hearing the news. They left garrison troops in their respective castles and set out immediately to present themselves at Nobunaga's camp at Miidera—but Ikkō sect adherents had fortified a stronghold in Tatebe village and, in concert with the irregular fighters of Kusatsu and Tanaka, blocked the road. Niwa and Kinoshita charged at them on their way up, drove them off in rout, boarded boats at Yamada Ura, and arrived at Ōtsu. Thereupon the two generals were posted at Zeze and Ishiyama, and Niwa Nagahide was ordered to construct a pontoon bridge at Seta.

As the days passed, the ongoing turmoil caused provisions in Kyoto to run out, bringing hunger and thirst to people of all stations, and both sides were reduced to dire straits. When the beginning of the twelfth month arrived, Nobunaga summoned Akechi Jūbei Mitsuhide and discussed with him the matter of concluding peace with the Asakura—this being because Mitsuhide had formerly resided in Echizen.
