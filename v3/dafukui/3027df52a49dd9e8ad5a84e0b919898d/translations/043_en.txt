Shogun Yoshiaki-kō also moved his headquarters and established his camp at Nakajima. Nobunaga placed his main camp at Tenma-no-Mori. It came to Nobunaga's ears that the Venerable Kennyo Shōnin of the Osaka Honganji, being in constant communication with the Hosokawa and Miyoshi factions, had been secretly loading provisions onto ships and sending them to Noda and Fukushima. Nobunaga thereupon deliberated on cutting off the Ikkō sect entirely. Upon hearing this, the Monzeki Kennyo Shōnin Kōsa assembled his eldest son, the Venerable Kyōnyo Shōnin Kōju; his second son, Kōshōji Sachō; his third son, the Venerable Junnyo Shōnin Kōshō; the monks of the in-ke affiliated families; the monastic officers (bōkan) of the Shimo clan and others; and the temple's warrior-retainers—Awazu, Toshima, Matsui, Shichiri, Taga, Koizumi, Nakamura, Nishikawa, Yokota, Uehara, and others—and held a council. They fortified the temple precincts at Osaka, constructed outworks (toride) at two locations, Kawaguchi and Rōnogishi, installed the Junkōji and Jōrakuji temples as command positions with their congregations as garrison, and joined forces with Noda and Fukushima. The lord of Echizen, Asakura Saemon-no-kami Yoshikage, was petitioned to send a relief force on account of

ties of kinship. During this period, skirmishes occurred day and night. Nevertheless, the Osaka main citadel was so advantageously situated that there appeared to be no immediate prospect of storming it, and the days passed without resolution.

On the fourteenth day of the ninth month, with the intent of conducting a forage raid on the surrounding area from within the Osaka temple complex, Shimo Azechi Hōkyō Raijū led more than five thousand men out toward the vicinity of Moriguchi. The Mino forces, looking down on them, sent a large contingent—Hayashi Shinzaburō, Mōri Kawachi-no-kami, Sassa Kuranoske, Fukutomi Heizaemon, Nomura Etchū-no-kami, and others—charging at them and striking from the flank. However, the Shimo forces rallied, aligned their firearms, and opened fire, laying low more than two hundred men, and the Mino forces were unable to advance. Nomura Etchū(-no-kami) Takakatsu and Shimo Yoshirō crossed spears, and Takakatsu was killed. Sassa Kuranoske was also gravely wounded and withdrew. As the Mino forces found themselves in dire straits, Maeda Matasaemon, Kanematsu Matashirō, Yuasa Jinsuke, and Nakano Matabe'e fought the rearguard action and managed, with great difficulty, to withdraw.
