More than several dozen trees, each with roots rising seventeen to eighteen *shaku* from the ground and measuring four to five *shaku* in girth, spread out in all directions. Nearby there is a small shrine, said to venerate the Great Shrine of Izumo.

On the second day of the intercalary seventh month of Ryakuō 1 (1338), Nitta Yoshisada was destroyed at Fujishima village in Echizen Province, and Shogun Ashikaga Takauji came to govern the realm. His son, the Middle Counselor and Middle Captain Yoshiakira, succeeded him. Takauji's reign lasted twenty-two years; he passed away on the twenty-ninth day of the fourth month of Embun 3 (1358), at the age of fifty-four. Yoshiakira died on the seventh day of the twelfth month of Jōji 6 (1367), at the age of thirty-eight.

The ancestor of Oda Kazusa-no-suke Nobunaga, lord of Owari Province, was Oda Tachi-no-Saemon Tsunekatsu. He was the adopted son of Tsuneyoshi, the head priest of the Oda Shrine in Echizen Province. During the Kenmu era, he accompanied Ashikaga Owari-no-kami Takayori and later joined the ranks of the military commanders. When Takayori's son, Buei Jibū-no-tayū Yoshimasa, received Owari Province in addition to Echizen during the Eiwa era (1375–1379), four trusted senior retainers — Kai Mino-no-kami, Asakura Danjō

**[Left folio]**

Saemon Chifuku, Nakatsukasa-no-tayū, and Ninomiya Sakon-shōgen — were stationed at various posts as guards of Echizen. The brothers Tachi-no-Saemon Nobunobu and Jirō-Saemon Nobuhiro, sons of Oda Tsunekatsu, were appointed as administrators (*shikken*) of the eight districts of Owari. Nobunobu's son, Ise-no-kami Tsunenobu, resided at Iwakura in Owari, while Nobuhiro's son, Yamato-no-kami Tsunetō, resided in the second enclosure (*ni-no-maru*) of Kiyosu Castle, and both revered their lord Buei. After several generations, the Buei house had no natural heir, and Ōno Sahyōe-no-suke Yoshitoshi and Shibukawa Jibū-no-tayū Yoshikado competed over the headship of the clan. From this point the family fell into disorder, and the retainers of both provinces acted as they pleased, causing strife to arise.

Around this time, Ise-no-kami Nobunobu, six generations descended from Oda Ise-no-kami Tsunenobu, rose from Iwakura and swept through the province. Meanwhile, Yamato-no-kami Munenobu, five generations descended from Yamato-no-kami Tsunetō, schemed from Kiyosu to bring the whole province under his control. Munenobu's younger brother, Danjō-no-jō Nobusada, who had taken the tonsure under the name Getsuan, lived at Shōbata and deferred to his elder brother's authority. Getsuan's son was Bingo-no-kami Nobuhide, and Nobuhide's son was Lord Nobunaga.
