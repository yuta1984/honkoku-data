What Mitsuhide proposed was reasonable, but given that the matter had already been reported to the shogun, further delay would be problematic—and so it was resolved to advance through Wakasa without warning. For the security of the various directions, the following deployments were made: Mori Sanzaemon-no-jō at Usamiyama in Shiga District of Ōmi; Aochi Suruga-no-kami at Katata; Yamaoka Mimasaka-no-kami at Seta; and Inaba Iyo-nyūdō Ittetu together with his son Ukyō-no-shin Sadamichi at Moriyama in Yasu District. On the twentieth day of the fourth month, [the army] departed Kyoto via Yase, crossed over through Kutsuki, and encamped at Kumagawa in Wakasa. At this location, a man named Matsumiya Genmban defected from the Takeda and surrendered to the Oda, and so Sakai Ukon-shōgen Shigeyasu was left at Kumagawa with fifteen hundred horsemen added to him. Takayoshi and Gotō Kisaburō Yorimoto deployed with over one thousand horsemen to the west of Imazu in Ōmi Province, suppressing the enemy fortresses. Nobunaga changed his route from Kumagawa in Wakasa, bypassing Obama Castle, and took the Awagara Pass, proceeding to Shinshiro, where he was accompanied by Kumagai Naizen. From there, with Awaya Etchū-no-kami of Sakaki serving as a guide, he advanced and deployed at Tsuruga. When word of this reached Ichijōgadani, Yoshikage was greatly enraged and declared, "Not a single one shall be allowed to escape—cut them all down." He dispatched several thousand horsemen under Yamazaki Nagato-no-kami, Takumi Echigo-no-kami, Kawai Aki-no-kami, Kobayashi Bitchū-no-kami, Uozumi Bingo-no-kami, and Tomita Shimōsa-no-kami, ordering them to reinforce Asakura Nakatsukasa-no-tayū Kageharu, the commander of Kanegasaki Castle. Yoshikage himself also took to the field, establishing his main camp at Kinome Pass and deploying troops to guard every approach. Nobunaga first ordered an assault on Tezutsuyama, a branch fortress of Kanegasaki, intending to destroy this stronghold. The vanguard attacked climbing from the west.
