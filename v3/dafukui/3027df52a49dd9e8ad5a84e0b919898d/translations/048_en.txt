—(Kawajiri Yohei) Shigeto was stationed (in various positions), and with the appearance of marching on the capital maintained, forces were distributed to all directions. Then, on the morning of the thirteenth day of the ninth month, they swept suddenly from Ōtsu to Higashi-Sakamoto, and attacked Mount Hiei (Enryakuji) from multiple directions, burning down shrines, temple buildings, and monks' quarters. It was said to be in retribution against those who had sided with the Asakura, and so forth.

Wani Echigo-no-kami Ujinaga, a cadet branch of the Sasaki clan, resided at Wani Castle and had in recent years allied with Asai together with the people of Kido and Tanaka, following them to Echizen. Whether intending to attack Mt. Hiei from the rear, he ascended with a force of some three hundred men. He encountered Akechi Mitsuhide and every one of his men was killed. Nobunaga, greatly impressed, granted the district of Shiga to Mitsuhide as a reward. To Kyoto, Murai Minbu-shōyū was sent up with greetings for the shogun, and Nobunaga returned to his home province of Mino.

In the spring of Genki 3 (1572), Nobunaga conducted operations in western Ōmi. Advancing from Ōtsu via Sakamoto, he attacked the two castles of Kido and Tanaka. The castle lord of Kido, Tago Sakon-hyōe Ujihisa, and the castle lord of Tanaka, Takashima Etchū-no-kami Takayasu, offered resistance and the castles did not fall immediately. Leaving Akechi Jūbei (Mitsuhide), Kyōgoku Nagato-no-kami, Nakagawa Hachirozaemon, Yamaoka Mimasaka-no-kami, and Ichihashi Kurōzaemon—

**[Left folio]**

—and others behind, Nobunaga crossed the Hira Pass and entered the capital. Having heard that Nobunaga was attacking the castles of western Ōmi, the castle lord of Nagahama in the Sakata district of northern Ōmi, Isono Tamba-no-kami Hidemasa, stationed his retainers Hikida Jirobei and Ishida Fujikurō at Nagahama with a reinforcement of over two hundred cavalry to hold it in his absence, while he himself boarded ship with over a thousand cavalry and crossed to Takashima district. At that moment, Kinoshita Tōkichirō learned that the garrison left at Nagahama was small in number. He coordinated with Sawayama castle, set out from Yokoyama in the middle of the night, and swept down on Nagahama. The castle deputies Ishida and Hikida were unable to mount an effective defense and fled toward Yamamoto (Yamamotoyama). Kinoshita's forces pursued them and took more than thirty heads. Nobunaga, greatly impressed by Hideyoshi's military achievement, rewarded him with the castle of Nagahama along with an estate of 25,000 kanmon.

In the lower ten days of the seventh month of the same year (Genki 3), Nobunaga led a large force into Asai district in Ōmi Province, established his main camp at Hibari-yama near Odani, pressed against the Asai forces day and night, set fire to several places at the town entrance of Odani, and ordered the fields to be stripped of grain (karita).
