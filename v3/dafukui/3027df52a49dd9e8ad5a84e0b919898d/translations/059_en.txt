【Right Page】

While [the enemy] had barricaded themselves in Oyama Castle in Kahoku and fought against [our forces], this time Sakuma Genba exhausted his stratagems and, on the twenty-second of last month, took Oyama Castle and utterly destroyed the strong enemy. The heads of nineteen commanders were presented, including Tsubosaka Hōki, Tsubosaka Shingorō, Akatsuka Jinuemon, Kaburagi Uemon, Matsunaga Tamba, Kongōji Saburōuemon, Kurose Tōzaemon, and others (and so forth).

Shibata Genazemon Katsusada, a kinsman of Katsuie, was a cousin of Akechi Mitsuhide and the brother-in-law of Akechi Samanosuke [i.e., married to Samanosuke's sister].

After the suppression of the Kashu Ikki (peasant uprising in Kaga Province), all weapons and military equipment were confiscated, melted down, and reforged by blacksmiths into agricultural tools, which were then distributed to the people. The metal was also used to make hardware for castle fortifications, or forged into chains for the pontoon bridge over the Kuzuryū River.

Tsutsui Junkei reached the age of forty with no natural heir. This time, through the intermediary of Nobunaga, he took as his adopted son the second son of Koretō Hyūga no Kami (Mitsuhide), a boy of seven years called Jūjirō.

【Left Page】

Hashiba Chikuzen no Kami Hideyoshi was sent down to Harima Province as guardian of Otsugimaru Hidekatsu, the fifth son of Lord Nobunaga, who was then eight years of age. He entered the province from Nagahama in Ōmi Province. As Banshū (Harima) was largely pacified, he consulted with Kodera Kanbei Takakatsu and Takechi Hanbei Shigechika, a retainer well versed in military strategy, and built a castle at Himeji, where he took up residence.

Koretō Hyūga no Kami Mitsuhide, holding Fukuchiyama in Tamba, in late [year unknown] October, captured Onigadake and other small castles, and stationed Shiouten Tajima no Kami and his son at Fukuchiyama. Passing through Hikami District, he surveyed the castles where Akai Gorō of Takami and Akai Akuuemon of Hōzuki were barricaded, and moved his main camp to Kaneyama. That night, snow fell suddenly, causing great difficulty for everyone. Taking advantage of this, Akai Gorō and Akai Akuuemon led over a thousand elite horsemen on sleds and unexpectedly pressed upon Kaneyama from two ri away.
