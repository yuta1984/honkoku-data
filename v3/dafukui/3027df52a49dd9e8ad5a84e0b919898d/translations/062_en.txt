Ittetsu was furious, but his retainers Nawa Izumi no Kami and Saitō Kurannosuke remonstrated with him, saying: "Since the time of our late lord Toki, the Three Men of Western Mino — Ujike, Inaba, and Andō — have been old friends; that you offer no assistance is altogether too harsh a thing." From this point on, Nawa and Saitō were treated as if they had been dismissed. Finally, in the latter part of the sixth month, they departed from Sone and made their way to Sakamoto, where they placed themselves in Mitsuhide's care. As Mitsuhide had known them since the time he had lived in Akechi in Mino Province, he received them with compassion. Thereafter, through consultations among Hyūga no Kami (Mitsuhide), Nagaoka Hyōbu no Tayū, and Inoko Hyōsuke, a request was made to Ittetsu for a pardon for the two men. Nawa Izumi no Kami was permitted to return to service (under Ittetsu). Saitō Kurannosuke Kazumi, however, was taken into Mitsuhide's own service and kept in his household.

Nobunaga held a grand horse review on the eastern riverbank in Kyoto [text damaged]; Hyūga no Kami Mitsuhide served as the administrator. 【Left Page】

In the spring of the Year of the Horse, Tenshō 10 (1582), Nobunaga set out in force to pursue and destroy Takeda Katsuyori. After Katsuyori's defeat and death, in Kai Province, Nobunaga summoned Koretō (Mitsuhide) and said: "On this occasion, your military contribution amounted to only half a share, yet your horses, men, and equipment were conspicuously more magnificent than the rest, surpassing all the other retainers — this is an utterly unreasonable thing." Whereupon he struck Mitsuhide's cheek with his own fist.

Furthermore, Koretō had been entrusted with hosting Tokugawa Ieyasu upon his visit to Azuchi. Nobunaga, saying that Mitsuhide "had gone too far in his lavishness," ordered his pages to beat him; four or five pages struck Mitsuhide on the head with their fans. Among them, Mori Ranmaru also rose from his seat, repositioned his fan, and struck him forcefully with the iron rivet of the fan, so that blood flowed from the top of his head, and Mitsuhide withdrew. Ranmaru was twenty-two years of age and had not yet undergone his coming-of-age ceremony (genpuku). He served as a herald (sōja-yaku) and had, on the second day of the previous month, become the castellan of Iwamura Castle in Mino Province, holding a domain of fifty thousand koku.

On the twenty-first day of the fifth month, Ieyasu departed from Azuchi to Sakai in Izumi Province. At this time, a notification arrived from Nobunaga, stating that since he would soon be leading a campaign to the western provinces, everyone was to depart their residences on the first or second day of the following month, hasten to Bitchū, and follow Hideyoshi's orders.
