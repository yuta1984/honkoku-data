—(Oda) Kurō (Nobuhari) was appointed commander-in-chief. (The Asakura forces resolved) to overwhelm the garrison at Usayama under Mori Sanzaemon-no-jō (Yoshinari) and at Katata under Aochi Suruga-no-kami, destroy them, and advance on Osaka (or: "to crush them in a pincer and destroy them"). On the seventeenth day of the ninth month, they rested men and horses at Ogoto Castle and determined to attack the enemy fortifications two days hence (the nineteenth). Messengers were dispatched from Usayama and Katata to report [to Nobunaga] that Asakura's force of twenty-eight thousand men had encamped at Ogoto, filling the area as far as Kinukawa, Chino, Ōgi, Nauka, Kozukayama, Omizo, and Takashima. Upon hearing this, Nobunaga declared, "Then we shall first turn against Asakura," and assigned Shibata Shūri-no-suke Katsuie to the rearguard at Noda, Hachiya Hyōgo-no-kami Yorishige to Fukushima, and Akechi Jūbei Mitsuhide to Osaka, whereupon Yoshiaki-kō and Nobunaga returned to the capital.

Asakura Yoshikage, with Asakura Nakatsukasa-no-taifu, Yamazaki Nagato-no-kami, Uozumi Bingo-no-kami, Asakura Izumo-no-kami, Daiyōji Umanojō, and Kanemaki Tamba-no-kami as his vanguard, launched his assault on Usayama at dawn on the nineteenth day of the same month. Nobunaga's younger brother, Oda Kurō Nobuhari, was stationed in the castle at Katata, but he said to the castle's commander, Aochi Suruga-no-kami Yasusuke: "The situation at Usayama is precarious. Since this castle does not lie on Asakura's line of advance, if we allow them to station a containing force here while they storm Usayama and then march directly on the capital,

**[Left folio]**

we shall have cause for regret with no remedy. We must join forces with Usayama and mount a defense." Accordingly, he installed Ikai Jinsuke as caretaker of Katata, and led some thirteen hundred horsemen to Usayama. The Asakura forces advanced and attacked. Nobuhari and Yasusuke saw this and declared, "There is no benefit in retreating into the castle," and immediately sallied out to face the attackers. Mori Sanzaemon Yoshinari, watching from a distance atop Usayama, thought, "We cannot allow Nobuhari to be slain," and likewise sortied from the castle to give battle to the Asakura forces. Nobuhari and Yasusuke pressed forward at the very front of the fighting, but Asakura Magosaburō Kagekane wheeled away a force of over a thousand elite cavalry and struck them in the flank. Asai Iwami-no-kami and Ōnogi Tosa-no-kami crossed from Nagahama by boat and also struck from the flank. Mori, Oda (Nobuhari), Aochi, and their men were surrounded by enemies front and rear and could hold no longer. Mori Sanzaemon Yoshinari charged into the very midst of where Kitashō Tosa-no-kami Kageyuki stood in reserve, and was in the end slain by Ishida Jūzō. Nobuhari also charged into the camp of Daiyōji Umanokami Kaguharu, grappled with Igarashi Kotarō, and was—[stabbed/cut]
