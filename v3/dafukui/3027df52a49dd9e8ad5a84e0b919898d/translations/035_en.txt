**[Right folio]**

Nobunaga gave his consent to the two envoys, who then returned to Echizen and reported the matter. Yoshiaki was greatly moved, and on the eighteenth day of the seventh month of Eiroku 11 (1568), he departed from Ichijōdani. As an escort along the route, Yoshikage dispatched Maeba Tōemon Kagesada with five hundred mounted men to accompany him, and they arrived at Tsuruga. From there, Asakura Nakatsukasa-no-tayū Kagenori joined Maeba with over three hundred mounted men, and upon reaching Odani-no-shō in northern Ōmi, Asai Bizen-no-kami Nagamasa received Yoshiaki and installed him in Odani Castle. Meanwhile, Nobunaga sent Fuwa Kawachi-no-kami and Murai Mimbu-no-shō as his guides, and the two Asakura envoys returned from Odani. Asai also escorted them as far as Sekigahara. On the twenty-fifth of the same month, they arrived at lodgings in Gifu in Mino Province, where Nobunaga had an audience, and a council of war was held regarding the military measures to be taken against Shōtei (Rokkaku Shōtei) of Mitsukuri in Ōmi and others. Nobunaga attached his own envoys to the three envoys sent by the *kubō* to admonish Shōtei, but he refused to comply. On the eighteenth of the following month, as Yoshiaki was about to set out on the return journey, a retainer of the Sasaki clan named Nomura Etchū-no-kami Takakatsu—

**[Left folio]**

—who bore a grudge against the Mitsukuri lay monk (known as Bukkanshi), suddenly defected and rushed to join Nobunaga. Furthermore, Gamō Chūzaburō Ujisato, who resided at Hino in Ōmi, also submitted to Nobunaga. Command of the rearguard on the return march was entrusted to Akechi Jūbei Mitsuhide. From along the route, Sasa Kurannosuke Narimasa and Fukutomi Heizaemon Sadatsugu were dispatched as envoys to Odani Castle of Asai Shimotsuke-no-kami Sukemasa's son, Bizen-no-kami Nagamasa, bearing the message: "Since the *kubō* Yoshiaki is now marching to the capital to subjugate the rebels, and as Nobunaga and the Asai are connected by kinship, you are urged to join our side." However, as no clear reply was forthcoming, Fukutomi returned from northern Ōmi in anger.

In the ninth month (lower decade) of Eiroku 12 (1569), seeking to catch Yoshiaki off guard while he was in the capital, Miyoshi Yamashiro-nyūdō Shōgan, Miyoshi Shimotsuke-nyūdō Chūkansai, and Miyoshi Hyūga-no-kami Nagayasu were appointed as commanders of the main force, Yakushiji Kurōzaemon Sadaharu as the vanguard, and Saitō Uhyōe-no-tayū Tatsuoki as the second unit. Their combined force of over five thousand mounted men departed from the port of Sakai, passed through Ōsaka-Hirakata, setting fires here and there as they advanced on the capital.
