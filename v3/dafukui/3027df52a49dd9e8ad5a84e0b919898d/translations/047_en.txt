—(the envoys) conveyed the terms of the peace. Since both the Oda and the Asakura were senior retainers of the Shiba clan, there had been no fundamental enmity between them. It was only by the shogun's command that they had advanced as far as Tsuruga; they should likewise conclude peace with the Osaka Honganji. Henceforth there must be no alienation toward the Asakura house—such was the message conveyed, and so forth. Upon hearing this, Yoshikage remained unconvinced. He surmised that it could only be an attempt to escape an immediate crisis caused by exhausted provisions during a prolonged campaign. His own side, he reasoned, was accustomed to fighting in the snow and well-supplied by its provisioning chain; now was the very moment to coordinate further with Settsu (i.e., the Honganji) and, seizing upon the enemy's fatigue, launch an attack to crush them. Asai Bizen-no-kami, Asakura Nakatsukasa-no-taifu, and Asakura Tosa-no-kami, among others, voiced agreement with this view. However, Asakura Shikibu-no-taifu and Asakura Magosaburō argued that the Osaka Honganji was an ecclesiastical institution and therefore an unreliable ally, and that since Nobunaga was a general of great renown, peace would be the wiser course. This position was transmitted back to the envoys. Thereafter the matter reached the Emperor's ears; Senior Counselor of the Third Rank of Sanjō, Sanetsumi, and Middle Counselor Shigenoi Fuyutaka were dispatched as imperial envoys bearing a written oath on Nobunaga's behalf. Yoshikage accepted and on the fifteenth day of the twelfth month withdrew to Echizen. Nobunaga (likewise returned to his camp).

On the twenty-fifth day of the fourth month of Genki 2 (1571), Nobunaga marched out to Tsushima in Owari to suppress the Nagashima Uprising, and summoned the people of Nagashima to Tsushima. The Ikkō priests and the ringleaders of the uprising, reasoning that if they were to sacrifice their lives they might as well die in open battle, appointed Shimotsumauchi Sanmi-bō as their commander, and uprisings broke out in more than fifty villages in the vicinity of Nagashima. On the evening of the twelfth day of the fifth month, at the Awami-Kashima-guchi assault force, the commanding general Shibata Shūri-no-suke was the first to suffer defeat, sustained wounds, and withdrew to Kōzu (Takasu) and Komano. The second-in-command, Iga Iga-no-kami Noritoshi, was also routed and retreated toward his home base. The third-in-command, the castle lord of Ōgaki, Ujijie Hitachi Nyūdō Bokuzen (Tomokuni), was slain at Ōta village, and the army suffered a crushing defeat. Lord Oda returned to his camp.
