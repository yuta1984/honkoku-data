—(Nobunaga) advanced to Ōmi, encamped near Odani, and built a subsidiary fort (tsukeshiro) at Toragozenyama, installing Kinoshita Tōkichirō with fifteen hundred cavalry to hold the fortification, while engagements with the enemy castle of Odani continued without cease. Asakura Yoshikage, upon hearing this, resolved to rescue the Asai father and son, mustered a large force, set out from Ichijōdani, arrived at camp at Hikida Castle, crossed the Tonezawa pass, sent his vanguard toward the area around Yanagase and Kinomoto, added strength to both Yamamoto and Odani, and prepared to face the Gifu forces in opposition. Nobunaga left Kinoshita Tōkichirō behind at Toragozenyama and returned with haste to Mino Province. The Asakura forces urgently attempted to storm the outpost on the facing hill at Toragozenyama, but Kinoshita Hideyoshi defended well and held out. As the days passed and snow at last began to fall, Asai Bizen-no-kami Nagamasa went to Yoshikage's main camp at Amenomori lodgings and recommended withdrawing to camp, whereupon all returned to Echizen.

On the twenty-second day of the twelfth month, Lord Ieyasu and Shingen engaged in battle at Mikatagahara. The vanguard of the Tōtōmi (Tokugawa) forces was defeated. Hirate Kenmotsu Kiyozumi, who had been sent as reinforcement from Nobunaga, was also killed in battle. On the Tōtōmi side, Honda Higo-no-kami, Sakakibara Settsu-no-kami, Torii Shirōzaemon, Matsudaira Yaemon and others below them were also killed in battle.

**[Left folio]**

Should further difficulties arise, a messenger (urgent dispatch) came from Tōtōmi to Nobunaga requesting that reinforcements be again sent. Accordingly, Sakuma Uemon-no-jō, Hayashi Sado-no-kami, and Takigawa Sakon-shōgen, with a force of over five thousand, assembled at Kasadera, Narumi, Atsuta, and other locations in Owari Province.

In the first year of Tenshō (1573), the Shogun, Lord Yoshiaki, came into conflict with Nobunaga, pulled up (destroyed) the bridge at Seta, built a fortification (tsumenoshiro) at Ishiyama to block the road from Gifu, and also prepared the old fort at Katata, installing Ōdate Iyo-no-kami Nobusada, Isshiki Harima-no-kami, Isogai Shin'emon, the same Ichishō, Watanabe Genbei, and Wani Jirobei, together with vagrants (masterless men) from the surrounding area, to hold it. At this time, it is said that there were secret communications from Takeda Shingen to Yoshiaki, and so forth. The rest is omitted. (Katata was surrendered by Hachiya Hyōgo; Ishiyama by Yamaoka Kōjōin.) Niwa Gorōzaemon and Akechi Jūbei stormed and took these positions — on the twenty-fourth day of the second month, perhaps. Akechi especially distinguished himself. Since he had achieved military merit in the siege of Katata Castle at this time, western Ōmi was granted to Akechi as his domain. He established his residence at Sakamoto, and from this point changed his title, being granted the name Hyūga-no-kami.
