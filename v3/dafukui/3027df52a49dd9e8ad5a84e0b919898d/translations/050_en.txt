As connecting links along this route, Miyabe Zenshōbō and his son were stationed at Miyabe village, and Takenaka Uneme and Hiratsuka Inaba-no-kami at Hassōzan. Yanada Saemon-tarō and Chūjō Shōkan were assigned five hundred additional cavalry and added to Kinoshita Tōkichirō's forces to hold Toragozenyama. At this time, the Asakura retainers Maeba Kurōbei Yoshitsugu and Tomita Magokurojū Nagahide, who had marched out from Echizen to reinforce Odani, had a change of heart, and leading some two hundred cavalry of principal retainers — including Maeba Sōkichirō, Tomita Yojirō, Keya Inosuke, and Masui Jinnai — they surrendered to Nobunaga's camp. Furthermore, in order to bring over to their side Abe Awaji-no-kami, the castle lord of Yamamoto, and Asami Tsushima-no-kami, the castle lord of Yakio, Sasaki Shuri-no-taiyū Yoshihide, who was residing near Kannonji, was used (by Nobunaga) as an intermediary to arrange a reconciliation. Nobunaga then returned to camp in Mino Province and remained in his home domain throughout the summer.

On the first day of the seventh month, a report came from Akechi's quarters at Sakamoto in Ōmi Province, stating that the Shogun, Lord Yoshiaki, had reversed the peace agreement made the previous spring, stationed troops in Nijō Castle,

**[Left folio]**

and had moved himself to Makishima (in Ujiro), where he constructed a fortification. On the third of the same month, Nobunaga departed Gifu, arrived at camp at Sawayama, crossed the lake (Lake Biwa) by atakebune (large warship), arrived at Sakamoto, passed through the Shiga pass, and entered the capital. He attacked Nijō Castle. The castle troops — Hino [Teresuke] and Takakura [Noritsune] and others — all submitted hostages and surrendered. Shizuhara Castle was stormed and taken by Akechi's forces alone. The commander Yamamoto Tsushima-no-kami was also persuaded by Akechi to hand over the castle and become a surrendered prisoner. Retainers of Hyūga-no-kami (Akechi) — Fujita Dengo and Fujita Tōzō — received the castle and confined Yamamoto in a nearby place. Watanabe Kunai-no-shōyū and Isogai Shin'emon, who were at Ichijōji, also sued for surrender, and so forth. Nobunaga advanced on Makishima. At this time, Araki Settsu-no-kami, Tsutsui Junkei, Nagaoka Hyōbu-no-tayū, Maeba Kurōbei, Tomita Magokurō, and others garrisoned the vicinity as a check on Yodo Castle. The castle (Makishima) surrendered, and the Shogun Yoshiaki shaved his head and departed the castle. Sakuma Uemon Nobumori was ordered to escort him as far as Wakae in Kawachi Province and to banish him — this being because Miyoshi Sakyō-no-tayū Yoshitsugu resided there and had long been the Shogun's sworn enemy.
