The groundbreaking ceremony (*kuwahajime*) was held, and the construction was completed swiftly. On the sixth day of the fourth month, Shogun Yoshiaki was moved into Nijō Castle. As rewards for distinguished service (*gunkō*) at the Battle of Honkokuji, Araki Kazukenosuke Murashige was granted the province of Settsu and was appointed Settsu-no-kami. Akechi Jūbei received an additional stipend (*kaon*) of three thousand *kan*, and three members of his clan—Akechi Yahei-ji Mitsuharu, Akechi Jirōemon Mitsutada, and Akechi Jūrozaemon Mitsuchika—were summoned into service. After the castle's completion and the ceremonial move-in (*watamashi*), Kinoshita Tōkichirō was stationed in Kyoto to guard the shogunal household, and on the thirteenth day of the fifth month, Nobunaga returned to his home province.

In mid-second month of Genki 1 (1570), Tokugawa Mikawa-no-kami [personal name omitted] came for the first time from Hamamatsu in Tōtōmi to Gifu to meet with Nobunaga. As they were on terms of brotherly friendship, Nobunaga was exceedingly pleased. Taking this opportunity, it was decided that a visit should also be made to the shogun in Kyoto, and in early third month they proceeded to the capital, where an audience was held with Lord Yoshiaki. Thereafter, [Nobunaga] led a military expedition to the domain of Asakura Saemonkami Yoshikage in Echizen, seeking [his] assistance in punishing the Miyoshi, but—

**[Left folio]**

—when Nobunaga requested this, Yoshikage made the pretext of a *ikki* uprising in Kaga Province and did not accompany the party on its return to the capital. Moreover, he had been sheltering Saitō Uhyōbu-no-tayū Tatsuoki, the former lord of Mino Province, and when the Miyoshi faction attacked Kyoto the previous spring, it was Yoshikage's doing that Tatsuoki was placed as commander of their vanguard force. Furthermore, the remnants of the Sasaki clan and Azai Shimōsa-no-kami and his son, who refused to follow Nobunaga's orders, did so relying on Yoshikage as their backer. For these reasons, it was decided that Yoshikage should be destroyed, and a council was held on the advance into Echizen.

Among those consulted, Akechi Jūbei, having resided in Echizen in previous years, was ordered to provide guidance on the route. Akechi reported: "[First,] the road to the northern provinces (*Hokurikudō*) offers three routes: the eastern Ōmi route, the western Ōmi route, and the Wakasa route. On the eastern Ōmi route, remnants of the Sasaki clan are spread across various points—Yokoyama, Nagahama, Yamaki, and Odani. On the western route, there are enemies at Wani, Kido, Tanaka, and Shinshiro. Therefore, it would be advisable to advance on Tsuruga in Echizen via the Wakasa route. In Takai District of Wakasa Province, Hemi Suruga-no-kami of Takahama has already surrendered to our side from the outset. As for Takeda Yoshimune of Obama in the same province, though he was an enemy, he has recently died, and his adopted son Ukyō-no-suke Yoshiyori has come over [to our side…]"
