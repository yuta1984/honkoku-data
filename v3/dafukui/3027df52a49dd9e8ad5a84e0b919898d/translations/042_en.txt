—Uozumi, Kurosaka, Tomita, Magara, and other fierce warriors, more than two hundred men in all, were killed in battle. Thus, although Azai's forces had gained the upper hand, because the Asakura had been defeated, the Ōmi forces also fell into disorder and were routed; Imamura, Kano, Yuge, Azai Hosoe, Hayasaki, Endō, and several others were killed, and the survivors withdrew to Mount Oyoriyama, and from there returned to Odani Castle. Nobunaga's banner corps withdrew as far as Kozekinoma; however, because the lord Tokugawa had established his main camp on the bluff of the Anegawa River, the Mino forces rallied and pushed back toward the Anegawa. Afterward, Yokoyama Castle was also taken by storm, and Yokoyama, Ōnogi, and others submitted in surrender, opened their castle, and fled toward Nagahama. Kinoshita Tōkichirō was installed as castellan of Yokoyama, and Niwa Gorōzaemon was installed as castellan of Sawayama. As the victory at the Battle of Anegawa was due to the distinguished service of the lord Tokugawa, Nobunaga expressed his deep admiration and gratitude; the lord Tokugawa returned to his castle at Hamamatsu, and the lord Oda also returned to Mino Province.

The Miyoshi clan had lost the advantage in a battle in Kyoto in the first month of the past year of the Snake [Genki 1, 1570], their power having greatly diminished, and
