**[Right folio]**

In Tenbun 15 (1546), a castle was built at Nagoya, and Nobunaga took up residence there at the age of thirteen. Later, the daughter of Saitō Yamashiro-no-kami Yoshitatsu, lord of Mino Province, was presented as the daughter of his grandfather Dōsan and given in marriage to Nobunaga. In the eighteenth year of the same era (1549), his father, Bingo-no-kami Nobuhide, passed away.

In the middle of the fourth month of Kōji 2 (1556), when Nobunaga's father-in-law, Saitō Yamashiro-no-kami Yoshitatsu, came to battle against his own son, Uemon-no-tayū Tatsuoki, Yoshitatsu asked Nobunaga for reinforcements. Nobunaga, taken aback, advanced as far as Daira-guchi in Mino Province, but turned back upon learning that Yamashiro-no-kami had that very morning lost his life at the hands of Tatsuoki. At this point, forces sent from Tatsuoki's side — including Makimura Mondono-suke, Hayashi Han'dayū, Kagai Yahachi, and Iwata Kageyu — gave chase, and Nobunaga's Owari forces were hard pressed and only just managed to withdraw into Kiyosu.

Thereafter, Nobunaga destroyed the Imagawa clan, and after pacifying Owari, he attacked and destroyed Tatsuoki, avenging his grudge and endeavoring to fulfill the original wish of his father-in-law Yamashiro-no-kami Yoshitatsu. Nobunaga employed various stratagems against Mino, and the Mino warriors turned their backs on Tatsuoki and came over to Nobunaga's side.

**[Left folio]**

Inokuchi (Gifu) had a formidable castle on Mount Inaba, but it was short of provisions and water, and was attacked from all directions. From the first day of the eighth month of Eiroku 8 (1565), the siege was pressed, and on the fifteenth day, Tatsuoki emerged from the castle and sought terms of surrender. He crossed the Nagara River and dispersed. Crossing the Haiboshi Pass, he secluded himself in Ōno District and threw himself upon the mercy of the Asakura clan.

Akechi Jūbei Mitsuhide, who had been residing in Echizen and serving Asakura Yoshikage, began to be distanced from Yoshikage's inner circle around the winter of Eiroku 8. The reason for this was a denunciation made by Kuratani Gyōbu-no-tayū Tsugutomo. Previously, the lay monk Kin'go Norikage had also said that "a man of such refined talents and eloquence as Mitsuhide is the sort who would deceive senior hereditary retainers and belittle his lord — a man with ambitious designs." When Tsugutomo passed along this accusation, Yoshikage kept Mitsuhide at a distance. Moreover, the fact that Yoshikage was now providing support to Saitō Tatsuoki, the enemy of the Akechi clan, was something Mitsuhide found pointless. At this juncture, he received an invitation from Nobunaga, extended with great warmth on the grounds that Nobunaga's connection to Mitsuhide was through his father-in-law Yamashiro-no-kami (Dōsan). And so, on the ninth day of the tenth month of Eiroku 9 (1566), Mitsuhide traveled from Echizen to Gifu in Mino Province.
