(Continued from previous page) Hearing that (Wakabayashi) Rakusuke had constructed a new fort, they attacked and demolished it, capturing and killing men known as Wakabayashi Nagato, Awaga Saburō, Amaya, Kawashima, Yasui, Inamura, and others. From there they advanced on Hirose Village and established camp at Fuchū Ryūsenji, where they joined forces with Shibata Shūriyō (Katsuie) and Koreto Hyūga-no-kami (Akechi Mitsuhide) to assault and break through the fortifications at Ryūsenji. They took the heads of the commander Miyake Gonnosuke, as well as Kai, Yū, Ikeizumi, Ōshima, Shirasaki, Uriu, Matsumura, Hattori, Ikarashi, Takahashi, and others. The ikki forces were utterly defeated, and Shimoma Chikugo Hōkyō, Shimoma Izumi, and others — along with seven hundred monks and more than two thousand villagers — were either crucified or beheaded. Thereafter, Nobunaga resided for a time at Kitanosho and issued his directives for the settlement of the region. Among these, Shibata Shūriyō Katsuie was appointed *shugo* (military governor) of Echizen, installed at Kitanosho, and granted a fief of five hundred thousand *koku*. In the vicinity of Fuchū, fifty thousand *koku* was granted to Sassa Kuranousuke Narimasa; thirty thousand *koku* to Maeda Matasaemon Toshiie; and twenty thousand *koku* to Fuwa Hikozō. The domain of Ōno — thirty thousand *koku* — was bestowed upon Kanamori Gorōhachi Narichika, and twenty thousand *koku* upon Hara Hikojirō.

**[Left folio]**

Around autumn of the fourth year of Tenshō (1576), there were rumors that Takeda Ukyō-no-suke Yoshiyori, lord of Obama Castle in Wakasa Province, had fallen gravely ill. Nobunaga dispatched a messenger to Hemi Suruga-no-kami of Takahama in the same province, instructing him to destroy the Takeda. As Suruga-no-kami was himself unwell (*shōrō*), he sent his son, Shinano-no-kami, as commander at the head of more than three hundred horsemen, with Nakatsumi Sakyō, Uozumi Ichinoshō, and Sawamura Daigaku as the vanguard, advancing toward Obama. A retainer of the Takeda, Mutō Kōzukenosuke of the Saburi Valley in the same province, hearing of this, intercepted them and gave battle. Hemi Shinano-no-kami was struck by gunfire and killed, causing the army to rout and withdraw to Takahama Castle. His father, Suruga-no-kami, in his grief and indignation, pressed into the Saburi Valley and scattered Mutō Kōzukenosuke's forces. Mutō, gravely wounded, retreated into the mountains and died there. Suruga-no-kami then moved on Obama, but Takeda Yoshiyori had already died of his serious illness. As Hemi too was critically ill, he set fire to several places in the vicinity and returned to Takahama. When a great number of Takeda retainers bore down upon them, Hemi also perished.
