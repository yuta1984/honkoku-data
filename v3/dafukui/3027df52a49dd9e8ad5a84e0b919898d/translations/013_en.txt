　　(The lord of Kuratani said that) Mitsuhide, who came from Mino Province, although possessed of resourcefulness and talent, was by nature arrogant, looked down upon even the senior hereditary retainers, and presumed to take the upper seats above his peers. One such as this will, in time to come, disrespect his lord, inevitably harbor resentment, and prove to be a man of dangerous ambition—so said Kingō Norikage-nyūdō [Asakura Norikage], his lordship [Kuratani] recounted. From this point on, [Mitsuhide] was removed from attendance as a close retainer [kinshū], and when others of the same rank received rewards and stipends, no such provisions were made for him. Around that same time, Saitō Tatsuoki, the lord of Mino Province, having been defeated by Nobunaga of Owari, wandered in exile, crossed the Haebōshi Pass [possibly "Haiboshi" or "Haisei" pass], retreated into Ōno District, and sought the patronage of the Asakura house. Since Tatsuoki was the Akechi family's enemy, [Mitsuhide] thought it pointless to remain in the same province—and at just that moment, a summons arrived from Nobunaga. Mitsuhide had originally been a retainer of Yamasiro-no-kami Yoshitatsu, who was Nobunaga's father-in-law, and Mitsuhide's uncle, Sōshuku-nyūdō, had died in loyal service to Yoshitatsu; thus Nobunaga called him [to his side] in order to repay this obligation. Accordingly, on the ninth day of the tenth month of Eiroku 9 [1566], [Mitsuhide] came from Echizen to Gifu in Mino, and so forth. On the grounds that it was his homeland, a forfeited estate [kesshо̄] of 4,200 kan in Anpachi District of Mino Province

[Marginal note in red ink, upper portion]
Yoshiaki's wanderings

　　On the nineteenth day of the fifth month of Eiroku 8 [1565], Matsunaga Danjō incited his lord Miyoshi Yoshitsugu to rebellion, surrounded the Shogun's residence, and Yoshiteru-kō was murdered at the age of thirty. In order to harm [the Shogun's] younger brother, Yoshiaki-kō—[they] also killed Rokuonji Shūkō, and sent an attack force to Nara intending to do away with Ichijōin Kōkei. Kōkei fled into Kasugayama, passed through Iga Province, and made his way to Kōka in Ōmi Province, entering the residence of Wada Izumi-no-kami Hidemori, a retainer of Sasaki Shuri-no-taifu Yoshihide. Thereafter he moved to the village of Yajima, returned to lay life [genzoku], and came to be known as Yoshiaki. The mother of Sasaki Yoshihide was a daughter of Yoshiharu-kō and was therefore the elder sister [or sibling] of Yoshiaki-kō, so one would expect no discourtesy to be shown—but since Mitsukuri Sakyō-no-daibu-nyūdō Jōtei's son, Uemon-no-jō Yoshisuke, wielded control over all affairs, and the Mitsukuri father and son were kinsmen of the Miyoshi, there arose talk of not merely failing to show respect to Yoshiaki, but of actually putting him to death.
