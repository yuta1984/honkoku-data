　　(The lord of Kuratani said that) this Akechi who had come from Mino Province, though possessed of wisdom, resourcefulness, and refined accomplishments, was by temperament arrogant—he looked down upon even the long-serving senior retainers and presumed to take a seat above his peers. A man of such character, sooner or later, will make light of his lord, will surely harbor resentment, and will be one with ambitions—so Kingo Norikage-nyūdō had remarked, he said. From that time on, [Mitsuhide] was removed from among the intimate attendants, and when rewards and favors were distributed to those of the same rank, no such consideration was given to him. Around that same time, Saitō Tatsuoki, the lord of Mino Province, having been defeated by Nobunaga of Owari and left wandering, crossed over the Haisei [possibly "Haebōshi"] Pass, took up quiet residence in Ōno District, and came to rely upon the Asakura household. Since Tatsuoki was the enemy of the Akechi house, [Mitsuhide] thought there was no benefit in remaining in the same province; and just at that juncture, an invitation came from Nobunaga. Akechi had originally been a retainer of Nobunaga's father-in-law, Yamashiro-no-kami Yoshitatsu, and since Mitsuhide's uncle, Sōshuku-nyūdō, had given his life in loyal service to Yoshitatsu, [Nobunaga] invited him in order to repay that debt of gratitude, it is said. Accordingly, on the ninth day of the tenth month of Eiroku 9 [1566], [Mitsuhide] traveled from Echizen to Gifu in Mino Province, and so forth. On the grounds that it was his home province, the confiscated lands [*kyūsho*] of four thousand two hundred *kan* in Anpachi District of Mino Province were

[Marginal note in red ink, upper portion]
Yoshiaki's wanderings

　　On the nineteenth day of the fifth month of Eiroku 8 [1565], Matsunaga Danjō urged his lord Miyoshi Yoshitsugu to revolt, surrounded the Shogun's residence, and Lord Yoshiteru was murdered at the age of thirty. [The Miyoshi and Matsunaga] sought to harm his younger brother Lord Yoshiaki and Rokuonji Shūkō as well, and dispatched a punitive force to Nara intending to eliminate Ichijōin Kakukyō. Kakukyō fled and took refuge at Kasugayama, passed through Iga Province, and made his way to Kōka in Ōmi Province, entering the residence of Wada Izumi-no-kami Hidemori, a houseman of Sasaki Shuri-no-taifu Yoshihide. Thereafter he moved to the village of Yajima, renounced his vows, and came to be called Yoshiaki. The mother of Sasaki Yoshihide was a daughter of Lord Yoshiharu and was the elder sister of Lord Yoshiaki, so [Yoshihide] ought not to have treated him carelessly; however, since Mitsukuri Sakyō-no-taifu-nyūdō Yoshisada's son, Uemon-no-jō Yoshisuke, managed all affairs [as regent], and since the Mitsukuri father and son were related by marriage to the Miyoshi, there arose talk of not only failing to show respect to Yoshiaki but of putting him to death…
