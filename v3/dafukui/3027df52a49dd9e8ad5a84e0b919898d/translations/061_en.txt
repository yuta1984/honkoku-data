Hyūga no Kami (Mitsuhide) said, "If that is so, I shall take you into my service," and accordingly lodged him in Kaibara Castle in Tamba Province.

Akechi (Koretō) Hyūga no Kami, intending to pursue and destroy the Akai faction in Tamba, led the combined forces of Tanba and Ōmi Provinces — a total of over eight thousand five hundred horsemen — and arrived at Kanayama in Tamba on the ninth day of the eighth month of the same year (Tenshō 7, 1579).

Akai Gorō Munaka of Takami Castle and Akai Akuemon Munashige of Hōzuki, having won a victory by exploiting a sudden heavy snowfall the previous winter, were growing ever more formidable in their power. Lord of the province Mitsuhide, intending first to attack Hōzuki Castle, announced that the main camp should be moved to a place called Yahata. Leaving over two thousand horsemen at Kanayama under the command of Akechi Jūrozaemon, Fujita Denge, Okuda Kunai, Murakoshi Sanjūrō, and Miyake Shikibu, he himself went around through Okumura

to the west and arrived at Yahata Hill, where he set up his battle formation. The Hōzuki forces, upon seeing this, sallied forth from the castle and engaged in skirmishes for several days. Time and again the Koretō (Mitsuhide) forces appeared to be at a disadvantage, and the Akai forces pressed forward with growing confidence. Mitsuhide held council and laid an ambush; luring the enemy out and catching them in a pincer attack, the Akai forces were routed. Akai Gorō, having suffered wounds in several places, fled in the direction of Harima. Akai Akuemon was cut down and killed by a man called Hayashi Hanzaburō, a retainer of Akechi Samanosuke. From this point on, Amada District and Hikami District were fully pacified, and the entire province came under uniform control.

Among the many former retainers of Lord Nobunaga who were dismissed and had their domains confiscated, Sakuma Uemonojō Nobumori and his son had their seven thousand or more followers dispersed; they left the fortification at Tennōji and retreated to Kōyasan. Nobumori had been engaged with the Ōsaka Honganji for eleven years, maintaining the main camp at Tennōji for five years from the Year of the Rat onwards — a man of considerable past merit.
