—no matter how many ambush forces the enemy had positioned, their numbers were now unknowable (to the enemy), and the Kanegasaki forces appeared to be withdrawing. At this, Hideyoshi charged in with a force of some four hundred men under cover of (gun)smoke and drove them all the way back to the castle gate. A man called Mōya Shichizaemon closed the gate at just the right moment, but the fifty or so men who had been caught outside were cut down. Hideyoshi, employing his strategic cunning, tended to the wounded, took hostages, and then withdrew.

　　(Note: It is said that the footsoldiers remaining with Hideyoshi at this time numbered some seven hundred, but whether this is accurate is difficult to determine.)

Please make inquiries on your end regarding the above. I cannot vouch for the truth or falsity of this account, but what I myself recall is as I have written here. As for Tezutsu Castle, I believe that Tsunahakuwa, Hikita Sakon, and the Kehi Shrine priests took refuge within—though I understand they met their deaths there. That is all. —Tsunahakuwa
