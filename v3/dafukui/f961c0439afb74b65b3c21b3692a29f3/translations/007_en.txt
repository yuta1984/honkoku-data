(Continued from the previous page) ...the people of the province were secretly won over, and the matter was reported to the Venerable Kennyo (Kōsa) at Osaka. It was proposed that they raise an ikki (uprising), strike down the three men, and thereby appease the vengeful spirit of the late lord (Yoshikage). [side note: "raise"] Upon receiving this report at Osaka, the response was "most reasonable," and Shimottsuma Chikugo was appointed as commander-in-chief. On the twentieth day of the eleventh month, with Asakura Magosaburō, Shichiri Mikawa no Kami, and Horie Jinsuke serving as the samurai commanders, a force of over eight thousand men, all clad in snow gear and wearing snowshoes (sledge-shoes), advanced upon the Toyohara (Toyotomi) encampment. In the pre-dawn hours of that day [side note: "of"], they raised their battle cry and launched a full frontal assault. The soldiers within the castle had no foot coverings suited for snow, and every one of them fled. Those who offered no resistance were [side note: "pardoned"]. Sakurada Harima no Kami, also attempting to flee, hid himself in the shadow of a riverbank, but was taken alive, beheaded, and cast aside.

For the castle at Ōno, Awaga [side note: "or Awaga"] Shika Saburō, Wakabayashi Nagato and his son [side note: "Awata"], and Kotabe Jihachirō served as commanders, with a force of over eight thousand. They too advanced in the pre-dawn hours, raised their battle cry, and made preparations to set fire to the interior of the castle, it is said. Shikibu no Tayū (Vice-Minister of Ceremonial) fought valiantly and charged out as many as three times, but without proper snow gear it was difficult to maneuver effectively, and he was ultimately struck down and perished. For the castle at Fuchū, Keya Inosuke, Mitani Gonojō, and Awatabe Shinshichirō served as the principal commanders, with a force of over two thousand. They surrounded it in the pre-dawn hours of the same day, and those within the castle fell into a state of panic and confusion.
