Hideyoshi, being a man of quick action, cleared away the snow from the old castle at Tezutsu-yama, put it in order, and issued orders that the samurai of Wakasa Province should man it as a garrison force, so it is said.

Item: Lord Nobunaga's departure on campaign to suppress the ikki of Echizen Province took place in the first ten days of the third month of the second year of Tenshō [1574]. Toward the Nakakawauchi-guchi (Middle Kawauchi Pass), at the border between Ōmi and Echizen Provinces, went: Shibata Shūri no Suke, Takigawa Sakon-shōgen, Inaba Iyo no Kami, Ujike Sakyō no Suke, Iga Iga no Kami, Marugame Hyōgo no Kami, Ichihashi Kurōzaemon no Jō, Fuwa Kawachi no Kami, and others.

Toward the Tsuruga-guchi (Tsuruga Pass entrance) went: Hashiba Chikuzen no Kami [side note: "Tōkichirō; at this time referred to as Chikuzen"], Niwa Gorōzaemon no Jō,

Taga Shin'emon no Jō, Yamazaki Genta-zaemon no Jō along with the Machi no Akada [side note: "that is, Akaza"], Ogawa Mago [side note: "also written as Shi"] Ichirō, Takanose, Aobe (father and son), Isono Tanba no Kami, Fukusumi Heira-zaemon no Jō, Saitō Shingo, Maeda Mata-zaemon no Jō, Nonomura Sanjūrō, Azai Shinhachiō, and others, who advanced to engage the enemy.

Lord Nobunaga at this time established his main encampment at Tsuruga and observed the state of the ikki forces. He found that the commoners and rōnin (masterless samurai) throughout the province were of one mind, well-acquainted with the terrain, and had fortified the difficult passes along the coasts, mountains, and roads in every direction. Armed with firearms, they lay in wait to engage the approaching enemy forces and had secured their positions. Nobunaga therefore sent in shinobi (covert agents) to reconnoiter the situation...
