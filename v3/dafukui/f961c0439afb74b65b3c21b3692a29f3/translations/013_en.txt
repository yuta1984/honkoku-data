**[Right folio]**
Higuchi opened the castle and withdrew. Shortly thereafter, Hideyoshi came rushing to the scene, but since the Ikki forces had installed capable rōnin (masterless samurai) as their commanders, upon seeing the attacking force arrive, they gathered their troops and retreated into their respective fortified camps. Hideyoshi then had further fortifications added to the stronghold at Kinome Pass and handed it over to Isono and Aibe. Hideyoshi went up to Gifu to report these matters to Lord Nobunaga. Upon hearing the report, Nobunaga flew into a rage, banished Higuchi to the Asama area in Ise Province, and later ordered him to commit ritual suicide (seppuku). At this time, Hideyoshi reportedly stated that Higuchi's reputation (lit. "shadow") was unfavorable.

**[Left folio]**
Originally, when Lord Nobunaga had retreated from Kanegasaki in Tsuruga, Echizen Province, there had been a need for someone to remain behind as the rearguard, yet not a single person had volunteered. Hideyoshi stepped forward and remained behind to serve as rearguard. That same summer, Lord Nobunaga ordered Hideyoshi to go to Takenaka Hanbee's quarters, and through this arrangement, Higuchi Sarobee, the acting commander (jindai) for Hori Jirō — a man of considerable influence among Azai's retainers — was brought over to their side. On that occasion, [Nobunaga] said to Hideyoshi: "You are altogether a man of great resourcefulness. Apply your ingenuity day and night without fail to win over Azai's retainers and bring them to our side, so that Azai may be destroyed.
