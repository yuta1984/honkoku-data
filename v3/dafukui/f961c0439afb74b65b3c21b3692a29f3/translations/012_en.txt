Item: On the third day of the eighth month of Genki 2 (1571), at Lord Nobunaga's siege fort at Kinome, Higuchi Saburōbei was on garrison duty as the month's watch commander. While he had been diligent at first, as time wore on a certain carelessness had crept in, and the watch-posts were being kept somewhat negligently. The ikki forces, having sent spies in secret to ascertain the situation, chose a night of violent wind and rain,

[Left folio]
and seven or eight hundred ikki warriors launched a night assault upon Kinome Castle, where Higuchi was garrisoned. Hearing of this, nearby ikki allies rushed to reinforce them, swelling their numbers to two or three thousand; pressing the attack without relenting for even a moment, they quickly broke through the first and second gates. Higuchi, being a man of valor, barricaded himself in the innermost keep (tsumecastle) and fought back with such ferocity that the conflict sparked fire. The ikki forces sent a messenger with the following proposal: "You should withdraw from this place. As for your lives, we shall give them no concern [i.e., we shall spare them]." Upon hearing this, Higuchi reflected: "If I could hold out two or three more days, Hideyoshi would surely come to our relief—but hemmed in as we are in the innermost keep, it is difficult to buy any more time."
