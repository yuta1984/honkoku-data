(…having had Ryōzaemon read it aloud and listened to its contents,) he found that the document spoke of "not forgetting disorder even in times of peace," and set forth matters of conduct and tactics — nothing else particularly suspicious. Moreover, given that there was a palpable air that he would be cut down on the spot should he refuse unexpectedly, he yielded to the pressure and affixed his seal. Then, in the early part of the current month, he was unexpectedly summoned late at night by Heihachirō, who wished to speak with him in person. When he went to meet him, he found Heihachirō shaving fire arrows, and eight others gathered there as well. Heihachirō told him: "Since last year, rice and grain have run out and the common people have been driven to desperate poverty — this is ultimately the result of failures in governance. We have strong views regarding the castle authorities and the Town Magistrate, and should matters come to a head, you should join our cause." Although Sukejirou had misgivings about this,

he could see that even if he were to voice his objections, they would not be heeded, and there was clearly an atmosphere in which he could be struck down at once if he opposed them. While it was not simply that he wished to preserve his own life, he resolved that rather than dying a meaningless death like a dog, his first duty was to the benefit of the public authorities (kōgi), and so he gave a measured response appropriate to the situation. Moreover, since it was Heihachirō's habitual manner to mock and criticize the administration of government and various officials, Sukejirou had felt it would be wrong if matters were allowed to drift along on mere words and loose talk without resolution, and so he had been keeping his purpose set on eventually ascertaining the true depths of the matter and reporting it to Yamashiro-no-kami.
