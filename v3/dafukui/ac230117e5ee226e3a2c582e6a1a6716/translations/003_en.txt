I was instructed to hear the full details, and so I met with him in person. According to his account: during the year of己 (Tsuchinoto-i / 1829), when Okubo Suruga-no-kami was serving as Osaka Machi-bugyō (Town Magistrate), Sukejirou had been appointed to a post called "machi-metsuke" (town inspector). This role entailed overseeing the general conduct of the magistrate's yoriki and dōshin, gathering intelligence about conditions in the city, and handling covert duties on behalf of the magistrate's office. By convention, he did not meet with anyone outside of close relatives, and accordingly had not visited Heihachiro's residence thereafter. However, during the sixth month of that same year, Watanabe Ryōzaemon — a yoriki-dōshin under Yamashiro-no-kami's command and a disciple of Heihachiro — came to visit him and conveyed the message that in the event of any disturbance or emergency, one should be prepared to stake one's life for the sake of loyalty and filial duty. Though Sukejirou found this exceedingly suspicious,

[Left folio]

he replied that, since such conduct was in accordance with the natural way of human virtue, he supposed he would indeed be prepared when such a time came. Thereafter, other disciples came periodically, each in turn asking in a casual manner whether his resolve was in good order. Regarding the entire matter, since Heihachiro was habitually given to talk of military affairs and political commentary, and was known as a man of bold and forceful character, Sukejirou had assumed this was simply a reflection of a martial and military-minded disposition, and thought nothing more of it. Then, on the sixth day of the current first month (January 1837), the aforementioned Watanabe Ryōzaemon, along with Kondō Kajigoro — a dōshin of the same unit — arrived dressed in formal attire (清服), bringing with them a written document prepared on hōkōgami (奉公紙). They told him that if he was in agreement with the group, he should affix his seal to it. However, as the document was written in classical Chinese (kanbun), he was entirely unable to read it, and so had Ryōzaemon read it aloud to him, and listened to its contents…
