**Official Proclamations of the Shogunate (公儀御触)**

**Genroku 13th Year, Year of the Dragon (1700)**

Memorandum (Referenced in the old Shikimoku manuscript)

● Regarding the abandonment of children, this practice is strictly prohibited. Previously, it was proclaimed that those unable to care for children should report the circumstances to the relevant authority — servants to their master, those in shogunal domains to the daikan (magistrate), those in private domains to the village headman (nanushi) and five-household group (gonin-gumi), and those in town areas to the town headman and five-household group — so that arrangements for the child's upbringing could be made. Nevertheless, cases of child abandonment continue to occur, which is most reprehensible. Anyone who abandons a child shall be severely punished. Ensure that child abandonment is strictly forbidden henceforth. The above.　Seventh month, Year of the Dragon.

As per the attached document, this has been communicated by the Senior Inspectors (Ō-metsuke). All parties are to take heed. The above.
Second day of the eighth month.　To All Concerned.　From the Inspectors (Metsuke).

**Kyōhō 6th Year, Year of the Ox (1721)**

Memorandum

● One: The iris helmet (shōbu kabuto) and crest ornaments (tatemono) are to be finished in bare metal (hakuoki — polished and left uncoated).

One: The bowl and neck guard (hachi and shikoro) of the helmet are all to be finished in black lacquer. If family crests or designs are to be painted on, only minimal coloring using red ochre (tan), white lead powder (gofun), and malachite green (rokushō) is permitted.
　However, they must not be wrapped in woven fabric.

One: Spears and naginata (glaives) must not be given a bare metal finish. Other lacquering and coloring shall follow the same rules as the helmets.
　However, doll figures must not be used as decorations.

Even for iris helmets intended as gifts/tribute, they must not be made to a higher standard than these regulations permit. If they are made more simply than the standard, the previous practice shall be acceptable. The above.　Fourth month, Year of the Ox.

As the above has already been communicated to the Town Magistrate (machi bugyō), all parties are to take heed accordingly. For the current year, since some items were already ordered and produced at the request of townspeople, these may be sold. However, no orders for items outside the regulations will be accepted whatsoever. From next year onwards, these rules must be strictly observed as proclaimed.
　　　The above was communicated to the Senior Elders (go-karō-chū) on the 23rd day of the fifth month.
