[Attached slip]

◦ Written notice issued by Senior Inspector (Ō-metsuke) Lord Andō Chikugo-no-kami to the domain representatives (rusuigumi) in Edo.

**Memorandum**

It has previously been decreed that the number of attendants during the sankin-kōtai (alternate attendance) processions must not become excessive. You are to continue to ensure that the number of people is kept as small as possible. When large numbers gather all at once, both horses and people at the post stations fall into hardship. Therefore, those who cannot be left behind should be dispatched on different days, divided between advance and rear parties, so that the roads do not become congested. The above.
　　　Fourth month, Year of the Snake.

**Memorandum issued by Lord Andō Chikugo-no-kami**

Regarding the regulations for post horses and porters along the highway routes: as is already well known, the established quotas are fifty horses and fifty porters for the Tōkaidō, and twenty-five persons and twenty-five horses for the Nakasendō (Central Mountain Road). Until now, horses and porters have been assembled in advance from nearby villages so that there would be no shortage when daimyo processions passed through. However, the Nakasendō has been suffering increasing hardship year by year, and petitions have been filed since the time when Kamio Bizen-no-kami was in charge. After I (Andō Chikugo-no-kami) was appointed to the additional post, petitions continued to be made frequently. Shogunal domains have received support each time, and private domains have also received assistance, beginning with the Owari domain; but going forward, such arrangements will become difficult to maintain. Eleven post stations along the Nakasendō are suffering particular hardship. As they are all situated in mountain valleys, there are no auxiliary villages (sukegō) available to provide assistance. The eleven stations have been coordinating among themselves to share horses and porters, but since each station can barely manage to provide twenty-five horses and twenty-five persons for its own quota, they say it is impossible to assist other stations. I therefore consulted with Lord Kumi Inaba-no-kami, and after summoning the post station residents for inquiry, it was confirmed that, as stated above, due to the absence of auxiliary villages, it is impossible to provide more than the stipulated quota. I then consulted confidentially with the Senior Council (rōjū), and received instruction that this matter should be communicated privately to those who travel the Kisō Road. As your domains are large, you presumably have many retainers; please dispatch them in stages rather than all at once. Furthermore, even during your processions, please give consideration to the above horse and porter quotas when making your arrangements. This is not being issued as a strict command, but is being communicated privately to each of you. There is also no need to report this to the duty official (goyōban).

　The above was communicated to the Edo domain representatives of: the (Fukui) domain (Lord Wakasa-no-kami), Lord Matsudaira Aki-no-kami, Lord Kanamori Izumo-no-kami, Lord ■■■, Lord Tōyama Izumi-no-kami, and Lord Kutsuki Kenmotsu.

◦ Written notice passed from shogunal officials to domain representatives (rusuigumi)

**Memorandum**

Sanjūsangen-dō Hall was destroyed by fire some years ago, and a replacement site was granted in Fukagawa. Lumber and other materials were also given to the hall keeper Kyūemon; however, as the construction of the hall by his own means alone is impossible, he has petitioned to conduct a fundraising campaign (kanjin). Since the hall serves as a facility for archery practice, Lord Yanagisawa Dewa-no-kami and Senior Councilor Lord Matsudaira Ukyō-no-daibu have already contributed funds. Accordingly, it is considered appropriate that lords of ten thousand koku or more should also donate funds to Kyūemon. The specific amounts are noted in the attached document below. The above.
　　　Fourth month.
