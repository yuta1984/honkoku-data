**[Continuation on Coastal Defense]**

Furthermore, regarding areas beyond small hills and elevated terrain, arrangements should be made in advance with neighboring domains to dispatch reinforcements, and signals and procedures for all contingencies should be established in advance. The overriding priority should be genuine practical readiness. As far as possible, unnecessary expenditure should be avoided; locally stationed samurai, farmer-soldiers, and the like should be employed as suits each domain's circumstances, and the establishment of a permanent, lasting defense should be the primary concern.

This is because the foreign barbarians come by ship, and when conditions become unfavorable to them, they may move on to a neighboring location. Japan is a nation surrounded on all four sides by ocean, and since there are no fixed locations where they might appear, it is impossible to predict when or where they might arrive. Thus, if coastal defenses are maintained in ordinary times, there should be no cause for national embarrassment. Since these foreign peoples are not from a single nation but from many Western countries, if ships from these various nations begin arriving in an unceasing stream, the burden of responding to them will lead to exhaustion, and this will in turn lead to the impoverishment of the country. It is therefore desirable that every region maintain permanent defensive preparations. However, as has been repeatedly said before, even if such preparations appear formidable, if they lack practical effectiveness while incurring enormous costs—and if the domain becomes impoverished as a result—this will itself become a source of discord among the people. With this in mind, one should take care that, in the name of "thorough preparations," one does not become fixated merely on outward appearances while in reality driving the country into poverty, and, worse still, ending up with preparations that lack genuine practical utility. One should simply disregard outward appearances and make every effort to establish defenses that are genuinely practical and permanent.

When it is judged that the Western nations covet our country, it will not be possible to counter their superior force unless the entire country mobilizes its combined strength for defense. Therefore, in the event of an emergency, neighboring domains should thoroughly coordinate arrangements to combine their forces and provide mutual assistance. Throughout all of Japan, regardless of rank or social class, if the foreign barbarians were to commit disrespectful and unlawful acts that defile the national dignity, who would fail to be angered? If all understand that the intent is to resist with the combined strength of the entire nation of Japan, then the lords will not forget their duty as the nation's protectors; the bannermen (hatamoto), their men, and the retainers (gokenin) will fulfill their service at the seat of government; farmers will do their part as farmers; townspeople will do their part as townspeople—each contributing according to their station, exerting their utmost effort in their respective roles. If all earnestly understand that this constitutes repayment of the national benevolence they have enjoyed for two hundred years, it will correspond to the intent of exhausting the full strength of the entire nation. Therefore, all parties along the coast should exert their strength in unity and cooperation. Furthermore, since the impoverishment of individual domains is a matter of grave concern, it is essential to exercise ingenuity so that preparations are simple yet sufficient in practical terms. One should not be bound solely by the phrase "thorough preparations" in the proclamation, nor incur wasteful expenses on outward appearances alone; rather, practical and lasting defense should be the foremost priority. It should also be noted that, depending on circumstances, an inspector may be dispatched for the sake of the people's peace of mind to observe the state of preparations within each domain. This should be communicated and explained in advance to the various houses accordingly.

**[On Prohibiting Crowding During New Year Visits and Ceremonial Calls]**

During New Year courtesy calls and other ceremonial occasions when rounds of visits are made to the Senior Councillors (rōjū) and Junior Councillors (wakadoshiyori), large numbers of spectators have been gathering in front of the gates, causing considerable congestion. Henceforth, such occurrences are to be prevented. Should spectators be found present, the officials on duty may question and record their names and identities, and all parties should be aware of this in advance. The above is to be proclaimed accordingly. Twelfth Month.

**[On Prohibiting Spectating and Mounted Entry at Fires]**

Regarding fires: it has long been proclaimed that persons who merely come to watch should not appear, and that those on horseback should not ride into the vicinity of the fire source or the scene of the blaze. Nevertheless, people continue to gather near the fire, obstructing firefighting efforts. Therefore, all parties shall strictly adhere to the previously issued proclamation, and household retainers down to the lowest ranks shall be firmly instructed to commit no violations. The above is to be proclaimed accordingly. Twelfth Month.

**[On the Memorial Service at Nikkō-san]**

A memorial service (hōji) is to be conducted at Nikkō-san.

Fourth Month:
- 13th Day: Opening Day
- 14th Day: Middle Day
- 15th Day: Concluding Day

During the memorial service at Nikkō-san this Fourth Month, as has been customary, formal attendance on ceremony days and internal gatherings (uchi-yoriai) shall be held. Needless to say, interrogation under torture, hand-shackles, confinement, or the application of restraining ropes shall be dispensed with during this period.

- Item: During the memorial service, construction work, musical performances, festivals, and other religious services need not be suspended.
