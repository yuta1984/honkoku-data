When the clansman Ōtanda Tsunetaka and others arrived leading two thousand troops, Yoshisada was startled and overjoyed, saying, "How could you have come so quickly?" Tsunetaka replied, "Some days ago, a man traveled through Echigo announcing that you had received an imperial command and raised the banner of righteousness, urging all to come with haste. And so we shook off our sleeves and rushed here. The rest of our clansmen will follow in succession—please wait a little while." Shortly thereafter, five thousand men from the various Minamoto branches of Echigo, Kai, and Shinano gathered, and all regarded this as a divine turn of events. Furthermore, warriors from Musashi and Shimotsuke—Oyama Hidetomo from Shimotsuke and Chiba Sadatane from Shimōsa—along with warriors from neighboring provinces gathered without prior arrangement, and the army swelled to over twenty thousand men, its spirit instantly reviving. Takatoki's generals, Sakurada Sadakuni and Nagasaki Takashige, led a great army to intercept them, and at the Iruma River they fought Yoshisada's forces, clashing over thirty times in a single day. As dusk fell, both sides withdrew. The following day they fought again at Kumegawa, where Yoshisada's forces dealt them a crushing defeat. Takatoki then dispatched his younger brother Yasaie and others as reinforcements. Yoshisada, unaware of their arrival, was caught off guard before dawn, suffered a severe defeat, and withdrew his forces.

At Horigane, Miura Yoshikatsu of Sagami came and submitted to Yoshisada. Yoshisada was greatly pleased and treated him with courtesy, asking him for his counsel: "By what strategy might we safely achieve victory?" Yoshikatsu replied, "When two armies face each other, what certainty is there in victory or defeat? Yet the momentum from beginning to end will surely follow where Heaven's will points. Why do you doubt?" Yoshisada said, "My forces are exhausted and theirs are spirited—I fear it will be difficult to withstand them." Yoshikatsu said, "When an army wins and its general becomes arrogant and its soldiers grow complacent, this is the very reason Xiang Liang was swiftly defeated. Yesterday I observed the enemy: grown accustomed to their recent victory, their arrogance and complacency are extreme, and their northern commander is about to follow in Xiang's footsteps. I guarantee your victory in tomorrow's battle. Allow me to test them in one engagement." Yoshisada agreed. The following morning, Yoshikatsu furled his banners and advanced slowly. The enemy, thinking that Miura men had come to join their side, was at ease and made no preparations. Yoshikatsu then suddenly raised a battle cry and pressed forward, while Yoshisada advanced simultaneously from three directions.
