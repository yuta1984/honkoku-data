The day on which (Yoshisada) raised troops in Kōzuke was the eighth day of the fifth month. The day on which Takauji attacked Rokuhara was the seventh day of the same month. Yet Takauji memorialized (the throne), saying: "Your subject raised troops only after hearing that the rebels in the capital had been defeated." But when east and west are separated by more than eight hundred ri, how could he possibly have learned of this within the space of a single day? His false accusation is thus self-evident. This is his first crime.

The day that Yoshiakira entered Kamakura was the third day of the sixth month. The day that I, your subject (Yoshisada), annihilated the rebel forces was the twenty-first day of the fifth month. Yet to falsely accuse your subject of having relied upon that young and feeble one (Yoshiakira) in order to establish his achievements — this is his second crime.

At the very beginning of the recovery of the capital, without obtaining imperial sanction, he arbitrarily (dealt with) the Prince, and beyond the scope of his jurisdiction he freely applied punishments to those beneath the wheels (of his carriages, i.e., during his march). This is his third crime.

The great rebellion having just been pacified, with the border regions not yet settled, the imperial prince (Prince Morinaga) had been commanded to open a headquarters in the eastern marches in order to pacify them. Yet Takauji, while outwardly pretending to assist and support him, inwardly harbored ambitions of usurpation. This is his fourth crime.

He styled himself "Governor-General of the Eastern Provinces," abandoned the laws and ordinances of the court, raised troops to dispense private favors, gnawed away at the people (like a woodworm) and amassed great profits by exploiting the multitudes. The portents of his insubordination and rebellion are plain for all to see. This is his fifth crime.

Although the flourishing of the Restoration was truly brought about by the sagely virtue (of the Emperor), the plans by which disorder was quelled and the rightful order restored largely originated with Prince Morinaga. Yet Takauji, by every conceivable means, fabricated slanders and ultimately caused the Prince to be condemned and exiled. This is his sixth crime.

In the matter of the Prince's demotion, the Sacred Will was solely that he undergo self-reproach and self-correction; yet Takauji seized upon this to indulge his private designs, confining the Prince in a dungeon. This is his seventh crime.

Ashikaga Tadayoshi, pressed by the rebels (Tokiyuki's forces), abandoned Kamakura and fled; yet rather than striking at the enemy pursuing him, he instead sent men to put the Prince to the sword. There is no greater act of treason than this. Though the matter has not yet become officially known, all who walk the roads are aware of it. This is his eighth crime.

Now these eight treasons are such as Heaven cannot tolerate and Earth cannot bear. If they are set aside and left unpunished, the bonds of human morality will surely perish. I humbly beseech (Your Majesty) to cast Your illuminating gaze upon this matter and, following the imperial edict, punish Takauji, Tadayoshi, and their fellow traitors."

This written memorial was sent down for deliberation by the court nobles. The Sangi (Consultant) Fujiwara no Kiyotada memorialized, comparing the two memorials: "The fault (wrong) lies with Takauji.
