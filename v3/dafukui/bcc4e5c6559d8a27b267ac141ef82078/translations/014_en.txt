"…muster the troops of the province; go you (Yoshisada) to that place (Echizen) and raise great forces in the northern lands to plan the restoration once more. However, once I have entered the capital, you will in turn bear the name of rebel. Therefore, I now transmit the throne to the Crown Prince, and entrust him to your care as you depart. In all matters, great or small, make your decisions in accordance with the imperial decree. Regard the Crown Prince as you would regard me." When the Emperor had finished speaking, he wept, and all present were deeply moved and withdrew in tears.

That very night, Yoshisada went to the Hiyoshi Shrine and offered this prayer: "O divine spirit, if you have power, grant that I may raise my forces once more and destroy the rebels. If this cannot be accomplished in my own lifetime, then let it surely be accomplished by my descendants." He then placed a treasured sword on the altar of the shrine.

The following day, the Emperor returned to the capital. Yoshisada, escorting the Crown Prince and Prince Takanaga, made haste toward the northern provinces. Upon reaching Shiotsu, the enemy blocked the road ahead, so they detoured via the Kinome Pass. The weather brought cold rains, the sky was freezing, and everyone was forced to sleep in the open. Some removed their armor and huddled together to endure the cold; others burned their bows and arrows to warm themselves. Many froze to death. Kōno Michiharu and Tōno Michikoto encountered the enemy and fell in battle without being able to fight effectively; Chiba Sadatane, driven to extremity, surrendered to the enemy. After struggling through difficult terrain for three days, Yoshisada finally reached Tsuruga. The local lord Kebii Ujiharu welcomed him into Kanasaki Castle.

Yoshisada dispatched his eldest son Yoshiaki to Echigo and Yoshisuke to Somayama, sending them separately to recruit troops from the various provinces. When Ashikaga Takatsune came and surrounded Yoshisuke, Yoshiaki turned back from the road and routed the enemy with a surprise attack. One day, the snow cleared, and they put boats on the water near the shore; while paying respect to the Crown Prince, the assembled ministers each took up instruments and performed music. Just then, a fish leaped into the boat; Fujiwara no Saneyō, citing the precedent from the time of King Wu of Zhou (a propitious omen), had it split open and offered it as a sacrifice to Heaven. All were overjoyed, and the gathering came to a close.

Takauji again ordered his generals to dispatch a large force to attack the castle. The castle soldiers fired arrows and hurled stones down from the heights and drove the enemy back; the enemy was unable to draw near. In the twelfth month, the Emperor made his progress to Yoshino and sent Watari Tadakage bearing an imperial edict, instructing Yoshisada to plan the restoration of imperial rule.

In the spring of the second year, Wakiya Yoshiharu came with troops to relieve the castle, but was defeated by the enemy. From this point, both provisions and reinforcements were completely cut off. Everyone gathered fish and seaweed to stave off hunger, but there was not enough. They also slaughtered horses for food. When the snow melted and the roads reopened, the enemy gathered day by day until they numbered nearly one hundred thousand. Yoshisada secretly crossed over the castle walls with Yoshisuke and Saneyō and hurried to Somayama, intending to assemble troops and devise a plan to drive back the enemy.
