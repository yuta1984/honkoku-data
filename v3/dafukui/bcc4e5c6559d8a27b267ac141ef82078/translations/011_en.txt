At the time of departure, the army had been proclaimed as one hundred thousand strong, but by this point losses had reduced it to fewer than twenty thousand. A courier was dispatched at full speed to report this to the court, and the entire court was shaken with alarm. The court council ordered Kusunoki Masashige to come to their aid.

Yoshisada questioned Masashige on the court's deliberations and on military strategy, and also said: "I know full well how extremely difficult it is to resist the enemy's crack troops with our exhausted soldiers. Yet on the eastern campaign in former years I allowed the rebels to run rampant, and now again on this western campaign I have not managed to capture so much as a single castle, and must withdraw at the mere rumor of the enemy's great army — what will people say of me? Therefore, heedless of defeat or victory, I intend to remain here and fight to the death." Masashige replied: "To advance when you see the opportunity, and to withdraw when the time calls for it — this is the way of an excellent commander. Why should you trouble your heart over idle talk from the world at large? In the year just past, Your Excellency executed (the great fool), and this spring you drove off the cunning rebel (Takauji). Even if it be said that the Sacred Fortune brought this about, can it truly be said it was not due to Your Excellency's magnificent strategy? In the subtleties of military affairs, who could venture to find fault?" Yoshisada's heart was relieved, and together they conversed and held a banquet that continued until nearly dawn. The next morning, he assigned positions to the various divisions of the army, and himself held Wada to meet the enemy's naval forces. The enemy's banners and pennants by the hundreds of thousands obscured the sky, and their great and small warships covered the sea; our army, beholding this, was entirely dispirited.

Troops from Sanuki landed directly and advanced on Tsunoshima, but Yoshisuke struck and routed them. Hosokawa Jōzen then directed his ships toward Konbe-no-ura. While Yoshisada and Yoshisuke and the others were blocking them along the marsh-shore, Takauji's great army seized the opportunity to land from Wada-saki, and Tadayoshi came from Suma as well. Masashige, assailed by the enemy on front and back, lost his entire army.

Yoshisada gazed upon the enemy's flags and banners and said: "Those coming from Nishinomiya are all detachments (of Takauji's forces) — not the main body — and are not worth engaging. But those coming from Minatogawa are Takauji and his brother. I will personally take them on." He reversed his formation back to Ikuta-no-mori, divided his forces into three units, and fought with every last ounce of strength. But his numbers were too few, and he was at last defeated, commanding the rearguard as he withdrew. His horse stumbled, so he climbed to high ground to await the cavalry that followed. As the enemy came rushing together from all sides, Yoshisada shot and killed one man, while the remaining enemy soldiers hesitated and did not advance. He then loosed arrows in a storm-like volley in all directions. Yoshisada's famed suit of armor was called "Usugane," and his two prized swords were called "Kikō" and "Onimaru"; wearing that armor and wielding those swords, he cut down sixteen incoming arrows, his swiftness and agility godlike.
