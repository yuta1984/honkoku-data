"Since ancient times, the Minamoto and Taira clans have alternately served as protectors of the imperial court, with their fortunes rising and falling in succession. Our house, bearing the distinguished name of the Minamoto, has long been subject to the rule of the Taira of Kamakura (the Hojo clan), but this was never our true intention—we were merely carried along by circumstance. In recent days, Takatoki's crimes and wickedness have reached their limit, and his ruin is imminent. I shall now raise the banner of righteousness: to pacify the imperial house above, and to restore our family's honor below. However, since (the Emperor's authority) has already been weakened, we cannot act without receiving an imperial command. I have heard that Prince Daitō is hiding in the mountains nearby. Go and devise a means to obtain his decree."

Yoshisada then sent Yoshimasa, who by stratagem obtained (the decree). Its text was fashioned in imitation of an imperial rescript, reading: "To spread benevolent governance and rule the ten thousand nations is the virtue of an enlightened sovereign; to quell disorder and pacify the four seas is the duty of a military retainer. In recent years, Hojo Takatoki has held the court's laws in contempt, fanning the flames of treachery, and his accumulated wickedness has reached such a degree that Heaven's punishment is about to descend upon him. If one were to raise the banner of righteousness to set the Emperor's heart at ease, the imperial gratitude would be exceedingly profound, and the reward would be by no means slight. Swiftly devise a strategy for the punitive expedition and achieve the effect of peace and tranquility."

Yoshisada was greatly pleased. Feigning illness, he led his troops back to Kōzuke Province, where he gathered the young men of his clan to deliberate on raising an uprising. At that very time, Takatoki had dispatched a large army westward under the command of his younger brother Yasaie, levying provisions from the eastern provinces. Because Serada was known for its many wealthy families, it was specially assessed at sixty million (kan), with a strict deadline of five days. Yoshisada, in anger, executed two of the officials and had their heads displayed. Takatoki was furious and halted the westward advance, turning his forces around to attack Yoshisada. Yoshisada thereupon made his resolve, raised the banner of righteousness before the Ikushina Shrine, read the imperial decree aloud three times, and advanced his forces to Kasagake-no (the field of Kasagake). His clansmen and retainers—Ōdate Muneuji and his sons Yukiuji, Ujiaki, and Ujikane; Horiguchi Sadamitsu and his younger brother Yukiyoshi; Iwamatu Tsuneuji; Satomi Yoshitane; Eda Yukiyoshi; Momoi Naokata—numbered barely one hundred and fifty men, and as dusk fell they headed toward Echigo...
