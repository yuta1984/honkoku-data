(Yoshisada) again led his generals to Hyōgo, where he defeated Tadayoshi at Toyohara-kawara and Hosokawa Kazuuji at Segawa. Takauji thereupon boarded a ship and fled westward. Yoshisada received the submission of those who surrendered and joined his side, then withdrew, and for his achievements was appointed Middle Captain of the Left Imperial Guard (Sakon'e Chūjō).

After remaining there for about a month, many troops across the Sanyō and San'in regions rose up in support of Takauji. The Emperor issued a decree placing Yoshisada in command of military affairs across the sixteen provinces of Sanyō and San'in, ordering him to march and suppress them. However, illness struck at an inopportune moment and he was unable to depart. He first dispatched Eda Yukiyoshi and Ōtachi Ujiaki and others to set out ahead. When his illness cleared he took to the road, and halting at the Kakogawa in Harima, he assembled a force of sixty thousand and advanced to the post station of Ikaruga. There he deliberated over attacking Akamatsu Norimura's (Enshin's) Shirahatajō Castle. Norimura feigned surrender, and Yoshisada, believing him, spent over ten days shuttling back and forth relaying the court's intentions. In the meantime, Norimura completed his defenses in full, and then spoke in contemptuous terms disparaging the court. Yoshisada was furious and pressed the attack day and night, determined to take the castle, but it was too strongly fortified to fall.

He therefore detached Yoshisuke (Wakiya Yoshisuke) together with Eda Yukiyoshi and Ōida Ujitsune and others to attack Funasaka-yama. Using Kojima Takanori of Bingo Province as their guide, they attacked from front and rear simultaneously and took it. Yoshisuke advanced to lay siege to Mitsuishi, Yukiyoshi seized Mimasaka, and Ujitsune entered Fukuyama in Bitchū.

Before long, Takauji and Tadayoshi came advancing eastward by both land and sea. Tadayoshi first attacked Fukuyama; Ujitsune withdrew and took up a defensive position at Mitsuishi. Yoshisuke sent a courier racing to report the defeat to Yoshisada. Yoshisada replied: "In the present situation, if we try to block the land forces, the naval forces will immediately bypass us and strike at the Imperial palace. It is better to withdraw our army, place the Imperial capital at our back, give battle once, and thereby check both the land and naval forces together. Break off the siege of Mitsuishi and come join us." He also sent a courier racing to Mimasaka to recall Eda Yukiyoshi, who personally lifted the siege of Shirahatajō and withdrew, encamping to the west of the Kakogawa for two days while awaiting the arrival of Yoshisuke and Yukiyoshi.

It happened to be the rainy season and the river was running high. His men urged that they cross first, as the enemy was pressing close. Yoshisada said: "That the enemy presses upon us while our crossing is not yet complete — this is precisely what is called 'deploying with one's back to the water': the very stratagem by which the Marquis of Huaiyin (Han Xin) engineered his remarkable victory."
