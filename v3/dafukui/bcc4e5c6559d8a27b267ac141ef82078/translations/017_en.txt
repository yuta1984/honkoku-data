**Eulogy (賛)**

Alas! Can the principle ordained by Heaven be easily altered or treated with negligence? The foolish look upon Heaven and see only vast obscurity; the wise look upon Heaven and see clear brilliance. Those who see obscurity view Heaven through the lens of immediate circumstance; those who see brilliance view Heaven through the perspective of the long passage of time. When one views Heaven through the long passage of time, its decrees become evident; when one views it through immediate circumstance, errors may arise. Therefore, the foolish are ever remiss toward Heaven, while the wise are ever reverent toward it. Those who are reverent preserve their blessings well, while those who are remiss ultimately receive their punishment. This is the natural principle of the Heavenly Way, and it is also the very reason why the wise and the foolish are distinguished from one another.

Consider the absolute loyalty and self-sacrificing devotion of the Left Imperial Guard Commander (Nitta Yoshisada): he ultimately died without having fulfilled his aspirations, and his entire clan was exterminated as enemies of the state. Indeed, Heaven seemed truly obscure and unfathomable. Yet after the passage of several hundred years, the one who laid the foundation for the great unification of the realm emerged from the very lineage of the Nitta clan. Then who truly gained and who truly lost? The glory and decline, the prominence and obscurity of a single moment in time, must surely be insufficient matter for argument. O Left Imperial Guard Commander — can your aggrieved spirit not now be consoled on this very day?

Furthermore, the sincerity of the Commander's prayers to the gods has at last been answered, and the fact that our Enlightened Sovereign (the Tokugawa lord) aided in the founding and establishment of this temple is truly a noble ceremony of commending loyalty. Who could say that the Heavenly Way is without wisdom, and that it may be treated with negligence?
