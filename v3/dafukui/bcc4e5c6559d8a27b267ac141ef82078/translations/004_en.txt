Yoshisada and Yoshisuke proceeded from Kesōzaka and Horiguchi; Sadamitsu and Ōshima Moriyuki proceeded from Kōfukurozaka; Ōdate Muneuji and Eda Yukiyoshi proceeded from Gokurakujizaka—all directing their march toward Kamakura. At this time, news of the defeat of the forces in the capital first reached Kamakura, and when it was further heard that our army was drawing steadily nearer, the entire seat of government was thrown into turmoil and on the verge of collapse. The shogunate therefore dispatched all its troops to seize strategic positions and resist Yoshisada. They fought in close battle for three days and nights; Yoshisada's forces pressed into Yamauchi, but Ōdate Muneuji was defeated and killed in action. Upon hearing this, Yoshisada quickly drew out his finest cavalry and, under cover of night, took a shortcut through the lanes between Katase and Koshigoe and hurried forward. The enemy held the steep slopes to the north, had erected palisades connecting to the shoreline in the south, and had arrayed warships upon the sea—there was no route through. Yoshisada then dismounted, removed his helmet, faced the sea, and prayed in silence: "Today, the Son of Heaven wanders in exile in the western lands because of lawless traitors. I, your subject Yoshisada, have dared to take up arms, venture deep into enemy territory, and have sworn to pacify this national crisis and demonstrate my loyal sincerity. I humbly entreat you: may the Dragon King of the Sea look upon the sincerity of your subject's heart, cause the ocean tides to recede, and open a path for the army to advance." He then cast the golden sword he wore at his side into the sea. By dawn, the tide had suddenly ebbed for several leagues; the warships all drifted away, and the sandy flats lay broad and level, passable for the army. Yoshisada, moved and overjoyed, declared: "The Grand Duke Wang's striking the mountain to bring forth a spring, and Empress Jingū's casting her jewel to repel the tides—these were marvels of divine power from the distant past, yet today I have witnessed such a thing with my own eyes." He then raised a great shout, rallied his men, and led them into the seat of government. The enemy had no time to mount a defense. Just then, a fierce wind blew in off the sea, and fire was set to the houses along the shore; in an instant it spread throughout the entire city, smoke and flames filling the heavens. Warriors and women alike wandered in confusion, crying out in terror, while Yoshisada's forces pressed the attack with the momentum of the blaze. Takatoki fled to Kasaiyatsu and took his own life; his clansmen and commanders either fell in battle or committed suicide, and all were annihilated. Fifteen days after the army had set out, Kamakura was completely pacified. Immediately three messengers were dispatched at a gallop to report the victory to the imperial encampment.
