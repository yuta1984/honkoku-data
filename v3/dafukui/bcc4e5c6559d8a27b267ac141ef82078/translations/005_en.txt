...some urged waiting (for news that) the Kantō (had been pacified), but the Emperor did not heed this counsel and set his palanquin in motion. Upon reaching Hyōgo, the victory dispatch happened to arrive. The Emperor was greatly delighted and bestowed official ranks upon the messengers according to their grade, and from afar conferred upon Yoshisada the post of Samanosuke (Assistant Director of the Left Stables). Yoshisada remained in Kamakura, putting down the remaining enemies, pacifying those who had newly submitted, and his prestige and renown shone throughout the Eight Provinces of the Kantō, so that there was no hero or man of power who did not hasten to listen to his commands.

In the first year of Kenmu (1334), (Yoshisada) came to court, was promoted to Junior Fourth Rank Lower Grade, appointed Sahyōe no Kami (Commander of the Left Gate Guards), concurrently held the post of Harima no Kami (Governor of Harima), and administered the governorships of Kōzuke and Harima.

Now, at the time of Yoshisada's pacification of Kamakura, Ashikaga Takauji's son Yoshiakira was still young and had accompanied the army in the field. When the rebellion was suppressed, those attending upon Yoshiakira sought to attribute the merit to him. Meanwhile, Takauji, then enjoying imperial favor, made use of a favored consort (to slander) Prince Morinaga. The Emperor, failing to perceive the error, heeded (these words) and had the Prince seized and placed in the custody of Takauji's younger brother Tadayoshi, who imprisoned him in Kamakura. Moreover, since Yoshisada's achievements were great and his renown weighty, (Takauji) sought to eliminate him, but before he could act, in the second year (of Kenmu, 1335), Hōjō Tokiyuki raised a rebellion in the Kantō. Tadayoshi killed the Prince and fled. The Emperor, not yet aware of this, ordered Takauji to suppress Tokiyuki. Takauji thereupon took up position in Kamakura and rebelled, seizing in their entirety the estates held in the Kantō by Yoshisada's kinsmen, and further erected boards on stakes to publicly enumerate Yoshisada's crimes, dispatching urgent missives to the provinces to recruit soldiers for the purpose of attacking Yoshisada. Yoshisada likewise confiscated all the Ashikaga clan's landholdings within his jurisdiction, and submitted a memorial to the throne setting forth Takauji's treasonous conduct, which read as follows:

"The meritorious achievements by which the royal forces accomplished the restoration of imperial rule include, to the south, Kusunoki Masashige, and to the west, Akamatsu Enshin. Beyond these, the righteous armies of each province all rose up with valor to confront the enemy. Yet Ashikaga Takauji, as a mere wandering traveler, had followed the rebels (the Hōjō) and resisted the royal forces; and only when he had already seen the imperial armies riding the momentum of victory did he gradually begin to devise his own salvation, all the while maintaining an ambiguous, fence-sitting posture. It was only after the defeat and destruction of Nayose Takaire that he at last proceeded to Tanba to publicly declare his submission. Had the righteous banner (Yoshisada's army) not achieved victory, and had the rebel generals not offered up their heads, would he alone have been willing to marshal his abilities to stand against so formidable a foe? Yet despite the paltry nature of his contributions relative to the great rewards he has received, he instead envies the achievements of others and seeks to deceive the sacred imperial ear in order to destroy the loyal and the good. Looking at his words alone, his true intentions are plain to see. I, your subject (Yoshisada)..."
