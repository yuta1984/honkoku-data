(Continuing from the previous folio) ...Matsuda Shōgen turned back, raised his voice and announced his name in challenge, and held the enemy at bay for a time in battle; but realizing the situation was hopeless, he folded up the front skirts of his armor (kusazuri), slashed open his belly, and fell forward upon the point of his sword. In that moment's opening, Harima-no-kami galloped across the Katsuragawa river and headed off along the Tamba road. It was all a most disgraceful affair. In this battle, which began at the Hour of the Dragon (approximately 7–9 a.m.) and lasted until the end of the Hour of the Serpent (approximately 11 a.m.), five hundred and seventy-three soldiers from Harima Province — men known throughout the western provinces as formidable warriors — fell in battle, and from the Ukon-no-baba, the Suzume-no-mori, and the foot of Nijō, all the way to the vicinity of Umezu and Katsura, there was not a single foot of ground left open, so covered was it with the bodies of men and horses. Among the Kyoto forces as well, more than one hundred and seventy men fell — beginning with Akamatsu Uma-no-suke [marginal note: "later referred to as Uma-no-kami"], Togashi, and Tomita — valiant warriors among the lords and their retinues. The second battle at the Uchino approach was now over.

Ōmiya, and Lord Kazusa-no-suke, Kobayashi, and all the others have been killed at the hands of the Ōuchi forces; the remaining troops have scattered in all directions and their whereabouts are unknown—" before he could finish his words, he choked with tears. Upon hearing this, Mutsu-no-kami said: "All along, they kept warning me against this plan, yet having concluded in their own minds that this scheme could never succeed, they nevertheless agreed to join it and died in battle — I understand it now. How shameful this is for me." With that, he and the more than one thousand horsemen accompanying him pressed forward to Sanjō-bōmon Ōmiya, raised the battle cry three times, and charged. Ōuchi Gondaifu had been holding his position at his original encampment on the south side of the Jingikan forest, facing Ōmiya avenue. Akamatsu Kazusa-no-suke Yoshinori, with some thirteen hundred horsemen, advanced his great banner bearing the pine (matsu) emblem at Nijō-Inokuma and declared: "This morning the Ōuchi forces bore the brunt of the fighting and shattered the enemy. Our force is the reinforcing fresh troops — we shall be first into battle!" — and advanced southward from Nijō.
