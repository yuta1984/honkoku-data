**[Right folio]**

(Continuing from the previous folio) ...they rode out toward the Great Ōmiya avenue west of Kasuga. The troops of Harima-no-kami — the Tsuchiya party — saw this and charged in riding abreast, but the Tsuchiya party was worsted and appeared to fall back, scattering southward. As the Sasaki forces crushed them in a sudden close-quarters assault, fifty-three men — beginning with Ōashi Jirō-zaemon-no-jō, the same Heiji-zaemon-no-jō, the same Kunai-zaemon-no-jō, the same Jirō-uemon-no-jō, Seki Heizō, Koma Dewa-no-kami, Honbu Ōmi-no-kami, the same Jirō-uemon-no-jō, Yonehara Heikurō, and the same Heigurō — lay down their pillows side by side at Reizei-nishi-no-Ōmiya, repaying their lord's benevolence in the depths of the underworld. Meanwhile, Harima-no-kami, with only some thirty horsemen, had held his position at the Toyonawa approach, a little south of Nijō; but as warriors from the western provinces came galloping in from all directions until his force had grown to some two hundred horsemen, he once again raised a great battle cry and charged into the midst of the combined forces of the Hosokawa and Hatakeyama encampments, fighting with utter disregard for his life. As a result, the troops of Uemon-no-suke fell back and dispersed, withdrawing in the direction of Uchino. Since His Lordship the Shogun had staked the safety of the realm on the outcome of this battle, he commanded: "Advance the banner!"

**[Left folio]**

So commanding, He drew the "Futatsu-mei" sword from his belt, advanced to the very fore, and declared: "Cut down every last one of them — Mitsuyuki and all those who conspired in rebellion — not sparing a single man, hang their heads at Rokujō-kawara, and put an end henceforth to the treachery of our companions-in-arms!" With this command, his mounted escort of over three thousand horsemen raised a tremendous battle cry and surged forward — whereupon a wondrous thing occurred. At the very moment the banner advanced westward, a flock of mountain doves came flying from the direction of the Kitano forest and wheeled about above the banner. Among them flew a pair of auspicious doves with tails of some two shaku in length, circling for a time before flying off in the direction of the southwest (the kun direction) over the encampment of Harima-no-kami. All who witnessed this were deeply moved in their faith, believing: "Surely Hachiman Daibosatsu and Kitano Tenmangū are manifesting this miraculous sign to drive away the wicked rebels!" — and all felt heartened and reassured. Then, at the very moment those doves flew away, Harima-no-kami was defeated and withdrew, heading toward Umezu. Near the Katsuragawa river he appeared on the very verge of being killed, but then...
