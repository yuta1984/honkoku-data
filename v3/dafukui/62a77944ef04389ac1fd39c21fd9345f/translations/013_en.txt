**[Right folio]**

Believing that the end had now truly come, Ōshū declared, "Let me cut open my belly in peace. Draw up the curtains." At the place along Oshikōji-Ōmiya where there was a small grove of bamboo, a curtain bearing the three-stripe *hikuryō* (三引両) crest was drawn up. However, Ōshū repositioned his horse to face north at a spot slightly north of Oshikōji where there was a small burial mound, and held his ground with just nine horsemen, master and retainers together. Kojirō held his position on the Inokuma road with seven horsemen.

Ōshū had been struck above his left eyebrow by a naginata, and blood poured into both his eyes; his vision darkened and his mind grew confused. Attempting to calm himself, he leaned forward onto the front saddle bow, and was in the process of urging his horse slightly forward when Isshiki Sakyō-no-daibu rode up and struck him two powerful blows with his sword "Doromaru," measuring four shaku and eight sun in length. How could he possibly endure this? As Ōshū fell to the left (the bow-hand side), Isshiki Umanokashira dismounted in close pursuit and, taking up his sword "Kunikatsu" — received from the Shogunal palace on this occasion — cut down onto Ōshū's right (sword-hand) shoulder as he attempted to rise, driving his knee down upon him and slicing through. The wound was grievous, and Ōshū could not rise again.

**[Left folio]**

He lay down with his head to the west. Sakyō-no-daibu also dismounted, struck at his helmet with one blow, readjusted his grip, and thrust into the inner helmet. Ōshū called out the nembutsu in a loud voice only once, and the scene was fearsome as if he were a demon or god.

Ujikiyo, said to have been forty-eight years of age, fell at Oshikōji-Ōmiya at the hands of Akinori (詮範) and his son, and was finally slain. Yamaguchi Danjō also died in battle at the same place. Of the seven horsemen accompanying Yamana Kojirō, five were killed and two disappeared without a trace. His horse was cut down and killed, and just as Kojirō was on the verge of being slain himself, Kano Heigo came galloping up from somewhere, immediately dismounted, and said "Please mount this horse," lifting Kojirō up onto it. As the enemy pressed in without a moment's gap, Kano called out repeatedly, "Kojirō-dono's foster-brother Kano Heigo is here!" — cut down two enemy horsemen — and then fell in battle himself. Seeing this, Kojirō also rode forward, intending to die in battle; but the faint sound of Ōshū's final nembutsu reached his ears, so he drove back the enemy he was engaged with and looked toward Ōshū — only to find that Ōshū had already been slain, and his severed head was being held aloft.
