**[Right folio]**

(Continuing from the previous folio) ...Yamana Kazusa-no-suke rode through and was looking about for a way to charge into the Gosho encampment as far as Nakamikado-Ōmiya, when Settsu Samanosuke, Tominaga Sakon-no-shōgen, and some fourteen or fifteen of the Omamawari (bodyguard retinue), totaling around twenty horsemen, dismounted and attempted to scale the old earthen wall along Ōmiya. However, their horses had already sustained wounds and were in a state no better than fallen leaves mixed with frost dissolving in the morning sun, their footing uncertain; the horse slipped off the embankment, and Kazusa-no-suke tumbled straight down to the left (bow) side without error. He could not rise. Tominaga Sakon-no-shōgen came rushing over and drove two and three successive blows from beneath the kusazuri (armor skirt) into the inner face of his helmet — and so Yamana Kazusa-no-suke, for all his valor, was at last struck down at Nakamikado-Ōmiya.

〇 Meanwhile, Yamana Harima-no-kami Mitsuyuki had descended from Mineno-dō from the evening of the twenty-ninth day of the twelfth month, crossed the Katsuragawa River at midnight, established his encampment at Umezu, and was waiting for dawn to break.

**[Left folio]**

When day broke, at the Hour of the Rabbit on the last day of the month, it seemed that battle was underway around Nijō-Ōmiya — the battle cries were heard in great numbers from as far north as Reizei and Nakamikado down to the avenues of Sanjō and Shijō — and so (his men said): "Our advance from this direction (the western approach) is surely delayed. We must quickly coordinate with that force and press our attack through the western approach to the capital." They searched for the commander, Harima-no-kami, but he was nowhere to be found in the encampment; everyone's faces turned pale as they searched, yet he could not be found. It had been arranged that Ōashi Jirō Saemon-no-jō would lead the Tsuchiya party straight through Ninnaji and advance to the front of the torii of the Taishōgun-dō. The soldiers, finding Harima-no-kami nowhere to be seen, stood dumbfounded and at the ready — when two or three of the servants of Kazusa-no-suke and Shuri-no-suke came running up and reported: "Our side has lost the battle at Ōmiya; Lord Kazusa-no-suke and Lord Shuri-no-suke, along with many others, have been killed. It is plainly clear that our side has been defeated — why are you still lingering here like this?"
