Item: On the same day, regarding the same matter, the resident deputy Ōseki Shingorōzaemon (Naomi) brought and submitted the following written memorandum:

　　　　　　Memorandum of Oral Statement

　　　　　　Regarding the Kogitsune-maru sword previously submitted: as the Shogun's inspection has been completed, it has been returned, and we have received it back.

Item: Concerning Anaga Shrine in Echizen Province — regarding the history and records of its establishment through the invitation [of the divine spirit] of the Kasuga Myōjin, and whether among the court nobles and warrior families there is a tradition of devotion by members of the Fujiwara clan, and whether there are any objects or items transmitted from Daitō-an sub-temple of Kenninji in Kyoto to said shrine — these matters are not known from here [in Edo].

Item: Since said shrine is located within Anaga village, the local people customarily refer to it as "Awaga Shrine."

[Later annotation:] The term "軽事 (keiji / light/trivial matters)" — is this appropriate? Or might it be a miswritten character?
　　　　　　It reads "軽事." [Later annotation:] The character "軽 (○)" may be a mistake for "経 (○)" [meaning "경위/circumstances/history"].

The aforementioned Kasuga Shrine of Anaga Village, Asuwa District, Echizen Province, is situated to the northeast (艮, the ushitora direction) of the royal capital [Kyoto], and it is transmitted that during the reign of Emperor Go-Reizei it was designated as a chokuganjo [imperially-vowed temple/shrine] to guard against evil from the demon gate [kimon, the inauspicious northeast direction].
