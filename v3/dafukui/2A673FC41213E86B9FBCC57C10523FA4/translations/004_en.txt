Item: On the eleventh day of the first month, as a congratulatory gift to mark the successful completion of the three-day New Year's ceremonial observances, a letter was dispatched to Edo, and one type of ceremonial dish was presented.

Item: On the twenty-first day of the same month, regarding the matter of servants and retainers (hōkōnin) within the domain, the following proclamation was issued by written notice:

　　　Ordinance

　　　First: Although it has long been prohibited by domain law to hire servants and retainers of the domain (go-kachū) at high wages, it has come to our attention that year after year there are those who seek higher wages, which is utterly unacceptable.
　　　　　Until now, because gold and silver exchange rates have been disordered, the situation was left as it was; however, since new gold coinage will come into circulation next spring, wages for servants should properly be set in accordance with the standards that prevailed when Keichō gold coinage was in use. Nevertheless, since servants have been in short supply in recent years, the matter has been left as is. At the time of the next change of service in spring, servants hired this spring (the Year of the Boar) shall be engaged at wages calculated on the basis of the old gold (inukin) equivalence. Should anyone hire servants at wages exceeding those permitted by the prohibition, it shall be deemed a transgression on the part of the master, and all parties should be aware of this.

　　　Second: Among the lower-ranking servants, there are those who, at the time of their change of service, falsely claim unavoidable business, or feign retirement (hikokomi-kamishimo), and go into hiding for a time, taking advantage of the shortage of servants to seek higher wages. Therefore, from the end of this year, the town magistrates (machi-bugyō) and district magistrates (kōri-bugyō) shall be so instructed, and from the coming first month, those who file notice of becoming merchants, or who claim retirement or withdrawal, shall — even if their parents, children, or siblings face consequences — for a period of five to seven days...
