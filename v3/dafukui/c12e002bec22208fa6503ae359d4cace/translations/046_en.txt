As the petition had been granted in accordance with the request, the Chief Elder (okaro), Ashida Zusho (Kenō), was dispatched as a messenger of thanks to Lord Sagami-no-kami.

Item: 23rd day of the 5th month — At the Great Abbot's Quarters (daihōjō) of Zōjō-ji Temple, the following memorial tablets enshrined therein:

　The memorial tablet of Jōkō-in-sama, with an offering stipend of 3 persons' rations (ninfuchi);

　The memorial tablet of Ryūhō-in-sama, likewise 2 persons' rations; the memorial tablet of Keiju-in-sama, likewise 1 person's rations;
　together with 2 silver pieces for the water-offering (mizumuke) ceremony in the 7th month, to be offered every year henceforth, and additionally 1 person's rations as stipend for the officiating monk (sōsōryō) —
　the above provisions were so decreed. The said memorial tablets were restored on this occasion, and new altar cabinets (zushi) and implements placed before the tablets were newly commissioned. Furthermore, a record of the circumstances of the enshrinement of the memorial tablets was composed in Chinese (kanbun) by Ten'yō-in and submitted, and has accordingly been inscribed and kept within the altar cabinet.

[Record composed by Ten'yō-in, in Chinese:]
　　　In the 2nd year of Kan'ei, the year of Kinoto-Ushi [1625], the Lord of Echizen, Ryūhō-kō, reverently enshrined the spirit tablet of his predecessor lord, the Chūnagon [Middle Counsellor] Lord Minamoto, at Zōjō-ji Temple in the Eastern Capital, so that incense and fire might be offered in perpetuity across the generations. The said lord's personal name (imina) was Hideyasu.
