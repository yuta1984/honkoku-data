　　　　　　　　Matsudaira Ogimaru-dono

　The aforementioned letter of acknowledgment (o-uke-josho) was dispatched to Lord Sagami-no-kami via the messenger Hayashi Chūdayū ■■, an o-tsukai-ban (messenger official).
　　　Note: The aforementioned letter of acknowledgment bears no official seal (han-gyō), as [the domain lord] is of young age.

　As the aforementioned petition had been granted as requested, the Senior Elder (o-karō) Ashida Zushо Yoshinō was dispatched as a messenger to Lord Sagami-no-kami to convey the domain's gratitude.

One entry: 23rd day of the 5th month. Regarding the memorial tablets (ihai) enshrined in the Great Abbot's Quarters (daihōjō) of Zōjō-ji Temple:
　A stipend of three persons' rations (ninfuchi) was granted as an offering for the memorial tablet of Lady Jōkō-in;
　likewise two persons' rations for the memorial tablet of Lady Ryūhō-in; and one person's rations for the memorial tablet of Lady Keiju-in;
　furthermore, two silver coins (hakugin) per year henceforth as an offering for the water memorial ceremony (mizumuke) in the 7th month; and additionally, one person's rations as stipend for the officiating monk (kusō).
　The above was decreed. The said memorial tablets were restored on this occasion, and new fittings were ordered for the miniature shrines (zushi) housing them as well as the ritual implements placed before the tablets. Furthermore, an account of the circumstances of the enshrinement of the memorial tablets, composed in classical Chinese (kanbun) by Ten'yō-in, was submitted and has been recorded and kept within the miniature shrine.

　　　In the year Kan'ei 2 [1625], the cyclical year kinoto-ushi (乙丑), the lord of Echizen (Etsuko), reverently enshrining the memorial tablet of his late predecessor — the Chūnagon [Middle Councillor], Lord Minamoto — placed it at Zōjō-ji Temple in the Eastern Capital (Edo), and commanded that incense and offerings be made there for generations. The lord's personal name (imina) was Hideyasu.
