Item: On the 14th day of the fourth month, at Nikkō-zan,
the hundredth memorial service for Lord Taiyū-in (Tokugawa Iemitsu) was conducted. As today was the middle day of the service,
in order to inquire after their well-being,
a messenger — the castle keeper (go-rusui) Katō Chōuemon Masatoshi — was dispatched to: the duty senior official (go-yōban) Lord Sakai Uemon-no-jō Tadayori; the Senior Councilor attached to the Ōgosho, Lord Nishio Oki-no-kami Tadanao; and the Senior Councilor attached to the Dainagon, Lord Akimoto Tajima-no-kami Suzutomo.

Item: On the 18th day of the same month, the customary gift of fish (o-sakana) for the end-of-abstinence celebration (o-shōjin-age) was not presented.

　　　Note: As this fell during the period of the hundredth memorial service for Lord Taiyū-in, an inquiry had been made on the 13th to the acting duty official (go-yōban-dai) Lord Matsudaira Ukon-no-shōgen, and the response received was that there was no need to present such a gift.

Item: On the 20th day of the same month,
as the Kubō-sama (the Shogun) made a visit to Tōei-zan (Kan'ei-ji temple), after his return, a letter was sent for the first time from the inner household (go-naishō) to the senior ladies-in-waiting (rōjochū) of the Main Keep (Honmaru), to inquire after the Shogun's well-being.
　　　Note: On the 29th day of the previous first month, at the time of the visit to Zōjō-ji temple, the senior ladies-in-waiting of the Main Keep had communicated that, for every visit to either Tōei-zan or Zōjō-ji, a letter should be sent after the Shogun's return to inquire after his well-being. Accordingly, this letter was sent for the first time on this occasion.
