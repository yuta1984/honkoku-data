One entry: On the same day, for the same reason (the Boys' Festival celebration), female messengers, including one Yasoda, were dispatched from the inner quarters (奥向き) to pay respects to both the Main Keep (Honmaru) and the West Keep (Nishinomaru).
　　Note: On this occasion, the respects to the West Keep were also concluded at the Main Keep.
　　　One sub-entry: Regarding the dispatch of female messengers upon receipt of the aforementioned gifts, as this is established precedent hereafter, it shall not be recorded.

On this occasion, since the two senior ladies-in-waiting, Sakio and Yasoda, had repeatedly served as messengers on behalf of the Shogun, they were each awarded two scrolls as gifts.

One entry: On the 7th day of the 5th month, a martial arts viewing by the Senior Councillors (Rōjū) was held at the Main Keep. As Lord Shigemasa was still young, his chief retainer (karō) was appointed to represent him (as proxy).
　From today until the 10th, kenjutsu (swordsmanship) was scheduled; from the 17th to within the 23rd of the same month, archery and firearms were scheduled.
　　Note: While in residence in Edo, it is customary for a proxy to attend the martial arts viewing on one's behalf. As this is established precedent hereafter, it shall not be recorded.

One entry: On the 8th day of the same month, a circular notice from the Grand Inspectors (Ōmetsuke) was relayed through the caretakers (rusui) of Lord Matsudaira Aki-no-kami Yoshinaga and Lord Matsudaira Ōinokami, stating:
　　　　　Upon the demise of the Sentō (the Retired Emperor), those of fourth court rank holding 100,000 koku or more are to send messengers to Kyoto to present funeral offerings (kōden). This is to be conducted in the same manner as during the death of the Nakamikado-in in the 2nd year of Genbun (1737, the Year of the Snake).
