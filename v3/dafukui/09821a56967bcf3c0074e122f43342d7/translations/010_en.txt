**[Enkyō 4 (year of the Rabbit, 1747), continued]**

　…should be handled [and referred to as such]. *[1st month]*

　The above was communicated in writing by Watanabe Sachū on the 8th day of the 3rd month.

○ On this occasion, as the *Kōgi* (the Shogunate) has graciously announced the appointment of an adopted heir (*yōkunsama*), a letter of congratulation is to be sent to the Young Lord (*Wakatono-sama*). Regarding the addressee of said letter, it is to be directed to one's counterpart colleagues (*dōyaku-chū*) in Edo.

Item — In addition to the customary salutations (*go-rei*) already established for the New Year, summer and winter greetings, attendance on the lord's alternate-year journey to Edo (*go-sankin*), and return to the domain (*go-kikoku*), on the occasions of receiving the bestowal of hawks or skylarks, and furthermore whenever a notice is issued by the Senior Councillors (*go-karō-chū*), a letter of congratulation or the like addressed to both lords (*ryō tono-sama*) is to be sent in the same manner [as described above]. Beyond the above, letters are to be sent as occasions for communication arise.

**Item — The 1st year of Kan'en (year of the Dragon, 1748)**

○ From this point forward, in the event of thunder, earthquakes, or the like, when they are generally of a strong degree, it is not necessary to go and inquire after [the lord's] well-being (*go-kigen ukagai*). However, in the case of exceptionally violent thunder or earthquakes, this is a different matter. During the night, it is not necessary to go [to inquire]. Should the situation be such that one ought to go, [one should do so] on the following day to inquire after [his well-being]…
