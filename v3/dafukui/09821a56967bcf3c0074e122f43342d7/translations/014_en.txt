...letters of an ordinary nature are absolutely unnecessary; routine duty-letters (*kinjo*) of any kind are strictly prohibited. Furthermore, regarding lodging-place letters (*yadojo*), they should be written to be as light as possible, and should be marked "lodging letter" (*yadojo*) on the outside. In addition, if there are various unavoidable lodging-related matters (*yadoyō*), these too should be written to be as light as possible before being submitted. In the end, if letters accumulate with the courier, everyone's business will be delayed, which will in turn create obstructions. Moreover, it has come to our attention that some persons are relying on those in proximity (*mukayori*) to make requests of officials on their behalf; as was established in previous years, requesting such intermediary arrangements is also strictly prohibited.

Regarding routine duty-letters for the New Year, mid-summer greetings, mid-winter greetings, and congratulatory or condolence occasions — as previously announced, these are likewise strictly prohibited.

The above matters were announced in previous years as well; however, as things have again recently fallen into disorder, they are hereby announced once more.

　　　12th month

The above was communicated on the 16th day of the 12th month at the Edo residence, through a written notice (*kakitsuke*) by Inagaki Yasuemon.

**Item — Hōreki 6th Year (1756), Year of the Rat**

〇 Notice delivered by hand-document (*tegata*): As the accumulation of accompanying letters (*yosejo*) with each courier dispatch has been causing difficulties, this matter was thoroughly announced at the Edo residence some time ago. Accordingly, this time a separate document (*besshi*) has been issued as written below. Please be informed accordingly. That is all.

> 7th day of the 2nd month　　　To: Suzuki Genbei — Sasa Jiken'emon

　　　　　***Memorandum***

〇 During courier (*o-hikyaku*) transit — both in the domain and in Edo — the accumulation of large numbers of accompanying letters has repeatedly caused difficulties. Accordingly, this time a thorough announcement has been made at the Edo residence: henceforth, the submission of excessively heavy (*jūkō*) letters, as well as letters of request (*tanomi-jo*) of that kind, is strictly prohibited. Should a letter be found to be excessively heavy, it will be examined at that time and returned. Furthermore, since a prior announcement on this matter was already made in previous years, everyone should bear this in mind: except for letters of genuine urgency, routine duty-letters for the New Year, mid-summer greetings, mid-winter greetings, and congratulatory or condolence occasions are, needless to say, entirely unnecessary.

　　　2nd month, Year of the Rat
