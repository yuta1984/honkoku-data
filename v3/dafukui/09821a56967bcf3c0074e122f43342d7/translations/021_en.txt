- Regarding the luggage and letters of those remaining in Edo (while their lords are in the home domain): these shall be made as light and compact as possible. After all, if letters are left behind, it will cause inconvenience to each person's affairs.

- For those permanently stationed in Edo (jōfu) and their relatives, as well as those receiving stipend rice allotments (gigi-saku) and the like: unavoidable business matters and correspondence with other places shall be treated as exceptions. Outside of these exceptions, ordinary correspondence—whether for New Year's greetings, summer or winter salutations, matters of celebration or condolence, or petitions to one's superiors—shall absolutely not be conducted.

The above points have been repeatedly communicated previously, but as discipline has slackened and things have become disorderly, this notice is hereby issued anew. Ensure there are no misunderstandings.

**Tenmei 1st Year (1781), Year of the Ox**

On the 3rd day of the 6th month, the following was communicated by Inaba Oribu: Regarding temple visits (go-sankei) and the sending of proxy representatives (go-naidai) in Edo, it was decreed that—as in the home domain (Fukui)—henceforth the same shall apply to both temples in Edo. The separate written document was handed over as follows:

**Monthly Visits**

| Posthumous Title | Temple(s) |
|------|------|
| Jōkō-in | Zōjō-ji / Tentoku-ji |

→ The above shall be visited in person. However, when visits are postponed, the senior councillors (o-karō-chū) shall act as proxy representatives.
