**Item — The third year of Kanpō (the year of the Boar, 1743)**

　　*[Interlinear note (warisho):* On the 10th day of the 1st month, Amagata Kazuma reported the following: although this evening is the Setsubun celebration, as a measure of frugality, the practice of receiving the ceremonial soybeans (*o-iwai no daizu*) customarily distributed at the celebration is to be discontinued from this year for a period of five years. Furthermore, whereas in previous years the memorial rites (*o-nichi*) for Jufuku-in were held at the main keep (*honmaru*), from this year they are to be conducted at the stables (*o-umaya*). Accordingly, there will be no guard duties at the main keep (*honmaru o-ban*), and therefore the same-duty colleagues (*dōyaku*) will not be required to come in for overnight duty; this was communicated to Sakai Genba.*]

**Memorandum**

One: The congratulatory greeting (*o-iwai*) to be offered upon the lord's arrival in Edo (*o-sanpu*) shall be performed on the day following the notification issued by the Senior Councillors (*o-karōchū*).
One: The greeting for summer heat (*shoki*) shall be performed on the day following the entry into the *doyō* period (the hottest days of summer).
One: Regarding the offering of congratulatory greetings upon the lord's receipt (*o-haikyō*) of larks and wild geese from the falconry (*o-taka*), the date shall be announced by notice.
One: The greeting for cold weather (*kanki*) shall be performed on the day following the entry into the *kan* period (the coldest days of winter).
One: The New Year's greeting shall be performed on the 2nd day of the 1st month.
One: The congratulatory greeting (*o-iwai*) to be offered upon the lord's departure from Edo (*o-itoma*) shall be performed on the day following the notification issued by the Senior Councillors.

Should there be any matter other than the above that is to be communicated by letter, the relevant date shall be announced by notice at that time.
　　*Note:* Should the 4th, 10th, 26th, or 27th days — all of which are days of abstinence (*o-shōjin-bi*) — fall within the designated dates, attendance is to be on the following day. Attendance may take place at one's convenience between approximately 8 a.m. (the Hour of Five) and noon (the Hour of Nine). Furthermore, those who have a compelling reason (*koshō*) preventing attendance shall communicate this through a colleague or a person with whom they are on close terms.
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　21st day of the 3rd month of the year of the Boar

○ The practice of sending letters to Edo to convey congratulations and inquiries after the lord's health (*go-kigen ukagai*) is henceforth to be discontinued entirely. Instead, one is to present oneself in person to the *Sōja-ban* (the domain's announcer/liaison officer) at the guardhouse at the Nanatsu-gura, as was decreed before the lord's departure from the domain (*o-hakka*). Please be informed of this accordingly. A separate written notice is attached herewith. That is all.
　　　　　　21st day of the 3rd month
　　The above notice was circulated from Shibuya Yaze(tsu)'s office.

**Item — The first year of Enkyō (the year of the Rat, 1744)**

○ When dispatching express couriers (*hikyaku*) to the home domain (*o-kunimoto*), the official letters (*o-yōjō*) sent out by the administrative offices have been observed to be excessively heavy and bulky. Even if private letters (*yadojō*) are enclosed within official letters at one's request, under no circumstances is the receipt of such private letters to be permitted. Should excessively bulky letters be found after this notice has been issued, even if they are official letters, they will be opened and inspected (*kiri-hodoki ginmi*) at the *o-yūhitsu* room (the scribes' office). The supplementary letters (*yosejō*) sent alongside are also becoming progressively heavier and bulkier. There are also routine ceremonial letters (*tsutomejō*) being included. From this point forward, such ceremonial letters are absolutely not to be handled; letters are to be prepared as concisely and lightly as possible before being submitted. Furthermore, letters of referral or intercession (*torinugi-jō*) are strictly prohibited. Should there be actual business to communicate, it is to be submitted directly to the aforementioned *o-yūhitsu* room. As there are many persons currently on duty assignment (*o-tsume*), should bulky letters exist, private letters (*yadojō*) for each individual will inevitably be left behind undelivered — this is the nature of the problem. Each person is to understand the situation as described above and take care to ensure that letters are not excessively heavy or bulky. Although this matter has been decreed and circulated on numerous previous occasions, as it is being formally re-issued on this occasion, each person is to abide by these instructions. That is all.
