**Item — The third year of Kanpō (the year of the Boar, 1743)**

《Marginal note (split-column entry): On the 10th day of the 1st month, Amagata Kazuma reported the following: Although the Setsubun celebration is to be held this evening, as a measure of frugality (*kensetsu*), from the current year for a period of five years, the custom of receiving ceremonial soybeans (*daizu*) as a celebratory gift is to be discontinued. Furthermore, while it has been the custom each year for the Jukōin memorial observance (*hima-machi*) to be held at the main citadel (*honmaru*), from this year it will be conducted at the stables (*umaya*); accordingly, there will be no duty at the main citadel. Having received this notification, I also informed Sakai Genba that the colleagues on duty need not come to lodge overnight.》

**Memorandum**

- Item: The felicitations (*o-yorokobi*) offered upon the lord's return to Edo (*go-sanpu*) shall be presented on the day following the issuance of a notice by the senior councillors (*go-karō-chū*).
- Item: For the heat of summer, [felicitations shall be offered] on the day following the beginning of the *doyo* period (the hottest period of summer).
- Item: The date for presenting felicitations upon the receipt of hawks' larks and geese bestowed by the lord shall be announced separately as occasions arise.
- Item: For the cold of winter, [felicitations shall be offered] on the day following the beginning of the *kan* period (the coldest period of winter).
- Item: For the New Year, [felicitations shall be offered] on the 2nd day of the 1st month.
- Item: The felicitations offered upon the lord's departure (*o-itoma*) shall be presented on the day following the issuance of a notice by the senior councillors.

Regarding any matters other than the above that are to be communicated by letter, the date shall be announced at the appropriate time.

　　Note: Should the scheduled date fall on the 4th, 10th, 26th, or 27th — these being days of abstinence (*shōjin-bi*) — attendance shall be moved to the following day. Persons may appear at their convenience between the hour of five (approx. 8 a.m.) and the hour of nine (approx. noon). Furthermore, those who are unable to attend due to circumstances (*koshō*) may communicate this through a colleague or through a person with an appropriate connection (*mukayori*).
　　　　　　　　　　　21st day of the 3rd month, year of the Boar (Kanpō 3)

○ The practice of sending letters to Edo to offer felicitations or inquire after the lord's wellbeing is to be discontinued entirely henceforth. Instead, one is to present oneself in person at the guardhouse (*bansho*) at Nanatstukura to meet with the *sōja-ban* (the official who manages formal audiences). This has been decreed prior to the lord's departure (*go-hakka*). Please be informed accordingly. A separate written notice is enclosed herewith. That is all.
　　　　　　21st day of the 3rd month
　The above notice was circulated by Shibuya Yaetsu.

**Item — The first year of Enkyō (the year of the Rat, 1744)**

○ When a courier (*hikyaku*) is dispatched to the home domain (*go-kunimoto*), the official correspondence (*go-yōjō*) submitted by the administrative offices has been found to be excessively long and repetitive. Even if personal letters (*yadojō*) are included within official correspondence as a favor, receiving such letters is entirely unnecessary and should not be done under any circumstances. Should excessive and lengthy letters be found after this notice has been issued, even if they are official correspondence, the *go-yūhitsu* room (the official secretariat) will open and inspect them. In addition, joint letters (*yorijō*) are also tending to become progressively longer and more cumbersome. Furthermore, there are also routine greeting letters (*kinjo*). From this point forward, routine greeting letters are to be handled under no circumstances. Letters should be composed as concisely and briefly as possible before being submitted. Moreover, letters of intermediary transmission (*toritsugi-jō*) are strictly prohibited. If there is business to communicate, it is to be submitted directly to the *go-yūhitsu* room. Because the number of persons currently stationed in Edo (*go-tsume*) is large, the existence of lengthy letters will inevitably result in a backlog of personal letters for each individual at their lodgings. With this understanding, all persons are to take care to ensure that their letters are not excessively long or repetitive. Although this has been decreed and notified on numerous previous occasions, the situation remains as described; therefore, it is hereby decreed once more, and all persons are to abide by it accordingly. That is all.
