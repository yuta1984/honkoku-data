## Item: Kanpō 3, Year of the Boar (1743)

　《Interlinear note: On the tenth day of the first month, Amagata Kazuma informed us of the following: Although the Setsubun celebration was to be held this evening, in the interest of frugality (*kenyaku*), the practice of receiving ceremonial soybeans as a celebratory gift is to be suspended for five years starting this year. Furthermore, whereas it has been customary each year to hold a memorial observance for Jukōin at the main keep (*honmaru*), from this year the ceremony will be conducted at the stables (*o-umaya*). Accordingly, guard duty at the main keep is not required. Upon being so informed, he further communicated to Sakai Gemba that the colleagues on duty need not remain overnight.》

---

　　　　　　**Memorandum**

Item: The day to present congratulations upon the lord's return from attendance at Edo (*sanpu no orei*) shall be the day following the notice issued by the Senior Elders (*go-karō-jū*).

Item: For the heat season, the day following the start of *doyō* (the midsummer period).

Item: The day for presenting congratulations upon receipt of falconry game (larks and wild geese) bestowed by the lord shall be announced by notification of the specific date.

Item: For the cold season, the day following the start of the *kan* (midwinter period).

Item: For the New Year, the second day of the first month.

Item: The day to present congratulations upon the lord's departure (*ohima no orei*) shall be the day following the notice issued by the Senior Elders.

　For any matter beyond the above for which felicitations are submitted by letter, the specific date shall be announced by notification at that time.

　　Note: Should the designated day fall on a day of abstinence (*shōjin-bi*)—namely the fourth, tenth, twenty-sixth, or twenty-seventh day of the month—attendance shall take place on the following day.

　Attendance is to take place between the fifth hour (*itsutsu-doki*, approximately 8:00 a.m.) and the ninth hour (*kokonotsu-doki*, approximately noon) at one's convenience. Furthermore, those who are unable to attend due to an impediment (*koshō*) are to convey that fact through a colleague or an appropriate acquaintance.

　　　　　　　　　　　　Twenty-first day of the third month, Year of the Boar

○ Henceforth, the practice of submitting congratulations and inquiries after the lord's health to the Edo residence by letter is to cease. Instead, audiences are to be conducted with the *sōja-ban* (reception official) at the Nanatsugura guardhouse, as has been commanded prior to the lord's departure. Please be informed of this. A separate written notice is enclosed herewith. The above.

　　　　　　　　　Twenty-first day of the third month

　The above written notice was circulated by Shibuya Yaetsu of the tax office (*zeikata*).

○ Communicated by letter: Regarding the presentation of congratulations and inquiries after the health of Her Ladyship (*go-mae-sama*) and the Lady Jukōin (*Jukōin-sama*)—on occasions when congratulations are presented to the lord, you are hereby instructed to seek an audience with the *sōja-ban* at the Nanatsugura guardhouse. Please take note of this accordingly. The above.

　　　　　　　　　　Lord Akita Sangozaemon　　Amagata Kazuma

## Item: Enkyō 1, Year of the Rat (1744)

○ When couriers (*hikyaku*) are dispatched to the home domain, the official dispatches (*goyōjō*) submitted by the administrative offices have been observed to be excessively bulky. Although personal letters have been included within official dispatches as requests, receiving lodging letters (*yadojō*) is absolutely prohibited henceforth. Should excessively bulky materials be found after this notice, even if they are official dispatches, they will be opened and examined at the *goyūhitsu* (official secretarial) office. Furthermore, consolidated letters (*yosejō*) have also been growing increasingly bulky. Routine duty letters (*kinjō*) have also been found. From this point forward, routine duty letters are absolutely not to be handled. All correspondence is to be prepared as concisely as possible. Moreover, forwarding letters (*torinugi-jō*) are strictly prohibited. Should there be actual business (*yōji*), it is to be submitted directly to the *goyūhitsu* office.

　Since there are many people currently on duty, if bulky letters exist, they will inevitably lead to the accumulation of lodging letters for various individuals as well. With this in mind, everyone should take note of the above and ensure that their letters are kept as concise as possible. Although this matter has been announced and circulated on numerous previous occasions, since it is being proclaimed again on this occasion, everyone is to abide by these rules. The above.
