(Continued from the previous page.) One shall examine (court appointments and provincial governorships) and take one's seat accordingly. This is therefore why people aspire to court appointments. Writing the name of one's residence in the place of one's "name-place" (nadokoro) is also done out of deference to the recipient. Furthermore, as a certain person has told: a letter of greeting (shōsoku, 御消息) was sent from a Bishop (sōjō, 僧正) who was the uncle of a man called Udaiben (右大弁, Senior Secretary of the Right) — himself a son of a Regent (setsuroku, 摂籙) — to that very Udaiben. When the attendants (tonobaradomo) of the household examined the letter and read its endorsement on the back, they reportedly returned it, saying: "The address of this letter is in error. It is contrary to proper form." Even though he was the recipient's uncle, since he was a Buddhist cleric (shukke, 出家), (the sender) had placed himself below (the recipient), and that is reportedly why they said as much. According to the conventions of the court nobles (kuge), even if a person is a senior relation (oyakata, 親方), when that person has taken Buddhist orders (shukke), it is the custom to regard them as lower in standing. In the warrior houses (buke), letters to and from disciplined clerics (jikai no shukke, 持戒の出家) and learned monks (gakushō, 学匠) are treated with graduated levels of respect accordingly.

[Left folio]

One must show proper reverence. One must thoroughly master the rules of etiquette.

One item: When writing something, one places one's hand at the beginning of the paper and writes accordingly. It is unseemly (birō, 尾籠) to leave wide margins (i.e., to waste paper excessively). Regarding the ceremonial paper (raishi, 礼紙): when showing full respect, one writes "I advance and humbly report" (susunde gonjō su, 進て言上す). One may also write "jōkei (上啓, reverently addressed above)." When showing somewhat less respect, one writes "I advance and report" (susunde kei-sōrō, 進て啓候).

One item: Regarding the inner envelope (naifū, 内封) and the outer envelope (gaifū, 外封). The inner envelope is sealed with a band (obi, 帯), a line of ink is drawn, and it is wrapped on top (uwamaki, 上巻). On this, "shinjō (進上)" or "kinjō (謹上)" must not appear. Below, only the name-place (nadokoro, 名所) is written. For those who have shaved their heads (i.e., taken Buddhist orders), only the dharma name (hōmyō, 法名) is written. When using a rolled letter (tatemibumi, 立文), one writes "shinjō" or "kinjō" in accordance with the recipient's standing, and in one's own name-place one writes one's court title (kantō, 官途) and personal name (jitsumyō, 実名). Those who have shaved their heads should write "novice monk" (shami, 沙弥).
