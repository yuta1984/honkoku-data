Sutras (shakkyō, copied sutras) should be written in the standard script (kaisho/shin). Therefore, following the style set down by the Dainagon-dono and Yukinari for the manner of writing things, even when using a Japanese brush (wahitsu), being excessively formal in one's kaisho is not good. One should write to such a degree that the strokes and dots remain orderly and unconfused. When having a person copy an entire set of the Lotus Sutra (Hokke-kyō), one should instruct the most skilled calligrapher among them to copy the first volume and the last eight volumes. Even the brushwork of Daishi (the Great Master, Kūkai) in copying sutras was not written in pure kaisho — and therein lies its significance.

One: The signatures of the bhikkhus and monks (biku/sō) at the end (oku) of an ordination certificate (kaichō) should be written in three lines. One writes leaving a slight margin from the edge (of the paper). The position for the title "Daitoku" (Great Virtue) corresponds to the area above and below this. The place to write the abbot's (zasu's) monogram (hangyō) is below that. The duty roster (banchō) should, in any case, have the three scripts — formal (shin), semi-cursive (gyō), and cursive (sō) — intermingled in its writing. When the number of people (listed) is small, it cannot be helped, but one should not leave too much blank space at the beginning and the end. Below the character "定" (sadame/jō, "fixed/established"), it is acceptable to have a blank character space (ketsujū). One writes with the character "定" set slightly apart (pulled away). Furthermore, the characters at the right side of the end (oku) should be written slightly lower. Below the era name (nengō) and date, one writes densely without leaving space.

One: Regarding the matter of outer title labels (gedai). In the case of a scroll (makimono), one writes above the binding cord. In any case, one should write so that the cord does not pass through the middle of a character. As for the outer title of a bound volume (sōshi), writing it slightly larger causes no problem.
