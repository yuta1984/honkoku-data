That which is written as "Yorihakarai shittatsu kudan no gotoshi" (依仰執達如件, "Transmitted in accordance with the directive, as stated above") is what is called (a Hōsho, letter of transmittal). It should carry a year-era name. Those without a year-era name do not write "kudan no gotoshi" (as stated above), but instead write either "tokoro oose-idasa-reru nari" (所被仰出也, "that which has been commanded") or "jōi nite sōrō" (上意にて候, "in accordance with the superior's wishes"). All documents of the Kubō (the Shogunal House / public authority) that carry a year-era name are folded from the inside to the outside and rolled. These must not have a "reishi" (礼紙, an attached blank courtesy sheet). Furthermore, (the sender's) personal name (jitsumyō) is absent, with only the kana name and the御判 (gohan, monogram/cipher). On the outer wrapping (uwamaki), the personal name is written. In general, if there is a "kotogaki" (事書, a subject heading), and if one writes in accordance with the prescribed manner described above, one writes "kudan no gotoshi" (as stated above). On Rinji, Inzen, and Ryōji, the issuing party's own personal seal (gohan) must not appear; instead, (another person) is instructed to affix the seal on (the superior's) behalf. All of these are (in the nature of) Hōsho (transmitted documents). From a Provincial Governor (kokushi), (the governor's) own personal seal (gohanjō) appears. In the opening section of a Rinji, the phrase "Tenki ni yori shittatsu kudan no gotoshi" (依天気執達如件, "Transmitted in accordance with the Imperial Will, as stated above")

also appears, and in the case of a Ryōji, "kudan no gotoshi" (as stated above) is also written. For documents of this kind (i.e., these formal correspondence types), one must in all cases refrain from twist-folding (yorioри). A "ukebumi" (請文, letter of acknowledgment/receipt) should also be folded without twisting, and furthermore it uses "ranshi" (乱紙, paper in a loosely disordered arrangement/unlined paper). In addition, beyond these, there are the "oshibumi" (押書, pledge document), the "fu'an" (符案, notice of adjudication), and the "kashō" (過書, transit permit/pass) — the transmitted teachings regarding such matters are varied and differ (from source to source). An oshibumi is a document used to take formal custody of a thing and to firmly confirm a pledge. In this, one writes only one's own name at the top (the opening). This too should be folded. What is called a "fu'an" is something that, when a legal matter (sata) has reached a settlement (rakkyo), is affixed (posted) to the wall of the adjudication office (satadokoro). It is like a "kabegaki" (壁書, wall notice/posted proclamation). However, a kabegaki bears an official seal (gohanjō). What is called a fu'an is: (a record of the fact that) the case of a person called So-and-so of such-and-such a province came before a formal deliberation (hyōjō) on such-and-such a day, was settled (rakkyo), and that an authoritative directive (gochi) bearing the official seal was awarded…
