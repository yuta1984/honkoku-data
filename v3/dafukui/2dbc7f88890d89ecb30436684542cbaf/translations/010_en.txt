It is a document recording the number of people, horses, and cattle to be inspected and passed through (kanka, 勘過), and "as stated above" (kudan no gotoshi, 如件) is written therein. Furthermore, regarding monogram seals (gohan/kaō), they should be present beginning with the provincial governor (kokushi) and others. Concerning the monogram seals of the Shogun and those below him, the oral seal (kuchihan, 口判) and the placement below the date (hinosita, 日下) vary case by case. For a confirmation of landholding (ando, 安堵) directive (kakikudashi, 書下), one should by all means place (the seal) beneath the date, and the oral seal (kuchihan) is a matter of bestowing favor (go-on, 御恩). Regarding the matter of Hōsho documents, while the phrase "in accordance with the command" (Ōse ni yorite, 依仰) is desirable in the written text, if one were to use the oral seal (kuchihan) without that phrase, this would be called "without precedent" (unashi, 得無), and it should not be the authentic, transmitted original form (honryō, 本領). This is something one must thoroughly understand.

One item: When writing letters and the like to those of higher status, one writes to one's peers (ともがら) with the closing "in reverent awe, respectfully" (Kyōkyō kingen, 恐々謹言), "respectfully submitted" (Kinjō, 謹上), and "[to] Lord Such-and-such" (Nanigashi-dono).

[Left folio]

When one wishes to show a slightly higher degree of deference than "Kinjō (謹上)," one writes "Kinkin-jō (謹々上)." This is used when a cadet branch member (shoshi, 庶子) addresses the head of the main house (sōryō, 惣領) with reverence. In such cases, one should not write the person's name and clan name directly; instead, one writes the name of a person of the inner household (uchi no hito), or alternatively writes "to those in close attendance" (Kinjū no onaka, 近習の御中) or "to the assembled persons" (Hitobito no onaka, 人々の御中).

One item: Concerning the reply letters (ukebumi, 請文) for Imperial Decrees (rinji, 綸旨), Retired Emperor's Edicts (inzen, 院宣), and Provincial Edicts (kokusen, 国宣). In responding to these, one should truly write: "With this intent, please cause (the matter) to be reported and conveyed in an appropriate manner; in utmost reverence and fear, respectfully" (Kono mune wo motte, shikaru beki yō ni sōtatsu seshime tamae, seikō seikyō, kingen, 以此旨可然様令奏達給へ誠惶誠恐謹言) and submit it. When one writes "respectfully presented" (shinjō, 進上), the proper written form is invariably to close with "in reverent awe, respectfully" (Kyōkō kingen, 恐惶謹言). In a migyōsho (御教書, official letter) sent down from the residence of the Regent (Denka, 殿下) to a physician's household, the phrase "reverently received" (kinpō, 謹奉) has been written. However, it is said that there is a discrepancy (chigaime, 違目) on the part of the person responsible for composing the Hōsho.
