(Continued from the previous page.) (The writing of "kinpō [謹奉]") appears as such, but perhaps it was written with the physician in mind. Furthermore, regarding those of the rank of samurai and below: when showing respect (to the recipient), one writes "Shinjō (進上, respectfully presented) / Kyōkō kingen (恐惶謹言, in reverent awe, respectfully)"; and to one's peers, one writes "Kingen (謹言, respectfully) / Kyōkyō kingen (恐々謹言, in reverent awe, respectfully)." As for whether there should be a hōsho (奉書, official directive) addressed to those of samurai rank and below, (I) am not aware of such a thing. It is also said that this does not reach the level of adjudication (osata, 御沙汰) from those above, but I have heard that the samurai of the household of the later Lord Kyōgoku (京極殿) and others were taught in this manner. According to the conventions of the court nobles (kuge), a "request letter of a certain person" (ukebumi, 請文) is written with one's name and place name, below which one writes (the closing formula). Upon examining a letter sent to the elder brother of a certain person who had been granted permission in the matter of proper etiquette, it was written as: "Respectfully presented (Shinjō, 進上) [to] Lord Ōmiya (大宮殿)." This person's court rank was Udaiben (右大弁, Senior Secretary of the Right), and their residence was at Ōmiya (大宮). It was written: "In reverent awe, respectfully (Kyōkō kingen, 恐惶謹言), letter of Uemon no Suke (右衛門佐) Such-and-such."

[Left folio]

Upon examining the reply to that letter, it was written: "Reverently received (Kinpō, 謹奉), reply from Lord Bōjō (坊城殿), Udaiben (右大弁) Such-and-such." In a commoner household (zokka, 俗家), one must particularly show reverence to one's elder brother. It is decreed that even if one sits in the same place as one's parents, one must not sit in the same place as one's elder brother. If one's paternal father-in-law holds a high court position and possesses extensive landholdings, one should revere him just as one would one's own parent. If one's maternal uncle is of equal standing, one should truly write "Kinjō (謹上, respectfully submitted)," write "the residence of Lord Such-and-such," and truly write "Kyōkō kingen (恐惶謹言, in reverent awe, respectfully)." If the closing is "Kyōkyō (恐々)," then one should by all means write it sincerely. In any case, one should treat matters concerning one's family with the utmost respect, treating them as full persons worthy of reverence. Those who do not know the proper conventions are utterly called "beasts" (chikushō, 畜生).
