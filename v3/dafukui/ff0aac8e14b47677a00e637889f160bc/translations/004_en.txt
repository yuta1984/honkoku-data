【Left folio】

　　　　　　Letter from a traveler aboard a British vessel

I humbly address this letter to you. I trust that you continue to be in excellent health and high spirits. I am pleased to inform you that I have crossed the sea safely, and I hope this will set your mind at ease. Following my previous letter sent from Hong Kong, I departed from that port on the evening of the 12th day of the 1st month, and arrived at Singapore (新嘉坡, Shingapōru) on the 19th, going ashore. There I met a castaway by the name of Otsukichi from Onōura in Owari Province. (Until recently he had served as a British interpreter and had dealings with Mizuno Chikuzen-no-kami.)

Since last year, throughout the Shanghai area of the Qing dynasty, terrible violence and plunder have erupted — extending across thousands of *ri* from Nanjing to the region of Suzhou and Zhejiang. All males between the ages of fifteen or sixteen up to forty have been spared, while the remainder — the elderly and the young alike — have been killed entirely, leaving only the sounds of weeping ghosts (鬼哭) across the desolate wastelands. The forces of the "Long-Haired" rebels (長毛, i.e., the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom) number some two to three hundred thousand, while British and French garrison troops remain stationary, watching the situation and apparently intending to take action afterward. Although the Long-Haired rebels initially practiced benevolence and righteousness, the killing and wounding of today is unceasing. The Qing dynasty has, moreover, newly enthroned a young ruler (changing the era name to "Tongzhi" — this being the first year of that era), and the people of the court have all advanced in official rank through bribery, oppressing the common people. Even if men of outstanding ability exist, they are not employed — truly lamentable. It is said that at present, more than half of the population has joined the Long-Haired rebels to foment disorder. Should this not serve as an entirely cautionary mirror (for Japan)?

One item: It is reported that in the current warfare between the Qing and the (Taiping) forces, armor is no longer used.
