## (Song of the Kumasuke — Palanquin Bearers)

In a song sung by palanquin bearers (*kumasuke*):

> "Hey there, sister — the *shimada* topknot you slept in this morning turned, in just one night, into a 'kororiage' (*kororiage* = rolling/fallen), reflected in the water's surface."

## (Aphoristic Verses in Chinese-style Parallelism)

> The wise destroy the martial; Heaven makes light of traitors; the lord [punishes] traitors; loyalty aids the loyal retainers of the realm.
> Court officials compete; the Mandate of Heaven is just; the people have life; the lord's authority is stern.
> The flatterer stirs up the brave; the retainer holds fast to [the principle of] death; the retainer chastises [those who deviate from] the Way; preparations against the barbarians grow strong.

## Yokohama Newspaper, Mid-8th Month, Bunkyū 2nd Year (1862)

This newspaper [article] was heard from the American "Wenrīto" and written down as if from dictation; there may therefore be omissions and errors.

---

Item: The everyday clothing worn by Japanese samurai is unsuitable. Moreover, since they go barefoot, in the event of any disturbance or emergency there would be great harm.

　　　《Marginal note: In Wenrīto's mind, could this be a veiled reference to the incident at the *Unjobasho* [Customs House]?》

Should it naturally come to an exchange of gunfire or sword blows, the small-patterned (*komon*) or striped fabrics interspersed with various white colors typically worn by Japanese men would, when blood is shed, of their own accord shock the eye — causing even a minor wound to appear as a grave one, thereby sapping one's fighting spirit. Hakama and similar garments carry the risk of becoming entangled in objects. Going barefoot poses no great immediate harm when running about indoors, but should one naturally rush out into the garden or beyond the gate, it would cause harm. Western soldiers wear footwear both indoors and out, and dress in woolen (*rasha*) tunics and trousers… *(continued on next page)*
