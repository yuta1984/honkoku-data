*(Continued from previous page)* Even if it be Britain or France, should they act unreasonably and with willful disregard, if [Japan] refuses to acquiesce and displays a resolve to begin fighting, they will truly become nations with which a peace accord can be established. Moreover, even if Britain or France were to initiate hostilities against Japan at this time, they would without fail first inform America, which first came [to Japan]. At that point, America would surely act to prevent any fighting [against Japan]. If Britain or France were to violate [the peace], America would join forces with Japan and turn against them. This is because when America first came to Japan, it concluded a treaty stating that it had come here wishing to ensure that Japan would not face war. Furthermore, British and French merchants are keen to purchase rice from Japan. This is because "in the vicinity of California, tens of thousands of Chinese people reside, and rice prices are exceedingly high — one hundred kin of rice (equivalent to sixteen kan-me) fetches four and a half to five tōraru [dollars]." Although merchants would thus stand to gain great profit, since the American treaty prohibits the purchase of rice, Japan does not currently sell rice to foreign countries. If this prohibition did not exist, Japan would by now have no rice left. Were that to happen, it would truly become the spark of upheaval in Japan, it is said.
