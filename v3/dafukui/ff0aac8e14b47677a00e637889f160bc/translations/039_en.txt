*(Continued from previous page)* Cotton bales should also be stockpiled at the Ōta encampment as a reserve. Beyond this, there are numerous recent inventions in the art of warfare and in military equipment alike that are altogether beyond the reach of Japanese people [to replicate at present]. One should go to America without delay and observe the battles firsthand. There is no better policy than this for opening Japan [to the wider world], it is said.

Item: Although Japan has been gradually purchasing warships and steamships, since all of these are procured from merchants, ships that have been worn out through more than twenty years of use are being accepted as "good enough" for service. This is an exceedingly dangerous matter. If one truly wishes to obtain warships, one should select young officers and men of both high and low rank, dispatch several of them to America to observe the war, place them within shipyards to work alongside [the builders] and construct new vessels, and then, once they have mastered the art of navigation and returned to Japan, they will without doubt be of service in combat, it is said.

Item: Although nations such as Britain and France are inclined to wage war against Japan, since America came [to Japan] from the outset, concluded a treaty with Japan, and has upheld its commitment to preventing war [against Japan], it is likely that Japan will not face armed conflict. Nevertheless, in years to come, war will inevitably be unavoidable. Therefore, even though Britain and France may presently cause minor harms toward Japan, for now patience must be the foremost priority, and preparing to hold back [from retaliation] must be the first order of business; in the meantime, one must learn the arts of combat as quickly as possible and strive to achieve strict and thorough military preparedness.
