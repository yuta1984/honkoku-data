He was led into the room called the "middle sitting room" (nakaza-shiki), where Heihachirō appeared. Kōemon of Moriguchi waited in a lateral seat, and Heihachirō informed him as follows: "Although I have been distributing alms myself, the town magistrate has provided no relief whatsoever. I intend to make this known to the town magistrate. Moreover, it is difficult to predict when a great upheaval may occur; at such a time, you should stand on my side. This is not a matter of rebellion — it is simply that the annual taxes (nengu) are oppressively heavy in both shogunal and private domains, and I intend to see to it that the taxes are reduced before long. This will be for the benefit of the peasants. For that reason, please affix your seal to this document." He had the document read aloud to [Shibanosuke]; though [Shibanosuke] could not recall the precise wording, it contained phrases to the effect of "a great matter for the realm (tenka no daiji)," "allies should help one another," and "you shall abide by our instructions." Understanding it to be for the benefit of the peasants, [Shibanosuke] wrote his name — Kimura Shibanosuke — in his own hand, and affixed his personal seal and signature. Those who also signed in a joint pledge (renban) included: Seta Sainosuke, Koizumi Fuchijirō, Watanabe Ryōzaemon, Takeue Mantarō, Kōemon of Moriguchi-juku, Chūbee of Hannyaji village, Risaburō of Yuge village, Yaemon of Kinohashi village, and many others,

though [Shibanosuke] could not remember all their names. Furthermore, [Heihachirō] instructed him that when the uprising broke out, he was to bring approximately thirty laborers (ninso­ku) and come promptly; and that if he were to divulge the foregoing to anyone, he would be cut down — he was made to understand this clearly. As there was also a matter to be entrusted to him concerning the distribution of the written documents (fuda), he was told by Heihachirō to come and stay the night on the 18th. Accordingly, he went around the hour of the fifth watch (approximately 8 p.m.), and was in the middle sitting room. Present in that gathering were Watanabe Ryōzaemon, Kōemon of Moriguchi-juku, Risaburō of Yuge village, and Yaemon of Kinohashi village; food and sake were served, and [Shibanosuke] ate. He was intending to leave before long; though it seemed there were quite a number of people in the inner rooms as well, Heihachirō emerged from where he had been sitting and announced that there was a traitor (uragiri). Those who had been seated in the room all rushed into the inner quarters, whereupon [Shibanosuke], overcome with fear, fled the premises and returned to his village. As there was a matter he needed to inquire about at the home of Jinemon, a peasant in the village, he states that he was at Jinemon's house from around the ninth hour of noon (approximately noon) until past the eighth hour (approximately 2 p.m.) on the same day.
