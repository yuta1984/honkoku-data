　　21st day, 2nd month, Year of the Rooster　　　　　Ikeda Iwanojō

　　　A Record of Accounts Heard Concerning the Osaka Disturbance

On the 19th day of the 2nd month, at the Hour of the Dragon (approximately 8 a.m.), Ōshio Kakunosuke — a yoriki under Eastern Magistrate (Higashi-machi Bugyō) Atobe Yamashiro-no-Kami — along with his retired father Heihachirō (a man who had once investigated a case involving Christians, and who was widely versed in Confucian scholarship and military studies), Koizumi Fuchijirō, Seta Sanosuke and his son, five or six dōshin (constables), as well as members of the Sesshū Tsukuri-gumi (or Shizō-gumi), rōnin from Izushi and Hamada, and peasants — totaling approximately three hundred men — formed a rebel band and rose in revolt. Ōshio Kakunosuke and Koizumi Fuchijirō entered the town magistrate's office and committed acts of violence, killing a retainer of Atobe Yamashiro-no-Kami. Koizumi was reportedly killed at Semba. Ōshio (Kakunosuke) fled, and around the middle of the Hour of the Dragon (approximately 9 a.m.), set fire to the yoriki residential quarter in Tenma. Using fire arrows, large and small cannon, incendiary pole-arrows, and fire-starters loaded onto three carts, they launched a fire attack. Despite there being no wind, a great fire broke out; first they attacked Kokukuji Temple by fire, then took position in the temple district of Tenma...

...set up their position, and on three white banners inscribed the characters reading "Tenshō Daijingū" (Shrine of the Great Deity Amaterasu). Their forces were formidable, and within a short time a great conflagration had broken out. The Tenmangu Shrine immediately went into fire-alert readiness. The Jōdai (Castle Deputy) Lord Ōi Ōino-kami, the Jōban (Standing Guard Commander) Lord Endō Tajima-no-Kami, the West Guard Commander, the Inspector Deputies, the Town Magistrates, the Sakai Magistrate, the Naval Forces, and others all proceeded to the castle and conducted repeated inspection rounds. The sounds of fires burning and the sounds of gunfire were intense. At the southern approach to Tenmabashi Bridge, the Jōdai's forces took up a firm position, and the Kozume forces also held firm; the rebels attempted to cross and there was fierce fighting. They managed to hold back the rebels vigorously, but one force of rebels circled around to Naniwabashi Bridge and fired incendiary pole-arrows into the Semba district, where fires broke out immediately. From around the Hour of the Serpent (approximately noon), with fires raging from two directions, forces sortied from the Tamazukuri-guchi yoriki residential quarter. From the Ōtamon (large turret) of the castle, the gunnery and armor magistrates came out and deployed many troops. The yoriki of Tamazukuri fired their muskets in a hanging volley (tsuri-uchi), forcing the rebels to retreat; as a result, the rebels were pushed back across Tenjinbashi Bridge, and a stockade was set up at the Ōte-guchi entrance and near the large bridge.
