Furthermore, at this critical juncture of deciding on expulsion of the barbarians and potential decisive battle, it is essential that lord and subject be united in harmony. Should the Shogun return to the Kantō and the two parties be separated east and west, the communication of intentions between lord and subject would be severed, a state of natural estrangement would develop, and the situation of the realm could reach a point of no recovery. As regards the Shogun's return to his castle at this time, since this cannot be sanctioned in the imperial judgment, [the Shogun] is to remain in Kyoto, diligently advance plans for defense and security, and thereby set [His Majesty's] mind at ease — such is the imperial thinking. As for negotiations with the English barbarians, [the Shogun's party] is to proceed to the port of Osaka and conduct talks of refusal there. Should hostilities break out, it is thought that if the Shogun were to personally take charge and direct all matters, it could become an opportunity to restore the vitality of the Imperial Nation. As for the defense of the Kantō, it is commanded that suitable persons be selected and assigned to the task.

One: Today, following direct and thorough deliberation with the Shogun, the Shogun having accepted the articles set forth on a separate sheet, it is expected that the Shogun will also issue directives [to the lords]. The Regent has commanded that this be communicated for general awareness.
　　　　　19th day of the 3rd Month

One: On the 10th day of the 4th Month of the Year of the Boar [Bunkyū 3, 1863], at Ryōgoku Hirokōji in Edo, two severed heads — said to have been in the possession of rōnin — were exposed for public display: one of a man approximately 37 or 38 years of age, and another of approximately 28 years of age. The notice board (tattefuda) read as follows:
　　　"These individuals, under the guise of loyal patriotism and devotion to the nation, disturbed the city, greedily consumed food and drink and money, and threw the people's minds into disorder. For this outrageous conduct, they are hereby exposed [in public punishment]."

One: On the morning of the 17th day of the 4th Month, at the public notice board (kōsatsujo) on the south side of the western end of Sanjō Bridge, [the following notice was posted]:
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Tokugawa [Shogun's personal name]
　The above-named person — after his recent visit to Kyoto, though there were numerous items commanded by the Imperial Court — has outwardly assumed the appearance of reverently obeying imperial commands while throughout using falsehood and deception to manipulate matters to his advantage, allowing all things to pass in procrastination and negligence. Even regarding the deadlines for rejecting and negotiating with the foreign barbarians, he has deceived the imperial ear and caused delay. Not only has he pressed for permission to return to Edo, but also, during the imperial procession to Otokoyama [Iwashimizu Hachimangū], though he had been granted the honor of attendance in the imperial retinue, he suddenly feigned illness [■]; and further, the Middle Counselor of Hitotsubashi [Yoshinobu], at a time when there was official business before the divine presence at the Hachiman Shrine, fled the scene. In all, [these persons] have shown contempt toward those above them.
