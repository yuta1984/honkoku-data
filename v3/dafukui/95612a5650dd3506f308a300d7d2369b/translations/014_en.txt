(Continuing from the previous page — Notice from the Grand Inspector)

Having reverently received the imperial edict [regarding Jōi], negotiations of refusal are to be carried out promptly [with foreign powers], and should the foreign barbarians fail to submit, they are to be driven away without delay. Accordingly, all parties are to fully understand this and exert themselves in loyal service so as not to bring disgrace upon the nation.
　The foregoing is to be communicated to all persons, whether holding ten thousand koku or more, or less.

　　　　　　　　　　　　To the Grand Inspectors (Ō-metsuke)

For the purpose of guarding the Imperial Palace (Kinri Gosho), from among those holding one hundred thousand koku or more, retainers are to be selected at the rate of one man per ten thousand koku, choosing men of sound constitution, good conduct, and brave and capable character, and they are to be sent to Kyoto to perform guard duty. Furthermore, the management and supervision of these men shall be the responsibility of their respective lords, and they shall be rotated on a one-year basis.
　Since there is also a communication from the Imperial Palace regarding the foregoing, the intent is to be understood and persons are to be selected and sent without delay. For further details, please consult Makino Bizen-no-kami.
　　　3rd Month

Since the Shogun has accepted the arrangement to continue his stay in Kyoto, it is hereby commanded that [all parties] proceed to Edo promptly, prepare thoroughly for defense, and, should the English barbarians initiate hostilities, fight to the death in decisive battle.
　　　23rd day of the 3rd Month

Written notice passed to the Lord of Owari at the Regent's (Kanpaku) residence by the Lord Nagayoshi Sanmi:

Regarding the Shogun's return to Edo — although this has been requested on multiple occasions, it is difficult to predict what manner of disturbances might arise should the return take place. This being so, as it is truly a matter of the utmost importance, [His Majesty] is deeply troubled in mind. As this is born of consideration for both the realm at large and the Tokugawa house, [the Shogun] is requested to remain in Kyoto for a little while longer. [You are requested] to work toward a situation in which the foundations of expelling the barbarians are established, the imperial will is fulfilled, and the people's minds are set at ease, so that [His Majesty's] anxieties may be relieved.
　　　Same day

Although the arrival of the English barbarians and the pressing situation in the Kantō region make the Shogun's return for the purposes of defense a reasonable matter, the strategy for guarding and defending Kyoto and the nearby coastal waters should be directed by the Shogun [from Kyoto].
