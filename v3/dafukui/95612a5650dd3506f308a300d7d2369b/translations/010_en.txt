[Continued from Matsudaira Echizen-no-kami (Shungaku)'s letter]

As for places geographically separated from the imperial homeland, such as Oki and Tsushima, it is requested that arrangements be made for mutual cooperation in defense, not only with neighboring countries but among ourselves as well.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Matsudaira Echizen-no-kami

On this occasion, British warships arrived at Yokohama harbor and presented three demands, all of which were impossible to accept. Having responded accordingly, it appears that hostilities will shortly commence. Therefore, leave of absence has been granted, and we are resolved to dedicate ourselves fully to the duty of protecting the domain as a bulwark of the realm.

　　　Second Month (February)

---

One item: On the twenty-third day of the second month, a wooden signboard was erected at Sanjō Bridge, and the heads of wooden effigies were discarded on the riverbank nearby.

　　　Ashikaga Takauji　　Second Generation Yoshiakira　　Third Generation Yoshimitsu

The treachery of these individuals has been particularly condemned by earlier scholars and is well known to the public, so there is no need to repeat it here. However, since we are subjecting these effigies to a symbolic execution, it would not be superfluous to briefly indicate their crimes.

The way of the great imperial land has, since the age of the gods, taken loyalty and righteousness as its foundation. Yet the arch-traitor Minamoto no Yoritomo of Kamakura appeared in the world, troubled the imperial court, and was the first to act in defiance of the sovereign. Subsequently, the Hōjō and then the Ashikaga compounded these crimes to such a degree that heaven, earth, gods, and men alike would demand their punishment. Nevertheless, at that time the realm was thrown into disorder, and proper distinctions of authority were confused; the imperial court lacked the power to rectify these crimes—a cause for profound regret and lamentation.

Even now, upon viewing the relics of these men, we cannot contain our indignation. Though we are of modest ability, had we been born five hundred years ago, we would have been unable to restrain ourselves for even a moment, clenching our fists and gnashing our teeth, longing to rip out their living heads with our bare hands.

Now is the time of the restoration of all things to antiquity and the renewal of old abuses, and opportunities to correct the crimes of these disloyal wretches are arising one after another. Therefore, we have conferred among ourselves and, in order to punish the great crimes of these traitors and clarify the principles of righteousness and loyalty, last night we removed the effigies of Takauji and his descendants from Tōji-in temple, beheaded them, and exposed their heads publicly, thereby somewhat venting our long-accumulated indignation.

　　　　　[Year designation not recorded]

With the arrival of the great general Lord Oda, the lineage of these traitors was exterminated. This, one must say, is a matter for rejoicing. Yet since that time, up to the present day, those who surpass even these villains have appeared in great numbers, and their crimes exceed even those of the Ashikaga.
