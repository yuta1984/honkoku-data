**[Conclusion of the Yoshiwara Incident]**

(Utagosuké) had replied that she would not consent, but that if (the actor's party) absolutely wished to meet her regardless, they should come to the Ōsakaya. When they went there, they found a large party of actors accompanied by male and female geisha. After presenting ceremonial gifts (shugi) to all those present, as they were leaving, the wife of Kodanji, known as "Koto," requested that Utagosuké be released (from her contract). Utagosuké, upon hearing this, went to the establishment of Izumiya Chūbē, summoned the male geisha, and rebuked them, saying it was incomprehensible that persons of riverbed status (kawaramono, i.e., actors) should speak so rudely to a Yoshiwara courtesan, saying things like "send her back." She then sent five hundred hiki to Kodanji and two others, and three hundred hiki to Koto, requesting that they come to pay their respects in return. However, since Kodanji and his wife were said to be ill, Ichizō and Tsuriya came in their stead to offer thanks and apologies.

However, the proprietress of Utagosuké, a woman named Kin, upon hearing of this affair, became furious: a person who was not even a proper client had caused the courtesan unnecessary and extravagant expense, had furthermore introduced her to actors, and this touched upon the matter of oversight and discipline of courtesans. Harimaya Gihē, Izumiya Chūbē, and Takinokawaya Morizō — these three — submitted a letter of apology together and the matter was provisionally settled. However, since the story had spread widely and in great detail, an investigation was conducted, and the findings were as recorded above.

Accordingly, on the grounds that the teahouse operators had treated actors — who are not permitted to mix with ordinary members of society — as if they were ordinary clients, the business operations of the male and female geisha attached to the teahouses were suspended. Chūbē, Gihē, and Morizō, who should not have been involved in such affairs but had nonetheless handled them improperly, likewise had their business operations suspended. As for Utagosuké, she had upheld the dignity proper to a courtesan, and was commended by the Yoshiwara district as a whole; it was also decided that a reward would be given to her thereafter.

The above was submitted by the Yoshiwara district in the first month of the Year of the Boar (1863).

**[British Naval Demands]**

Item: A courier letter dispatched on the ninth day of the third month of the Year of the Boar (1863) brought the following communication:

Concerning the arrival of several British naval vessels at Kanagawa, a letter has been submitted. Its contents are as follows: They demand that Shimazu Saburō (Hisamitsu) and all his associates involved in the killing be arrested without exception, and that their heads be struck off in the presence of British witnesses. If this cannot be accomplished, they demand that 350,000 ryō be paid as indemnity. Furthermore, they will proceed to Kagoshima in Satsuma domain to receive 30,000 dollars as provision for the wives and children of the British nationals who were killed at the hands of the Shimazu household. Should these demands first be refused, it is difficult to predict whether war might not ensue; therefore, they request that an important official serve as inspector (kemmshi) and board a naval vessel.

They have set the ninth day as the final deadline for the above response. Should the deadline pass, they will immediately dispatch naval vessels to Osaka and all ports, seize vessels entering and leaving, and furthermore burn Edo. They state that this matter has been unavoidably raised because Japan bears responsibility with respect to British national honor and the treaty obligations. Accordingly, the reply scheduled for the ninth has been postponed.

　　Sixth day of the third month

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Matsudaira Yamashiro-no-Kami

Concerning the arrival this time of several British naval vessels at Kanagawa, orders have been issued for the guarding of the Hamachō garden (Hama御庭). Accordingly, personnel are to be quietly assembled and stationed at temples and other locations in advance, so that instructions may be given as the situation demands. At that time, units are to be promptly stationed at their assigned defensive posts and carry out guard duties as commanded. This has also been communicated to Kuze Kenkichi; please take note accordingly.
