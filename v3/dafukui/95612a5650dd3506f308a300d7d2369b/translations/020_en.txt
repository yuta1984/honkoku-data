Lord Matsura Hizen-no-kami, acting as assistant, had approximately three hundred men, dressed in chain-mail armor (kusari katabira) and campaign surcoats (jin-baori). Lord Ōkubo had a mixed force of some with light armor (kogusoku) and some with chain-mail plating, totaling approximately five to six hundred men. Lord Sōma's men all wore full armor topped with campaign surcoats, numbering approximately seven to eight hundred men. Together forming four units, all bearing unsheathed spears and carrying large paper lanterns aloft, they pressed forward to the Honjo Ogasawara rōnin quarters — a truly magnificent and awe-inspiring deployment. Lord Matsudaira Ukyō-no-suke wore light armor topped with a campaign surcoat, with the same equipment as above, and pressed forward to Bakurochō. In the early hours of this morning, eleven rōnin were arrested; then around the Hour of the Snake [9–11 a.m.], another fourteen were arrested, and they were marched in procession to the Takinoguchi Hyōjōsho [the Senior Council's courthouse]. The rōnin all wore white "ashinaka" (short straw sandals), war helmets (jingasa), cords of light blue cotton, field hakama trousers, campaign surcoats, and carried both long and short swords; some among them also carried iron war fans (tessen). The sight of fourteen of them standing in a bold line before the gate of the courthouse was something to behold. I watched for approximately half an hour to see what would happen, but growing rather bored, I then went to watch the security cordon at Honjo. Passing the Otake warehouse, I found Lord Sakai's force of approximately one thousand men already withdrawing.

　Nota bene: Since the rōnin had a record of disorderly conduct and disturbances, they had been summoned by the magistrate's office but had refused to appear; it was for this reason that the forces descended upon them last night. Accordingly, it appears that Udono and Nakajō were brought along by Lord Sakai as guides and intermediaries for negotiations.

Now at the Hour of the Horse [11 a.m.–1 p.m.], among Lord Sakai's withdrawing force, I observed Udono and Nakajō, followed by two rōnin — their appearance likewise consisting of white ashinaka sandals, war helmets, campaign surcoats, and field hakama, with both swords; one was a large man of approximately six shaku [~180 cm] in height — both bound with rope. These were led away to the courthouse. Next, Lord Matsura, Lord Ōkubo, and Lord Sōma withdrew in turn. As they had been maintaining their watch over the Ogasawara quarters since the previous night, they still appeared in their field-deployment attire. Of the more than two hundred men in that residence, only two were taken to the courthouse; the rest remained at the residence. How remarkable a spectacle I have witnessed of late! What will come of this I cannot say — depending on the rōnin's answers, further developments are unpredictable. I hereby report only what I have personally witnessed from last night through today.
