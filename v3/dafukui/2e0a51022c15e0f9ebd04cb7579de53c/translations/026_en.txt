Note: On this occasion, Lord Munenori also went to the castle to pay his respects and present offerings; the details are recorded in Lord Munenori's own family record.

Item: On the 29th day of the 7th month, as the formal expression of gratitude for the adoption ceremony had been successfully concluded, Lord Ogimaru sent a messenger — Itami Hyōgo Naotaka, a senior retainer (karō) of the Hitotsubashi household — to Lord Munenori with the following congratulatory gifts:
　One tachi sword, one gold coin as a substitute for a horse, twenty gold coins as a substitute for a sword (Nagamitsu), one set of three sword fittings (mitokoromono) with gilt shakudō nanako ground and family crest, five formal summer robes (katabira), five unlined robes (hitoemono), one box of kombu, one box of dried sea bream, and one cask of sake.

　Note: As the mounting (koshirae) for the aforementioned sword had not yet been completed, it was handled by means of a written inventory (meiroku). Subsequently, on the 21st day of the 9th month, when the mounting was completed, it was sent with a letter from the Hitotsubashi household's attendant official (goyōnin), carried by an attendant inspector (okachi-metsuke).

Item: On the last day of the same month, for the same reason as above, Lord Munenori sent a messenger — his senior retainer (karō) Sakai Geki Tomotaka — to Lord Ogimaru with the following gifts:
　One tachi sword, one gold coin as a substitute for a horse, one sword (Moriye), one short sword (wakizashi) by Kanemitsu, five formal summer robes (katabira), five unlined robes (hitoemono), one box of kombu, one box of dried sea bream, and one cask of sake.
