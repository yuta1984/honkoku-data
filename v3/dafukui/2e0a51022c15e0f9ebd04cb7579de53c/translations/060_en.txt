Regarding the accompanying ladies-in-waiting, from the senior lady-in-waiting (o-toshiyori) down to the junior attendants (o-tsugi), an audience with the Ōgosho-sama (retired lord) was granted at the lower-level veranda (o-shimo-dan no en-bō). The senior ladies-in-waiting Sakio, Yasoda, and E each received three silver coins (hakugin); the junior lady-in-waiting Kamei and one o-chūrō (middle-ranking lady-in-waiting) each received two silver coins; and one o-omote-tsukai (messenger lady), one o-yūhitsu (secretary lady), and one o-tsugi (junior attendant) each received one silver coin.

The senior attendant ladies, High瀬-dono and Sunano-dono, announced in the anteroom (o-tsugi-no-ma) that these gifts had been granted by the Ōgosho-sama and the Dainagon-sama. Additionally, five silver coins were distributed to all the ladies-in-waiting collectively.

Regarding Yoshimaru-sama's farewell audience, a second formal audience was granted, during which a box of toy horses (o-teasobi: a handcrafted horse toy) and a tiered lacquer box (o-sagejū) were presented as farewell gifts. Additionally, two carriers of serving vessels (gyōki) and one set of supplementary lacquer boxes (o-soejū) were gifted to the accompanying side attendants.

　　　Note: Regarding the tiered lacquer box, as the castle was occupied with affairs that day, it was circulated via written notice from the guest reception room to our senior ladies-in-waiting on the following sixteenth day.

At around 8:30 (half past the eighth hour, approximately 3:00 PM), the party departed.

　　　Note: The o-hiroshiki gate was opened with one panel, and the rear gate (o-ura-mon) was opened with both panels. As before, both the upper and lower guards took their deferential seated positions.
　　　Additionally, two Iga retainers came forward to see the party off.

As an expression of gratitude for the above, a formal letter was subsequently sent from Lord Munenori's home domain to the Senior Councillors (o-rōjū).
