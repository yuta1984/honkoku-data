From the inner quarters (go-naisho), a box of dried squid (surume), one cask of sake, and additionally a box of five sets of braided cords (kōji/obi-cords) — two red, one light yellow, two purple — were presented as offerings to the Ōgosho-sama.

　　Note: The aforementioned cords were offered in the manner of a personal gift (otedayori) within the Ōoku.

At the lower dais (ge-dan), five rolls of satin (donsu) were received as a gift, presented via a formal gift-list (mokuroku).

　　Note: These were passed from a senior lady-in-waiting (rōjochū) to Takarada, the otoshiyori (senior lady-in-waiting) of the Hitotsubashi household.
　　One: In the resting room,
　　　　On behalf of Lord Munenori, a box of dried sea bream (hoshi-dai) was received from the Ōgosho-sama, presented via a senior lady-in-waiting's gift-list, with Lord Yoshimaru acting as proxy (ondairi). The expression of thanks was likewise conveyed through a senior lady-in-waiting.

With the above concluded, in the resting room, Lord Yoshimaru was served ozōni (New Year's soup) and a formal meal (three soups, nine dishes), and thereafter went out to view the garden and the teahouse for his enjoyment and recreation.

One: To "Okume-dono," the personal attendant of the Ōgosho-sama, and to the senior ladies-in-waiting: three silver coins (gin-mai) each; to the guest attendants (okyaku-ōsetsu) and the omote-tsukai (outer messengers): two silver coins each; to all ladies-in-waiting in general (sō-jochū): thirty silver coins in total.
　To the senior ladies-in-waiting attached to the Dainagon-sama: three silver coins each; to the guest attendants and omote-tsukai: two silver coins each; to all ladies-in-waiting in general: thirty silver coins. Furthermore, gifts were given respectively to the personal attendant ladies-in-waiting (otsugi), the junior ladies-in-waiting (ochūrō), the Jōguchi attendants, and the ladies-in-waiting attached to Lord Manjirō.
