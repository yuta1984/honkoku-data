A small Noh chant (ko-utai) was commanded to be performed.

After the above ceremony was concluded, following the san-kon (three-cup ritual), the Shichi-Go-San celebratory meal was to be presented; however, due to his lordship's young age, the Shichi-Go-San meal was cancelled.
Instead, a formal meal of three soups and nine dishes was served.

　　Note: During the above ceremony, the chief retainers (karō) were stationed at the southern veranda of the ante-chamber, wearing long formal hakama. The chief retainer of the Hitotsubashi household, Itami Hyōgono-kami, also appeared at the seat above them.

The above formal ceremony was thus concluded.

Ogimaru-sama retired to his chambers and changed into a half-hakama (han-bakama), after which he proceeded to the front reception hall (omote-shoin), accompanied by Munenori-sama. The sliding screens (fusuma) in the shoin were opened, and he received guests including members of the same clan and invited visitors. A further banquet was thereafter held.

Item: On the same day, in connection with the above occasion,
　Ogimaru-sama sent a messenger to Tokugawa Gyōbukyō Munetada-sama and his consort (misuchū-sama) to offer congratulations.
　Through the chief retainer Honda Yagara Masa-■, one box each of fresh sea bream (sen-tai) was presented.
　From Gyōbukyō-sama and his consort as well, a messenger — Kōno Yaichi-zaemon — was sent,
　presenting Ogimaru-sama with one box each of fresh sea bream in return.

　　Note: After the relocation, it was planned that messengers (yōnin) would send letters daily to the Hitotsubashi messengers to inquire after Gyōbukyō-sama's health and well-being, but the details are unclear.
