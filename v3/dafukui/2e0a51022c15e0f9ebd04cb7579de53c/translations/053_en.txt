At the time of receiving the dried abalone from the Dainagon-sama, [Lady Ogimaru] presented: one long sword (tachi), one gold coin in lieu of a horse, and three rolls of crepe silk (chirimen). From her personal household (go-naishō), she additionally presented: one box of dried squid (surume), one cask of sake, and one box containing ten purses (o-kinchaku).

From the Dainagon-sama, [Lady Ogimaru] received: five rolls of shuchin (a type of satin-weave silk), and one box containing fifteen netsuke (ornamental toggles) and fifteen o-ojime (cord fasteners).

　　　Note: At the time of the aforementioned audience and the presentation of formal thanks, senior lady attendants (rōjochū) handled the proceedings. Takarada, the senior attendant (o-toshiyori) of the Hitotsubashi household, served as an escort (o-soiae). On this occasion, the senior attendant was stationed at the veranda alcove (en-bō).

From there, [Lady Ogimaru] had an audience (go-taiken) with Manjirō-sama. From her personal household, she presented: one arrangement of fresh sea bream (sendan), and additionally one box of toy figures (o-teasobi) — crafted horses. From Manjirō-sama, she received: one box of toy figures — crafted cranes — and one variety of side dish (o-sakana).

　　　Note: To Munenori-sama, in the Karakko-no-ma (Chinese Boy Chamber):
　　　　　From the Kubō-sama: one variety of food and one cask,
　　　　　From the Dainagon-sama: one box of side dishes (hako-zakana), with a gift list (o-mokuroku) presented by a senior lady attendant,
　　　　　These were received on behalf of Yoshimaru-sama, acting as proxy (go-myōdai). Formal thanks were likewise conveyed through the senior lady attendant.

When the above proceedings concluded, in the Karakko-no-ma, [the party] partook of zouni (rice cake soup) and a formal meal (o-ryōri) consisting of three soups and nine dishes.

　　　Note: The Dainagon-sama entered the Karakko-no-ma and is said to have enjoyed himself there.

To the senior lady attendants (rōjochū) in attendance upon the Kubō-sama, as well as to Lady Ochise(?), three silver coins (gin sanmai) each were given; to those overseeing the reception of guests (o-kyaku-ōsetsu) and the outer messengers (omotezukai), two silver coins each; to all other lady attendants (sō-jochū), thirty silver coins in total; and to the door keepers (o-jōguchi) and middle-ranking senior attendants (o-chūrō), etc. …
