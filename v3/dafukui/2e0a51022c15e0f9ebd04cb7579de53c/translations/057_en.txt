After Lord Gijimaru's relocation, he made his first formal visit to the inner chambers of the Western Citadel (Nishinomaru Ōoku) to offer New Year's greetings. He was dressed in noshime (formal patterned robes) and hemp-cloth formal divided skirts (asa-kamishimo).

　　Note: As on the fifteenth of the previous month, a senior lady-in-waiting (otoshiyori) accompanied him in his palanquin.
　　Item: Two manservants (okobito), serving as attendants, proceeded ahead to the designated waiting area outside the residence.
　　Item: At the Wadakura Gate, Sakashita Gate, and the inner rear gate, one manservant and an attendant were stationed at each.
　　Item: From the Sakashita Gate, one manservant dressed in hemp-cloth formal attire with the skirts tucked up (momодachi) stood before Lord Gijimaru's palanquin and guided the procession as far as the Ohiroshiki (the outer administrative area of the Ōoku).
　　Item: At the Sakashita Gate and the rear gate, one door of each gate was opened, and both the upper and lower guards took the lower seat (gesture of deference). Other passersby similarly assumed a deferential posture.

Both doors of the Ohiroshiki gate were opened, and the head of the Ohiroshiki guards, Andō Tarōemon, came out to receive Lord Gijimaru at the formal entry (shikidai). From the shikidai, male servants received the palanquin, and from the inner locked entrance (jōguchi), lower-ranking ladies-in-waiting handled the palanquin, and Lord Gijimaru entered as far as the inner corridor beyond the locked entrance while still in his palanquin.

　　Note: It had been arranged that he would change into long formal court trousers (nagahakama) in the resting room, but as with the visit to the Main Citadel (Honmaru) on the fifteenth of the previous month, this was announced through a senior lady-in-waiting (rōjochū).

Lord Gijimaru had a formal audience (omemie) with the Retired Shogun (Ōgosho-sama) at the third mat from the lower dais (ge-dan), and the Retired Shogun's words (jōi) were addressed to him. Upon the upper dais (jōdan), Lord Gijimaru personally received a gift of long abalone (naga-awabi) from the Retired Shogun's own hands.
