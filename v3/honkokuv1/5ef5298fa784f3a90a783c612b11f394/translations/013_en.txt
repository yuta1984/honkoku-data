Household goods and furnishings were smashed to pieces and swept across the entire surface of the river. Moreover, the corpses of people as well as horses were likewise torn apart, and a great number of them flowed downstream. After the Hour of the Cock (approximately 8 p.m.), the floating debris began to diminish, and thereafter the flow ceased.

The above is as reported by Kanzō, the village headman (nanushi) of Kanamachi Village in the Higashi-Kasai domain.

　　　7th Month
　　　From Zen'emon and Kahee of Kiryu Village, Jōshū [Kōzuke Province]

The following is a copy of a letter received at the Shirokiya branch paper shop on Edo-dōri 2-chōme, addressed to Shirokiya Shōemon:

Item: On the night of the 2nd day of this 7th month, around the Hour of the Rooster (approximately 6 p.m.), Mt. Asama in Shinshū [Shinano Province] began to rumble, a little ash fell, the sky turned red and black, and [conditions became] like a misty moonlit night. On the 7th of the same month, the situation was as described above, but from around the Hour of the Rabbit (approximately 10 a.m.), [ash] began falling like muddy rain, the rumbling sounds were like thunder, and by around the Hour of the Monkey (approximately 2 p.m.), [the ground] shook as if in an earthquake, and the darkness became extremely severe. Those who were inside their homes were utterly at a loss and fell prostrate, or chanted nenbutsu prayers.

Item: From our location, it is ten ri to Takasaki, five ri to Isesaki, and seven ri to Maebashi [indicating how widespread the effects were in the surrounding region]...
