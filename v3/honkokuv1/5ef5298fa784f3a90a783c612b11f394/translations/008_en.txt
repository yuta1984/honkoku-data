The rumblings resonated even as far as the houses in the surrounding villages, sounding like thunder, lightning, and earthquakes. However, since the smoke was blowing in an eastward direction, sand and ash did not fall in the nearby villages; rather, it is reported that small stones and sand were scattered toward Kōzuke Province (Jōshū). This was reported to me by my deputies (tedai) stationed at the Minage Shinden administrative office (jinya) in Shinano Province. Accordingly, I hereby submit this general report. In addition, a separate document regarding the above matters has been submitted to the Office of Tax Collection (o-torikago-kata) as well.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　37 ri from Edo
Rice yield over 800 koku　　　　　　　　　　Kitamaki Village

The above two villages are located along the Agatsuma River in the same province. On the 9th day of this month, at around the Hour of the Rabbit (approximately 10 a.m.), a volcanic mudflow (yamatsunami), along with mud, rocks, and burning stones, poured forth in enormous quantities. From Kawashima Village as far as the Moku御 Checkpoint (Moku-gosekisho), and through Kitamaki Village, all houses, rice fields, and farmland were entirely swept away. Only a small number of houses on the mountain side survived. The number of people who drowned and died is unknown. We can only surmise that there are likely no survivors. On the off chance that there were those who had gone out to work in the fields or to cut grass (fodder), they may yet be alive — but even if their lives were spared, it is reported that there is nothing in store for them but starvation (katsu-mei).
