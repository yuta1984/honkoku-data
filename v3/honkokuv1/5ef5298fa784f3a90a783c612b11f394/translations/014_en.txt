However, on the morning of the 8th, the day after, we sent messengers to the three locations mentioned. Around the eighth hour (2 AM), when they passed along the river at Maebashi, approximately eighty or more houses were flowing downstream. Although the Tone River had been at only six-tenths of its normal level, within the span of half an hour, the water rose to a height of five or six jō (approximately 15–18 meters). Human corpses, smashed into pieces, flowed downstream in great numbers. Among them, three or four people were piled atop corpses, still alive, calling out "Please save us!" but there was absolutely no way to help them, and everyone fled for their very lives. After the seventh hour (around 4 AM), the tremors suddenly intensified; large and small rocks came flowing downstream while still burning, flames rose up even from within the water, fire leapt into the air, and the smell of smoke entered our noses and eyes — there was nothing to be done. What happened here is far beyond what words and paper can convey. On the morning of the 9th, before the fifth hour (around 7 AM), mud pushed into a place called Shiba-juku to a depth of one jō (approximately 3 meters). Within that mud lay countless dead, and the corpses floating on the surface of the mud were decomposed or charred. There were many dead women and children. Among them, six people were found half-dead, and the local residents gathered together and pulled these six people out, administering various treatments.
