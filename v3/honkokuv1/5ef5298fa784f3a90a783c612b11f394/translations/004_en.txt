The ash and debris accumulated, and it was not until the evening of the 8th that the thunder and lightning finally ceased. As for Nakazato Village, which suffered the same calamity but is situated approximately three ri farther away [from Mount Asama], the accumulated deposits there were about five to six sun in depth. Such is our situation—there has been no disaster of this magnitude in over one hundred years. Our fields and paddies, without distinction, have been rendered as good as ruined, and we are utterly at a loss as to what to do. Not only the standing trees and bamboo groves, but every green thing across the fields and mountains has vanished entirely. Naturally, fodder for our horses is in short supply, and we have fallen into a state of extreme hardship. Furthermore, even if we were to clear away the ash and gravel from our fields, we cannot imagine it being done in time for this year's harvest. We find this situation utterly lamentable. Until midday on the 8th, we were unable even to leave our homes, and therefore could not come forward to make our appeal. With no other recourse and in deep sorrow, we gathered together within our homes, read sutras, and offered our prayers. We humbly and earnestly beseech you, with all urgency, to please send an inspector at the earliest opportunity, so that we, the farmers who are barely clinging to life, may be able to continue our agricultural livelihoods. Respectfully submitted.
