(Continued from previous page) ...after a time, the shaking began from the southeast, and it became a great earthquake. The trembling was so intense that one could doubt whether heaven and earth had not become one. The ground split open for two to three shaku, water welled up from within, mountains collapsed, and people's houses crumbled in succession like a row of falling shogi pieces. People ran to open spaces, and in groups of five or seven, holding each other's hands, they threw themselves face-down upon the ground—yet even so, they were rolled about over distances of three or four ken, turned onto their backs, then flipped face-down again. The great shaking continued for about half an hour (hantoki), then gradually came to a stop. During this time, countless numbers of people lost consciousness.

On the fourth day of the eleventh month of the same year [the fourth year of Hōei, 1707 CE], at the hour of the Ram, there was a great thunderstorm. On the twenty-third day of the same month, Mount Fuji began to erupt from the Subashiri opening at the mountain's foot. The sound of it was like a great clap of thunder. In the surrounding provinces, a great earthquake struck, and ash-like sand rained down, darkening the sky so completely that one could not distinguish anything even at close range—it was as though it were a dark, moonless night.
