(Continued from previous page) ...There were many fires, and in Kyoto and the Kinai region the cold was intense, with passing showers falling every day.

Around the third and fourth days of the seventh month of the same year [Tenmei 3, 1783 CE], great earthquakes continued day and night in Shinano Province and the surrounding provinces, accompanied by thunderous roaring sounds as sand and stones began to fall. On the fifth and sixth days, the rumbling grew increasingly violent, becoming especially severe by nightfall. On the seventh day of the same month, even in broad daylight it became as dark as night; rocks were hurled through the air, and hot ash rained down upon many places in the vicinity. Mount Asama erupted, and Mount Kusatsu and other mountains burst into flame simultaneously, with fierce fire scattering in all directions. The mountain fields and plains at the foot of the mountains instantly became great rivers, with boiling water gushing forth like waterfalls. This torrent poured into the course of the Tone River, washing away the villages nearby.

In the fourth year of Kansei [1792 CE], in the Shimabara domain of Hizen Province, a mountain erupted and hot water gushed forth and flowed, killing many people. The entire province shook, and the light of the fires illuminated the surrounding provinces.

On the third day of the eleventh month of the sixth year [of Kansei, 1794 CE], at the hour of the Rat, a great earthquake struck Edo.
