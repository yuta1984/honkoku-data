The dead were innumerable, and the sounds of weeping and screaming filled the streets.
Furthermore, fires broke out in various places from collapsed houses.
After the hour of the eighth bell, a tsunami
struck, and many people and horses perished in Bōsō.
The inner waterways (rivers and inlets) surged and receded

four times. From this time, the earth
shook numerous more times.
In Sōshū (Sagami Province), Odawara suffered
particularly catastrophic losses, with approximately
2,300 dead. From Odawara to
Shinagawa, 15,000 people (perished);
Bōshū (Awa/Kazusa/Shimōsa), 100,000 people; and Edo, 30,000 (people).
