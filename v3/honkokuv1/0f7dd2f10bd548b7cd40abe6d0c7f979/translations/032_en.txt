In the spring and autumn of the ninth year [of Bunsei, 1826 CE], Edo was struck by earthquakes on several occasions.

On the twelfth day of the eleventh month of the eleventh year [of Bunsei, 1828 CE], a great earthquake struck the area around Nagaoka in Echigo Province.

　A copy of a letter of reply from Sanjo in Echigo Province:

　　Your letter of inquiry has arrived safely. I should have replied to you immediately, but regrettably this has been delayed. As you noted, the cold is gradually deepening, yet I trust you are well on your end. Now, regarding the great earthquake that struck our area, I am most grateful for your kind gift of a box of bonito sent as an expression of your concern. Your thoughtfulness is deeply appreciated. On the morning of the twelfth day of the eleventh month, from around the Hour of the Fifth Bell [approximately 8 o'clock in the morning], a tremendous shaking began, and a great earthquake struck. In a daze, all members of the household crawled out from both the front and back of the house, whereupon our residence and storehouse were reduced entirely to rubble. Furthermore, fire broke out in the vicinity and everything was reduced to smoke in an instant. Among those who had been struck on the spine, shoulders, hips, and head, some were vomiting blood from their eyes, noses, and mouths, writhing in agony as if gone mad in their desperate attempts to flee.
