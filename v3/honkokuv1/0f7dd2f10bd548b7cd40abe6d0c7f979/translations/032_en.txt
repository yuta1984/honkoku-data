In the ninth year [of Bunsei, 1826 CE], earthquakes struck Edo several times in both spring and autumn.

On the twelfth day of the eleventh month of the eleventh year [of Bunsei, 1828 CE], a great earthquake struck the area around Nagaoka in Echigo Province.

　A copy of a letter of reply from Sanjo in Echigo Province:

　　Upon receiving your kind letter of inquiry, I ought to have replied at once, but I have been delayed in doing so. As you noted, the cold season is gradually setting in, yet I trust that all is well on your end. Now, regarding this great earthquake that has struck our area, you have most graciously sent a box of dried bonito as a gesture of sympathy, for which I am deeply and humbly grateful. On the morning of the twelfth day of the eleventh month, at around the hour of the fifth bell [approximately 8 o'clock in the morning], a tremendous shaking began and a great earthquake struck. In a daze, members of the household crawled out from both the front and back of the house, while the residence and the earthen storehouse were reduced to rubble. Furthermore, fire broke out in the surrounding area, and everything was instantly consumed in smoke. Among those who had been struck upon the spine, shoulders, hips, and head, some, vomiting blood from their eyes, noses, and mouths, writhed and struggled as if driven mad in their desperate attempts to escape, unable to endure the suffering any further — and among them were the wounded and the dead, [the account continues on the next page]
