[On the Principles of Earthquakes and the Generation of Tsunamis]

...(the earth has cavities like the cells of a beehive,) water permeates underground, and *yōki* (yang energy / warm vital energy) constantly flows in and out. It is the natural state of things for the yin and yang to harmonize and maintain a proper balance. However, if the yang energy becomes obstructed and cannot escape outward, and months and years accumulate, the earth swells and the water contracts — and for this reason, well water dries up and the atmospheric conditions grow warm. This is just like the way a rice cake (*mochi*), when grilled over fire, puffs up and swells due to the heat. When the earth is about to tremble, the sky seems to be closer and the stars appear nearer — but this is not because the earth is rising and the sky is descending; it is the same principle by which mountains appear closer just before rain is about to fall.

When at last the yang energy that has been lying dormant within the earth bursts forth, this causes the earth to quake and move. For this reason, the shaking is violent at first, then gradually subsides. At this time, great surging waves (*takanami*) from the ocean tide rise up mightily and surge backward — this is what is known as a *tsunami*. This happens because the ground in the area that had been swollen collapses and sinks.
