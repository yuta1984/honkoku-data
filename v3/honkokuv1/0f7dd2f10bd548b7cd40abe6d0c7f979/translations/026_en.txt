(Continued from previous page) ...many stone walls collapsed, and there was widespread damage to both samurai residences and townspeople's houses. Odawara in Sagami Province suffered more severely than other regions, with commoners' houses damaged and destroyed. Hakone mountain collapsed, blocking the roads. Simultaneously, fires broke out in various places and many people died. Those who fled to the coast perished in the tsunami.

The fourth day of the eighth month of the first year of Hōei [the era name having been changed in the third month, 1704 CE]: flooding in Edo and the surrounding provinces. The Tone River, Hachijō River, Nakagawa River, Ayase River, and Ara River rose to great heights; in Katsushika District of Shimōsa Province, [the Sarugama area], the rivers overflowed and the embankments collapsed, the ground sank by several jō in places, causing great losses to fields and farmland, and many people also perished.

The fifteenth day of the ninth month of the third year of the same era [the third year of Hōei, 1706 CE]: a great earthquake in Edo.

The fourth day of the tenth month of the fourth year of the same era [the fourth year of Hōei, 1707 CE], at the hour of the Ram (around 2 p.m.): a great earthquake struck the Tōkaidō region, causing ruptures in the ground. Along the coast, many people perished in the tsunami.
