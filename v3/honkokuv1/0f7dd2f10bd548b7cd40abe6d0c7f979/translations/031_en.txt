In the second year of Bunka [1805 CE], a great earthquake struck Dewa Province.

From the twenty-first to the twenty-fourth day of the second month of the sixth year [of Bunka, 1809 CE], the mountains of the Matsumoto domain in Shinano Province rumbled and shook. At dusk on the twenty-fourth day, the ground cracked and burst open over an area of more than five hundred ken from north to south and more than nine hundred ken from east to west, with rocks collapsing and crumbling. In four villages, including Nakatani Village, and six branch settlements, twenty-seven houses along with their fields sank and subsided into the ground; however, there were no human casualties.

On the first day of the first month of the seventh year [of Bunka, 1810 CE], a great earthquake struck Sado Province and continued without cease for several consecutive days.

On the fourth day of the eleventh month of the ninth year [of Bunka, 1812 CE], a great earthquake struck Edo and the surrounding provinces. The area around Kanagawa and Hodogaya was particularly severely affected, with residential houses collapsing and breaking apart.

On the twelfth day of the sixth month of the second year of Bunsei [1819 CE], a great earthquake struck Kyoto and the surrounding areas of Ise and Mino Provinces.

From the sixteenth to the nineteenth day of the intercalary first month of the fifth year [of Bunsei, 1822 CE], a great earthquake struck Ezo, occurring more than one hundred and fifty times in total.
