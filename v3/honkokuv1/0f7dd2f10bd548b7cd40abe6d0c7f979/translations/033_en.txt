Countless people fled, but wherever they went, the ground split open, spewing sand upward, and men and women of all ages, young and old alike, had their feet caught in the earth as muddy water gushed forth, making it impossible to pass through the streets. The wind blew fiercely, and on top of that, sparks were sent flying, and as people were engulfed in fire and smoke, they fled in all directions — east, west, south, and north. It was a terrifying event, the likes of which have never been heard of in all of history. From Murakami, Shibata, Yoita, Nagaoka, Muramatsu, Kuwana, Aizū, Takasaki, and other domains, the officials of the regional magistrate offices and the hatamoto [direct retainers of the shogun] each extended their assistance and relief, for which we are deeply grateful. The sky grew cloudy and snow began to fall, and as the cold intensified, there was nowhere even to take a little shelter, and it became impossible even to stand still. As the wind continued to blow, there was no way to endure it, and the situation was truly pitiable beyond words. As for my own household, not to mention myself, even among my relatives, there was not a single injured person — please set your mind at ease on that account. The dried bonito you sent as a gesture of sympathy was distributed among everyone during the winter, and thanks to your kindness, our spirits have been lifted.
