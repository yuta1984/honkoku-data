[A Brief Account of Earthquakes (continued)]

...It is for this reason that [the seawater] surges up violently. However, this does not mean that there is an invariable principle whereby an earthquake must always produce a tsunami. It depends solely on whether or not the fire energy arises within the sea. There are also cases in which earthquakes give rise to floods. These occur because the fire energy, upon activating, breaks apart mountain ranges, causes underground water sources to overflow, and pours into rivers and valleys.

Furthermore, as regards tsunamis and their like, damage tends to be greater in areas with narrow mountain valleys, and lesser in flat and open terrain. The reason for this is as follows: in narrow terrain, the force of the water easily surges upward, whereas in flat terrain, there being nothing to obstruct it, the force of the water quickly diminishes. Such, in broad terms, is the nature of these phenomena.
