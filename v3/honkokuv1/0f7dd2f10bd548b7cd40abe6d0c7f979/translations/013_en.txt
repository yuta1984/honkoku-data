[On the Regions Prone to Thunderstorms and Folk Customs for Warding Off Lightning]

In regions such as Shimotsuke (present-day Tochigi Prefecture), Suruga, Tōtōmi, and Mikawa (present-day Shizuoka and Aichi Prefectures), Hokuetsu (the northern Echigo region, present-day Niigata Prefecture), and the Shinshū (Nagano Prefecture) roads and their surroundings — lands with tall mountains — great thunderstorms are frequent throughout the year. For this reason, as shown in the illustration, it is customary to erect a sickle (kama) on top of a house or at the eaves as a method of warding off lightning strikes. This is a practice passed down from ancient times, and there must surely be a meaningful reason behind it. Furthermore, it is said that there are also regions where, during great windstorms, the same practice of standing up a sickle — called "kazakiri" (wind-cutting) — is performed. This too must have its own good reason.
