[On the Transmission of a Method for Detecting Earthquake Precursors Using Magnets]

...(Until this very day,) there had been nothing made clear to the world (regarding earthquake precursors). When a man named Latchmenton [transliteration of a French name] traveled to the Republic of Argentina (*Arukentontens*, noted as a country name) as an envoy of France, a method for detecting earthquake precursors was transmitted to him from a school in Paris (*Parēsu*, noted as the name of the great capital city of France). That method consists of attaching a small piece of iron to a magnet, and nothing else is required. During an earthquake, the magnetic force that attracts the iron temporarily disappears; consequently, the attached iron invariably falls. This falling of the iron is taken as the precursor of an earthquake.

Latchmenton states: A certain accomplished and learned general/officer who had resided for many years in Arequipa (*Arekīha*, noted as a place name) in Argentina (*Arukenten*, noted as a place name) frequently tested and thereby confirmed the magnetic needle's remarkable capability. This is because Arequipa is a region where earthquakes are extremely frequent. This great discovery became known to the world for the first time through the school of Paris...
