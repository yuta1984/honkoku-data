[On Lightning Protection Methods in the West and Japan, and on Earthquake-Resistant Construction]

As recorded in the *Kenrin Hikkei* ("Essential Compendium for Officials"), methods of lightning protection exist in the West. In Japan too, from ancient times, following the building methods of China, three-storied and five-storied pagodas have iron chains drawn from the very top of the *kurin* (the nine-ringed finial) down to the four corners — and this was surely intended as a means of warding off lightning strikes and repelling evil winds (*fūma*).

Here, alongside the method of constructing earthquake-resistant buildings (*jishin-dono*), I also present a method for avoiding lightning damage. The construction method for such a building begins with burying pine logs — laid both lengthwise and crosswise — deep into the ground, layering them to firm the foundation, and following the construction style shown in the illustration. Skilled craftsmen (*ryōshō*) should exercise their ingenuity in various ways. For a simpler approach, four pine logs in the foundation, placed lengthwise and crosswise, should be sufficient to stabilize it. The construction of houses and other buildings should likewise be devised according to these principles. There is no room here to go into full detail, but as for the advantages and disadvantages, skilled craftsmen should earnestly consult knowledgeable persons to arrive at the best approach.

○ Incidentally, it is worth noting that in earthquakes, one should carefully consider the fact that well-balanced shrine buildings (*miyashiro*), halls and pagodas (*dōtō*), and fire-watch towers generally do not collapse. (However, if the ground itself splits open, there may be nothing to be done.)

　　　　　　　Compiled by Hokuho Kanjin (The Recluse of the Northern Peak)

In general, at the time of an earthquake, the weather is remarkably warm, the light of the stars is exceptionally large and bright, and even the smaller stars of the Pleiades (*Bōshō*; the *Subaru* and *Shen* constellations) can be counted with clarity. Kites (*tobi*) soar and crows (*karasu*) cry out — all of these signs first manifest in mutual resonance throughout the great sky, and only then does the earthquake occur.
