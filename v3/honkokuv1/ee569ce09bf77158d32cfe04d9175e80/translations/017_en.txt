First, water should be brought in; second, fire, the horse, the sword, attendants and family (*kenzoku*), and miscellaneous goods; and after all of this, the master of the house enters. Not a single person should enter empty-handed.

1. For those of middling station (*chūjin*) and below, the order is: water, fire, miscellaneous goods, attendants and family, living creatures, and then the master of the house.

1. When the monk Kūkai (Kōbō Daishi) was forty-two years of age, he received an imperial decree (*choku*) and wrote the placard (*gaku*) for the Great Audience Hall (*Daigokuden*). The central stroke of the character *dai* (大, "great") was severed, turning it into *ka* (火, "fire"), making it read *Kagokuden* ("Hall of Fire Extremity") — and thereafter the palace (*ōuchi*) is said to have burned.

1. On land used as a public thoroughfare, even if the width is narrow, alternating stone bridges and earthen bridges should be installed and kept in place, as this can serve as preparation for emergencies. Carts (*kuruma*) may be dispensed with.

The table above shows only the years of great calamity (*taiyaku*) among the Nine Luminaries. The Fire Star (*Kayō-sei*) is not technically a "personal deadly direction" (*tekisetsu*), yet it portends fire disasters, illness, and quarrels. Even in years when one falls under the influence of the other stars listed here, if one performs the Star Festival (*hoshi matsuri*) at the winter solstice each year and carries out acts of releasing living creatures (*hōjō*) and secret virtue (*intoku*, good deeds performed without seeking recognition), then the immediate extinction of the Seven Calamities (*shichi-nan sokumetsu*) is without doubt.
