1. Some people avoid a three-mat (*sanjō-shiki*) room even when it lies in an auspicious, generative direction, on the grounds that three is the number of Fire. It is customary to incorporate associations with Water in a dwelling: roof tiles are often decorated with the *tomoe* (comma-shaped swirl) pattern, which represents the form of waves. Similarly, the *seigaiha* (blue ocean wave) pattern is frequently used on decorative paneling at entryways (*genkan*). However, six-mat rooms (*rokujō-shiki*) are the number of Water, and yet when I examined a small house that had two such six-mat rooms, the yin energy was overwhelming and the situation was exceedingly inauspicious.

Since one should also avoid fiery associations when moving into a new home (*watamashi*), one ought to shun days with the number three, as well as days associated with *hinoe* (丙, Yang Fire), *hinoto* (丁, Yin Fire), *mi* (巳, Snake), and *uma* (午, Horse). Days associated with *mizunoe* (壬, Yang Water), *mizunoto* (癸, Yin Water), *i* (亥, Boar), and *ne* (子, Rat) are Water days and are auspicious. On the occasion of the move, grilled fish and other broiled dishes are unnecessary and should be avoided; hanging scrolls (*kakemono*) featuring ink paintings (*sumie*) or calligraphic ink works (*bokusei*) are appropriate. Furthermore, reckoning direction from the temporary lodging (*kariya*), one should move on an auspicious day selected in advance and clear of such inauspicious forces as the Two Gold Deities (*ryō konjin*), the Permanent Gold Deity (*jō konjin*), the Year-Breaker (*saiha*), the Obstruction (*fusagari*), and the Personal Deadly Direction of the Master of the House (*shujin honmei tekisetsu*).
