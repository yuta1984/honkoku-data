He gave the palanquin bearers two coins (kōhō) and returned home. As the morning wind had grown somewhat strong, he proceeded to store all his furnishings and fusuma sliding doors in the storehouse — and it was just around that time that a fire broke out in Sakuma-chō in Kanda. However, it burned only an empty house, and the storehouse remained unharmed. Truly, it was through the oracle of the Great Teacher (Kōbō Daishi) that he wisely perceived the coming of such a disaster — this is the virtue of a person of faith. The golden saying, "The plan for the day lies in the morning," is indeed a thing to be feared and heeded.

Now, although there is no end to enumerating the fire disasters of Edo, since it is human nature to forget the heat once it has passed the throat (i.e., to forget hardship once it is over), I have written this down — naming it "Bōka Yōjin Tsuchi" (Essential Earthen Precautions Against Fire) — with my humble brush. Heedless of reproach from people near and far, who may find fault with this smoke rising not from Mount Asama but from my own pen, it is my earnest wish that on every day the God of Wind rages, my own children and their kind will take the utmost care.
