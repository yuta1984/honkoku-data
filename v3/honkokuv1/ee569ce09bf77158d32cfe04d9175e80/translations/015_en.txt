1. *Hinoe* (丙, Yang Fire) is the elder brother of Fire, and *hinoto* (丁, Yin Fire) is the younger brother of Fire. The elder (Yang) represents the fire of the hearth (*kamado*), while the younger (Yin) represents lamplight and such. Furthermore, the Tiger and Rabbit [signs] belong to Wood and therefore generate Fire; the Dog, Dragon, Ox, and Sheep [signs] belong to Earth and therefore, from Fire, generate Earth. The principle here is that all things, when burned, become earth. Conversely, Earth can also generate Fire. Earth is the mother of the Five Phases (*gogyō*), and all things in creation are born by receiving the energy (*ki*) of Earth.

1. Before beginning construction on a burned-out lot, one should perform the prayers of a land-purification ritual (*jijimatsuri*). For impure sites, any buried jars or vessels should be removed, and one should dig to a depth of four shaku four sun (approximately 1.3 meters), disposing of the old soil and replacing it with clean earth, before beginning construction of the new dwelling. There are other methods for warding off impurity; these will be provided upon request.

1. There is in the world the practice known as *kasō* (house physiognomy / geomantic divination for dwellings). Originally, in China (*Morokoshi*), it was used exclusively for the mansions and pavilions of nobles, and was not employed by commoners or those of lower station. In recent times, however, the practice of house-divination reading (*kasōmi*) has spread throughout the various provinces — and yet, those who can truly assess the broad fundamentals (*taikyoku*) of an old established house are few. In this regard, the school of Takaoka (*Takaoka no ichiryū*) is worthy of trust.
