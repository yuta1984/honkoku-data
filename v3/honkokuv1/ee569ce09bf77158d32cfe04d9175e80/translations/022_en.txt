**Record of Major Fires in Edo, with the Twenty-Eight Lunar Mansions and the Five Phases (continued)**

1. **The 7th year of Bunsei, the year Kinoe-Saru (甲申, 1824)** — 1st day of the 2nd month
[Lunar mansion: *Hitsu* (畢宿, "Net"); Phase: Earth (*tsuchi*)] — *Izumi no Mizu* ("Water of the Spring")

Fire spread from Kanda Sankawachō as far as Nihonbashi.

2. **The 11th year of Bunsei (same era), the year Tsuchinoe-Ne (戊子, 1828)** — 5th day of the 2nd month
[Lunar mansion: *Ki* (鬼宿, "Ghost"); Phase: Water (*mizu*)] — *Kamado no Hi* ("Fire of the Hearth/Stove")

Fire spread from Kanda Tachō through the Honchō area, reaching as far as Kamakuragashi and Ogawamachi.

3. **The 12th year of Bunsei (same era), the year Tsuchinoto-Ushi (己丑, 1829)** — 16th day of the 2nd month
[Lunar mansion: *Ryū* (柳宿, "Willow"); Phase: Wood (*ki*)] — same fire type (*Kamado no Hi*, "Fire of the Hearth")

Fire spread from Otowachō as far as the Sugamo area.

　　— Also: on the 21st day of the 3rd month, during the middle of the Hour of the Snake (c. 10 a.m.), starting from Sotokanda Sakumachō riverbank — to the east, fire reached as far as Ryōgoku, Ōkawabata, and Tsukudajima; to the west, as far as the Ohori-bata-dōri (the moat-side street); to the south, it stopped at Shibaguchi Kawagishi-dōri and Shiodome. To the southeast (the direction of *Tatsumi*), fire reached as far as the Tsukiji shoreline. The Western Hall (*Nishi Midō*), three theater bridges (*shibai-bashi*), more than sixty earthen storehouses (*dozō*), approximately 1,200 or more buildings, and an uncountable number of large and small boats were destroyed. Nine emergency shelters (*osukui-goya*) were erected, and people donated rice, money, and various goods. The number of those who perished in the fire or drowned was beyond reckoning.

　　— Also: from the 8th to the 10th day of the 5th month, memorial rites for hungry spirits (*segaki*) were performed at temples of various Buddhist sects.

　　— Also: on the 6th day of the 4th month, during the Hour of the Sheep (c. 1–3 p.m.), with southerly winds blowing, fire spread from Azabu Nagasaka as far as Iikura Katamachi, the Tanigawa-chō area, and the Tamike-bata (the shore of Tameike Pond).
