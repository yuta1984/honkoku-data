...(their residences are fitted out) with nageshi-style shoin studies, three-story earthen storehouses, and hipped roofs—extravagances utterly unbefitting their station in life—yet they remain unaware that divine punishment will befall them. Furthermore, even after suffering a fire, they proceed to rebuild their homes in an even more lavish manner than before—truly a lamentable state of affairs. Women in particular spend enormous sums on cosmetics and toiletries, which serve no protective purpose against evil; they seek out luxury goods at will, from footwear down to their loincloths. No matter how rare a treasure or fine an object may be, when it is consumed along with the storehouse in a moment's smoke, one can only weep—it is the height of futility. Many people do not understand the proper use of gold and silver. If one were to keep the possibility of fire in mind, would it not be far better to be moderate and restrained in all things?

Even among townspeople's households, if the wife is that of someone holding the rank of *noshime* or above, or serving as a purveyor to the authorities (*goyōtashi*), she may wear a *uchikake* robe of white or black ground at the time of her wedding without censure. However, when a husband wears a subdued *fusa-kosode* while his wife dons *rinzu* (glossy silk twill), such people disregard the regulations set down by the authorities, and though they flout the proper order of rank through the sheer power of their wealth, because there is neither reprimand nor anyone to report them, many remain ignorant that their conduct is in the wrong.
