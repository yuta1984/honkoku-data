1. Once every month, on the day of the Dragon (*tatsu no hi*), at the Hour of the Dragon (*tatsu no koku*), draw water from a well located in the direction of the Dragon (east-southeast), and have a person born in the Year of the Dragon sprinkle it upon the roof. This is a charm for fire prevention. However, if no well in the direction of the Dragon nor a person born in the Year of the Dragon is available, this step may be omitted—but one must not forget to observe the correct time and day.

1. There is something called "a western eave (*nishi-hisashi*) on an eastern ridgepole (*higashi no mune*)." If one extends a ridgepole from the center of the house directly toward the east, and then extends the eaves down toward the due west, it is said that a misfortune will certainly befall that household. As this has been borne out in practice, one should be deeply cautious about it.

1. There are many inauspicious days at the outset of house construction; one should carefully choose the day, and the act of using a water level (*mizumori*) when laying the foundation stones and base timbers itself serves as a fire prevention measure. Beyond this, one should offer prayers and practice hidden virtue (*intoku*—performing good deeds without seeking recognition).
