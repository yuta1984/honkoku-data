To speak in terms of astronomical calendrical reckoning (*lixiang*): in the time of the sage-king Yao, the day of the winter solstice had the sun positioned at the lunar lodge *Xu* (虚, the Void). During the Zhou, Han, Tang, and Song dynasties, it was invariably in *Dou* (斗, the Dipper). In the past three hundred years it has been in *Ji* (箕, the Winnowing Basket). Having already shifted and migrated in succession, one can see, can one not, that the fixed stars (*hengxing*) arrayed across Heaven — which are now moving southward — will later move northward? That where now it is *Ji*, later it will return again to *Xu*? That the current position (*chan*) of *Ji* and *Dou* produces short days, while in a future age the station (*she*) of *Shen* (参, Orion) and *Jing* (井, the Well) will produce long nights? Who governs and maintains all of this? Who drives and causes it to proceed?

Master Ziliu (游子六, i.e., You Yi 游藝) took the stationary Heaven as his foundation, took the north and south celestial poles as his pivotal axis, and used the oblique winding of the ecliptic (*huangdao*) around the celestial equator (*chidao*) — following the sun's entry into the northern and southern hemispheres — as his standard of reference. From this, the movements of the moon and stars, the seasons and their climatic signs, the divisions of day and night, the differences of dawn and dusk, the lengthening of days in winter and spring, and the shortening in summer and autumn — none of these can be understood except through the shifting and migration of the ecliptic, and all of them can thereby be grasped. It is for this reason that, having at last read it through, I found it resonating deeply with my own heart. It clarifies and elucidates — nothing more than that. It questions and corrects — nothing more than that. And yet how truly profound it is!

Master Pingzi (平子先生, i.e., Zhang Heng 張衡) said: "The Great Undifferentiated (*taisu*) began as a faint stirring, without yet any sign or omen. It was a unified body of uniform color; the primal yin (*kuntun*) had not yet divided." Master Hengqu (横渠先生, i.e., Zhang Zai 張載) said: "Qi fills the Great Void (*taixu*) in vast and boundless profusion." He also said: "The sun, moon, and five planets all rotate to the left (*zuoxuan*)." At the time these words were spoken, the matter had not yet been fully worked out.

My ancestor (*taizu*), having observed the celestial phenomena, came to firmly conclude that the Seven Luminaries (*zhengzheng*, i.e., the sun, moon, and five planets) all rotate to the right (*youxuan*). This was, I believe, because he had perceived that the retrograde degrees (*tuidù*) are convenient for calculations (*busuan*). Master Ziliu then thoroughly investigated to the utmost the movement of the governing qi and the subtle workings of the divine transformation (*shenhua*), and with unwavering resolve, he illuminated: why Heaven moves; why Earth is still; how wind and rain, clouds and thunder, snow and dew, rainbows and the Milky Way flow and are distributed everywhere. Furthermore, he clearly discerned that the moon's actual motion (*shixing*) is slow and does not keep pace with the degrees of Heaven; that Jupiter, Mars, and Saturn (*mu, huo, tu*) are somewhat slower than the sun; that Venus and Mercury (*jin, shui*) revolve around the sun in small epicycles (*xiaolun*) and travel somewhat swiftly; and the mutual production of water and fire, the comings and goings of heat and cold, the ebb and flow of the tides — he illuminated all of these as the constant and immutable canons (*changjing*) of Heaven, vivid and exact (*zuozuo*), not to be altered.
