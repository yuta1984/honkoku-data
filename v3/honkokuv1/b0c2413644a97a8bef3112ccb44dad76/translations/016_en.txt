(...continued from the previous page) ...each having its upper and lower position. Ziliu held that the Seven Luminaries (*qī yào*) and the arrayed lunar lodges (*liè xiù*) are affixed to Heaven, revolving in layer upon layer, enveloping the Earth. Zixuan held that Heaven has left and right, but has no east, west, south, or north. Ziliu held that the meridian (*zǐwǔ*) of the Earth is still and fixed in position, while the meridian of Heaven moves and does not remain in one place. Ziliu championed the theory of the Nine Heavens (*jiǔ chóng*), whereby each of the Seven Luminaries is bound to one layer of Heaven. Zixuan swept all of this away and dissolved it entirely, saying: it is like the stones lodged in a mountain — whether at the peak, the midslope, or the foot — though they differ in height, they are in truth one body. Ziliu said of the precession of the equinoxes (*suìchā*): it is the result of the fixed stars (*héng xīng*) advancing eastward and creating a discrepancy with the solar cycle; it does not differ from the intrinsic motion of the Seven Governors (*qī zhèng*) along the ecliptic (*huángdào*). Zixuan said: there is in origin no discrepancy (*chā*) in the year; it is only that, as the fixed stars (*jīng xīng*) complete their revolution of Heaven and shift their degrees — sometimes slower, sometimes faster — they merely appear to have a discrepancy. Zixuan's insight is truly a new language (*xīn yǔ*) that captures Heaven. Ziliu's understanding directly gazes into the innermost depths (*kǔn'ào*) of Heaven and Earth. The two men's explanations have their agreements and disagreements; yet what they agree upon naturally coheres without need of argument, and what they disagree upon ultimately converges upon the same conclusion. How remarkable is Ziliu's learning! Is it not entirely fitting that Zixuan should so urgently recommend him without reserve?

Now, it has long been the case that Heavenly learning (*tiānxué*, astronomy) has not been transmitted. The words of the ancient sages and worthies were concise, but not detailed. The men of idle discourse (*yóután zhī shì*) were detailed, but not comprehensive. As for the court astronomers and calendar specialists (*tái guān chóu rén*), they kept to the established forms of their predecessors, consulted the old texts of the past, guarded their specialization and clung to antiquity, demonstrating their skill only in the petty matters of omens and auspices (*jī xiáng*), and forgetting the grand matter of the movement and stillness of Heaven and Earth. Western scholars (*Xī Rú*) opened the initial path, and Ziliu together with Zixuan took up its thread. If there exist in the world those of brilliant intelligence who will not allow this interrupted learning to fall into oblivion, and who, at the advent of the *wǔ* cycle (*wǔ yùn*), illuminate the learning of the Yellow Emperor and Qi Bo, and give wondrous expression to the function of the Six Directions and the Central Five (*liùhé zhōngwǔ zhī yòng*), then this will surely have its origins in Ziliu. I have therefore inscribed this at the beginning of the volume, and along with it submit it to Zixuan for his consideration.
