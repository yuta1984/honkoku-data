Among those [throughout successive generations who were learned in astronomy], there were figures such as Luoxia Hong, He Chengtian, the monk Yixing, Wang Pu, Li Chunfeng, and Guo Shoujing, who each in turn inherited and transmitted [this knowledge]. All of them were rooted in the *Book of Changes* (Yi).

Shennong once tasted the hundred herbs, classified medicinal substances, examined their properties of cold, warm, neutral, and hot, distinguished the principles of their roles as sovereign, minister, assistant, and guide, and thereby composed a book of medical formulas. The Yellow Emperor (Huangdi) probed upward into the ordinances of Heaven and plumbed downward into the principles of Earth; he examined the Five Vital Forces (wuqi), established the Five Movements (wuyun), penetrated deeply into the nature of life and destiny, recorded the workings of yin and yang, and composed the *Inner Classic* (Neijing). Tang Yao reverently followed the great Heaven, ordered the calendar according to the movements of the sun, moon, and stars, and respectfully bestowed the seasons upon the people. Yu Shun illuminated the Xuanji Yuheng (the armillary sphere) and brought the Seven Regulators (the sun, moon, and five planets) into alignment. The Great Yu leveled the land and accomplished what Heaven ordained, governing the Six Treasuries and the Three Affairs with sincerity. Cheng Tang reverently exalted the Way of Heaven and permanently upheld the Heavenly Mandate. King Wen descended to dwell at the left and right of the Heavenly Lord (embodying the divine will). King Wu proclaimed: "Heaven and Earth are the father and mother of all things." The *Rites of Zhou* (Zhouli) distinguished the omens of good fortune, calamity, flood, drought, abundance, and famine through the appearance of the Five Clouds, and discerned the harmony of Heaven and Earth through the Twelve Winds, determining the ominous and auspicious signs of discord and disharmony. The *Zhou Yi* (Book of Changes with its Ten Wings) elaborated upon the Ten Wings, contemplated the transformations of yin-yang and firm-yielding (gang-rou), exhaustively explored the principles of nature, destiny, the Way, and virtue, and thereby transmitted its teachings to posterity.
