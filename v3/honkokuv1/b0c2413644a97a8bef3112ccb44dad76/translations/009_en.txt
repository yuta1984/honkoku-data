Rather than subjecting all of them to thorough criticism, we have merely held up the principal ones as golden and silver markers [i.e., prominent signposts of error].

Item Three: As for the explanation of the movements of the sun, moon, and five planets, this is treated in somewhat adequate detail. However, upon reaching the chapter on "rightward motion" [the westward diurnal motion of the heavens], the text draws entirely from the unsubstantiated theories of Song Confucian scholars, and does not accord with what celestial learning (*tengaku*) and calendar specialists have established or argued. As a result, the text is inconsistent from beginning to end. We humbly ask that gentlemen of learning examine this with due care.

Item Four: Throughout the text, many calendrical figures (*rekisū*) are presented. Their values in arc-minutes and arc-seconds do not correspond to those of the *Juji* Calendar or the *Jikken* Calendar. Furthermore, the statements that the maximum equation of the sun (*eishuku kyokusa*) is eight degrees and twenty-five minutes, and that the solar eclipse limit is eight degrees for the yang calendar and twenty-one degrees for the yin calendar, each have their own separate explanations, and all other such values have been treated in the same manner.

Item Five: The distances in *ri* for the Nine Heavens are not derived from the explanations of You Ziliu (You Yi / 游藝). They appear to be taken from the *Ryōgi Genran* (*Liangyi Xuanlan*), where the so-called "distances in *ri* from the earth" are in conflict with the orbital dimensions of the five planets' movements. Moreover, the Primum Mobile (*Sōdōten*) has no tangible form or shape — how, then, could it possibly be measured? Upon reflection, the figures given in the *Genran* for the distances from the earth and the sizes of the seven luminaries are nothing more than the old theories of Western scholars (*seijū*). Today, neither China nor the West employs them any longer. We ask that this be duly noted.

Item Six: What is recorded in this book — for instance, the discussions of the "epicycle" (*shōron*), the "apogee" (*saikō*), the "perigee" (*saiki*), and the "eccentric circle" (*fudōshinsken*), and also the discussion of the orbital revolutions of the three planets Jupiter, Saturn, and Mars — is likewise sketchy and incomplete, and cannot surpass the theories of Europe (*Ōropa*) or the Islamic astronomers (*Kaikal*). We ask that gentlemen of learning be fully aware of this.
