The *Spring and Autumn Annals* attached events to days, days to months, months to seasons, and seasons to years, recording in full all heavenly disasters, earthly upheavals, and the strange occurrences among insects, plants, and trees, thereby presenting warnings to the world. There was no sage of antiquity who did not rely upon this Way (the study of astronomy and calendrical learning). Even after the Three Dynasties, among the Confucian scholars such as Dong Zhongshu, Liu Xiang, Cai Yong, Guo Xiang, Cheng (Yi and Hao), Zhang (Zai), Shao (Yong), and Zhu (Xi), there was none who did not discuss celestial phenomena and measure calendrical numbers. From Qibo onward, the great physicians such as Yu Fu, Lei Gong, Bian Que, Cang Gong, Liu (Wansu), Zhang (Zhongjing), Zhu (Danxi), and Li (Shizhen) all understood astronomy, studied the computation of celestial movements (tuibu), and thoroughly investigated medicine, drugs, and needle-stone therapies (bianjì).

This being so, the two schools of Confucian scholars and physicians have especial reason not to neglect devoting their minds to this field of learning. How much more so for the ruler, who governs the world by modeling himself upon Heaven. Now, a sage of brilliant wisdom sits above; the seas do not raise their waves; the Seven Luminaries (qiwei) move in their proper courses and inauspicious vapors dissipate; the Five Planets are in harmony and rain and sunshine follow [Heaven's will]. Even military officials, in their leisure from archery and chariot-driving, are all able to esteem culture and love learning, and there is none who does not diligently practice the teachings of filial piety, brotherly respect, loyalty, and trustworthiness. The beauty of the scholarly spirit may truly be said to surpass all previous ages.
