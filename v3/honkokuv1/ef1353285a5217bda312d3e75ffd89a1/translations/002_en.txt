Compiled through the third month (Hanamatsuki) of the fourth year of Kaei, Year of the Boar (1851)　Ōharamachi, Ōmachi, Kita-Azumi District, Nagano Prefecture
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Owned by Takizawa Jūkichi

On the night of the twenty-fourth day of the third month of Kōka 4, the Year of the Hinoto-Hitsuji (1847), a great earthquake struck. In the domain of Matsushiro, Iwakura-yama and a place called Magose collapsed. The Kokūzō-dō (a small temple hall dedicated to Ākāśagarbha Bodhisattva) located at the summit of Iwakura-yama fell to the level of approximately four-fifths of the way down the mountain. Three pine trees were toppled onto the hall, burying it in the landslide debris. The extent of the collapse was approximately 8 chō (about 872 meters) along the river, approximately 130 ken (about 236 meters) wide, and approximately 72 ken (about 131 meters) high. Furthermore, on the northern slope of Iwakura, at a place called Oyaki-Furujuku, the rear mountain behind Anniwa was pushed around, collapsing into a place called Shimo-Nagai. The height of the resulting embankment was a gradual slope of about 1 chō (approximately 109 meters), about 2 chō (approximately 218 meters) wide, and about 10 ken (approximately 18 meters) along the river, where it came to rest in a double layer downstream. Furthermore, deep within the Tojiri River, a landslide occurred and blocked the channel, forming a pond. As a result, not a single drop of water flowed as far as Tabajima. At this time, Zenkō-ji Temple was in the midst of its periodic public display of its sacred image (Gokaicho).
