**Right folio:**

Relief measures were provided, it is said. Now, as for the lord of Matsushiro (the Sanada domain), in order to pray for the souls of those who had perished due to homes crushed by the earthquake, or lost to fire or flood, he instructed the domain's principal temple, Chōkokuji, as well as temples of all Buddhist sects throughout the domain, to conduct sutra-reading ceremonies (*dokkyō*) at Saijoyama. Afterward, the priests returned to their respective temples, where they performed *segaki* (hungry ghost) rites and distributed alms (*segyō*) for the deceased among their parishioners, and grave tablets (*tōba*) were bestowed upon them. Even wayfarers (travelers who had perished) were treated the same and received *tōba*. All of this was supported through offerings (*ofuse*) made by the lord to each temple, and at the depth of his benevolence and compassion, the people were moved to tears. Around that time, the magistrates (*bugyō*) who had been dispatched to various locations returned to their posts, and the deputy magistrate (*daikan*) dispatched to Matsumoto conveyed the following message:

**Left folio:**

"Henceforth, revere your superiors, strive above all in loyalty and filial piety, and devote yourselves in faith to the gods and buddhas" — so it was proclaimed. These were truly grateful words, beyond what could be expressed in speech. At that time, on the fourteenth day of the fourth month, they (the officials) returned to their posts.

Furthermore, following the great earthquake, on that very night there were some sounds of thunder, and the earth shook repeatedly. From the next day onward, the thunder gradually subsided, but until around the eighth day of the fourth month, there were numerous earthquakes too many to count. From this point on, frequent earthquakes continued to occur, and their dates are recorded below on the left:

The night of the eighth day of the fourth month; the thirteenth day of the fourth month; the twenty-seventh day of the fourth month; the twenty-eighth day of the fourth month — after the earthquake, the setting sun was said to be truly crimson in color, and people remarked upon it.
