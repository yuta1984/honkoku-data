Those living along the (Sai River) need hardly be mentioned, but even the people of the Ariake village (area) feared that (the land) might revert to the ancient lake it once was, and great numbers of people went on pilgrimage to the Kannon of Hotokesaki. The lord of Matsumoto Castle conveyed instructions to the chief headmen (ōshōya) of each district, and it is said that a proxy pilgrimage (daisan) was dispatched to the Grand Shrine of Kashima in Hitachi Province. This occurred after the waters had receded.

Furthermore, an inspection was made of Misumi-dera (temple), and (the lord) declared, "There is nothing to worry about." The common people likewise reasoned that, although Mount Iwakura is fundamentally a soil mountain, and though large boulders had indeed fallen from within it, because it is a mountain of deep earth, (the boulders) fell during the great earthquake; and since Misumi-dera stands on cliffs of solid single-slab rock on both banks, there should be no danger of rockfalls for ten thousand years—

—and so they felt relieved. Yet the vast sea of water from below continued to rise day by day, brimming and threatening to overflow, and people were at a loss as to what to do. They lay awake through the nights with worry, and during the day could not keep their hands to their work.

Furthermore, the people of the villages below the Hanakura embankment—all the way through Kawanakajima and into Echigo Province—feared that the waters might surge upon them at any moment. All climbed to the mountains, and everyone lived in shelters outside (their homes). Then, on the thirteenth day of the Fourth Month, at the hour of the seventh bell (around 4 p.m.), the embankment that had been thrust and buried at Hanakura broke. The roar resounded throughout heaven and earth, and all at once the waters swept away in a great rush. By the time of the sixth bell of evening (around 6 p.m.), the waters had gradually subsided to their normal level.

And so, I shall record below the villages in the Matsushiro domain that were struck by the floodwaters.
