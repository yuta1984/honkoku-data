An Account of the Great Earthquake, Fires, and Flood at Zenkoji Temple in Shinano Province

On the twenty-fourth day of the third month of the year Koka 4 (1847), the cyclical year of Hinoto-Hitsuji (Yin Fire-Sheep), the Gokaicho — the special public display of the sacred image at Zenkoji Temple — was in full progress. As a result, the temple precincts and the surrounding town were bustling with visitors, and at night countless lanterns lit up the area as brightly as midday, making it a scene of great festivity and prosperity.

However, on that very night, at the Hour of the Boar (around 10 o'clock in the evening), a sudden great earthquake struck with a roar like mighty thunder. The temples and the town were simultaneously shaken to pieces, and everything was plunged into darkness. Before long, fires broke out at several locations and spread into a great conflagration. The sounds of wailing and crying from every household echoed throughout the heavens and earth. Those who had managed to escape unharmed struggled desperately to rescue their parents, spouses, children, and siblings, and there was no one with the means or wherewithal to fight the fires.
