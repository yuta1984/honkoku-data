In the upper reaches of the Susobana River, at a place called Kawaura within Kinasa village, a mountain had collapsed due to the earthquake of the twenty-fourth day, damming the water — but when this dam was suddenly breached all at once, the water level surged and rushed out. However, it did not amount to an especially severe flood, and the villages were greatly relieved. Further upstream in the mountains, at a place called Kyōhara, the Fujisawa-gumi had eighteen households, of which only four remained standing; eighty-four people perished. At a place called Shirokoshi in Umegi village, all four houses were completely destroyed, and twenty-eight people died — not a single person in that place survived. Ōta-gumi had nine houses, of which not one remained; fifty-three people died, and in addition many unidentified persons also perished — only a single person in the entire village survived. Ori village, Arai-gumi, had thirteen houses and fifty-seven inhabitants, of whom not one survived. Nenbutsuji village's Gaunin — a rare and great temple within the domain —

fell into the valley, and after catching fire was buried under the earth. The vicinity of this village was entirely destroyed and buried under the earth as well. There are a great many such places in the mountains as noted hereafter, but they cannot all be recorded in detail.

　　　　　On the Flooding of the Sai River

Upstream from the Tabashima ferry crossing, near the boundary between Sarashina District and Minochi District, near Minochi Bridge, at a place called Akaiwa village, a mountain collapsed and buried a valley dozens of jō deep. Furthermore, at a village called Yashiro village, mountain collapses occurred at two locations, filling the valleys and blocking the swift current; the water accumulated for several days.
