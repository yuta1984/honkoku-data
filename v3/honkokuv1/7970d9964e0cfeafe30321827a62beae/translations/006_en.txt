The main hall came through unharmed. Every night, several hundred traveling pilgrims would normally lodge in the main hall for overnight vigils (tsuya), and on that particular night an unusually large number were gathered there — yet not a single person was injured, and all emerged safely. This was truly a matter for deep gratitude.

Item: The following temple-domain town quarters were destroyed by fire: Daimon-chō (upper and lower), Nagano-chō, Nishi-chō (upper and lower), Nishinomon (upper and lower), Amidain-chō, Tatemachi, Gomon-mae, O-nagaya, Sakura-kōji, Ara-machi, Shin-machi, Ise-machi, Yokoyama-kōji, Iwashi-chō, Tōzai-yokochō, Iwashi-kōji, Takei, Higashi-machi (upper and lower), Hirō-kōji, Hanaya-kōji, Shin-kōji, Kanei-kōji, Nanatsu-gama, Gochō (upper and middle), Gondō-machi and Ura-chaya-machi, and additionally Kawarazaki and Yokō-daimon.

Kataha (and others). At the time, the temporary sanctuary for the sacred image of the Nyorai had been erected in the direction of Ushi-Tora (northeast), in the open fields known as Horikiri. Under the guardianship of the Daikanjin and the three associated temples (sanjū-naka), along with the officials in charge, the御開帳 (special exhibition of the sacred image) was concluded without incident.

Item: Deaths by crushing within the temple precinct — eight resident monks. (Breakdown: three from the senior monks' group; five from the mid-ranking monks' group.)

Item: Deaths by crushing within the town — more than two thousand people.

Item: Regarding the places along the Hokurikudō road north of Zenkōji that were destroyed by the earthquake: Yokoyama was completely leveled; Ainoki suffered collapse and additionally several dozen buildings were burned. Oshigane village and Yoshida village were more than half destroyed. All the post towns and villages along the way as far as Sekikawa-juku in Echigo Province were destroyed.
