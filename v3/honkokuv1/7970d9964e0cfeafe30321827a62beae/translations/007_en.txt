(From Zenkōji to Sekikawa,) the distance is approximately ten ri. Along this road, at a place called Yoshimura, a nearby mountain collapsed and the inhabitants were buried alive under the earth. Echigo Takada was half destroyed. Imamachi was completely leveled by four earthquakes beginning on the twenty-fourth day of the third month, and then on the twenty-ninth day of the same month, a great wind and great fire burned the remainder to nothing.

Item: From Zenkōji to Iiyama is eight ri. Along this highway, Sanzai village, Ishi village, and Asano village were half destroyed, and within the various villages, much damage to fields and paddies was caused by flooding. The castle of the Iiyama domain was completely leveled — the entire castle site was flattened, with the ground rising and falling by more than one jō (approximately three meters) in various places. The town below was entirely heaved upward and completely destroyed, and on top of that, fire broke out and burned everything to nothing. Iiyama, being situated in a low-lying depression, had always been prone to occasional flood damage;

however, this time, because the flat ground had risen overall, there was no flooding — yet a great many people perished. From that area through the valleys of Echigo Province, many places were destroyed, with flooding causing damage to fields and paddies across several villages all the way to Niigata, a total stretch of roughly sixty ri, all of it in a state of great devastation.

Item: Following the great earthquake of the night of the twenty-fourth day of the third month, the Sai River decreased day by day in its water level, and the ferry crossing at Tabashima was reduced to a single crossing point. The upper reaches of this river flow out from the Susobana River, but that flow too diminished day by day, gradually ceasing altogether, until the Tamba River ran dry and could be crossed on foot. The villages throughout Kawanakajima and beyond were thrown into alarm; the inhabitants each built shelters in the nearby mountain forests, retreated there, and waited for the feared flood surge.
