[Continued from previous page:] Yorozuchō, Aomonomachi, Otowachō, Kiwara-dana, Hiramatsu-chō, Sanai-chō, Komatsu-chō, Kawase-ishichō, Minami-Aburachō, Shin'emon-chō, Hakoya-chō, Nushichō, Matsukawa-chō, Suzuki-chō, Jōwa-chō, Ōta-yashiki (Ōta residence), Gusoku-chō, Sumi-chō, and Takegashi survive. Tōri first block: the riverside side of Shirokiya burns. Nishi-gashi (West riverside); Gofuku-chō first and second blocks: one side survives. Tōri second, third, and fourth blocks; Nakabashi Hirōkōji; Minami-Denmachō second and third blocks; also Hosokawa-yashiki (Hosokawa residence); Moto-Daiku-chō; Sukiya-chō; Dōju-yashiki (Dōju residence); Himono-chō; Mishima-yashiki (Mishima residence); Kami-Maki-chō; Tomi-Maki-chō; Minami-Maki-chō; Okemachi first and second blocks; Minami-Daiku-chō first and second blocks; Minami-Kajichō first and second blocks; Kanō-yashiki (Kanō residence); Tatamichō; Gorōbē-chō; and Kita-Kon'ya-chō—the fire stops here. Furthermore, sparks fly as far as the inner citadel district (Marunouchi), burning the residence of Matsudaira Sagaminokami, though a small portion of the long tenement row (nagaya) survives. That night, around the fifth half-hour (approximately 9 p.m.), the fire is extinguished, and the people breathe a sigh of relief.
