The elder spoke, saying: "Alas, do you not know? Our ancestor [Master Sōkei] devoted many years to the rigorous study of Confucian learning. Among his writings were works such as *Shukitsu Butsugi* ('Queries on the Investigations of Zhu [Xi]'), *Tō Shushi Bikyō* ('Faint Echoes from the Streams of Zhu [Xi] and Si [River],' i.e., of Confucius), and *Shisho Shiseki Sanka Shika* ('Pointing Out the Flaws of the Three Schools in the Four Books')—some completed, others still unfinished drafts—when he unhappily passed from this world. I wished to carry on his aspirations, but from a young age I had already committed my person to official service, was consumed by public duties, and moreover suffered from frequent illness; thus I have yet to accomplish this. As for this particular book: long ago, our ancestor was taking a cure at the hot spring inn (*gekiyo*), when the proprietor, perceiving our ancestor's great learning and wide knowledge, pressed him with questions about hot springs. Our ancestor thereupon drew upon ancient sources and adduced contemporary evidence, speaking through the entire night. The proprietor was greatly delighted. At the time, I was present nearby and merely jotted down some notes. Could our ancestor have ever intended this to be circulated in the world? To engrave it on woodblocks now, I fear, would not be in accordance with our ancestor's wishes. Nevertheless, to relieve the world and benefit people was what our ancestor desired. Between storing it away deep in the mountain retreat, filling the bellies of bookworms, and setting it to the woodblock to bring benefit to the world—which is the better course?" And so it was resolved to commission the woodblock carvers...
