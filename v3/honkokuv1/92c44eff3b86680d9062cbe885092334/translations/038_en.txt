One item: Of late, I had occasion to read a work called Yakusen (薬選, "Selection of Medicines"), written by Kagawa Taichū, a disciple of Gotō Saichi of Kyoto. In its sequel volume, he takes up the subject of hot springs and, rejecting the theory that "hot springs are brought to a boil by sulfur," cites the view of Ina Jakusui instead, arguing as follows: "Within the earth there are veins of water and veins of fire; when a vein of fire encounters a vein of water, a hot spring is formed."

That this theory is correct is indeed correct. Yet it is an exceedingly rough and superficial account. Furthermore, this is nothing more than an idea borrowed from Yū Shiroku (游子六), and was not a theory first proposed by Jakusui. However, since the Tenkei Wakumon (天経或問, "Questions and Answers on Heavenly Principles") is a work that only recently arrived in Japan, it is possible — in one chance in ten thousand — that Jakusui had not seen it and happened to arrive independently at a view that coincidentally matched its theory. Nevertheless, Taichū, unaware that this was Yū Shiroku's theory, appears to have believed it to have been a view first advanced by Jakusui...
