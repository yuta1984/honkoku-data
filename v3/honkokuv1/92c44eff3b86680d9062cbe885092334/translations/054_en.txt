...Just as arrangements were being made to commission the woodblock carvers, before long, in the eighth month of autumn, the elder [my father] died of illness. Alas, how grievous! It has now been twenty-eight years since our ancestor [Master Sōkei] passed from this world. I was never able to receive instruction at his knee, leaning on his sword and looking up to his face; and my father, too, ascended to the register of the dead before reaching the age of fifty, unable to complete his work. O vast Heaven—whom do you favor? This year, in the sixth month of summer, in the year *Kinoe-Tora* [甲寅], I have had *Onsen Shōgen* ('Minor Remarks on Hot Springs') engraved on woodblocks, quietly carrying on the aspirations of my predecessors. I have accordingly set forth the words of my father [the elder] and respectfully composed this postscript. As for our ancestor's virtue, achievements, and scholarship, the preface by Master Hokuzan fully expounds upon them. I therefore venture no further superfluous words here.

&emsp;Grandson, Zenpaku Kei, [courtesy name] Kansō, prostrating twice in reverence, respectfully composed.
