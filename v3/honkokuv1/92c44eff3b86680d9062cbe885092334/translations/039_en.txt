Furthermore, Taichū's theory that "sulfur is something produced by hot springs — sulfur is, in short, the residue discharged by thermal springs" is likewise mistaken. As stated above, hot springs well up through the yang-fire (陽火) within the earth, and sulfur too is formed when the yang-fire within the earth steams and condenses the fatty essences (膏液) found in the soil. It is therefore perfectly reasonable that sulfur and hot springs should accompany one another — and indeed they do. Yet when all is said and done, a hot spring is a hot spring and sulfur is sulfur. To draw an analogy: the fire within the earth is the mother, and hot springs and sulfur are like siblings born of that same mother. Therefore, to say that "sulfur gives rise to hot springs" is, from the outset, wrong. Equally wrong is the claim that "hot springs give rise to sulfur."
