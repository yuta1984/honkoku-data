...However, that is a shallow and superficial understanding. As stated above, when the yang qi within the earth is obstructed by the high mountains on the surface, unable to seep out or disperse, it lies hidden and accumulates, stagnates and condenses, seethes and boils until it becomes fire, fumigating and steaming the fatty substance (膏液) within the earth and forming sulfur. The fire qi and the sulfur then steam one another ever more intensely, growing ever more stagnant and pent up, until at last the fire qi erupts violently within the mountain and reduces an entire peak to ash. This too is of the same kind as a fire well (火井) suddenly erupting — it is simply a grander manifestation of the same phenomenon.

To say that it was "solely through the eruptive force of sulfur that the fire burst forth" is the error of knowing the end result while failing to consider the fundamental cause. Therefore, hot springs are a product of the yang fire qi within the earth; sulfur, too, is a product of the yang fire qi within the earth. Since both arise from the same "bellows" (橐爚, takuyaku), it is only natural and reasonable that the hot springs found throughout the provinces carry the smell of sulfur.
