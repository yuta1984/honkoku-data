(Continued from previous page) ...this fire follows the earth's veins, and if the top of a cavern happens to be open and connected, it bursts forth to become a volcano (a mountain where fire burns) or a fire well (a well from which fire emerges). If, moreover, the mineral qi (stone vapors) comes into contact with it, it becomes sulfur or arsenolite. If the source of a spring passes through this, it becomes a hot spring. For this reason, fire dwelling within the earth assists the earth's qi and causes the ten thousand things to be generated. The five metals, the eight stones, and all manner of precious treasures are formed through the tempering action of fire. Among all things, however, there is nothing that approaches fire more closely than sulfur.

For this reason, when water passes over it, it becomes a hot spring. The people of the world know only that there is water in the earth, and do not know that there is fire there, producing such wondrous and mysterious effects — so the text states. Furthermore, it states that according to the principles of the Five Phases, water overcomes fire; yet the boiling of a hot spring occurs because the qi of fire readily enters into the very form of water. This theory is precise and well-argued, its reasoning profound, and its discernment surpasses all prior thinkers.
