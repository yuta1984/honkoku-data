Those suffering from scabies (*kaisen*), syphilitic sores (*baisō*), and all manner of skin ailments (*shosō*) who bathe in this spring will find that it drives out all harmful toxins (*jatoku*), expels putrid fluids and foul pus (*oshū*), and while the sores may appear to worsen noticeably at first, after nine days, or fourteen days, or twenty-one days, the afflictions will heal pleasantly and completely. It is truly a hot spring of the highest and most supreme quality. For this reason, many people from neighboring provinces come to take the waters. However, those who have not attained a true understanding of the nature of hot springs will raise various objections and criticisms, supposing it to be considerably inferior to springs such as Arima — but this is not so at all. Common people tend to prize what they have merely heard by reputation while dismissing what lies before their own eyes, yearning for what is distant while neglecting what is near at hand. This is their habitual way. It is a most lamentable thing.

One entry: Long ago, a Buddhist monk by the name of Renzen (*Shaku no Renzen*) came all the way down to this spring for a cure, and upon finishing his stay and making his way back, he composed the following verse at Dannoura in Nagato Province:

　*At night, recalling my distant home, I drift at last into dreams; then, clearing, I gaze upon a solitary island...* [remainder illegible]
