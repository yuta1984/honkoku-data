...it is because the channels through which the water travels to each respective pool differ, that such differences in color arise as described. In the hot springs of Kinosaki (*Kinosaki*) in Tajima Province (*Tajima no Kuni*), the *Arayu* ("new spring") and the *Kasayu* ("scab/skin ailment spring") are situated only a short distance apart from one another; and yet the *Arayu* brings out (*aggravates*) skin afflictions (*kasa*), while the *Kasayu* heals them. Similarly, at the *Mandara-yu* bath, the eastern tub (*tōsō*) aggravates skin ailments, while the western tub (*seisō*) heals them. This too follows the same principle explained above: it is because the channels of water flowing into each respective pool differ, and not because there is any difference in the fire that heats them.

One item: Now, a hot spring that is excessively hot is not desirable, nor, needless to say, is one that is too lukewarm. Only that which is warm and gentle (*onjū*) and pleasantly mild (*wakoku*) should be considered ideal. As for the theory of Kagawa Taichū, who claimed that "a hot spring of extreme heat is best, because the intense heat of extreme temperatures aids and stirs (*kondō*) the person's vital energy (*genki*), thereby rousing lingering illnesses (*shin'a*) and bringing forth chronic ailments (*haiko*)" — this is most laughably mistaken.
