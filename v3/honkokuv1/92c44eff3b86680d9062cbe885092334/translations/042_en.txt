...lead, alum-stone (*bandseki*), cinnabar (*shinsha*), arsenolite (*yoseki*), arsenic (*hiseki*), and all other such anomalous influences — a hot spring that does not absorb any of these alien vapors, that is untainted by such foreign influences, that remains in its pristine natural state of the innate union of yin and yang, and the wondrous merging of water and fire, and that furthermore wells up bearing together the vapor of pure-yang sulfur and the essence of the Greater Yin and yellow earth — this is to be regarded as the supreme, highest-grade beneficial spring.

Therefore, springs that emit an odor like that of *ohaguro* (iron liquor used in tooth-blackening), or that carry various other strange odors, are not good. As for odor, only the presence of a sulfurous smell is to be considered desirable.

Even then, if the sulfurous odor is excessively strong, this means the sulfur is overpowering. Furthermore, springs whose water is bitter in taste, or sour, or astringent, or that carry any of various unusual tastes, are exceedingly bad. Among these, astringency and sourness are particularly bad. Springs with such tastes will invariably drive skin ailments such as *shōsō* (scabies) and *baisō* (syphilitic sores) inward beneath the skin, giving the false appearance of a cure. One must therefore never bathe in such a spring. This is the lowest grade of toxic spring. As for taste, one should bathe only in water that tastes just like plain hot water (*sayu*)...
