...(a spring formed solely through contact with sulfurous vapors,) neither too hot nor too lukewarm, that upon touching the body feels warm and gentle (*onjū*), and gives a comfortable, enveloping warmth (*waku*); a spring after bathing in which a warm and comfortable feeling (*kuku*) pervades one's internal organs, skin, interior and exterior, inside and out, and lingers for quite some time — such a spring should be considered the finest, most excellent hot spring of all. Kaibara Tokushin of Chikuzen likewise stated: "One should not bathe in extremely hot water; water that is warm and moderate is preferable." This point is to be approved of.

One entry: At the foot of Mount Tenpai (*Tenpaisan*) in Mikasa District, Chikuzen Province, there is a hot spring. The village is called Musashi. This hot spring truly meets the criteria described above: it is untainted by any aberrant or unusual vapors (*iki*), carries no strange odor or flavor, and is a spring of pure, natural, unaltered water, bearing only the scent of sulfur. It is neither too hot nor too lukewarm; upon touching the body it feels warm and gentle (*onjū*) with a comfortable, enveloping warmth (*waku*). After bathing, a warm and pleasant feeling (*kuku*) pervades one's internal organs, skin, interior and exterior, inside and out, and lingers for quite some time. Moreover, even after bathing repeatedly and frequently, the skin does not become dry or rough (*kosō*)...
