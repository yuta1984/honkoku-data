One point, however, in Kagawa Taichū's theory that appears to reflect genuine insight is the following: "If one traces all the hot springs under heaven back to their fundamental source, there is originally no difference in principle between them. It is solely according to whatever substances happen to lie in the subterranean passage through which the water wells up that differences in beneficial or harmful properties arise."

Generally speaking, if the subterranean passage through which a hot spring wells up contains things such as gold, iron, lead, or copper, or other substances such as cinnabar (mercuric sulfide), alum stone, arsenic stone, or poison stone, then that water becomes toxic. On the other hand, if the passage through which the water wells up contains none of the substances mentioned above, and the spring rises purely through the influence of ordinary earth and stone — untainted and untouched by anything — then it is a hot spring in its original, pure, and unadulterated form, and constitutes a supremely excellent medicinal water.
