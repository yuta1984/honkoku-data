...smoldering, with smoke rising up — the sort of places that common people call "jigoku" (hells) — such as Mount Tateyama in Etchū Province, Kirishima in Hyūga Province, and Kajiya Jigoku and Konya Jigoku at Yumoto in Sagami Province — all of these are types of fire wells, in which the yang qi within the earth lies hidden, accumulates and stagnates, gathers and condenses, and ultimately boils up and erupts, issuing its fiery vapors forth onto the surface of the earth. In the Chinese text known as the Haitō Shokoku-ki (海東諸国記, "Record of Countries East of the Sea"), it is also written that there are many fire wells in Japan. Now, furthermore, when the fire within the earth travels along the veins and channels within the earth, and happens to meet and mingle with an underground water vein, erupting onto the surface — this is what we call a "hot spring" (温泉). That which meets and mingles with an ordinary (fresh) water vein becomes a "fresh hot spring" (淡湯泉, plain hot water), while that which meets and mingles with a saline water vein becomes a "brine hot spring" (鹹湯泉, salt hot water). Therefore, a hot spring is the meeting of yin and yang in the natural order of heaven and earth — a wondrous union of water and fire.
