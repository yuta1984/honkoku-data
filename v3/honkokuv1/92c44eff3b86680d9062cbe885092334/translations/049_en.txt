(Continuing from the previous page, the latter part of Renzen's poem:)

　*Once I sought out the hot spring of Saifu, and there I lingered, receiving its cure, for two full years.*

So goes the poem, as recorded in the *Mudai Shishū* (Collection of Untitled Poems). "Saifu" refers to the Chinzeifu (the Dazaifu administrative headquarters in Kyushu), and the area around Buzen province is all ancient territory of the Chinzeifu. This being the case, this hot spring appears to have been extraordinarily prosperous in ancient times — not merely renowned in the surrounding provinces, but a famous spring (*meitō*) known throughout the realm, to which people came for cures (*tōji*) crossing mountains and seas from far distant places.

Furthermore, the venerable Kaibara (Ekiken) states: According to one account, when Empress Saimei resided at the temporary imperial palace (*kōkyū*) at Asakura in Jōza District, she is said to have also made a royal visit (*miyuki*) to Musashi Village and taken the waters there for her health. Additionally, in the *Kokin Wakashū* (Collection of Poems Ancient and Modern), there is a record of a woman named Minamoto no Sane who traveled down from the capital to Tsukushi (Kyushu) for a cure, and at the forest of Kaminabi she composed the following poem:

　*Hitoyari no michi naranaku ni ōkata wa*
　*iki ushi to iite isa kaeri namu*

(This is not a road I chose to travel of my own will — and feeling, on the whole, that life itself has grown unbearable, I wonder if I ought simply to turn back.)
