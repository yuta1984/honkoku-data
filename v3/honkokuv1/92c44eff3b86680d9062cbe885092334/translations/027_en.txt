(Continued from previous page) ...In a book called *Zixu Zhi*, there is a theory that differs markedly from all these various explanations. According to this theory: "People commonly say that hot springs well up because of the presence of thermally potent mineral substances such as sulfur or arsenolite, and that the springs are driven by the energy of these substances. However, there are many places where hot springs exist and yet where no sulfur or arsenolite is to be found whatsoever. If that is the case, then it cannot be the power and energy of sulfur, arsenolite, or their like; rather, it is because a dragon dwells within the earth, and the vigorous vital energy of that dragon causes the springs to boil." There also appears to be a theory holding that it is precisely because a region is a warm country that the land there causes hot springs to boil. In his record of an excursion to hot springs, Tang Zixi of the Song dynasty writes: "People commonly say that sulfur causes hot springs to boil, but if one experiments by placing any amount of sulfur into water, the water does not become even slightly warm. Therefore, the theory that hot springs well up through the energy of sulfur is also difficult to accept. Moreover, since it is a warm country..."
