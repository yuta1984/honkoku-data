《Annotation text (split columns): This book is of benefit not only to physicians but to all who undergo hot spring cures (tōji); every such person should keep a copy at hand. It also discusses conditions in which hot spring treatment may in fact cause harm, and it examines the various Chinese and Japanese theories explaining how hot springs come to well up from the earth, carefully refuting their errors. In sum, it records all the historical origins and background of hot springs.》

Preface to "Onsen Shōgen"

Hara Shikōyō of Heian (Kyoto), who made Confucian scholarship his lifelong vocation, traversed half the realm in his travels, and from what he personally experienced and observed with his own eyes, he composed a work in one fascicle entitled "Onsen Shōgen" (Minor Observations on Hot Springs), in which he discusses the nature and properties of hot springs, their primary therapeutic uses, which to seek out or avoid, what is beneficial and what is contraindicated, and the regimen to be followed. In the matter of hot springs alone, he has left virtually nothing unexplored. That he named it "Minor" (小) is, I venture to say, an act of modesty.
