...(The *Onsen Shōgen* of Sōkei Sensei) is grounded in genuine human virtue, and is not of the same kind as those who compile numerous books of theory and argument in the world, yet produce little practical benefit in actual affairs. In years past, I nursed a long illness by soaking in the hot springs of Dewa, following entirely the prescriptions recorded in the *Shōgen*, and was able to recover completely.

I myself first relied upon this book and thereby gained benefit for my body. This is indeed the reach of Sōkei's virtue extending to me. Later, while passing through Shimōsa province, I had occasion to meet Onda Taiga, a Confucian retainer of Koga domain — who was none other than a disciple of Sōkei. Speaking with him, I privately admired the upright manner in which he served his friend (Tōgai), and came to fully believe that the words of Tōgai and Henshō were truly as they had said.

The *Book of Odes* (*Shijing*) says: "I think of the men of old, and they truly bring comfort to my heart." My feelings toward Sōkei are just the same. After the death of Confucius, his disciples wished to serve Yǒu Ruò just as they had served Confucius, and pressed Zēngzǐ on this matter, but Zēngzǐ would not consent — hence the saying of "the rivers Jiāng and Hàn, and the autumn sun." Furthermore, Mengzi, though himself a great worthy, noted that even someone like Yán Huí did not dare aspire to learn to be like Confucius, and declared: "What I desire is to learn from Confucius."

From this time onward, all those who carry on the Confucian lineage must take Confucius as their supreme teacher and founder. Thus, when disciples speak of "the Master" (*Fūshi*), even five young children would know...
