More concretely, after this, when the First Emperor of Qin suffered from a disease of festering swellings, he bathed in the hot springs of Mount Li (Lishan), whereupon his illness was promptly cured—this is recorded in the *Sanqinji* (Records of the Three Qin). From that time onward, Emperor Wu of Han, Emperor Xuanzong of Tang, and beyond them, the emperors of Qi and Wei, and the subjects of Tang and Song, all bathed in hot springs, and such examples are not few. Nevertheless, in Chinese medical texts, detailed discussions of hot springs and elucidations of their principles are nowhere to be found. The *Bencao Shiyi* (Supplement to the Materia Medica) by Chen Cangqi of the Tang dynasty was the first to record the efficacious properties of hot springs, yet it does not explain their underlying principles in any detail. The *Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica) by Li Shizhen of the Ming dynasty is likewise exceedingly cursory in its account, and seems to be the work of someone altogether ignorant of the true nature of hot springs. Furthermore, works such as Zhang Hua's *Bowuzhi* (Records of Investigation of Things) and Hu Zi's *Yuyin Conghua* (Collected Remarks of the Fisherman-Hermit) contain any number of references to hot springs...
