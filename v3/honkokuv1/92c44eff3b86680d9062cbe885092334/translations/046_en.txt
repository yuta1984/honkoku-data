However, since a full discussion of *genki* is not the principal purpose of this work, I shall omit the details of that argument here. Whether a hot spring is beneficial (*nō*) or harmful (*doku*) is not determined by whether the water is hot or lukewarm, but rather by the differences in the nature of the water channels (*yusuji*). Therefore, even among springs of extreme heat, there should exist hot springs that possess a cold and cooling (*kanrei*) nature.

A decoction (*sentō*) is a hot liquid, and yet a decoction of gypsum (*sekikō*) possesses a cold nature (*kanshō*) — it is just like that. Moreover, even if a spring is not particularly extremely hot, if it has not become tainted by any other (harmful) substances, and has been formed solely through contact with and infusion of pure yang (*jun'yō*) sulfurous vapors within the earth, then its nature should be warm and moderate (*on*), and thus ideal. Therefore, in selecting a hot spring, one should carefully and thoroughly examine whether it has been tainted by any unusual or aberrant vapors (*iki*), and seek out a spring whose water flows from natural, unaltered channels — one imbued solely with sulfurous vapors...
