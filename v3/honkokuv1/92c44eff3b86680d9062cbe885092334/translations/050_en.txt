...so goes the poem, as it appears in the record. Regarding the hot spring where this woman Sane underwent her cure (*tōji*), the text states only that it was in "Tsukushi," without specifying which particular spring it was. The term "Tsukushi" covers an exceedingly wide area, and even today there are a great many places with hot springs throughout Kyushu. Thus it is difficult to determine precisely which hot spring is meant. However, if one reasons by analogy from the fact that the monk Renzen bathed in the hot springs of Musashi on repeated occasions, it would follow that the spring where Sane took her cure was likewise in Musashi.

One further point: According to the oral tradition handed down by the people of that locality, in ancient times there was a man called General (*Shōgun*) Toramaru. His daughter suffered from an illness that proved very difficult to cure; but when she bathed in this hot spring, her illness was at once healed completely. Thereafter, Toramaru developed and established the hot spring, and from that time down to the present day, it is said that visitors coming for cures (*yokushya* / *tōji-nin*) have never ceased. Toramaru's main residence was located within Koga Village (*Koga-mura*), in the place called Suda...
