...as to why [hot springs] invariably carry a sulfurous smell: in general, sulfur is a substance formed when the yang qi within the earth, as described above, fumigates and steams the liquid of the soil (the "fat" or oil of the earth), binding it together into solid form. Indeed, when the yang qi within the earth encounters a place where the soil is thick, the ground hard, and a mountain stands upon the surface above, that qi finds it difficult to seep out or disperse; it lies hidden and accumulates, stagnates and condenses, swirling in a state of yinyun (絪紜 — a chaotic intermingling of yin and yang forces), seething and boiling, fumigating and steaming the fatty, oily substance (膏液) within the soil, steaming it and congealing it, until at last it forms and solidifies into substances such as ganshi (礜石, arsenical pyrite) and sulfur. This is precisely why sulfur is regarded as a supremely yang (至陽) medicinal substance and possesses a nature of extreme heat (大熱). There are any number of sulfur mountains in Japan, and if one were to observe and contemplate these places, their underlying principle would naturally become perfectly clear. In recent years, when Kirishimayama in Satsuma Province and Mount Fuji in Suruga Province suddenly and without apparent cause emitted fire from within the earth and erupted in violent conflagration, people throughout the world all said that it was the eruptive force (勢気) of sulfur that had caused the fire to burst forth...
