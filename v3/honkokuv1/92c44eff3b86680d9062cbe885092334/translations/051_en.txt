...a place called "Sudareto," it is said. Even today the remains of that residence can be found there. Furthermore, in the vicinity of the hot spring there is a *Yakushi-dō* (Healing Buddha Hall) built by Toramaru, which bears the name Chinkasan Buzō-ji (*Chinkazaan Musashi-ji* Temple). Beside this temple stands the burial mound (*tsuka*) of Toramaru. As Toramaru is a person whose deeds and traces are not to be found in official histories (*seishi*) or old records (*kyūki*), it is unclear exactly when he lived; yet since he was the founder of Musashi-ji Temple, he appears to have been a person of very ancient times.

That Toramaru founded Musashi-ji Temple is also attested by the venerable Kaibara (Ekiken). According to Kaibara's account, in ancient times this temple was a great establishment, with many halls and pagodas and numerous sub-temples (*in*). The passage in the second section of the lower volume of the *Uji Shūi* (Monogatari), entitled "The Merits of Taking Holy Orders" (*Shukke Kudoku no Jō*), which records the affairs of Musashi-ji Temple in Tsukushi, refers to this very temple. Furthermore, in the *Genkō Shakusho* it is recorded that in ancient times, at Musashi-ji Temple, Anraku-ji Temple, and Kanzeon-ji Temple in Tsukushi, a *Tsuina* (demon-expelling) ceremony was held every year on the seventh day of the First Month...
