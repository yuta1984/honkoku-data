Atop Mount Onsen (*Onsenzan*) in Hizen Province, there are numerous hot spring pools (*yutsubo*), and the distance between one pool and another is a mere four or five *ken*, or perhaps eight or nine *ken* apart. Since these pools are so close that one can see across to them at a glance, one might assume there could be no difference between their waters — and yet, one finds a spring with water as white as rice-washing water, another that is bluish-black in color, and yet another resembling the milky liquid from a whetstone. All of these springs are of extreme heat, such that no person could bathe in them. The local people call them "*jigoku*" (hells). Since bathing in them is impossible, their properties and medicinal effects cannot be known; yet even in color alone there are such great differences as described, and therefore it is certain that their properties must differ as well. Even though there should be no difference in the mineral composition (*doki*) of the ground where these pools lie, nor in the underground fire that heats them, the differences arise from variations in the water channels of each individual pool, or from differences in the channels through which the water, having already become heated within the ground, seeps upward...
