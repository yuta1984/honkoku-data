(Continued from previous page) ...and so there exist various other theories as well. According to the theory of Li Shizhen of the Ming dynasty, "hot springs are not limited to places with sulfur; they are also found in places where arsenic ore (hiSeki) exists. Therefore, it cannot be concluded that hot springs arise from sulfur alone." In a book called the Gyoin Sōwa (Fisherman's Hermit Collected Talks), it is stated that "there are hot springs that well up from cinnabar, hot springs that well up from alum stone, and hot springs that well up from realgar." Furthermore, a book called the Senkaku Ruisho (Encyclopedic Classified Writings), citing the hot spring inscription of Wang Bao of the Later Zhou, states: "In places where hot springs exist, there must always be three things at their root—alum stone, cinnabar, and sulfur—and it is through the power of these that the water is steamed and brought to boil." The Danqian Lu of Yang Sheng'an and the Wuzazu of Xie Zhaozhi are likewise of the same view. Although these various theories differ from one another in small details, they all ultimately share the same underlying idea: that within the earth there exist various mineral substances, and it is through the vital energy (seiki) of these substances that hot springs are brought to boil.
