(Continued from previous page) ...references to hot springs can be found, yet writings that truly understand their principles and discuss their efficacious properties in detail are nowhere to be found, whether in Confucian texts or medical texts. Most sources simply claim that "sulfur heats the hot springs," or alternatively that they are heated by realgar, alum stone, arsenic, and the like. This is nothing more than a fool recounting a dream, or a dwarf who cannot see over the crowd giving his opinion of a performance. It is only natural that those who discuss the hot springs' uses should be so superficial, given that they have not grasped the fundamental truth of hot springs. For this reason, physicians of the present day already lack sufficient documentary sources to draw upon, and furthermore, those who give serious thought to the matter are few—thus many remain ignorant of the true nature of hot springs, deceiving themselves and misleading others. Such cases are by no means rare. Since physicians who truly understand the principles of hot springs are already so scarce, most decisions regarding hot-spring cures are made through consultation among laypersons, and people undergo hot-spring treatments even for conditions for which such treatment is inadvisable...
