The Sangen-machi area burned. The Shinmei Shrine was unharmed. The residence of Lord Ogasawara Sado-no-kami burned, as did the lower residence of Lord Ōta Bitchū-no-kami.

○ On the opposite bank, Tokiwa-chō burned and the fire stopped at the edge of Takahashi Bridge. Beside the official rice storehouse (momigura), in Fukagawa Moto-machi, an entire block was destroyed with only three houses surviving.

○ In Minami-Honjo along the Tate-kawa canal, the areas of Aioi-chō and Matsuzaka-chō suffered numerous collapses. Futatsu-me, Midori-chō first and second blocks burned. The third block survived with collapses only. Furthermore, from the fourth block through the fifth block, Sannohashi, and Hana-machi, a small area burned before the fire stopped.

○ On the opposite riverbank (to the north of Tate-kawa), the areas of Matsui-chō and Hayashi-chō suffered damage in various places; Tokuemon-chō first and second blocks burned. The third block survived. From the direction of Shūmoku-bashi Bridge as far as Sakasai, there were numerous collapses scattered throughout.

○ In Kameido-machi, two separate locations burned slightly. The Tenjin Shrine was unharmed. The areas of Yanagishima and Oshiage suffered damage in various places. To the left and right of Hōonji-bashi Bridge, collapses were numerous, and approximately one and a half chō (roughly 160 meters) on either side of the upper bridge burned. The areas of Nagasaki-chō, Yoshida-chō, and Yoshioka-chō also suffered many collapses.

○ Small residences to the front, back, left, and right of Minami-warishita-sui (the southern drainage canal) suffered numerous collapses, so many as to be beyond counting. Along Kita-warishita-sui (the northern drainage canal), Matsukura-chō suffered particularly extensive damage,

—and the vicinities of Arai-chō, Benten-kōji, and the Ushino-gozen Otabisho (temporary shrine) burned.

○ Additionally, in the areas of Ishihara and Banba, and at Nakanogō and Haraniwa, fires broke out here and there in small numbers at scattered locations.

○ Across Azuma Bridge, the residence of Lord Matsudaira Suō-no-kami burned. In Koume and Kawara-machi, both the townspeople's quarters and the village areas suffered extensive damage. The embankment of the Sumida River cracked in numerous places and erupted sand (liquefaction). The areas of Ukeji, Terashima, and Suda Village all suffered extensive damage.

Taking stock of the entire city (go-funai) as a whole, the upland (yamanote) areas suffered fewer collapses, and places with higher ground sustained less damage. In areas such as the slopes below Akagi Shrine, Kaitai-chō, Furukawa-chō, along the stream through Kohinata, the base of Tomizaka slope, Sashigaya-chō, the Hongō Kikuzaka lower area, and below Kanda Myōjin Shrine and Yushima Tenjin Shrine—wherever the ground was low-lying—the damage was extremely severe. Although the shitamachi (lower city) districts suffered extensive damage throughout, with only a half-chō or one-chō's distance separating areas of greater or lesser severity, it is apparent that the earthquake too followed certain lines (i.e., patterns tied to ground conditions).
