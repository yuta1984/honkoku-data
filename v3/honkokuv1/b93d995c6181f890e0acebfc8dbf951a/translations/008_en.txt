Osukiya-chō and Dōbō-chō were more than half destroyed. A fire broke out from the second block of Ueno-chō; Roku-Amida and Jōraku-in survived, but south of there—the first block and the eastern side of Hirokōji—burned completely without exception. On the Onaridō road side, the fire stopped after burning the north nagaya (tenement row) of Lord Inoue Chikugo-no-kami's residence. The western side of Hirokōji suffered great collapses. Below Yushima Otoko-zaka (the men's slope), the street suffered few collapses, but below Onna-zaka (the women's slope), everything collapsed entirely.

○ From there, Naka-zaka suffered collapses and there were places where residential houses were buried. The residences of Lord Ishikawa Tonomo-no-kami and Lord Sakai Aki-no-kami burned, while Lord Hori Tamba-no-kami's residence suffered major collapse but was spared from the spreading fire. From there, the entire Onaridō road area—the back streets, Yamamoto-chō substitute land, Hanabusa-chō substitute land, Daimon-chō, Chōja-chō, continuing through Shitaya-chō second block substitute land—and Naka-Okachi-machi suffered minor burning.

○ In the areas of Shitaya Shamisenbori, Nichō-chō, and the vicinity before and after Torikoe, collapses were few in all places. From there, the Okura-mae avenue is as recorded previously.

○ In the Fukagawa area, a fire broke out from Aikawa-chō, across from Eitai Bridge, and burned through Moromachi, Kumai-chō, Kuroe-chō, Ogawa-chō, Nakajima-chō, Ōjima-chō, Shimo-Hamaguri-chō and surroundings, the precinct gate of Eitai-ji temple, Nakachō, and as far as Yamamoto-chō. The Hachiman-gū shrine, though surrounded by fire, was unharmed; the fire stopped at the very edge of the torii gate. In another location, the area around Wagura, Kita-Honjo substitute land, and Saga-chō substitute land also burned.

○ The areas around Enmadō-bashi Bridge, Sankaku-yashiki, Tomihisa-chō, and Hirano-chō suffered collapses. The temple district (teramachi) street survived in part.

○ The Mannen Bridge riverside and the second block of Isemachi burned. Sendai-bori and Kiyosumi-chō suffered severe collapses, and Umibe Daiku-chō and the Takahashi avenue area suffered widespread collapses throughout. The main hall of Reigan-ji temple was undamaged. Throughout this area, damage was severe as far as Ōgi-bashi Bridge.

○ To the north of Ōgi-bashi, in a place called Fukagawa Nishi-machi, about half a chō (approx. 55 meters) burned. In the Sarue area as well, collapses were numerous.

○ At Fukagawa Jōshin-ji temple, though the main hall collapsed, it remained structurally intact; three sub-temples within the grounds were destroyed, the inner gate fell, and the stone monument inscribed with the daimoku (sacred title) also toppled. The entire area around here suffered major collapse.

○ In another location, from Ofunakura-maechō through the Dainichi-yokochō area, the gate precinct of Hachiman-otabisho, and Yanagawa-chō, the small residences and the official rice storehouses (momigura) in this area suffered minor burning.
