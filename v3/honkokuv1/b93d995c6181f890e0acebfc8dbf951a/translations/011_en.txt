Among these, the gold, silver, treasures, clothing, and furnishings consumed in the fires were wiped out in an instant, leaving nothing but scorched wasteland — truly a catastrophe most lamentable.

○ After the earthquake, tremors continued to occur day and night without rest, and the people's fear was extraordinary. Then someone — it is unclear who started it — spread word throughout the city that "there will certainly be a severe earthquake tonight," and the rumor passed from person to person without any clear source. In order to avoid the anticipated disaster, people in open spaces at crossroads throughout the city constructed shelters using sliding doors and shoji screens as roofs, surrounded themselves with luggage and folding screens, and spread straw mats (mushiro) and rush matting (komo) on the ground to sleep outdoors. Such people could be found in great numbers throughout the neighborhoods. However, no further major tremors occurred, and people returned to their homes around mid-October.

Those whose homes had been destroyed by fire, however, had no house to return to, and remained in those rough mat-walled shelters; one can only imagine their hardship during heavy rains and storms.

Therefore, the honorable Shogunal authorities erected relief shelters for the destitute and issued a proclamation stating that people should come forward at their own discretion to seek assistance. All those sleeping outdoors gathered there, received cooked rice distributed each day, and were able to resume their livelihoods. Truly, this was a benevolent blessing of this august reign.

Locations of relief shelters:
Outside Saiwai-bashi Gate / Asakusa Hirokoji / Fukagawa Kaibe Shinden / Fukagawa Hachiman Shrine grounds / Below Ueno Hill / From Ueno Palace to the same location.

Total number of towns in Edo: said to be 3,818 chō. — End.
