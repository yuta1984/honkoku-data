This is a report accompanied by a map, submitted by Kawakami Kingorō, the magistrate (daikan) of Nakanojō in Shinano Province, based on information gathered from rumors and inquiries. On the night of the 24th day of the 3rd month, around the hour of the fourth watch (approximately 10 p.m.), a sudden major earthquake struck. The greater part of the outer walls and fortified enclosures of the Nakanojō jinya (magistrate's office) collapsed from the shaking, but the main buildings sustained no particular damage. In the villages surrounding the jinya, eaves and similar elements fell due to the shaking, but there were no collapsed houses or injured persons. Within the jinya compound, people had set up camp in the garden and no one was residing in the main building. From that same night until dawn, there were somewhere between eighty and one hundred tremors of varying magnitude. From the following day, the 25th, through to the last day of the month, tremors continued at a rate of approximately one hundred to one hundred and fifty per day, with five or six major shocks among them each day. From the 1st through the 5th of the current month, the frequency decreased to approximately thirty to forty per day, with intervals between them growing longer, though there were still two or three major shocks per day, and the tremors have not yet ceased even now. In many villages, large numbers of people are sleeping outdoors.

Item: Within the territory under our jurisdiction, in six villages within Minochi District, many private homes were completely destroyed and there are places with high numbers of casualties. In the remaining areas, damage to structures is widespread; we have dispatched inspectors (tedai), but the investigation has not yet been completed.

Item: Within Matsushiro Domain's Minochi District, there is a region known as "Yamanaka," a locality assessed at thirty thousand koku. In the villages of this area, the earthquake was particularly severe, with landslides occurring in every village, houses entirely destroyed, and a great many deaths.
