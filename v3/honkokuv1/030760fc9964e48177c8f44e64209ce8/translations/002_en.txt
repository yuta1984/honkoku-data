This is a report with an attached illustrated map, inquiring about rumors and hearsay, submitted by Kawakami Kingorō, the magistrate (daikan) of Nakanojō in Shinshū province. Around the hour of Yotsu (approximately 10 p.m.) on the night of the 24th day of the 3rd month, a sudden great earthquake struck. The perimeter walls and guard walls of the Nakanojō government office (jin'ya) largely collapsed, but the main buildings suffered no particular damage. The villages surrounding the government office likewise had eaves and similar structures fall, but no houses were completely destroyed and no one was injured. Even within the compound, people pitched field camps in the gardens and no one remained living in the main buildings. From that same night until dawn, there were eighty to ninety tremors of varying intensity. From the following 25th day through to the last day of the month, tremors continued at a rate of approximately 150 or 100 per day, among which there were five or six major shocks per day. From the 1st to the 5th of the current month, the tremors became more infrequent at thirty to forty per day, though among these there were still two or three major shocks per day; the tremors have not yet ceased even now. There are still many people in the villages who are sleeping outdoors.

Item: Within the area under this magistrate's jurisdiction, there are six villages in Mizuchi District where many houses are as good as completely destroyed and there are many dead; the remaining areas have mostly sustained damage rather than total destruction. Inspectors (tedai) have been dispatched, but investigations have not yet been completed.

Item: Within Matsushiro domain's Mizuchi District, there is a district called "Yamanaka," a place assessed at 30,000 koku. In these villages, the earthquakes were particularly severe, with landslides occurring in every village, all houses completely destroyed, and an extraordinary number of deaths.
