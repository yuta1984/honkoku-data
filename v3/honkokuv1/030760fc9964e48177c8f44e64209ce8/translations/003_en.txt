Along both banks of the Sai River there are two villages called Hirai and Nagai. The mountains on both sides of this same township collapsed, and the falling rocks blocked the Sai River. Houses and trees alike were buried under the collapsed earth, and the resulting formation resembled a great earthen embankment, stretching some eight chō in width and presenting the appearance of a naturally formed mountain. Not a single drop of water has flowed downstream; the upstream area has become like a lake, submerging several villages beneath the water. The inundated area is six to seven chō, or in some places fourteen to fifteen chō in width, and five to six ri or more in length. More than ten days have passed since the water began accumulating on the 24th, yet the water has not receded. The site of the collapse stands roughly eighty to ninety ken higher than the normal water level, and as the water continues to accumulate, the surface is still some thirty-odd ken above what it was. It appears that, perhaps because of the continued clear weather, the Sai River's water level has been dropping, while the water pooled in the broad basin is rising two to three shaku per day and night relative to the height of the blocked mountain. Should heavy rains fall on top of this, the situation would be exceptional, but absent that, it is thought unlikely that the water would break through within the next twenty to thirty days. Nevertheless, the villages downstream have been thrown into a panic, fearing that if the dam breaks all at once their villages will be utterly destroyed; people have fled carrying food up into the mountains, and in some cases entire villages have evacuated to other places. Accordingly, inspection officials were dispatched to observe conditions upstream, and their report indicates that in the present state there is no sign of an imminent breach; and that even if the water were to leak through, it would likely flow out gradually, like water from the spout of a kettle.

Item: It is said that approximately two-tenths of the buildings in the castle town of Matsushiro have been destroyed, though the severity of damage varies by location. Throughout the domain as a whole, the number of destroyed buildings and deaths is enormous; the reported figure to date stands at over two thousand persons, and the toll from the aforementioned Yamanaka township has yet to be determined, but rumor among retainers of the same domain has it that the total deaths may amount to approximately ten thousand persons.

Item: Zenkōji Temple was in the midst of its periodic kaichō memorial services, and vast numbers of pilgrims had gathered from all provinces. When the earthquake struck suddenly that night, those who attempted to flee had no time even to take two steps before being crushed; in households of ten persons, six or seven died, and in some cases every member of a household perished, such that the dead were piled like mountains. Moreover, fires broke out in three or four places and were spread by fierce winds, consuming nine-tenths of the townhouses. Only the main hall (hondō), the main gate (sanmon), and the head priests' quarters (hombō) survived; everything else was destroyed by fire. It was an unprecedented catastrophe. As further reports have come in, it appears that of the eight thousand persons registered within the temple's domain, more than three thousand have perished; beyond these, the number of those who died along the roads is unknown, amounting to many thousands. Furthermore, approximately two thousand pilgrims also perished.
