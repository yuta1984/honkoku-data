On the fourth day of the eleventh month of the seventh year of Kaei (1854), the Year of the Tiger, a great earthquake struck many provinces. The details are recorded herein. The shaking began at around half past the fifth hour of the morning (approximately 9 a.m.), and the conditions along the Tōkaidō road were as follows:

[Sagami Province] In the castle town of Odawara, the domain of Lord Ōkubo Kaga-no-Kami suffered relatively little damage, while the greater part of the Hakone Mountains collapsed.

[Izu Province] The post town of Mishima was greatly shaken, with many houses collapsing. A fire broke out near Myōjin-mae, burning down six or seven buildings.

[Suruga Province] In Numazu, both the castle grounds of Lord Mizuno Dewa-no-Kami and the townspeople's homes were mostly destroyed. The post town of Hara was unharmed. Both the town and surrounding rural areas of Yoshiwara were shaken and collapsed. The Fujikawa River flooded due to the tsunami, causing tremendous damage to people and horses. Iwabuchi also shook greatly and caught fire. The two post towns of Kanbara and Yui were unharmed. Okitsu suffered both the earthquake and a tsunami that swept away houses and storehouses. The post town of Ejiri collapsed severely and subsequently caught fire, burning in many places. The port of Shimizu similarly experienced a tsunami. In Fuchū (Sunpu), the stone walls of the castle grounds collapsed extensively; shrines, temples, townhouses, and earthen storehouses were mostly destroyed, with many killed instantly and many others injured. At the same time, a fire broke out in Egawa-chō and spread through Shinyoshi-chō, Tenma-chō, Monzen-chō, Imōji-chō, Kamiyokota-chō, In'nai-chō, Shimoyokota-chō, Odaidokoro-chō, and half of Hachiman-chō before dying down during the lower quarter of the Hour of the Monkey (around 4:30 p.m.). The coastal areas also suffered damage to homes from the great tsunami. The post town of Mariko was unharmed, though the earthen bridges before and after it collapsed. Okabe also suffered no damage. In Fujieda, a fire broke out from the main gate of the castle of Lord Honda Bizen-no-Kami, with the earthquake causing severe damage; the area burned from inside the castle grounds of Tanaka down to the lower post town. The post town of Shimada was mostly shaken and collapsed, and the Ōigawa River flooded due to the tsunami, damaging homes throughout the area.

[Tōtōmi Province] The post town of Kanaya was also mostly shaken, with many killed instantly and many injured; fires broke out, and Sagara and Kawasaki burned down. The post town of Nissaka suffered minor damage. In Kakegawa, the entire castle of Lord Ōta Settsu-no-Kami burned down, townspeople's homes were toppled by the earthquake, many were killed or injured, the earth cracked open about one shaku (approximately 30 cm) and vigorously spouted muddy water.
