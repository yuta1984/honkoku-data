(Continued from previous page) Fukuroi was greatly shaken, and every townhouse burned to the ground. The post town of Mitsuke was mostly shaken and collapsed, with the road cracking in all directions as far as Nakaizumi. In Hamamatsu, the greater part of the castle town of Lord Inoue Kawachi-no-Kami was shaken and collapsed. Maisaka suffered minor shaking. The post town of Arai was greatly shaken, and moreover the checkpoint (sekisho) was swept away by the tsunami. Shirasu ka was also about half destroyed.

[Mikawa Province] Futakawa suffered the same. In Yoshida, about half of the castle town of Lord Matsudaira Izu-no-Kami was shaken and collapsed; the castle grounds were equally damaged, and many townhouses were destroyed by the tsunami. The post town of Goyu was about thirty percent shaken and collapsed. Akasaka and Fujikawa suffered similarly. In Okazaki, the entire castle town of Lord Honda Nakatsukasa-no-Taifu was shaken throughout; the bridge at Yahagi came close to collapsing—one span on the east side, one on the west—the stone foundation walls of the bridge abutments gave way, water that was normally clear turned to muddy water, and the earth cracked open in many places, spouting muddy water. The post town of Chiryu was about thirty percent shaken and collapsed.

[Owari Province] The post town of Narumi suffered similarly. Miya (Atsuta) experienced both the earthquake and a great tsunami, sustaining damage throughout.

[Ise Province] In Kuwana, the castle town of Lord Matsudaira Etchū-no-Kami was struck by both the earthquake and a great tsunami, and was largely destroyed. In Yokkaichi, many houses collapsed and twenty to thirty earthen storehouses were destroyed. From there onward to the post town of Ōtsu, each area suffered minor shaking. Along the Ise road, the side roads through Shiroko, Kando, Matsusaka, and Yamada saw townhouses shaken and collapsed, while the coastal areas suffered extraordinary damage from the great tsunami throughout.

[Yamashiro Province] Kyoto shook slightly but suffered no particular damage.

○ [Settsu Province] In Osaka, on the same day and month, the great earthquake began shaking at around half past the fifth hour (approximately 9 a.m.). People throughout the city ran about in all directions—east, west, north, and south—and a great many houses collapsed or were warped and crushed; shrines and temples were shaken down, and the commotion was beyond description. That day, things calmed somewhat toward evening, but on the following day, the fifth, at dawn at the seventh hour (approximately 4 a.m.), another great earthquake struck and shook for about half an hour (approximately one hour). Furthermore, a tremendous roaring sound came from no particular direction, like hundreds and thousands of thunderclaps.
