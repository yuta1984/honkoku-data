And thus they manifested as the Lower Shrine (Shimo no Miya) on the plain of Fuji. Asama Daibosatsu declared: "When I departed from the province of Shimotsuke, a gray horse called Sendan was provided with a saddle of sandalwood; therefore, unto the end of all ages, gray horses shall be revered as sacred horses (shinme)." The eight retainers who served the lord became the Eight Princes (Hachióji), and the thirteen ladies-in-waiting each became deities in their own right. In this manner they became deities, leaving their divine legacy in the province of Suruga—this is according to the sacred origin legend (engi) of Princess Konohana-sakuya-hime. From early childhood she had devoted herself to the Buddhist faith and accumulated great merit through the recitation of the Lotus Sutra. Above all, the proof of Lord Dayū's loyal service is something without parallel, now and throughout all ages to come. Now, regarding this sacred origin text (go-honchi, go-engi): those who can read it themselves should do so three times per month. Those who cannot read should have others read it for them and should listen at least once a month. Those who hear it even once will earn the merit equivalent to seven good rebirths. Let no one ever doubt this. Should anyone harbor doubt, their wishes shall not be granted in this life, and in the life hereafter they shall fall into the evil paths.
