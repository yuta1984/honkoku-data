Now, after this, the Governor of Shimotsuke of the Genji clan in the capital set out for his province of Shimotsuke, as he had long wished to do, taking a great retinue of attendants with him. They departed the capital under cover of night. As he traveled, he composed verses in his heart: "How quickly now — crossing the Barrier of Osaka where one meets one's love — what of the waves of the Shiga shore? The days pile upon days; what will become of me in the end?" And musing, "Where are we now?" — traveling far and wide — reaching the land of Mikawa and the tangled crossroads of Yatsuhashi, he murmured to himself, "Is this the place I had heard tell of so long ago?" — and he composed and recited verses as he went.

Passing through the Yo no Nakayama (the "mountain of the middle of night"), and then past Kikugawa, he soon arrived in the province of Suruga. "Mount Ashitaka, shrouded in cloud — breaking through the night, Mount Hakone comes into view at dawn." The province of Sagami too was left far behind, and crossing the grasses of the Musashino plain, searching here and there — "Where is my beloved's robe of longing to be found?" — at last he arrived in Shimotsuke Province.

Setting aside the formalities of entering the province, he was eager to seek out word of the princess without delay, and shared his wishes with his attendants. They replied: "That is entirely understandable, my lord, but it would be rather too hasty. Please first send a messenger, and thereafter, whatever you decide shall be as you wish."
