Ansei Records of What Was Seen and Heard, Volume Two (Middle)

○ An Account of Those Who Abandoned Their Parents and Met with Disaster

When one hears accounts of earthquakes from various provinces, there are cases of the earth cracking and mountains collapsing, of rivers changing their courses, or of land sinking into the sea, of high places becoming low, or of water gushing up from flat ground. Such things need not be considered particularly extraordinary. However, Edo had never experienced such unusual occurrences since ancient times. As was stated in the previous volume, earthquakes were exceedingly rare in Edo; although it is said that a great earthquake occurred in the sixteenth year of Genroku in ancient times, the only written record of it is a brief account in the work called *Oritaku Shiba no Ki* (Told Round a Brushwood Fire) by Master Arai Hakuseki.

However, the state of the city — from which direction to which direction the greatest destruction occurred — is not recorded there.
