With no sense of being alive, as if drunk or as if gone mad, some lay face down, chanting the names of Amida Buddha and Kannon. Before long, the sea calmed, and the boat fortunately arrived safely at the opposite shore. Those aboard felt as though they had been brought back to life, and their joy knew no bounds.

Upon landing at the Atsuta post station, they saw that the great earthquake had caused the houses throughout the area to collapse and crumble without exception. Some people were crushed under beams and ridgepoles, and the cries and screams of men and women pierced the ears and struck to the core. It then occurred to them that the tsunami on the sea must have been caused by this earthquake—a terrifying thought indeed. Yet, upon witnessing this scene of devastation, they felt that remaining on the sea had been far more fortunate than being subjected to such a catastrophe on land, and they counted their blessings.

Since the Atsuta Shrine was nearby, they decided to make a detour to pray for safety on the rest of their journey. What was wondrous to behold was that, though the shrine was only eight or nine chō away from the post station, the shrine buildings had sustained not the slightest damage, and even the votive lanterns offered at the altar had not been extinguished.

Deeply moved by the sacredness of this divine nation, they engraved the feeling in their hearts, shed tears of gratitude, offered prayers for a while, and then departed. Returning to the post station, they continued gradually on their way toward Edo. However, the roads everywhere had erupted with mud, and walking was extremely difficult through the slippery, muddy ground. The houses along the way had uniformly collapsed and were in ruin, with not a single house where one could seek a meal or find lodging. Edo was still far away, and the thought of how they could possibly get there made their hearts sink further, draining what little strength remained in their bodies. Through great difficulty, they managed to find food here and there, and at night they lodged in houses that had partially survived the collapse, eventually making it back to Edo. There are many more stories of hardships endured along the way, but as they would be too numerous, they are omitted here.
