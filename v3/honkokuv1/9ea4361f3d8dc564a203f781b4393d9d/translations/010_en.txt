"None shall remain," and with that they parted ways. The courtiers, having always studied the way of waka poetry and never forgotten it in any moment of their daily lives, held it deep within their hearts — and that is why they could compose verse so readily even in the midst of such tumult. Had they not kept it always in mind, how could they possibly have composed in such a critical moment? Thus it is that in all things, it is one's ordinary, everyday preparedness that truly matters.

　◯On the trouble that comes from believing rumors

An acquaintance of mine lived in Shōtenchō in Asakusa, trading in clogs and umbrellas, and kept a household with his wife and one servant. On that very night he was caught in the earthquake, and moreover, a fire broke out in the nearby Saruwakachō and spread widely. Of the theater district, only five or six buildings remained standing; Morita Kan'ya's theater was fortunate enough to escape the flames, but the rest were consumed by fire. As the blaze spread in all directions, the people of Shōtenchō felt as though the very tips of their sidelocks were catching fire. The man who traded in clogs and the like ran out of his house, and husband and wife, hand in hand, made their way to the riverbank. The people of the neighborhood had all boarded boats and were fleeing to Mukōjima to escape the fire. This couple too hired a boat together, crossed to that side, went to find an acquaintance's home, and spent the night there.

The fire eventually died down considerably. Thinking "Our house has surely burned by now — there is little point in going back, but if any ironware remains, let us go and collect it," they again boarded a boat and came ashore on this side. Looking around, they found that the fire had spread to the west side of Shōtenchō, behind the theater district — but since the man's house was on the east side, not even smoke had touched it, and it was entirely unharmed. Upon seeing this, he felt as though he had been given an extra house, as it were, and arriving at his own gate, he thought: "Had I known there would be this much time, I could at least have carried out the cash box. But the situation was so sudden that I fled in a panic with nothing but the clothes on my back — I was foolish. By now, most things have surely been stolen by thieves." Filled with regret, he pulled open the door, stepped inside, and looked all around — and found everything exactly as it had been when they left that evening, with not so much as a speck of dust…
