(Continued from previous page) ...and no one gave any thought to the disturbance of the earth's veins. However, four or five days after that event, the great earthquake occurred. Afterward, when rumors from various places were heard, some said that well water had risen to more than halfway above its usual level, while others said it had fallen to twice its usual depth. Considering this, there is no doubt that the earth's veins had become disordered, causing the underground waterways to shift. Thus, the sudden gushing of a clear spring in the garden of the Fukudaya must certainly have been a forewarning of the earthquake. I recently went to see that water, and it had turned a murky blue-green with moss growing in it, and was by no means fit to drink. What will become of it after several more months have passed, I cannot say.

　　I will add the following account here. At the time of this earthquake, the tide along the coast of Awa Province receded to three times its usual extent. The children playing along the shore were delighted and went out onto the tidal flats, digging in the sand to gather clams. As kochi (flathead fish) and flounder were leaping about in the remaining pools of water, the young men all went down onto the tidal flats, absorbed entirely in catching the fish. Just then, an old man arrived and cried out at the top of his voice, "Everyone, quickly climb the mountain! If you do not, you will lose your lives!" Though no one understood his reason, they all followed the old man's urging and climbed up the mountain together. Once there, they asked him, "Why did you call us away with such urgency?" The old man replied: "Here is why. When I was young, the tide once receded greatly from this shore — and I think today's situation resembles that time. As it was a rare occurrence, everyone had gone out joyfully onto the tidal flats to gather clams and catch fish, greatly enjoying themselves, when the tide suddenly came rolling back in with a roar. Of those who had been on the tidal flats, not a single one escaped with their life — indeed, not even their bodies were left behind. This is what is called a tsunami. When a tsunami is about to come, the tide recedes fully out to sea, and when it does come, it is so ferocious that there is no time to escape. Dangerous, dangerous!"
