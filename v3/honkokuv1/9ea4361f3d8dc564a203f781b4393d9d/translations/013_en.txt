Even when a person of discernment occasionally tried to reason with them, saying "There is absolutely no chance of such a thing happening — calm your hearts and stay," the momentum of the panicking crowd was impossible to suppress. As a result, no small number of people evacuated with their entire households. However, nothing actually happened. Only then did people half-realize that it had been a baseless rumor, and returning home with regret, they found that in the interim, many households had had their possessions and household goods stolen. One can surmise that the thieves, having regretted missing their chance during the earlier period when people were staying in temporary shelters, cleverly fabricated such a rumor, watched for people to flee, and then stole to their hearts' content. One should keep such things well in mind in advance, so that no matter what rumors arise about some disaster, one can silently judge the logic of the matter, carefully consider whether it is true or false, and not be misled by such words — that is what it means to be a person of true wisdom and discernment. As for tsunamis, as stated in the first volume of this work, they occur when an earthquake causes the mud of the seafloor to surge upward, raising dark waves that for a brief time crash onto the land. It is analogous to filling a basin with water and shaking it with one's hands: if the shaking is gentle, the water's movement is also gentle; if one shakes it very violently, the water moves violently as well, spilling over the edge of the basin — the force of that surging water is governed by the same principle as a tsunami. Therefore, there is no logical reason for the sea to surge four or five days after a great earthquake. If there is no such logic, the event will not occur. From this, one should be able to discern that the rumor was false. That said, even a person of shallow intelligence and ignorance should know as much as this. It is simply that their fear at that moment was so overwhelming that their wits were clouded, and they could not perceive even something so obvious. Around that time, no one knew who had started it, but people were saying and crying out that "Tonight there will certainly be a great earthquake, and the next day one even greater than the very first will strike." Among my acquaintances was a gentleman who ordinarily had good judgment about such matters, but even he brought up this rumor, saying: "There is a common saying — 'Heaven has no mouth; it speaks through people.' Day and night there have been small tremors, and moreover, when one looks in all directions, the sky is hazy and unclear, and the stars are glittering oddly. Surely this contains the signs of an earthquake. This rumor may not be entirely without foundation..."
