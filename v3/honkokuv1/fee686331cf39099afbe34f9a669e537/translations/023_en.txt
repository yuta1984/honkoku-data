1. There is an effective substitute for a *tsuchido* (mud-plastered fire door) called *doromomen* (mud cotton). Take three layers of old cotton cloth sewn together, stitching them at intervals of two to three *sun* in a cross-stitch pattern both horizontally and vertically. Cut the cloth four to five *sun* larger than the space it is intended to cover, attach a thin cord around the edges as a loop, and prepare it so that the cord may be hooked evenly onto nails placed at the four corners. When fire breaks out, place the following mixture in a large half-barrel (*ōhangiri*) or a bucket: one *to* of water, one *shō* of salt, and one *shō* of dried powdered earth; stir together, soak the above-mentioned cotton cloth in the mixture, and hang it in place. If time permits, repeatedly pour water over it from both inside and outside. This is superior to a *tsuchido* (conventional mud-plastered fire door).
