Because the flying embers are not extinguished, they gradually spread from house to house, the fire grows fierce, and even storehouses and valuables are burned and lost — this is truly a lamentable thing. Therefore, during ordinary, peaceful times when nothing is amiss, one should carefully think upon this principle and understand that it is of utmost importance to extinguish flying embers quickly before they spread.

1. Those without a storehouse should construct a mud wall on a neighboring plot of land, approximately six shaku in height, with a width suited to the volume of household goods — either two or three ken — bending both ends inward by three to four shaku to form a U-shape (fold it in this manner, as illustrated). When a fire breaks out, press belongings up against this wall, stack them together, cover them completely with wet straw mats leaving no gaps, tie them down with rope so they will not be blown about by the wind, and pour water over them repeatedly. This takes up little space and serves as an excellent substitute for a storehouse. It stores goods more neatly than simply laying them out in a row, and is also good as a precaution against thieves. In the Kamigata region and the western provinces, those without a storehouse all construct such a wall to an appropriate scale...
