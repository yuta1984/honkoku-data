(Continued from the previous page) Even if there are gaps or rat holes through which drafts might pass, the force of the candleflames will prevent the heat and fire from outside from entering the interior. I myself have tested this principle through numerous fires and can attest that it has prevented buildings from burning down.

Now, for sealing the gaps in the earthen doors (tsuchido), one should prepare in advance by placing kneaded clay (nerituchi) in a bucket, burying it deep in front of the storehouse doors, and keeping it well covered with a lid. This way, even in the coldest winter, it will not freeze and will be ready for use. If such preparations have not been made and a fire breaks out suddenly, miso paste should be used to seal the gaps instead. This is a matter that should be thoroughly understood and regularly taught to all servants and lower members of the household.

One item: Once household goods have been stored in the underground storehouse (anakura), thick boards should be placed as a lid, covered completely with persimmon-tannin-treated paper (shibugami) without any gaps, and tatami mats placed upside-down on top of that. (If tatami mats are not placed on top, large timbers such as roof beams and ridgepoles may fall and break through the boards.) Then, more than one shaku of sand should be poured over this, and finally a small bucket filled with four or five shō of water should be placed on top. (Four or five scooping paddles [hera] for applying sand should be kept nearby.)
