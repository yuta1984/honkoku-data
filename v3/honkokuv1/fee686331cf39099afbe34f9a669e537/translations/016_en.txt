Therefore, when lighting a fire, pay close attention to the surroundings, remove anything that might easily catch fire, and if the stove is near a wall, frequently splash water into the gap between them. Fires originating from stoves have been heard of on numerous occasions, and the author himself has personal recollection of such cases, which is why this is recorded here. One must take great care and never be negligent in this matter.

When beating cotton late at night, the bowstring may snap and fly off, knocking over a lamp; the flame can then spread to the cotton and cause a fire. This happens quite frequently. To prevent this, one should keep two or three straw mats (*mushiro*) or thin rush mats (*usuberi*) nearby in advance, so that one can quickly throw them over any flame and smother it.

Oilskin raincoats (*tōyu kappa*), tobacco pouches, and all objects that have been treated or coated with oil — if they are packed away while still warm from sun-drying, they will invariably generate heat and spontaneously combust. One should quickly throw a wet straw mat over them, press it down firmly, and extinguish the fire. As this too occurs from time to time in various places, one should always cool such items thoroughly before putting them away.
