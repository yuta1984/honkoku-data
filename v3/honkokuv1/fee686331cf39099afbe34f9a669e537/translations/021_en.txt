1. One must not bring the fire of a tobacco pipe inside a mosquito net (*kaya*). If fire is brought inside the mosquito net when one is drunk on sake or exhausted, one may fall asleep and the fire may spread to the hem of the netting. Should a fire break out in such a case, one should quickly cut down the suspension cord (*tsurute*), and smother the flames by throwing a tatami mat or a thin rush mat (*usuberi*) over it. If there is a sick person or an elderly person inside, one should drag them out together with the bedding and protect them from the fire. Likewise, if fire catches on a sliding door (*to*) or a paper screen (*shōji*), one should knock it over and smother the flames by throwing a thin rush mat or similar item over it.

Those who live in retirement quarters (*inkyo-ya*), quarters for lay Buddhist devotees (*dōshinsha-ryō*), and other buildings set apart from the main residence should prepare wooden clappers (*hyōshigi*) and other noise-making instruments in advance, so that in the event of fire, burglary, or any other emergency, they may strike them as a signal to summon assistance and mount a defense. In all matters, even a small (fire must never be taken lightly…)
