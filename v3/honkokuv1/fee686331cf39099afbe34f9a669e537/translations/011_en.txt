Divide belongings into small, light portions; what would take ten trips to carry, if packed all together into one basket, will not damage the goods, will take up less space, and will be ten times more convenient. Furthermore, for those without a storehouse, stack belongings in an open lot, cover them with wet straw mats, and frequently pour water onto the mats — this will serve just as well as a storehouse. Considering these benefits, prepare many baskets by reinforcing them with paper, and they will prove useful not only in a sudden fire but also during the annual soot-sweeping season.

The spread of fire to temples and estates occurs solely through flying embers; therefore, when a fire breaks out, each household should quickly set up a ladder, take up a water broom, climb to the roof, and above all defend the houses on the windward side. If the house itself is not burned, all the valuables inside will naturally be preserved. Yet when people hear "fire," they assume the house will burn entirely, neglect it, and instead rush to tend to their household goods — this is a foolish thing.
