If this is not done, people will already be agitated and flustered at the news of a fire; and if one then cries out "Hurry! Be quick about it!" and adds to the commotion, they will become all the more panicked, and in the end nothing will get done. This is recorded here because it is something learned from experience having directed operations at fires on numerous occasions.

1. During the spring season, when the winds blow, if people of all ranks — whether master or servants — are to go out, they should first be made to store into the storehouse (*kura*) whatever household goods and furnishings the number of people available could manage to carry, and only then be allowed to go out. If this is done, then even should a sudden fire or spreading fire (*ruishō*) occur during their absence, it will be as though those people had been present and working, and there will be no cause for regret.

2. In times of emergency, carrying a *yatate* (a portable writing set) will prove to be of use. Furthermore, if people of all ranks — master and servants alike — each carry two to three hundred *mon* in cash, it will serve as a great convenience in urgent matters, whether for hiring people on the spot or for arranging food and provisions without a moment's delay.
