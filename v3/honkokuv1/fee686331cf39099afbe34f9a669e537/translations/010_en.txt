1. When spring arrives and the remaining winter cold has lessened, each household should, according to their means, place rainwater barrels on the roof and keep water reserves stored. For thatched or straw-roofed houses, large ropes should be hung over the ridge of the roof; for large roofs, wood or bamboo should be tied to ropes at the eaves and midpoint to serve as footholds, so that a good scaffolding is made for fighting fires from the rooftop. Of course, water brooms should be placed in various locations. If one begrudges the small cost of the rope and the labor required for these preparations,

and consequently fails to prevent flying embers during a fire and allows the house to burn, this can only be called suffering great loss for the sake of small savings. This is no different from the proverb "one who hoards a single coin suffers a hundred losses." It is truly a foolish thing. During the calm of ordinary times, think carefully about this logic, do not begrudge small expenses, and make preparations early each year.
