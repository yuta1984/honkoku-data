(Continued from previous page) — and it will also serve as a means of fighting the fire.

1. In the event of a fire, if the house appears to be in danger, water should also be drawn into a *kara-usu* (a mortar/large stone vessel), and a dampened straw mat (*nuremu-shiro*) should be draped over it.

2. Even if a fire breaks out at a distance, when the wind is strong, one should not spend time deliberating over which direction is windward or leeward, but instead quickly set about putting belongings away and in order. The wind can shift suddenly and without warning. Furthermore, unexpected fires from other directions are also impossible to predict. In any case, if one repeatedly practices the storing away of one's belongings, one will become skilled and practiced at it, and will be able to act swiftly when a fire suddenly breaks out. The effort spent in such preparation is a small thing; but should one be caught unprepared and suffer damage from a spreading fire (*ruishō*), there will be great loss and lasting regret.

3. The locations where candles are to be set and where lanterns (*chōchin*) are to be hung in the event of a fire should be tested and confirmed in advance by actually lighting them, and markers should be affixed to the most convenient spots.

4. When there are many people available to work during a fire, the master of the household should keep a watchful eye in all four directions, and, remaining calm so as to avoid mistakes, should direct that items be packed tightly and stacked starting from the back of the storehouse (*kura*), working forward toward the boundary between the outer rooms and the sitting rooms (*zashiki*)...
