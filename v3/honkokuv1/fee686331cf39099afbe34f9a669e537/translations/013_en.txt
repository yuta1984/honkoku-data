1. For those who cannot even construct such a wall, erect a small post of five to six shaku in height in a spot away from the house, and when a fire breaks out, use that post as an anchor around which to pile household goods, draping wet straw mats or thin rush mats face-down over them, and pouring water over them repeatedly — this makes it easy to protect one's belongings from fire. The reason this is recorded here is that the author has witnessed on numerous occasions how, when goods are simply scattered and laid out in various places without such precautions, flying embers alight upon them and the fire spreads from one item to the next until everything is burned.

1. For those who have neither a storehouse nor a place to erect a wall, dig a hole beneath the veranda or somewhere in the garden — even if only during the spring season when fires are frequent — and pack household goods and small items into chests or leather-covered wicker trunks. Remove any outer casings from items that have them and pack only the inner contents tightly, filling gaps with rice, tea, or cotton, then bind them crosswise with cord.
