1. Fires originating from woodsheds occur from time to time. In most cases, this is because charcoal embers (*keshizumi*) that still retain residual heat are carried inside and stored too hastily. Servants and household staff should be thoroughly instructed in advance always to spread the charcoal out on the earthen floor (*doma*) first and allow at least one full night to pass before bringing it inside.

1. Fires in rural areas (*zaikō*) most commonly break out in the sheds used for storing fertilizer for the fields. This happens because straw and chaff are burned to make fertilizer, and people assume the fire has died out and carry the material away too soon. While straw burns out quickly, scraps of wood mixed in with it may retain glowing embers unbeknownst to the carrier, who then moves the material indoors before it is safe. If the material is left to cool in the garden overnight, fire will not break out.
