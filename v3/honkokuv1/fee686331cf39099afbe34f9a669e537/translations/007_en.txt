One item: In the event of a fire, you should determine in advance where the elderly, wife, and children are to evacuate, choosing the location according to the wind direction. Without such preparations, they will wander about searching for a place to go and will find themselves in great difficulty. Now, the master of the house, when evacuating, should carry a spear (yari); the wife should carry a halberd (naginata), or alternatively a wooden sword (bokken) or a staff. Even for women, it would be better to wear a sword at the hip. Naturally, the first things to take when fleeing should be the most important items: official vermillion-seal documents (go-shuin) and black-seal documents (go-kokuin), as well as written records, commendation letters (kanjō), contracts, and gold and silver — all placed in a bag and hung around the neck so that the drawstring is not visible from the outside. (If the bag's string is visible, others may notice it, creating a dangerous situation.) Beyond these, appropriate amounts of spare kimono (kosode), food, and money should also be carried. One must never evacuate empty-handed. If there are many people in the party, a standard-bearer's matoi should be raised, and at night a family-crested paper lantern (chōchin) should be used so that the group remains well gathered together when evacuating.
