(Continued from previous page) — along with a *sanjaku tenugui* (a three-*shaku* length hand towel) and footwear, these should be bundled together per person with a sash (*obi*), placed in advance into a wicker trunk (*tsuzura*), and a label written in large characters reading "Clothing for Change in Case of Fire" should be affixed to the top, so that one does not panic in the event of a sudden fire.

1. Those who do not own a leather hood (*kawa-zukin*) should wear a padded cotton hood (*awase-zukin*) with a *shikoro* (a hanging flap covering the cheeks and neck) attached. Women should cover their heads with a dampened cotton hand towel and tie a *hachimaki* (headband) over it using a three-*shaku* hand towel. Without such precautions, should one's hair catch fire, it will be extremely difficult to extinguish, causing great hardship. Therefore, one should always bear this in mind.

2. Both men and women should always keep a three-*shaku* hand towel, cotton tabi socks, *hosohiki* (thin cord), and footwear within easy reach at all times.

3. When the wind is strong, the morning and evening meals should be prepared and eaten early. Moreover, from spring through the end of the fourth month, extra food should be prepared and kept ready for both morning and evening meals.
