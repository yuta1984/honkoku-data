1. When rendering oil or kneading medicinal compounds (*kōyaku*), one should keep the lid of the pot close at hand, and if the oil catches fire, one should quickly cover it with the lid. If one panics and throws water on it, the burning oil will leap upward and catch on the roof. This is something one should always keep in mind.

1. When construction or repair work (*sakuji*) is being carried out, a designated place for smoking tobacco should be established, and bringing fire into the worksite or temporary structures (*koya-ba*) beyond that designated area should be strictly prohibited.

1. Candles on paper holders (*shisoku*), hand-held candlesticks (*teshoku*), and in general all open flames should be strictly forbidden outside of living quarters (*ima*). Rules to this effect should be established as a matter of course, and lanterns (*chōchin*) and paper-covered lamp stands (*andon*) should be used instead.

1. Clerks (*tedai*) and those of lower station who return late at night from outside should not smoke tobacco after lying down to sleep while leaving the lantern lit. There have been cases of fires breaking out when people have fallen asleep from exhaustion in such circumstances.
