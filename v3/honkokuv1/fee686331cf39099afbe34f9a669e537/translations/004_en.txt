(Continued from previous page) [Interlinear note: When exposed to fire, leather tabi shrink and become useless. In the case of a rapidly spreading fire, this goes without saying.] Now, the reason for carrying a thin rope (hosohiki) is for use as a well-bucket rope. Also, if you have climbed onto a roof and your ladder has been taken away, tie the rope to a roof tile nail or a wind deflector (kazakaeshi). If it is a thatched-roof house, pull out some of the thatch, tie the rope to the bamboo framework inside, and wrap a hand towel (tenugui) around the part you grip — this makes it well-suited for lowering yourself down if you fall from the roof. It is also useful for carrying loads or extending a short ladder. There are many other benefits to carrying it as well.

A three-shaku hand towel, when soaked in water and wrung out into one's mouth, can relieve a parched throat. When one is choking from smoke, holding it in one's mouth will protect the throat from harm. There are other benefits to it besides, and one should certainly always carry one.
