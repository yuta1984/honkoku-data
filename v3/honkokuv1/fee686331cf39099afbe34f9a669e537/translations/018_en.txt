Charcoal made from chestnut wood (*kuri*) is prone to popping and spitting sparks; even a small flying ember can easily ignite dry surroundings and cause a fire. Therefore, when leaving a *kotatsu* unattended, one should cover the coals with ash so that no fire is visible, carefully inspect the surrounding area, and only then leave — if this is done, fires from *kotatsu* will not occur. All members of the household should be made to thoroughly understand the reasoning behind these precautions.

1. Fire-striking boxes (*hiuchi-bako*) should be replaced when they become old. A box that has long stored tinder (*hokuchi*) will naturally become as flammable as tinder itself on its interior surfaces; furthermore, if the lid no longer fits snugly at its joint, this is extremely hazardous. In the case of townhouse rows (*machiya*), fires breaking out from along a shared wall with a neighbor, or disputes in shared rental properties (*shakuya*) where it is difficult to determine whether a fire was caused by oneself or by another — such incidents occur everywhere.
