(Continued from the previous page) One should give instructions calmly and deliberately. If, instead, one merely shouts "Hurry up!" or "Get it in quickly!", everyone will become flustered and panic in the event of a fire, causing confusion and getting nowhere. Therefore, it is best to calm one's voice and give clear directions. Now, changes of clothing, foodstuffs, rice, miso, pots, kettles, and the like, as well as doors and sliding screens (shoji), should be put in last, as these will be immediately needed once the fire is extinguished. If the tatami mats are removed too quickly, people carrying goods may step through the exposed floorboards and injure themselves. As for the doors, shoji screens, and tatami mats, they should be placed vertically, with the longer items standing upright, and pushed in from the far end of the storage space. If stacked horizontally, anything taller than three shaku becomes difficult to lift alone. Naturally, leave space along the wall on the side exposed to the heat as you store things.
