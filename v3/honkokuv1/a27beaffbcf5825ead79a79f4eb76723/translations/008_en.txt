In the same reign [of Emperor Tenmu], on the seventh day of the eleventh month of the nineteenth year, the year of *Kinoe-saru* (甲申), mountains crumbled, rivers surged up, and buildings and temple pagodas across the various provinces were destroyed; great numbers of people and livestock perished. At this time, the hot spring of Iyo Province sank into the earth and ceased to flow again. More than fifty *man* (万, units of area) of farmland in Tosa Province collapsed and became open sea. That night, there came from the east a sound like drumbeats, and above all, the trembling did not cease day or night. At this time, the island of Izu split in two and separated toward the northwest. The island's mountain grew and increased by more than three hundred *jō* (approximately 900 meters). It is said to have been handed down that the earlier drum-like rumbling was the movement of the gods creating this island. From that time—

Twenty-two years passed, and then in the reign of the forty-second emperor [from the founding], Emperor Monmu, on the fifth day of the sixth month of the fourth year of Keiun, the year of *Hinoto-Hitsuji* (丁未), there was a great earthquake. At this time, in the great sky, a cloud appeared measuring eight *jō* in length and three *jō* in width, shaped like the faces of demons on three sides. Thirty-four years passed from that, and in the reign of the forty-fifth emperor, Emperor Shōmu, on the seventh day of the first month of the sixteenth year of Tenpyō, the year of *Kinoe-Saru* (甲申), there was an earthquake. The province of Mino was particularly severely affected.

One hundred and thirteen years later, in the reign of the fifty-fifth emperor, Emperor Montoku, on the eighth day of the third month of the third year of Saikō, the year of *Hinoe-Ne* (丙子), there was an earthquake in the Kinai region that toppled dwellings.
