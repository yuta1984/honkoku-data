Wells dry up, and the weather becomes unusually warm beyond what the season would call for. To use an analogy, it is like a rice cake (*mochi*) swelling up when heated over a fire. Just when the earth is about to tremble, it is said that the sky appears lower than usual and the stars appear twice their normal size. This is not because the earth is rising and the sky is descending; rather, it is like how, just before rain falls, mountains appear much closer than they actually are. Because yang energy (*yōki*) subdues the yin, splits the earth open, and bursts forth toward the heavens, the ground beneath shakes. This is precisely what an earthquake is. The initial shaking is extremely violent. This is the sign that the yang energy within the earth is bursting forth all at once. The subsequent tremors that follow are gentler. This is because the yang energy that previously remained in the earth is escaping little by little.

For this reason, even though all lands exist beneath the same sky, it may tremble in China (*Morokoshi*) and yet not move in Japan, or tremble in Japan and yet not move in China. It may be confined to within a single country without spreading to other lands. Or it may be that Edo is calm while Osaka (Naniwa) trembles, or that Osaka is at peace while Kyoto moves. This is due to the yang energy within the earth, and whether or not the ground swells in a given place. Because the yang energy that has congealed within the earth in a particular location seeks to burst forth from that spot, in severe cases the earth cracks and mountains collapse — occurrences that happen from time to time. Even within a single village, depending on the relative abundance or scarcity of (yang energy) in that area...
