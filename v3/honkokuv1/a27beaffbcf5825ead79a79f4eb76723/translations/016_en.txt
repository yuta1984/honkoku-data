(The sounds of things collapsing and breaking were) like countless thousands of thunderbolts crashing down upon one's very head. People out on the streets crouched down in the middle of the road, while those inside their homes prostrated themselves on the tatami, their hearts seized with terror at the thought that they might be crushed to death by the ridgepoles and beams at any moment. No one felt as though they were truly alive. The violent shaking continued three times in succession, and then after a short while it grew somewhat calmer. Household by household, people flung their tatami out into the street, and linking arms with one another, each scrambling to be first, they fled outside. Without anyone having arranged it, in the blink of an eye, throughout the streets of the capital both inside and outside the city, it became rare to find anyone remaining inside their homes; old and young, men and women, noble and common, high and low without distinction, all gathered knee to knee along the great roads—a sight unseen since the Hōreki era of old, some eighty years past, and truly extraordinary.

[Marginal note: In the *Ruijū Kokushi* (Classified National History), in the entry for the earthquake on the fifth day of the eighth month of the third year of Gangyō during the reign of Emperor Kōkō, it is written: "This night there was a great earthquake. The people of the capital came out from their dwellings (*rosha*) and remained in the streets (*chimata*)," as can be seen there.]

Now, as for the houses of Kyoto: some had collapsed; others had pillars warped and ceilings fallen in; and a particularly great number had their cooking stoves (*kamado*) destroyed. Earthen storehouses (*dozō*) were struck especially hard, and many crumbled and fell on the spot. Beyond these, walls on all four sides fell and large circular elements broke apart, causing a great number of injuries. Throughout all of Kyoto, there was not a single earthen storehouse left intact. And yet there was no one who said they would repair them, no one who even thought to clear the debris away. People simply prostrated themselves in the great road, chanting the names of the gods and buddhas.
