If I hold a memorial service for the dead — the dead will in turn watch over me.

If one reveres those who bestow charity upon others — those who give charity will in turn take pity on oneself.

If you wish to save your own life and flee — first let others flee before you.

When you see the grief of the injured — you yourself should feel their pain as your own.

When you hear of another person's safety — you yourself should rejoice together with them.

When you see a poor person, practice almsgiving — when you see something burning, flee at once.

---

One who is fond of women invites illness — it is just like falling [being struck down] by the trembling of an earthquake.

One's retinue/attendants follow one as if they were part of one's own body — one who is stingy with money will end up buried under plaster and rubble.

Even if you have money, do not try to carry it out [during an earthquake] — rather, flee at the first sign, then return once things have settled.

Even if you are of high standing, do not forget the food of the lower [classes; i.e., humble sustenance] — or perhaps one may first eat refined food, and afterward make do with humble fare.

It is very bad when [charity/rewards are] difficult to receive — [consider] the household goods given as a reward for grace [*onshō no kazai*].

And it is hard to forget living comfortably with ease [when that ease is lost] — [just as one cannot forget] the memorial services of a temple [*tera-in no kuyō*].
