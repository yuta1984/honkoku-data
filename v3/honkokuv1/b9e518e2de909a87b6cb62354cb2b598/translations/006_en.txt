If one has money, there are means/options — and if one is injured, there is life (proof that one is still alive).

Endure hunger and live in the temporary shelter (o-koya / relief huts) — even if there are starving people, if they are not counted/registered [they cannot receive aid].

It is just like walking aimlessly into the jaws of death — even if [your house] collapses, if you do not lament it [and focus on surviving].

It is just like counting only the safety of one's neighbors — a gentleman (kunshi) cherishes his land and place.

Townspeople cherish craftsmen/artisans — even if one enters a house on the verge of collapsing.

For those without a roof — it is still like rain under the eaves (even a crumbling eave is a blessing for those with no shelter at all).

Even if one passes through the gate of money — for a person without compassion.

---

It is just like a straw mat abandoned in the middle of a garden — an earthquake occurs between heaven and earth.

Distribute soba noodles beyond the (temporary) stalls — even relatives are, in a manner of speaking, like strangers.

Even under the eaves, tiles still fall — those who give charity do so morning and evening.

The rice that is given to you, eat it day and night (without discrimination of time) — do not quarrel while sharing a meal.

Perform acts of charity for one's own sake (i.e., for one's own spiritual benefit) — distribute rice and money for one's own sake.

For a person without a place to live — they certainly cannot enter a house with wooden pillars (i.e., a proper home).

For a person without even a rice bowl — they are no different from a beggar (okomo).
