## [Main Text]

On the night of the 24th day of the 3rd month of Kōka 4 (1847), the Year of the Hinoto-Hitsuji (Ding-Wei), around the Hour of the Boar (approximately 10 p.m.), a great earthquake struck seven districts of Shinano Province. The houses of townspeople in castle towns and surrounding settlements were simultaneously shaken to ruin, and fires broke out immediately in various places. The number of those who died by crushing or burning was beyond counting. At the same moment, Mt. Kokūzō collapsed at the Iwakura locality in Yamahirabayashi, falling into the Sai River. The villages upstream were inundated, becoming like a lake, while the riverbed at Tanbashima dried up to become like dry land — one could cross it without even wetting one's feet. In addition, mountains and valleys collapsed in many places, blocking the flow of water. On the 13th day of the 4th month, during the lower part of the Hour of the Monkey (approximately 5 p.m.), the blockage burst all at once, and the waves rose to several jō in height. From Nakajima, all the houses along the Chikuma River were swept away, and the number of those who drowned was beyond counting — thousands upon thousands.

The *Fusō Ryakki* (a Japanese historical chronicle) states: On the last day of the 7th month of the 3rd year of Ninna (887 CE), the Year of Hinoto-Hitsuji (Ding-Wei), in the reign of Emperor Kōkō, a great mountain in Shinano Province collapsed enormously, and the castles and dwellings of six districts were swept away to the ground; cattle, horses, men, and women were carried off and perished in such numbers as to form hills (and so forth). From the Ninna Ding-Wei year to the Kōka Ding-Wei year, nine hundred and sixty-one years have now passed, and we witness once again such a natural calamity. It is not only the people of this province who suffered: owing to the special opening of the sacred doors of the Zenkōji Nyorai (a major Buddhist pilgrimage event), travelers from all provinces died in numbers too great to count.

**Addendum:**

Friends from distant places have frequently written to inquire about the true state of affairs. While I would like to commit every detail to paper, I have written down the main points and had them carved into woodblocks for printing. For whatever is insufficient, I ask the indulgence of those who read this.

　　　　　　　　　Resident of Inariyama, *Miyashō*

**[Written in red ink]:** These blocks were finished being carved around the end of the 6th month (Minazuki). Even now, the earth trembles five to seven times day and night without ceasing.
