Yakura village, Iwashita village, Kawahara village, Yokohara village, Matsuo village —
　　　　all five villages submitted no report.
　　　　This was because the disaster struck so suddenly that no one was able to come forward to report it.
　　　　Needless to say, all inhabitants perished.

　　Of the 130 units of storehouses (dozō) owned by Tsuboi Sukezaemon, only one survived.
　　All the rest were swept away. Approximately 300 people resided within the properties, and all were swept to their deaths.
　　Naganohara village, Koto village, Yokokabe village, Harada village, Koyado village, and Kawado village —
　　all of these villages, being situated along the river, were swept away without a single survivor.

One　　Mishima village had a rice yield of 1,100 koku; of the holdings of the headman Seibei, approximately 500 koku were washed away by floodwaters.　57 houses were inundated and destroyed by floodwaters, 17 houses were filled with sand, and 8 horses perished.
　　Of the 153 households in Inma village, Kawanari village, Ono village, and Ureshima village combined, only six households survived.

One　　The Moku Guardpost was swept away, and the farmhouses, fields, and paddies along the riverside were all inundated and destroyed.
　　Kanai village suffered sand inundation, and in addition, the people and horses of the riverside villages were all swept to their deaths.
　　The above has been copied from a report (chūshin) submitted to Lord Ina Hanzaemon.
