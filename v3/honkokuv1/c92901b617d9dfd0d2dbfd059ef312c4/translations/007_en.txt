It had been clear, but around the hour of the Seventh (around 4 PM), it clouded over again. From the evening, a small amount of sand fell, and as night came, the doors and shoji screens creaked and rattled, and around Asakusa it felt as though there was a slight tremor. On the eighth day, it was cloudy all day, sand fell, and the rooftops became covered as if with frost. On top of that, white hair-like fibers drifted down scattered here and there. They were about four sun (approximately 12 cm) in length, with some measuring about seven sun (approximately 21 cm). On the ninth day, light rain fell and the sand finally stopped.

The approximate extent of the affected area described above stretched from Oiwake in Shinshū to Kōnosu-juku in Bushū; from Wataruse and Yorii in the south to Nakanojō village in Agatsuma District in the north, with the extent beyond the north being unknown. The area covered approximately twenty-eight to twenty-nine ri in length and over twenty ri in width, spanning the provinces of Musashi, Shinano, Kōzuke, Shimotsuke, and Echigo—extending as far as Takada in Echigo.

　At Kanbara Village in Agatsuma District, Kōzuke Province, at the foot of Mt. Asama in Shinshū:
　　Of over one thousand people, only seventeen survived.

　Nishikubo Village, Naka Village, Kusagibara Village, Haneo Village,
　Matsuki Village — in total, five villages: all dead (completely annihilated).
