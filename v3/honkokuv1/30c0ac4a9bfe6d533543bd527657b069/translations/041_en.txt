　　On the Positions and Degrees of the Major Stars

　Question: Each of the constant stars (*jīng xīng*) has its own name. How does one recognize them? How does one know which star is which, know to which grade each belongs, and know that a certain star lies to the south and another to the north?

Answer: The method for observing the stars is first to know the positional boundaries of the equatorial midpoint (i.e., the celestial equator), as well as the positional degrees of the north and south poles. At night, one measures the major stars among the fixed stars (*héng xīng*): the entry of (a star) into the position of a certain lunar mansion is taken as the right ascension (*jīng*, lit. "warp/longitude"), and the distance from the North Pole is taken as the declination (*wěi*, lit. "weft/latitude"). By combining these with the right ascension along which the ecliptic passes through the (twelve) palaces (*gōng*) and the declination of its north-south departure from the celestial equator, and placing (each star) according to its values, and then determining the grade of the size of its body, there will be no omissions.
