(Continued from previous page) …this circle is what is called the meridian circle. When the various luminaries reach this circle, it is said that they are "at the midnight position (子)" or "at the noon position (午)."

[Interlinear note: The midnight-noon (子午) positions of the twelve palaces of the ecliptic are determined by the divisions and degrees of the lunar mansions. The midnight-noon of Heaven is established according to the location where a person resides.]

This circle (the meridian circle) passes through the equator and extends to both the horizon and the two poles. Together with the equator and the horizon, it intersects them at right angles. It remains perpetually fixed and does not move. However, if a person on the surface of the earth moves north or south, this circle remains the same (single and unchanged). If one moves east or west, this circle accordingly differs for each location.

The meridian of the poles is the same as the meridian of the earth — it is still and fixed. From this fixed meridian, a line is extended that follows the Sun as it reaches the zenith and turns. Therefore, the meridian of Heaven moves and does not remain stationary.

[Interlinear note: Youxi (游熙) said: "As the Sun completes one circuit of Heaven, the meridian also follows and completes a circuit of Heaven. This is determined by the zenith of the location where a person resides. The remaining terrestrial branches are all the same."]

The purposes for establishing this circle (the meridian circle) are five:
First, it is used to divide the time periods of the half-day and half-night.
Second, it is used to find the degree at which the lunar mansions culminate high past the zenith. The ones that reach this circle are called "culminating stars" (中星, *chūsei*).
Third, this circle is used to reckon the day. In general, each day beginning from the midpoint of the midnight (子) hour, one correctly aligns with the position beneath this circle.
Fourth, it is used to examine the stars culminating at midnight, so as to determine the Sun's precise lunar mansion.
Fifth, it is used to divide the degrees of the full circuit of Heaven. One may also use the Sun to find the equator, and use the equator to find the north pole.

**The Horizon Circle (地平規)**

*Question:* The meridian circle, it has been explained, bisects the north and south poles, takes as its name the line passing through the zenith, and is established in accordance with the boundary at which the ascent and descent of all luminaries are exactly in the middle. What, then, is the explanation for the horizon circle?

*Answer:* The horizon circle is something that divides the heaven and earth equally in half, established in accordance with the point where a person's feet touch the ground, with one's gaze extended to the furthest boundary in all four directions. A person standing on the surface of the earth can see only half of the sky. The other half perpetually circles beneath the earth and cannot be seen by people. It is precisely this boundary between what can be seen and what cannot be seen; the various luminaries rise and set across it, and the distinction between light and dark, day and night, is made by means of it. Accordingly, this circle is defined, dividing (the sky) into four quadrants (四象), corresponding to the four directions. Each quadrant is bounded by ninety degrees.
