　　　　The Names and Positions of the Fixed Stars (*jīng xīng*)

**Question:** The sun is the progenitor of all things pertaining to *yang*, and is therefore named "Grand Yang" (*tàiyáng*); the moon is the mother of all things pertaining to *yin*, and is therefore named "Grand Yin" (*tàiyīn*). The five planets embody the colors of the Five Phases (*wǔ xíng*), and are therefore named Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth [i.e., Venus, Jupiter, Mercury, Mars, and Saturn]. The fixed stars (*jīng xīng*) are intricately interspersed and spread across the entire sky. How is it possible that each one of them has a name, and that those names carry meaning?

**Answer:** The stars as celestial phenomena were originally without names. However, the affairs of the terrestrial realm correspond above to heaven, and by observing them according to their ordered sequence, [the heavens are mapped onto earthly order]. Therefore, the interior [of the sky] is divided to form the Three Enclosures (*sān yuán*): the Purple Tenuity Enclosure (*Zǐwēi yuán*), the Supreme Tenuity Enclosure (*Tàiwēi yuán*), and the Heavenly Market Enclosure (*Tiānshì yuán*). The exterior is divided into the Four Quadrants (*sì wéi*): in the East — *Jiǎo* (Horn), *Kàng* (Neck), *Dī* (Root), *Fáng* (Room), *Xīn* (Heart), *Wěi* (Tail), *Jī* (Winnowing Basket); in the North — *Dǒu* (Dipper), *Niú* (Ox), *Nǚ* (Maiden), *Xū* (Emptiness), *Wēi* (Rooftop), *Shì* (Encampment), *Bì* (Wall); in the West — *Kuí* (Legs), *Lóu* (Bond), *Wèi* (Stomach), *Mǎo* (Pleiades), *Bì* (Net), *Zī* (Turtle Beak), *Shēn* (Three Stars); in the South — *Jǐng* (Well), *Guǐ* (Ghost), *Liǔ* (Willow), *Xīng* (Star), *Zhāng* (Extended Net), *Yì* (Wings), *Zhěn* (Chariot). The Twenty-Eight Lunar Mansions (*Èrshíbā Xiù*) are thereby established.

The Three Enclosures and the Twenty-Eight Lunar Mansions revolve together with heaven, yet those that remain constant and do not shift [relative to one another] are called the "warp stars" (*jīng xīng*, i.e., fixed stars). The sun, moon, and five planets move through the ranks of the lunar mansions without any fixed position, and these are called the "weft stars" (*wěi xīng*, i.e., the wandering stars or planets). The fixed stars are arrayed along the warp (*jīng*), and the seven luminaries (*qī yào*) travel along the weft (*wěi*). The warp and weft interweave in a complex pattern, and when all the stars are arrayed within it, the patterns of heaven (*tiān zhī wén*) become manifest.

Scholars of celestial learning (*tiān xué jiā*) speak of the Three Enclosures as follows: the Heavenly Market (*Tiānshì*) corresponds, it is said, to the position of the *Míng Táng* (the Luminous Hall). The Supreme Tenuity (*Tàiwēi*) corresponds, it is said, to the position of the imperial court. The Purple Tenuity (*Zǐwēi*) corresponds, it is said, to the position of the palace sleeping chambers. The position of the *Míng Táng* is the residence of the Son of Heaven during his provincial tours of inspection (*xún shǒu*). The position of the court is the residence where governance and adjudication are conducted. The position of the palace chambers is the residence for peaceful rest and repose.
