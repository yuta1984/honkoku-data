**Jupiter (continued)**
Its evening invisibility (夕伏) and conjunction invisibility (合伏) are each sixteen days and eighty-six ke, during which it travels three degrees and eighty-six fen each. It emerges in the east at dawn; combining its fast and slow phases, it travels for one hundred and twelve days, covering seventeen degrees and eighty-four fen. It stations for twenty-four days. Its morning retrograde (晨退) and evening retrograde (夕退) are each forty-six days and fifty-eight ke, during which it retrogrades four degrees, eighty-eight fen, and twelve and a half miao each. It then stations again for twenty-four days. Again, combining slow and fast phases, it travels one hundred and twelve days, covering seventeen degrees and eighty-four fen. Then it again undergoes evening invisibility and upon reappearing, one synodic cycle is complete. Roughly every eighty-four years [the pattern] repeats, but the exact days and times are likewise not identical.

**Mars**
Mars completes one rightward (retrograde/westward) circuit of the heavens in a little less than two years. Its evening invisibility and conjunction invisibility are each sixty-nine days, during which it travels fifty degrees each. It emerges in the east at dawn; combining its fast and slow phases, it travels for two hundred and eighty-four days, covering one hundred and sixty-six degrees. It stations for eight days. Its morning retrograde and evening retrograde are each twenty-eight days, ninety-six ke, and forty-five fen, during which it retrogrades eight degrees, sixty-five fen, and sixty-seven and a half miao each. It stations again for eight days. Combining slow and fast phases, it travels two hundred and eighty-four days, covering one hundred and sixty-six degrees, then again undergoes evening invisibility and upon reappearing, one synodic cycle is complete. Roughly every eighty years [the pattern] repeats, but the exact days and times are likewise not identical.

**Venus**
Venus moves rightward, traveling before and behind the Sun, and completes one circuit of the heavens per year. Its morning invisibility (晨伏) and conjunction invisibility (合伏) are each thirty-nine days, during which it travels forty-nine degrees and fifty fen each. It emerges in the west at evening; combining its fast and slow phases, it travels for two hundred and thirty-one days, covering two hundred and fifty degrees and fifty fen. It stations for five days. It undergoes evening retrograde for ten days, ninety-five ke, and thirteen fen, retrogressing three degrees, sixty-nine fen, and eighty-seven miao. It undergoes evening retrograde invisibility (夕退伏) for six days, retrogressing four degrees and thirty-five fen. Its conjunction retrograde invisibility (合退伏) is the same as the evening retrograde invisibility. Its morning retrograde is the same as the evening retrograde. It stations for five days. Combining fast and slow phases, it travels two hundred and thirty-one days, covering two hundred and fifty degrees and fifty fen, and then again becomes invisible at morning (晨伏, i.e., morning conjunction).

Regarding its motion: when [Venus] rises in the east before the Sun at dawn, it is called "Qiming" (啓明, the Morning Star); when it follows the Sun and sets in the west at dusk, it is called "Changgeng" (長庚, the Evening Star).
