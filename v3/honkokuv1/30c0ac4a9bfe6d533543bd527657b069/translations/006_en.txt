One need only point to the Pole Star to distinguish East, West, South, and North, and observe the Sun to determine the seasons and the alternation of day and night. Therefore, depending on where one lives, the manner in which the Sun shines differs.

If one lives on the equator — in the band of land extending twenty-three and a half degrees to the north and south — the Sun passes directly over the zenith at the two equinoxes (spring and autumn). At midday there is no shadow. After the spring equinox, shadows fall to the south; after the autumn equinox, they fall to the north. This zone is called the Warm Belt (Dantai / Torrid Zone).

Beyond twenty-three and a half degrees north and south, extending up to sixty-six and a half degrees, the Sun does not pass through the zenith of these lands, but is neither too close nor too far away. These two circles north and south constitute the Temperate Belt (Seitai / Temperate Zones). They are neither excessively cold nor hot.

[Interlinear note: This belt is mild and temperate, and sages and worthies are born here. China's position extends from eighteen degrees north of the equator to forty-two degrees north, and falls precisely within this zone.]

In both the north and south, from sixty-six and a half degrees onward to each respective pole, these regions form the Cold Belt (Reitai / Frigid Zones). On some days the Sun revolves around these lands and is continually visible; on other days it revolves and remains continually hidden. The periods of hiding and appearing may last several months or even as long as half a year.

[Interlinear note: Master Jie said: "Below the two poles, the celestial wheel revolves horizontally: half a year constitutes day, and half a year constitutes night. The land is extremely cold, and its people endure the cold."]

This is a general outline of the Five Zones (Gotai). From this one can infer that those who are distant from the equator, whether to the north or south, will have climates that are necessarily opposite to one another. For example, when the Sun is in the constellation Seiki (the Dipper/Capricorn; i.e., the Ox Palace, 丑宮): for those facing north it is the winter solstice, while for those facing south it is the summer solstice. This holds true for every seasonal node without exception.

Furthermore, from this one can infer that the terrestrial globe is the place where people dwell, and using the zenith one can divide the four directions; it is also possible to divide it into three hundred sixty degrees to correspond to the movements of Heaven. East and west are measured as longitude (kei), reckoned by the ecliptic; north and south are determined as latitude (i), established by the meridian (north-south line). If one is measuring north and south, the two poles serve as its endpoints.

[Interlinear note: Master You Xiong said: "Western learning measures the terrestrial globe and finds that its circumference is 90,000 li. If one travels 250 li northward, the altitude of the Pole Star increases by one degree. At 22,500 li, (the Pole Star) is ninety degrees high."]
