**On the Heaven of the Primum Mobile (宗動天)**

Question: The fixed stars are above the Seven Luminaries; there are stars that can be measured, and there are motions upon which one can rely. But as for the Heaven of the Primum Mobile—there are no stars that can be measured, and its motion has no basis (that can be directly observed). How, then, can we know that it exists above the Heaven of the Fixed Stars?

Answer: The Seven Luminaries and the fixed stars each have two kinds of motion in their revolutions. The first is a right-handed rotation from west to east, each having its own proper motion—for example, the Moon completing one circuit in twenty-seven days, and the Sun doing so in one year. The second is a left-handed rotation from east to west, completing one circuit per day. This appears as though there are two heavens, and it is for this reason that they can simultaneously produce these two motions. From this we know that above the heavens of the Seven Luminaries and the fixed stars, there is yet again a Primum Mobile (宗動天).

Above the Primum Mobile, the Heaven of Constant Rest (常静天) presides over it. The single heaven of the Primum Mobile pulls and governs all the other heavens, completing one circuit per day, while the various heavens exist within it, each settled in its proper place and each carrying out its own proper motion. The Seven Governors (Seven Luminaries) and the fixed stars, already carried westward by the Primum Mobile and completing one circuit per day, do so at a speed that is nearly beyond comprehension. Yet the various heavens also wish to accomplish their own proper motions, one tending eastward and one westward, their tendencies mutually opposing and conflicting. Thus those close to the Primum Mobile find it extremely difficult to move eastward, while those farther from the Primum Mobile find it progressively easier to move eastward. This is the origin of the varying speeds of the Seven Governors and the fixed stars.
