### The Sun's Degrees of Entry and Exit from the Celestial Equator

**Question:** When observing stars, one must first identify the celestial equator and the North Pole. The Pole has a star that can be pointed to. However, the equator can only be located through measurement. Without knowing the method of measurement, one wanders several degrees without any basis.

**Answer:** It is established that the sun enters north and south of the celestial equator by twenty-three and a half degrees on each side. There is no difference between the inner and outer [hemispheres], and its passage back and forth occurs gradually. The sun's position at each of the twenty-four solar terms is used as the standard for measuring this. [Note: The sun's distance from the equator shifts day by day, but the days of the solar terms alone are used as the standard, making it easy for people to measure.] From this, one can know the altitude of the celestial equator at one's location, and from that, one can further infer the altitude of the North Pole above the horizon at that location.

This reveals the boundary of the celestial sphere that divides it equally between north and south.]
