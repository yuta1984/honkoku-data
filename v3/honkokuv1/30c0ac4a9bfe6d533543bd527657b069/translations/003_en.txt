(Continued from the previous page)...Heaven having a body does not mean that Heaven makes itself into a body of its own accord. There is that by which it comes to be a body. Earth having a form does not mean that Earth makes itself into a form of its own accord. There is that by which it comes to be a form. Both Heaven and Earth have their origins.

The Book of Odes says: "King Wen, with a reverent and careful heart, served the Lord on High in radiant devotion." Guanyinzi also says: "Heaven does not make itself Heaven of its own accord — there is that which makes it Heaven. Earth does not make itself Earth of its own accord — there is that which makes it Earth."

[Interlinear note: The Xinyu states: Within the Great Void, primordial qi rubbed and stirred, steamed and became a heavy, clear white mist. The yin accumulated and the yang grew; breathing and exhaling without cease, it suddenly became open and hollow within, like a floating bubble. The sun, moon, and stars came to be affixed [to Heaven] from this. Thus the origin of Heaven's creation arose from the faintest vapor. Furthermore, within the empty bubble, yang qi burst forth and dispersed, drying into "wild horses" and dust motes, flying day by day and gathering as dew, swirling and condensing at the center, until it became solid within like a clod, resembling a pellet. The great earth, mountains, and rivers began from this. Thus Earth originally arose from the finest dust.]

The Gezhi Cao says: "The teeming multitude of all things, without a ruler, falls into chaos. That emperors and kings exist for humanity is because [this principle] is manifest and great." Do not all of these together speak of Heaven having a supreme ruler?

Indeed, among birds there is the phoenix, among beasts the qilin, among bees the hive, and among ants there is the queen — all have their kings and leaders. How much more so, then, must there be such a ruler for something as great as Heaven and Earth, which governs the movement of time and the generation of things, reaching to the heights above and coiling through the depths below?

The round Nine Heavens — who designed and measured them? Their movements and positions — who directs and governs them? Furthermore, the great barges of the rivers, the ships of the seas, the skiffs of Yue, the boats of Shu — sailing on the wind across the great waters, crossing shores and riding waves: is it the boats themselves that do this? There is a helmsman who steers them, as if a god were present within.

The Supreme Ruler of Heaven and Earth had no beginning before Heaven, and shall have no end after Heaven. Its full omnipotence as the pivotal axis operates within that which moves on in solemn, ceaseless continuity — and there is surely something about this that lies beyond what human thought and deliberation can reach. Therefore, [the sages] simply attributed it to Heaven and left it at that.

　　On the Structure of Heaven (Tianti)

Question: The vast blue expanse of Heaven is high, distant, and without limit. The stars and constellations are intricately arranged, and their forms and shapes revolve in their courses. Can one hear and learn about this?

Answer: The body of Heaven is like blue glass — transparent, luminous, and perfectly round. The Seven Luminaries and the ranks of constellations revolve in layer upon layer, enveloping the Earth.

[Interlinear note: The Seven Governors [i.e., the Seven Luminaries] revolve at different heights and depths. Hence the expression "layer upon layer."]

The Earth is like a pellet, positioned at the very center of Heaven, eternally still and unmoving, with people dwelling on all four sides. The uppermost layer is the Constant-Stillness Heaven (Changjing Tian), which serves as the ruler over all the Heavens.
