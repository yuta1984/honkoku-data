(Continued from previous page) …the longitudinal degrees of the horizon. By means of these, one can determine the degrees by which the north pole and the equator are separated from the horizon (earth). One can determine the portions of the rising and setting of the stars and constellations, and which stars are always visible and never set, and which stars are always set and never seen. One can determine the degrees at which the Seven Luminaries and the lunar mansions rise and set simultaneously, and the degrees at which they rise and set in succession. One can determine by how many degrees of latitude (緯度) the Sun and each luminary are separated from the equator when they rise above the horizon. One can discern the direction (azimuth) of the rising and setting of each luminary. One can calculate the degrees of gradual ascent of each luminary, from one degree up to ninety degrees — all of this can be known and examined.

---

*Question:* For all these circles, one must first make a model (象) before one can understand them. Even if the principles are analyzed and explained in detail, in the end one is still left bewildered. [Is this not so?]

*Answer:* The ecliptic, the equator, the north and south poles, the meridian circle, and the horizon circle — these are nothing more than constructs that take mutual observation of their forms to estimate the principles, and establish their representations as a frame of reference. If one does not understand these, and yet wishes to know the movements of the various luminaries, one will be utterly bewildered and at a loss. Therefore, if one wishes to understand the representations, one must first make clarifying these principles the foremost priority. If one has not thoroughly memorized these principles, even if one mounts an observation platform and operates the instruments, one will still be left in a state of bewilderment.

**The Sun (太陽)**

*Question:* Among the multitude of luminaries spread throughout the heavens, the Sun is their chief. Why does it not reside above all the heavens, but instead resides below the lunar mansions? Among the seven luminaries moving in their various courses, the Sun is particularly regular and correct. Why, then, does it have expansion and contraction (舒縮) according to the seasons, and why do the days have varying lengths — long and short — from day to day?

*Answer:* Regarding the Sun: the *Huangdi Jing* (黄帝経, *Classic of the Yellow Emperor*) states: "Heaven condenses its yang essence (陽精) to form the Sun. The Moon and stars make use of it, and the ten thousand things all make use of it. Therefore it resides in the center of Heaven, perfectly positioned to illuminate above and below, its warmth reaching down to the earth below, able to sustain and nourish the ten thousand things. For this reason it is called the chief of the stars and the Moon."

[Interlinear note: The *Huangdi Jing* states: "When Heaven is bright, the sun and moon are (by contrast) not bright." The *Qiancao* (潜草) states: "The light of the space between Heaven and earth is entirely the fire of the Sun." Wenjiao (文僥) said: "All transformations and phenomena in the space between Heaven and earth are the work of pneumatic light (気光)."]

If the Sun resided at the highest position, its warmth and heat would not reach (the earth), and the ten thousand things would have difficulty flourishing and multiplying. If it resided at the lowest position, the dryness and heat would be excessive, and the ten thousand things would suffer damage from parching and desiccation. (Continues on next page)
