(Continued from previous page) …these are called the longitudinal degrees of the horizon. By means of this circle, one can determine the degrees by which the north pole and the equator are separated from the horizon (i.e., their altitudes). One can determine the rising and setting divisions of the stars and celestial bodies, including which stars are always visible and never set, and which stars are always below the horizon and never visible. One can determine the degrees at which the seven luminaries and lunar mansions rise and set simultaneously, as well as the degrees by which they rise and set at different times. One can determine how many degrees of latitude each of the sun and luminaries is separated from the equator when it rises above the horizon. One can discern the directional bearings of the rising and setting of each luminary. One can calculate the degrees of the gradual ascent of each luminary, from one degree up to ninety degrees — all of these can be known and examined.

---

*Question:* For all these circles, one must necessarily construct a physical model (instrument) before one can understand them. No matter how clearly the principles are explained, one ultimately remains at a loss.

*Answer:* The ecliptic, the equator, the north and south poles, the meridian, and the horizon are nothing more than things established by taking physical forms and drawing out their principles from them, setting up their representations as a basis for reference. If one does not know these and yet wishes to understand the movements of the luminaries, one will be utterly at a loss with nowhere to turn. Therefore, if one wishes to understand the representations, one should first make it a priority to clarify these principles. If one does not have these principles thoroughly committed to memory, then even if one mounts a platform and turns a celestial sphere (an armillary sphere or similar instrument), one will still be left in bewilderment.

**The Sun (太陽)**

*Question:* Among the luminaries arrayed in their multitudes, the Sun is their progenitor. Why, then, does it not reside above all the heavens, but instead resides below the lunar mansions? The seven luminaries move in complex paths, yet the Sun moves with particular regularity. Why, then, does it have expansion and contraction (舒縮) according to the seasons, and why does the length of the day (永短) vary from day to day?

*Answer:* Concerning the Sun: the *Yellow Emperor's Classic* (黄帝経) states: "Heaven condenses its yang essence to form the Sun; the Moon and stars make use of it, and the ten thousand things all make use of it." Therefore, by residing in the middle of the heavens, it is perfectly positioned to illuminate and reflect light both above and below; its warmth reaches the earth below, and it is able to nurture and sustain the ten thousand things. For this reason it is called the progenitor of the stars and the Moon.

[Interlinear note: The *Yellow Emperor's Classic* says: "When Heaven is bright, the sun and moon are not bright (in themselves)." The *Qiancao* (潜草) says: "The light in the space between Heaven and Earth is all the fire of the Sun." Wenjiao (文僥) says: "All the changes and appearances in the space between Heaven and Earth are produced by pneumatic light (気光)."]

If the Sun were to reside at the very top (of the heavens), its warmth would not reach (the earth), and the ten thousand things would find it difficult to grow and flourish. If it were to reside at the very bottom, the scorching heat would be far too intense, and the ten thousand things would suffer damage and desiccation from it.
