In the void of the Great Void (太虚, *taikyo*), there inherently exists a divine transformative power (神化, *shinka*) that does not revolve, and this serves as its sovereign. Only thereafter can this Heaven travel its course throughout all eternity without transgressing its path.

Master Li〈personal name: Zhizao (之藻)〉said: "Measuring from the centre of the pole and drawing lines outward in a circle, one obtains the latitudinal degrees of north and south. From the pole to the midpoint of Heaven is ninety degrees, which constitutes the boundary of the equator. Those who dwell directly beneath the equator take the equator as their zenith. Those who dwell directly beneath the two poles take the two poles as their zenith. The apparent height or lowness of the poles is determined by the place where the observer resides. Drawing lines outward in sectors from the centre of the pole yields the twelve palaces, and within those palaces, finer lines are drawn to form the degrees of east–west longitude. Where these converge at the midpoint of Heaven, they become the longitudinal degrees of the equator."

[Interlinear note: All of these are determined by methods of measurement.]

The Sun follows the degrees of its path along the ecliptic, and although it reaches the extreme south and extreme north, it only departs from the equator by twenty-three and a half degrees. Therefore, the circles at the north and south celestial poles, which the celestial wheel (天輪, *tenrin*) encircles horizontally, are places the ecliptic does not reach — these are the extremes where the imbalance of long and short days and nights is greatest. Within those two circles, the *qi* of Heaven and Earth is exceedingly cold. Throughout these regions there is always some shadow of the Sun, and half a year constitutes day while half a year constitutes night.

**The Meridian Circle (子午規, *Shigo-ki*)**

*Question:* In the armillary sphere (*huntian*), the equator is taken as the central division, and the north and south celestial poles are taken as the "meridian" (子午, *shigo*, lit. "north–south"). Furthermore, the zenith is taken as "noon" (午, *go*) and the point below the earth as "midnight" (子, *shi*). Then what does it mean to take the equator as the meridian?

*Answer:* That the positions of the north and south celestial poles constitute the true meridian — this is correct. However, there is the "meridian of Heaven" and there is also the "meridian of the north and south poles." The meridian of Heaven simply takes the middle, and is named after the line that passes through the positions of the north and south poles and crosses the zenith; it is established in accordance with the boundary at which the ascents and descents of all the luminaries reach their midpoint.

The Sun makes one full circuit of the heavens in one day. When it appears in the east and gradually ascends toward the zenith, this is called "midday" (午中, *gochū*). The eastern half of the daytime above the horizon during which it ascends is called "rising" (升, *shō*). After passing the zenith, turning toward the west, and gradually sinking toward the horizon, this is the western half of the daytime and is called "descending" (降, *kō*). All other luminaries behave in the same manner. At the midpoint boundary between this rising and descending, one circle is established as the meridian (子午)… (continues on next page)
