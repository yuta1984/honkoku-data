(Continued from previous page) …there are immovable poles, which serve as its pivots. In the empty void of the Great Void (太虚, *taikyo*), there is inherently a divine transformation that does not revolve, which serves as its sovereign. Only thereafter is this Heaven able to circulate throughout all eternity without transgressing (its course).

Master Li [whose personal name was Zhizao (之藻)] said: "Measuring from the centre of the poles, one draws lines outward in a circle to form the latitudinal degrees of north and south. The ninety degrees to the middle of Heaven (the equator) constitute the boundary of the equator. Those who dwell beneath the equator take the equator as their zenith. Those who dwell beneath the two poles take the two poles as their zenith. The rising or lowering of the poles is determined by where a person resides. Drawing lines from the centre of the poles outward in segments, one forms the twelve celestial palaces. Within the palaces, dividing into finer lines yields the longitudinal degrees of east and west. Where these lines converge at the middle heaven, they form the longitudinal degrees of the equator."

[Interlinear note: All of these are determined by methods of measurement.]

The Sun revolves according to the degrees of the ecliptic and, even at its extreme south or extreme north, moves no more than twenty-three and a half degrees from the equator. Accordingly, the circles of the north and south poles, which the celestial rings encircle horizontally, are regions that the ecliptic never reaches — the extremities where the duration of day and night and the imbalance of light is at its greatest. Within these two circles, the qi of Heaven and Earth is exceedingly cold; throughout the circumference there is perpetual sunlight (or shadow), and half the year serves as day and half the year as night.

**The Meridian Circle (子午規)**

*Question:* In the armillary sphere (渾天, *konten*), the equator serves as the central division, and the north and south poles serve as the positions of "midnight and noon" (子午, *shigo*). Yet it is also said that the zenith is noon (午) and the nadir is midnight (子). If so, why does the equator serve as the meridian?

*Answer:* That the positions of the north and south celestial poles constitute the true meridian — this is indeed correct. However, there is a meridian of Heaven, and there is also a meridian of the north and south poles. The meridian of Heaven simply takes the middle: it is formed by dividing the line that passes through the positions of the north and south poles and crosses the zenith, and is established in accordance with the boundary at which the ascent and descent of all the luminaries are exactly in the middle.

The Sun revolves once around Heaven each day; it is seen in the east and gradually ascends toward the zenith, which constitutes midday (午中, *gochū*). This, the eastern half of the daytime above the horizon, is called "ascent" (升, *shō*). Passing the zenith and turning westward, it gradually descends toward the horizon; this is the western half of the daytime, called "descent" (降, *kō*). All other luminaries behave in the same manner. At the middle boundary of this ascent and descent, one circle is established as the meridian… (continues on next page)
