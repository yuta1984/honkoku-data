...year dingchou [first year of Taichu of the Han dynasty]: the solar retreat reached the twentieth degree of Dou (斗). In the ninth year of the Taiyuan reign period, jiashen, of the Jin dynasty: the solar retreat reached the seventeenth degree of Dou. In the tenth year of the Yuanjia reign period, guiyou, of the [Liu] Song dynasty: the solar retreat reached the fourteenth degree of Dou. In the twelfth year of the Kaiyuan reign period, jiazi, of the Tang dynasty: the solar retreat reached the tenth degree of Dou. In the fourth year of the Qingli reign period, jiashen, of the [Northern] Song dynasty: the solar retreat reached the fifth degree of Dou. In the fourth year of the reign of Emperor Duzong, wuchen: the solar retreat reached the fourth degree and two fen of Dou. In the first year of the Zhizheng reign period, xinsi, of the Yuan dynasty: the solar retreat reached the tenth degree of Ji (箕). In the seventeenth year of Hongwu, jiazi: the solar retreat reached the seventh degree of Ji. In the third year of the Jiajing reign period, jiashen: at first dusk (初昏), the Shi (室) lunar mansion culminated, and the solar retreat reached the third degree of Ji. At the winter solstice of the fortieth year of the Wanli reign period, renzi: the Sun was still at the third degree, nineteen fen, and nineteen seconds of Ji on the ecliptic, and at the fourth degree, four fen, and twenty-five seconds of Ji on the equator. The inner intersection (內道口, ascending node) was at the first degree of Bi (壁), and the outer intersection (外道口, descending node) was at the initial degree of Zhen (軫). From this point to the sixteenth year of the Chongzhen reign period, guiwei, spanning thirty-one years, only a difference of one fen and thirty-five seconds had accumulated.

[Interlinear annotation: This difference represents advancement, not retreat. Tang Dao has not yet measured [the position at] the winter solstice of the Shunzhi reign period year gengzi; the Sun [at that time] was at the third degree and twenty-nine fen of Ji.]

From the Jiajing jiashen year to the Chongzhen guiwei year — one hundred and twenty years — the Sun continued to advance and had not yet retreated. This shows that the subtle movement of the celestial Way also has variations of speed, and cannot be constrained by a fixed number of years.

[Interlinear annotation: Jie Zi says: "The precession is not only unequal in speed; according to the detailed measurements of You Zi [You Yan], there are also cases where [the Sun] first advances and then retreats again."]

Now, reckoning back from Chongzhen guiwei to Qingli jiashen: over six hundred years, the Sun accumulated a difference of over twelve degrees — thus one degree of difference per fifty years. From Qingli jiashen back to Kaiyuan jiazi: three hundred and twenty-one years, with the Sun accumulating a difference of five degrees — thus one degree per sixty-four years. From Kaiyuan jiazi back to the Han Taichu dingchou year: eight hundred and twenty-seven years, with the Sun accumulating a difference of ten degrees — thus one degree per eighty-three years. From Taichu dingchou back [the text reads "止" (zhǐ), which should read "上" (shàng)] to King Zhuangxiang's renzi year: one hundred and forty-five years, with the Sun accumulating a difference of two degrees — thus one degree per seventy-three years. From King Zhuangxiang's renzi year back [again "止" should read "上"] to Emperor Yao's jiachen year: two thousand and twenty-eight years, with a difference of twenty-eight degrees — thus one degree per seventy-two years. These are the figures obtained by dividing [the total period] and examining [each segment] separately. [The discussion of the total continues on the next page...]
