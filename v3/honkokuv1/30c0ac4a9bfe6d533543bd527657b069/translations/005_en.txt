…[The Sun] travels along the central line of the ecliptic. Its celestial centre (the centre of its orbit) does not share the same centre as the other heavens. Therefore, its revolution as projected upon the Earth's surface is not a uniform parallel motion. The eight degrees to either side of the ecliptic constitute the path along which the Moon and the five planets move in and out. The longitudinal and latitudinal motions of the Sun and Moon both revolve according to the pole of the ecliptic. Hence, they do not coincide with the pivot of the meridian (the north-south polar axis). Those among our Confucian scholars who discuss the "Nine Heavens" do so because the orbital degrees of the fixed stars and the Seven Luminaries each differ; stacking one above the other in sequence, they provisionally establish multiple layers in order to make measurements.

**The Body of the Earth (Chitai)**

Question: The *Tripiṭaka* (Buddhist scriptures) divides the four directions into four continents (shi-jū); Zou Yan stated that "the vast ocean surrounds the Nine Provinces (Kyūshū) on the outside"; and Wenchang said that "the waters reach up to the heavens." All of these regard the Earth as a flat land. Now it is said that the celestial body is perfectly spherical (kon'en). The ancients also said: "Heaven is like the white of an egg, and the Earth is like the yolk." This means that Heaven envelops the outside, and the Earth extends through the middle. Yet Heaven is the light and pure qi, and the Earth is the heavy and turbid dregs. If the Earth has already formed a spherical body extending through the middle, how can it float in space without falling? Since there are people on all four sides, how do they settle comfortably in their dwelling places? And can they truly distinguish the four directions?

Answer: Heaven and Earth are both spherical and are fundamentally connected to one another. The ancients said: "Reduce the Earth by one foot, and you add one foot to the Heavens." This being so, the Earth is also Heaven. It is called "Earth" only when we speak of it in terms of its form. It is simply that the void of Heaven (Tenkyō) revolves around the outside day and night, while the substance of the Earth (Chijitsu) remains fixed and unmoved at the centre. The hollows on the four sides of the Earth become rivers and seas; the protrusions become mountains and peaks; the flat parts become fields and land. Wherever people dwell and stand, all rest upon the spherical body. Heaven wraps around it and adheres to it; the revolving qi rises and falls without cease, sealing tightly on all four sides, leaving no room for the Earth to tilt or topple.

[Marginal note: The character 他 ("it/other") should be written as 地 ("Earth").]

The Earth has no choice but to congeal at the centre and thereby maintain itself. Moreover, since it has no particular corners or fixed directions, all four sides are equally "up," and there is nowhere to fall. It fits perfectly at the very centre of Heaven, and there is also nothing to lean upon. When we say that Heaven rises in the east and descends in the west, this too is said from the perspective of the place where a person happens to be standing. As for Heaven itself, there is no place where it is not rising, and no place where it is not descending. It revolves in its entirety, encompassing all…
