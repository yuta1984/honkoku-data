The palaces, lodges, gardens, and parks (*gōng guǎn yuàn yòu*) of the Son of Heaven are located here. The Stomach (*Wèi*) governs the various storehouses and the granaries of the five grains. The Pleiades (*Mǎo*) is named "Yak-Tail Standard" (*máo tóu*) and governs punishments and prisons. Therefore the Curled Tongue (*Juǎn Shé*) is appended to it. The Net (*Bì*) governs foreign military affairs. Between the Pleiades and the Net, there is the "Celestial Street" (*Tiān Jiē*) that divides (the two domains). The three central stars of Orion (*Shēn*) form the "Central Army" (*Zhōng Jūn*); the central star is the "Great General" (*Dà Jiāng*), and the flanking (stars) are the "Staff Officers" (*Cān Móu*). The two shoulder (stars) are the left and right generals, and the two foot (stars) are the vanguard and rearguard generals. The three small stars in the center are called "Punishment" (*Fá*), corresponding to the Celestial Commandant (*Tiān Dū Wèi*). The Turtle Beak (*Zī*) is the supply depot (*cáng fǔ*) of a marching army. The Seated Banner (*Zuò Qí*) is appended to it. The Well (*Jǐng*) governs the affairs of the water gauge (*shuǐ héng*), laws and ordinances, and equitable administration (*píng zhōng*). Among things, none is more level than water. Therefore the laying out of states, the planning of cities, the demarcation of fields, and the division into provinces all take their image from this (the levelness of water). Therefore the Four Waterways (*Sì Dú*), the Five Feudal Lords (*Wǔ Zhū Hóu*), the Southern River (*Nán Hé*), and the Northern River (*Běi Hé*) are all appended to it. A state must have an earth altar (*shè*). Therefore the Celestial Earth Altar (*Tiān Shè*) is appended to it. An earth altar must have its sacrifices. Therefore the Celestial Kitchen (*Tiān Chú*) is appended to it. The Ghost (*Guǐ*) governs the offerings and sacrifices to spirits (*cí sì*). The Willow (*Liǔ*) governs plants and trees as well as banquets and feasts. The Star (*Xīng*) represents the assembly of culture and civilization, (specifically) the embroidered decorations (*fǔ fú*) and (the patterns of) literary adornment (*wén zhāng*). The Extended Net (*Zhāng*) governs the rare treasures and articles of use (*fú yòng*) in the ancestral temples. Therefore the Celestial Temple (*Tiān Miào*) and the Exalted One (*Tài Zūn*) are appended to it. In the *jiǎzǐ* year of the Tianqi era, the five planets gathered in the Extended Net (*Zhāng*). The Wings (*Yì*) are the music bureau (*yuè fǔ*) of the Son of Heaven.

The Chariot (*Zhěn*) governs chariots, cavalry, and the transport of loads. And (with this,) one returns to the Horn (*Jiǎo*), (completing the cycle). As for phenomena such as shaking stars, falling stars, colliding stars, shooting stars, encroachment (*líng fàn*), stationary passage (*shǒu liú*), bristling rays (*máng jiǎo*), and occultation (*yǎn*)—each is divined according to its type. Guest stars (*kè xīng*), flowing stars (*liú xīng*), ominous stars (*yāo xīng*), auspicious stars (*ruì xīng*), comets (*huì xīng*), and broom stars (*bèi xīng*) are all emanations of the auspicious and inauspicious (*zāi xiáng*) vapors of the earth rising up to manifest in the sky. Therefore they appear at no fixed place, are seen at no fixed time, and likewise have no fixed form.

　　The Quantity of Fixed Stars

*Question:* The fixed stars already have names assigned in order. Yet there are also those with varying sizes (that are) unequal (to one another) and those whose numbers are uneven. Can it be said that none have been omitted?

*Answer:* Fixed stars (*héng xīng*) are also called "arrayed lodges" (*liè xiù*) and also called "warp stars" (*jīng xīng*). The term *héng* ("constant/fixed") means that their (positions) do not change from age to age. The term *jīng* ("warp") is used to distinguish them from the northward and southward movements of the Five Wefts (*wǔ wěi*, i.e., the five planets). Their number is exceedingly great, and none can fully exhaust (the count). They have now already been measured and determined; upon examining their sizes, they have been divided into six grades (*liù děng*). The first grade consists of the largest stars, such as the Five Emperors' Thrones (*Wǔ Dì Zuò*).
