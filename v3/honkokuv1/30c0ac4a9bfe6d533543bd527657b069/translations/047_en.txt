**Answer:** It is the principle of all things that what is at rest has only one state of rest, and what moves has only one motion. It is impossible that within a single moment, motion and rest should alternately appear; nor is it possible that two motions should simultaneously arise such that one goes eastward and the other westward. Now, when we observe the arrayed lunar mansions, the Sun, the Moon, and the Five Planets, we find that their motions differ from one another. It is for this reason that we say each has its own heaven (*tiān* 天, celestial sphere) to which it belongs.

As for Venus and Mercury specifically, the orbits along which they travel encircle the outside of the Sun's orbital circle. Because the [outermost] heaven revolves, the Sun revolves; because the Sun revolves, Venus and Mercury revolve. This is like a cotton-fluffing mill (*xù niǎn* 絮碾)—the iron central axle turns, and the two weights on the left and right alternately ride upon it. Therefore, at one moment [these planets] travel in front of the Sun, and at another moment they travel behind the Sun, so that they appear to have three motions.

The fact that every night one sees them rising in the east and setting in the west, making one complete circuit each day, is the **first motion**. This is caused by the uppermost, vigorously moving heaven (the *Primum Mobile*, *zōngdòng zhī tiān* 宗動之天) pulling them along. The fact that they accompany the Sun eastward, completing one circuit per year, is the **second motion**, arising from the heaven to which each planet belongs constantly rotating eastward. And the fact that [a planet] sometimes goes south and sometimes north, now fast and now slow, without constancy, is **yet another motion**—for the planet moves within the heaven to which it belongs as if tracing a circular ring (*quān* 圏).

Thus, within a single moment there are several motions, as if there were heavens pulling and attaching [the planets] to them, forming multiple layers. Hence, names were given [to these heavens] in order to await the starting point of observation. This does not mean that they are like physical objects stacked layer upon layer and separated from one another.

**[The Heaven of the Fixed Stars]**

**Question:** Even granting that their motions differ, the movements [of the Seven Luminaries and the fixed stars] appear to be thoroughly intermingled and interwoven. How can one know that the Seven Luminaries are below and the fixed stars are above?

**Answer:** The Six Luminaries (*liù yào* 六曜) are at times capable of occulting the fixed stars. 〔The reason for saying "Six Luminaries" is that the Sun has not yet been counted.〕 That which occults is below; that which is occulted is above. Furthermore, the Seven Luminaries all travel swiftly along the ecliptic, whereas the fixed stars, being close to the heaven of the *Primum Mobile* (*zōngdòng zhī tiān* 宗動之天), are the slowest of all. Among the Seven Luminaries, the Moon is the closest to the Earth, and therefore it is capable of occulting the Sun and the Five Planets.

Try setting up a single gnomon (*biǎo* 表) and observe when the Sun and Moon have both risen to thirty degrees [of altitude]. The shadow cast by the Moon will necessarily be thick and long, while the shadow cast by the Sun will necessarily be thin and short. Is this not precisely because the Sun is far and the Moon is near?
