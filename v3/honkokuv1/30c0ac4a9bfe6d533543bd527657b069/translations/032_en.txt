Taking the full calendar span as a whole: reckoning back from Chongzhen guiwei to Emperor Yao's jiachen year, over three thousand nine hundred and twenty years, a total difference of fifty-eight degrees accumulated. This amounts to one degree of difference per sixty-eight years, seven months, thirteen days, and three hours. Over twenty-five thousand one hundred and some years, a total difference of three hundred and sixty-five degrees and a fraction would accumulate, completing one full circuit of the heavens, and [the equinox] would return to its original position.

[Interlinear annotation: You Xiong [You Yi] says: "This is calculated with the lunar mansion Xu (虚) as the starting point of measurement; after more than twenty-five thousand years, [the equinox] returns to the original position of Xu. If one takes Dou (斗) as the starting point of measurement, then after more than twenty-five thousand years, it likewise returns to the original position of Dou."]

I [the author] further calculated: reckoning back from Chongzhen guiwei to Emperor Ku's jiazi year — four thousand and twenty years — a difference of only fifty-seven degrees accumulated. This amounts to one fen less nine seconds per year, one degree per seventy years and six months, and one full circuit of the heavens per twenty-five thousand seven hundred and thirty-five years. This is the result obtained by calculating the total span as a whole.

[Interlinear annotation: You Xiong says: "The various eminent scholars of ancient and modern times have not measured the fen and degrees with sufficient precision, causing the degree differences to vary in magnitude. However, this unified calculation also produces inconsistent results."]

The estimates put forward by scholars of successive dynasties each have their own basis. However, as for the methods of calculating the precession: scholars such as Guo Shoujing, Xu Heng, and Wang Xun measured gnomon shadows and verified seasonal qi, defining the tropical year as 365 days, 24 fen, and 25 seconds, and the full circuit of the heavens as 365 degrees, 25 fen, and 75 seconds. Comparing the stronger and weaker values, a difference of 1 fen and 50 seconds results, and accumulating over sixty-six and some years, [the equinox] retreats one degree — this they fixed as the precession of the equinoxes.

Zhen Zi Li [a certain scholar surnamed Li, style Zhenzhi] held that the fixed stars (經星) also occasionally shift their positions, but this movement is so subtle that within a hundred years the accumulated difference is not yet great; over forty-nine thousand years, one full circuit of difference would occur.

Lianru Xiong Gong [Xiong Mingyu] held that over more than twenty-five thousand years one full circuit of difference would occur, and that the heaven moving eastward takes the ecliptic as its axis — there is not only an east-west difference, but further, a north-south difference also arises from this.
