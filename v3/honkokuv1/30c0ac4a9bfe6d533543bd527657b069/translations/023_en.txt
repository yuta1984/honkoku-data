(Continued from previous page) ...the degrees by which (the Sun) has retreated also exactly exhaust their original number, and at last (the Sun) comes to conjunction with Heaven, completing one year.

For instance, if Heaven had no "excess degrees of advance," then the culminating stars of the four seasons would all be the same, the Sun would not be able to come to conjunction with Heaven, and the solar terms (節気) would not be able to follow the year in succession.

The Moon in truth moves slowly—in one day and one night it travels three hundred and sixty-five and one-quarter degrees of the full celestial circuit, but it cannot complete this. Compared to Heaven, it falls back by more than thirteen degrees. The advancing count—moving from east to west—is taken as following Heaven in leftward revolution (left-turning, i.e., the diurnal motion). The retreating count—moving from west to east—is taken as going against Heaven in rightward revolution (right-turning, i.e., the Moon's own orbital motion). For calendar makers, the advancing count is difficult to calculate; they simply calculate using the retreating count. For this reason, rightward motion is said to be the "fast" motion. Moreover, it is also said that "the Sun moves slowly and the Moon moves fast." If that is so, then the motion of the Sun conversely captures the true reality of its movement.

　　Heaven's Motion Exceeds One Degree (天行過一度)

　Question: The calendar takes the view that Heaven makes one full revolution per day, and that the Sun travels one degree per day. Now it is said that the Sun's own count is exactly right—yet it is claimed that Heaven's motion exceeds one degree. Is this not contradictory?

Answer: If one were to suppose that Heaven makes exactly one full revolution per day—precisely right—and does not advance beyond one degree, then how could it be that the culminating stars of the four seasons fail to coincide (each year at the same time)? In that case, every day would be the same, the Sun would no longer follow the degrees of the (fixed) stars, the solar terms would no longer have fixed defining positions, and there would be nothing by which to mark the year.

　　The Slow, Fast, Hidden, and Retrograde Motions of the Five Planets (五星遅疾伏退)

　Question: The motion of the Sun marks the years and follows the solar terms; the motion of the Moon brings about the last day of the month, new moon, quarter moons, and full moon (晦・朔・弦・望).
