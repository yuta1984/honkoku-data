**Mercury**
Mercury also travels before and behind the Sun, completing one circuit of the heavens per year. Its morning invisibility (晨伏) and conjunction invisibility (合伏) are each seventeen days and seventy-five ke, during which it travels thirty-four degrees and twenty-five fen each. It emerges in the west at evening; combining its fast and slow phases, it travels for twenty-seven days, covering thirty-one degrees and fifty fen. It stations for two days. Its evening retrograde invisibility (夕退伏) and conjunction retrograde invisibility (合退伏) are each eleven days, eighteen ke, and eighty fen, during which it retrogrades seven degrees, eighty-one fen, and twenty miao each. It stations at dawn for two days. Combining slow and fast phases, it travels twenty-seven days, covering thirty-one degrees, and then again becomes invisible at morning (晨伏), thus completing one cycle, it is said. Approximately every forty-seven years [the pattern] repeats, but the exact days and times are likewise not identical.

**On the Grand Conjunction of the Seven Luminaries**
As for the complex interweaving of the fast and slow motions of the Seven Luminaries (Sun, Moon, and Five Planets): when 4,617 years have elapsed to reach the eleventh month (子月), the dawn of the甲子 new moon day (甲子朔旦), with the winter solstice falling at midnight — the Sun and Moon align like two jade bi-discs joined together, and the Five Planets line up like a string of pearls. This is none other than the very first day of the calendrical epoch (暦元) of the Seven Luminaries. Calculating backward to the seventh year of the Yuanfeng era (元封七年, 104 BCE), year Dingchou (丁丑), in the eleventh month of the Han dynasty, one arrives precisely at that time.

Calculating forward from the third year of the Kangxi era (康熙三年, 1664 CE), year Jiachen (甲辰): 2,849 years hence, the eleventh month's甲子 dawn of the new moon will again be that moment.

**The Four Remainders: Luoji, Qibo**

[Question:] Luoji (羅計) and Qibo (気孛) were originally called the "remainders" of Fire, Earth, Wood, and Water [i.e., of Mars, Saturn, Jupiter, and Mercury]. Astronomers and calculators say: "Luoji are seen in the shadow of eclipses [i.e., at orbital nodes], while Qibo are not ordinarily visible, and when they do appear, it portends something extraordinary." Are these stars that are not normally visible? Or are they matters of eclipse calculation?

[Answer:] The Four Remainders were originally all designated as stars, and it was said that the lunar lodge degrees (宿度) through which they pass each carry auspicious or inauspicious significance. What is not understood is that these are constructs set up as the intersection points (交点) of the various orbital paths, because the motion of the Moon is difficult to calculate. On reflection, these are names only, established for the convenience of computing their degrees of motion — they have no physical existence whatsoever.

Rāhu (羅睺) [interlinear note: i.e., the path along which the Moon travels] is the ascending node (交点) of the White Road (白道, the lunar orbit). Ketu (計都) is the midpoint of the White Road (i.e., the descending node).
