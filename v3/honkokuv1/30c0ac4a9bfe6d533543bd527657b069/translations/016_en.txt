Thus it is called an "eclipse" (食). If a solar eclipse were not caused by the Moon's covering (of the Sun), then eclipses would have no fixed pattern — why would they always occur at the new moon (朔) and not before or after? Moreover, at the time of an eclipse, the degrees and minutes (of position) in the heavens are at just one location, but since people live on a spherical Earth, the magnitude of the eclipse (食分) varies according to east, west, north, and south, so that no two people see it the same way. Unless calendar-makers clarify the degrees of longitude and latitude for each region [Interlinear note: Youxi (游熙) states: Just as the heavens have degrees of longitude and latitude, so too does the Earth. When the two are combined, the discrepancy in measurement is not great.], there is no way to know the distances between different localities, and the time and magnitude of an eclipse, calculated for one region, cannot be applied to other regions.

For example, when going from west to east, if two places are separated by 7,500 *li*, the people to the east will see the eclipse eight *ke* (刻, approximately two hours) earlier (than those to the west). If the Earth were flat in shape, then people everywhere under heaven would see the eclipse at exactly the same time, with no earlier or later. If the degrees of north-south latitude exceed a separation of thirty-four minutes, then the Moon passes along the edge (of the eclipse path), and people will not see its covering — and thus there will be no eclipse.

　　Lunar Eclipse

　Question: Regarding lunar eclipses, the Song Confucians (宋儒) said: "The solar fire illuminates the outside, while the center (of the Sun) is in fact dark. When the time of full moon (望) arrives, the Moon falls into that dark region at the center, which is called the 'Dark Void' (闇虚, *àn xū*). It is the image of the Dark Void that constitutes the lunar eclipse." Is this correct or not?

Answer: Heaven is spherical in form, and the Earth sits at the center, just as the yolk of a chicken's egg is situated within the white. The periphery of the white (surrounding sphere) is precisely the heaven of the Twenty-Eight Lunar Mansions (列宿) and the Seven Luminaries (七曜). The Sun advances one degree per day, and the Moon advances more than thirteen degrees per day. Counting by the 360 degrees of the full celestial circuit, from new moon (朔) to full moon (望), the Moon traverses only half of the circuit. When the Moon is 180 degrees from the Sun, the Sun and Moon face each other directly (in opposition). The Earth's sphere lies as an obstruction between them, and the Moon's disk (月輪) is situated within the shadow of the Earth (上 here meaning "in" or "within"), while the Moon's disk is below the terrestrial sphere — the sunlight cannot illuminate it. Therefore, it loses its light and is called an "eclipse." As it gradually emerges from outside the Earth's shadow, and the Sun is able to illuminate it again, it gradually recovers its original light. From this, one understands that the Moon losing its light constitutes the eclipse. The eclipse is entirely caused by the obscuration of the Earth's shadow.
