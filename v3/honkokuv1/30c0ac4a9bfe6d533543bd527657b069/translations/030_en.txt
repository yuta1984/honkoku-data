Or does the difference necessarily keep on producing further difference, making it impossible to calculate and measure? Please explain this in detail."

[Answer:] "The precession of the equinoxes is brought about by the movement of heaven and the course of the Sun (日躔). In the course of one year, as the Sun's motion traverses the degrees of the full circuit of heaven, before it has reached the position of the fixed stars' remaining fraction, the Sun has already arrived at [the solstice], while the fixed stars have shifted slightly to the east. It appears as if the Sun has retreated, but in reality it is the fixed stars that have advanced. Therefore every year there is a constant movement eastward by fen and seconds [of arc], exceeding the original boundary — this is called the 'difference' (差).

[Interlinear annotation: You Yan says: "When ancient and modern [astronomers] speak of 'not reaching the remaining fraction,' this means the Sun has [already] reached (踵) its boundary [i.e., the solstice point], while the fixed stars have passed beyond it. Thus 'passing beyond' is expressed as 'not reaching.' It does not mean that the Sun is unable to reach the degree of the fixed stars."]
