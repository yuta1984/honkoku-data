At the area around Yui and Okitsu, the morning after that great storm, one looked out upon the state of the coastline and saw boats of all sizes broken and damaged, blown together and drifting upon the waves — a sight that struck the eye with astonishment. And yet, as one might expect of a seaside vista stretching as far as the eye could reach, the distant coves and inlets showed no sign that any storm had passed over them at all. The scenery was as magnificent as ever: the forests gleamed a deep, lush green, heavy with moisture, rising up as if wreathed in mist, and the villages in the distance appeared utterly tranquil and bountiful. What is recorded here is simply a rendering in illustration of the account as it was told.
