(Continued from the previous page) The large trees, known to people as ancient trees predating even those [planted at the time of the Shogun's entry], trees of immeasurable girth, either snapped or were uprooted and fell. The birds that had sought their roosts in the tops of these trees were swept away by the wind, struck by branches, and died in great numbers.

Furthermore, a village called Nakadai, which had about forty households, saw twenty-four or twenty-five of them collapse. Along the Nakasendō highway, in the post-town of Warabi, many houses also collapsed, and several horses were killed when crushed. The Toda Plain was flooded with water that overflowed, and it was said to have become like a sea.

◯ At the house of a pawnbroker (*shichimono-tosei*) called Ise-ya, residing below the Hakusan Palace in Koishikawa, on the night of the twenty-fifth day of the eighth month [of this year], in the midst of a great storm of wind and rain, something came flying in with a violent flapping of wings. The household members, amid the commotion of the storm that night, thought it must be a pigeon or crow driven in by the wind, and as they were preoccupied with concern for their own safety, they did not get a good look at it, and left it as it was while they sheltered from the storm. When the rain stopped and the wind grew calm, and before long dawn broke—at this point, a single strange bird was found crouching in a corner of the floor. So this must be the thing that flew in with such a vigorous beating of wings in the midst of last night's wind and rain—

**[Left page]**

—and the master of the house along with his male servants tried to catch it, but it made no attempt to flee. They then placed a wicker basket (*shimeshikago*) over it, brought it out into the light, and examined it. Its feathers were thoroughly soaked from the rain, and perhaps because it had been buffeted by the wind, it was in a very sorry state; yet its form was no ordinary bird. It resembled a duck but was slightly larger, its entire body was mouse-grey, and its wings were covered all over with speckled markings. Someone suggested, "Could this not be the bird called *utofu*, said to inhabit Mutsu [in northern Japan]?" The author [of this account] also saw it with his own eyes, but it was greatly unlike any painted depiction of the *utofu*. That bird—the *Yasukata-dori* [i.e., *utofu*]—has been cited in various books, and some even include accurate illustrations, so it is well known. A falconer (*takaya*) by the name of Eisuke also said he had never seen a specimen of the same creature as the one that had flown into the Ise-ya. On reflection, this must surely be a seabird from a distant island (*gaitō*). It appears to have been driven from its roost by that night's storm and to have wandered, just barely, to this place. Therefore, one may surmise that the storm that night was not confined to our country alone—the wind and rain must have been fierce throughout the lands to the south and east as well. This bird has since been summoned by a retired lord (*rōkun*) of one of the daimyo houses and is kept in the garden, [reportedly now] within the grounds of a residence in the Komagome area.
