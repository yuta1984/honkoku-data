◯ From there, the areas of Tamachi and Honshiba suffered much damage, while from Fuda-no-Tsuji toward Mita there was relatively little damage. At the residence of the Lord of Satsuma (the Satsuma domain lord), located along the Nanamamagari (Seven Bends) road, the main entrance and the great audience chamber reportedly collapsed. Along the shore road at Shiba Kanasugi, houses were swept by waves and toppled by wind, with virtually every dwelling suffering severe damage. The state of the wrecked ships in this area is too great to be captured in brief writing, so it has been entrusted to an artist to depict — please look upon that illustration to grasp the scene.

◯ A fire broke out amid the storm and wind from Katamon-mae Icchōme, burning down everything in the vicinity without exception. Along the main street, the fire spread as far as Udagawa-chō; on Hikage-chō, both sides of the street as far as Shinmei-chō were burned, and the fire stopped at the corner of Mishima-chō. Now, the owner of the house where the fire originated had braced himself for punishment and was conducting himself with great restraint, yet no order of reprimand came. The town officials therefore discreetly inquired into the matter and were told that, since the violent wind of this occasion was an extraordinary and unusual event, the mistake of the accidental fire could not readily be blamed upon anyone, and the matter would be let go without penalty. It was truly a merciful and compassionate ruling, and the man shed tears of gratitude and relief.

◯ In the area of the new row (shinami) in front of Saióji temple, many houses were damaged; among them, rear tenement houses and temporary constructions were entirely crushed. In general, not only in this area but throughout the city, many houses had not been properly repaired since the earthquake, and so all of those collapsed. In an earlier year (the Ansei Earthquake), the storehouses were shaken and wealthy households all suffered great hardship; but this time, the number of poor people who suffered hardship is beyond counting. Truly, that both the wealthy and the poor are brought to hardship alike may be said to be the workings of Heaven.

◯ Within the precincts of Zōjō-ji temple, the lodging quarters (shukubō) in many places suffered damage, and an extraordinary number of large trees in the outer grounds were toppled. In the area of Kiridōshi Nishikubo, townhouses and samurai residences were somewhat damaged. Along the road below Atago, the front fences of residences were damaged, and fire watchtowers fell in several locations.

◯ Between Shinbashi and Shibaguchi, as far as Owari-chō, there were collapsed houses scattered throughout. At the self-governing fire-watch tower (jishinban) in Ginza 3-chōme, the watchtower fell into the street with its temple bell still hanging from it.
