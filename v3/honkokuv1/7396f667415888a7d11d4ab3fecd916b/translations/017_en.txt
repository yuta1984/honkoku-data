(Continued from previous page) …there were buildings pitifully crushed. Boats that had been blown up onto the roads lay like fallen leaves swept to the water's edge. Along the coast from Honmoku to the Haneda area, boats that had been moored disappeared, while unknown vessels came drifting in. Many of the fishermen's small huts were consequently knocked down. Among these, it is reported that a walking-boat (*ayumi-bune*) from the shogunal embarkation dock in Edo drifted ashore at Haneda—this must have been carried off when the storm surge receded.

◯From Kawasaki to the Rokugō ferry crossing, the flooding was likewise severe, and all suffered great hardship. In the areas of Higashi-Kawabata, Yawatazuka, Zōshiki-machi, Kominami, Kamata, Ōmori village, Suzugamori, and Hamagawa, the residents, recognizing the storm surge as something akin to a tsunami, struggled through the water to flee toward higher ground. Those who fled too slowly had no time to escape and climbed onto their rooftops. The commotion was extraordinary. After dawn, many were left bewildered by the damage to their roofs and fences.

◯In Shinagawa-juku, because Mount Gotenyama lay directly above, people were preparing to flee there should the water rise any further—but gradually the wind and rain calmed, and once the tide receded, they felt relieved. Nevertheless, all manner of belongings had been swept away by the water,

and some managed only after much searching to recover them, while others lost important guest registers and were left in great distress. Furthermore, the official vessels and merchant ships of all sizes that had been moored in rows fore and aft of the Odaiba were blown together in a mass, and it took many days to move them back out into deep water. Among them, a newly built large ship of foreign design showed no visible damage, yet having been blown onto the shallows, it caused no small number of other boats to be smashed against it.

◯A person of my acquaintance employs many craftsmen, and so had home-pickled daikon radish stored—more than ten barrels' worth as provisions through to year's end—only to have all of it swept away by the storm surge. Gathering up what could be found around the house, they managed to collect about two barrels' worth, and laughed while telling the tale.

◯Takanawa Ushi-machi sits on low-lying ground and is close to the sea; when the storm surge rose to floor level, the owners were alarmed and called out to release the many cattle they kept from their stalls and let them flee. The cattle-hands too crowded around trying to rouse them, but the cattle—as the saying goes, "like pulling from the darkness"—would not move at all, and they were left utterly at a loss. And already the previous year at the time of the earthquake, the horses were let loose…
