In Hei District, across three towns and nineteen villages, two thousand seven hundred and eighty-three homes were lost, and more than thirteen thousand people perished. In Kunohe District, across two town districts and five additional villages, the number of dead amounted to roughly three hundred and sixty or more. In the neighboring Aomori Prefecture, over one thousand homes were swept away, and the count of the dead has yet to be determined. Moreover, the bodies washed up onto the sandy shores were beyond counting; piled here and there, the smoke of the impermanent funeral pyres rose and faded away, and even the sound of the waves breaking over the white wake left behind seemed dreadful and mournful to behold. (Yare!)

《The Great Tsunami Lament — Part Two》

〽 Now, here is one remarkable and wondrous tale. In the outer reaches of Kunohe District, there is a small village called Miyato-mura. There lived a fisherman by the name of Jinnosuke, who, with a household of four, led a humble and modest life. Yet he was ever an honest man, and together with his wife, his devoted filial piety toward his aged mother had become known to the authorities, earning him a commendation from the prefectural office. He continued to conduct himself with integrity and a strong sense of duty. That year, his son was nine years of age. On that fateful day, Jinnosuke had gone out fishing and was casting his line at sea.
