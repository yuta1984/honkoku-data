Just at that moment, the thunderous roar of the offshore tremor arose with terrifying force, and the black clouds were like no ordinary sight. As Jinnosuke hastened his boat homeward, a child's body came drifting toward him in the current. He pulled it aboard, and upon looking closely, there was no mistaking it — it was his own child. In his panic and astonishment, he tended to the boy, and whether by the grace of the gods or not, the child suddenly revived and drew breath. Though barely conscious and lost in a kind of darkness, he made his way home — only to find that his house had vanished without a trace, and the whereabouts of his mother and wife were unknown. Utterly at a loss, as if wandering through a dream, he pressed on through the night. When at last dawn broke, a wondrous thing was revealed: his wife had taken his mother by the hand and returned safely. Hearing the full account of what had happened, it emerged that the great waves, more than ten jō in height, had hurled the two of them up onto the mountain, and thus they had narrowly saved their dew-like, fragile lives. The joy of the family of four reunited was beyond words — truly, said everyone, this is the reward of honesty and filial piety — and all who heard were deeply moved.

Now, given the vast scale of this disaster, it is with great reverence that we report: from the Imperial Government, relief funds were bestowed — over ten thousand yen to Iwate Prefecture, three thousand yen to Miyagi Prefecture, and over one thousand yen to the neighboring Aomori Prefecture.
