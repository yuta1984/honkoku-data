This method is commonly known as "senbonzuki" (thousand-stroke pounding) and is a simple technique that even amateurs can perform with ease.

○ Along coastlines or the banks of large rivers, violent storms, waterspouts, and great tsunamis may demolish houses, blow roofs off from above the roof beams, or tear apart the foundations from the pillars, resulting in complete destruction. In the event of a great tsunami, it goes without saying that the foundation, pillars, and roof may all be separated from one another. Furthermore, while people would not drown if their houses remained intact, since the houses are destroyed, those attempting to take refuge on second floors or rooftops find even that recourse exhausted, and ultimately meet their end by drowning — a most lamentable state of affairs. Now, if a residence is constructed according to the earthquake-prevention method, it should absolutely never collapse. However,

the vertical pillars are simply placed atop the foundation with short, small tenon joints (hozo) inserted, and the roof is merely rested upon the eave beams at the periphery of the structure. Therefore, when battered by a great tsunami or violent storm, the structure will inevitably be torn apart. Accordingly, in villages and hamlets where there is danger of great tsunamis, violent storms, or waterspouts, large staple clamps (ōkasugai) should be used to firmly fasten the foundation to the pillars — two clamps on the inside and two on the outside, for a total of four per pillar — securing them tightly together.
