The figure on the right depicts a cross-section of the interior of a two-ken storehouse (dozō) entrance, showing diagonal braces (sujiちがい) made of boards one shaku wide inserted and notched into each pillar. Since this construction uses the competitive-bid method (nyūsatsu-fushin) with extremely inexpensive rough and poor-quality timber for the pillars, and no planing is applied, the carpenter's labor is not particularly extensive. Nevertheless, when diagonal braces in a cross pattern are driven in on all four interior sides as shown in the figure, the framework becomes as solid and immovable as a great boulder (daibanjaku), such that it will not sway even in the most severe earthquake; therefore, the walls sustain not even the slightest damage. For this reason, there is no fear of the fire spreading to the storehouse even in fierce winds or great conflagrations. A close friend of mine constructed his storehouse in this very manner, and in the great earthquake of the past
