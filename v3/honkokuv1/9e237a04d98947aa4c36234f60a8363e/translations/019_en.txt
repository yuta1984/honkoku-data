Prepare the back-cut boards (seita). Then carefully plane and fit their inner faces and joints together, and using ship nails of appropriate size, fasten them at three points: the ground ridge beam (jimune), the perimeter beams (jimawari no keta), and the central beam (hari), driving three nails at each point. The end-grain edges of the boards at the front and rear of the ridge should be joined by driving in two large clamps (kasugai) at each point. The carpentry costs are low, the construction is extremely sturdy, there is no risk of burglars entering through the roof, and even rats cannot get in. No matter how violent the earthquake or fire, the structure will not burn and will not break apart — a construction method unrivaled in all of history.

and after coating the entire perimeter firmly with oil-lime plaster (stucco), lay the roof tiles in the usual manner.
○ The reason for tiling a roof is fundamentally to prevent fire. Yet some attach large decorative ogre boards (oni-ita) to the left and right of the ridge, or build the ridge up high, or construct a box ridge (hako-mune) — all of which are useless vanity, merely decorating the outward appearance, and are in fact greatly harmful in the event of earthquake or fire. This wholly defeats the original purpose of tiled roofing. For this reason, when the ridge is kept low and finished with a gamburi-dome style cap, the appearance may not be handsome, but the ridge bears no excessive weight, and the structure remains resilient against earth(quakes)...
