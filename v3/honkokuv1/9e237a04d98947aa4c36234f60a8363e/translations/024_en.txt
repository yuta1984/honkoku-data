○ During great storms and waterspouts, the shutters (amado) are invariably blown off or torn apart, allowing wind to be trapped in the space beneath the roof, and houses great and small alike are blown up into the air or smashed down, resulting in many collapsed buildings and injured persons.
In general, in any house, the depth of the grooves in the threshold (shikii) and head rail (kamoi) is barely two to three bu on a carpenter's square, and the door frame edge (tobuchi) is no more than one sun square. This is the same for both upper and lower floors, and the danger this poses is self-evident. If one wishes to make these more secure, one should fit horizontal bars (mase) at approximately two points, located about one shaku above and below the rain shutters, placed with consideration for the convenience of securing the doors. Ordinarily, only the lower

bar should be used, and when going in and out during the night, one should step over it to pass. When both the upper and lower bars are used even on ordinary days, burglars will be unable to enter, and the structure will be extremely sturdy against great winds and waterspouts, and there should be no fear of the house being twisted and crushed. These preventive measures should be carefully and thoroughly contemplated in ordinary times.
