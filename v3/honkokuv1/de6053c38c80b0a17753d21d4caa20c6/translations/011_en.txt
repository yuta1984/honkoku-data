**The Dream Tale of Zenkōji**

In the spring of the year *Hinoto-Hitsuji* [the cyclical year corresponding to Kōka 4, 1847], a special viewing of the sacred image (*kaichō*) of the Tathāgata at Zenkōji Temple in Shinshū Province was being held. People of all ages and both sexes, from the Kantō and Kansai regions alike, came in great numbers, day and night, to make their pilgrimages, seeking to forge bonds of good karma for both this life and the next. Not only was the practice of overnight vigil within the main hall in full swing, but the temple lodgings and travellers' inns are said to have accommodated as many as five hundred to seven hundred guests at a time.

It was around the end of the third month that a certain person from our land [the narrator] journeyed to that place, paid reverence to the Tathāgata, and, overwhelmed by the profound sense of grace and gratitude,

held an overnight vigil within the hall. It was past the Hour of the Dog [around 8 o'clock in the evening] when, just as the narrator was about to inadvertently drift off to sleep, a single venerable monk pressed upon the narrator's shoulder and spoke these words: "You must not neglect the recitation of the sacred name. Before long, a great calamity shall befall this land. How grievous — how truly grievous this is."
