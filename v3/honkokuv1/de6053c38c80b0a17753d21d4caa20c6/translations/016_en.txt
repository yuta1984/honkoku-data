There was nothing to eat, no inn where one might rest — they lay down in the fields, spent the night on the roadside, and stumbled about on foot, a sight no different from the realm of the hungry ghosts. Yet in those very moments of hardship, when at last they managed to obtain even the coarsest of food, its taste must have seemed like the sweetest nectar. Truly, life depends on food — never is its preciousness felt more keenly than at such a time. And gazing far into the distance, one could see that in the villages nestled at the foot of the mountains, great boulders and rocks had come cascading down from the heights above, crushing both people and homes to dust. As the earth continued to tremble violently, entire mountains collapsed all at once in the blink of an eye, and the farmers in the villages at the foot of those mountains — in several places — were instantly buried alive beneath the earth. That not a single person in those places survived — to call it pitiful is to fall short of the truth. In the greatest river of Shinano Province, where a collapsed mountain blocked the flow, the waters overflowed in all directions upstream, and several villages were suddenly inundated with floodwater. The waters rose steadily, and the houses disappeared beneath the surface.
