"You — flee at once, quickly, quickly!" he pressed them on. But choking in the smoke, amid the agony of scorching heat like the Hell of Avīci, there was nothing to be done — they abandoned the father, and escaped with the mother, yet the weight of having left their parent to burn alive was beyond words. And there were others — a household of five or seven, master and servants, where a single manservant alone managed to escape, yet with no means to rescue his master, he left him behind in the flames to perish, and returned home to his province hollow-eyed and crestfallen, unable to face anyone. Among such stories too —

A young woman, still in her sleeping robes, was fortunate enough to have escaped and gotten out, but she did not know what had become of her parents or her husband, nor of any of their savings, and as news of the disaster reached her home province, one can only imagine the anxiety she endured until a person came to meet her. Beyond this, there were travellers who lost their way and wandered into the flames by mistake, others who fell into wells, those who sprained their feet in the fissures that had split the earth open, or broke their arms, or injured their heads, and some who, in the confusion, saved another's child while losing their own — the varieties of such suffering are without end in the telling. Such was the time of this extraordinary calamity —
