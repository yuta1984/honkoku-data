From last night until around the hour of the Ninth and Eighth bells today [approximately 12:00 noon to 2:00 PM], houses were damaged, and items such as square pillars of five to six sun, beams of four to five shaku in circumference, threshold boards, lintels, door planks, horizontal struts, ridge beams, and roof boards were blown away. Periodically, blue bamboo blinds, as well as lacquered food boxes, mortars, pestles, tubs, bowls, and similar items; pine and cedar trees broken off at heights of five to six shaku up to one jō, their branches stripped bare, reduced from root to crown to a frayed, splinter-like condition—all of these were flowing in such enormous quantities that they filled the river for sixty to seventy ken, making boat passage extremely difficult. Into this, jet-black muddy water came flowing, running at a depth of three to four shaku. The number of men, women, and Buddhist clergy who have drowned has reached sixty persons so far, with seven or eight horses also flowing along the riverbanks. As for what may be flowing in the middle of the river, there is no way of knowing.

Boarding a boat to investigate, we found a broken saddle with a tag reading "Kōshū Gunma District, Kawashima Village" caught on the bank at the Gongendō landing. Upon inquiring with boatmen from a landing called Fujinoki in Kōshū [Kōzuke Province / present-day Gunma], we were firmly informed that Kawashima is a place accessible to those going to a spa, located approximately twenty chō beyond Ikaho. Whether due to the muddy water or not, carp, catfish, sturgeon and the like floated to the surface and drifted to the riverbanks in great numbers, dying there. As there had already been a notice [from the authorities] regarding this matter, and as this is an extraordinary event, we hereby report through the post-station relay system. Respectfully submitted.
