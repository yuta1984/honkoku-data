Regarding the above two villages, located along the Agatsuma River in the same province (Kōzuke): on the 8th of this month, at the Hour of the Rabbit (around 10 a.m.), a mountain flood (lahar) of mud, rocks, and burning stones burst forth in overwhelming quantities. From Kawashima Village to the Mokuhashi Checkpoint, and throughout Kitamaki Village, all houses, fields, and paddies were swept away without exception. It is noted that a small number of dwellings survived in the mountainside areas. The number of people swept away is not yet known. We can only surmise that there are likely no survivors; however, it is unclear whether those who had gone out to tend their fields or to the fodder grounds may have escaped. Even should any survivors remain, it has been reported that they would likely be on the verge of death from thirst and starvation at this time.

Regarding the four post-stations on the Nakasendō — Karuizawa, Kutsukake, Oiwake, and Itahana — due to the great eruption of Mount Asama, there were tremors and thunder, and from the night of the 7th of this month, large rocks and ash fell and accumulated to a depth of approximately one shaku and one sun. Furthermore, at Karuizawa post-station, from that same night, burning rocks and ash rained down, setting buildings ablaze, and the entire post-station was consumed by fire without exception. Regarding injured persons and those who perished, Endō Kichiemon has reported that further investigation will be carried out and the findings duly communicated. The foregoing is hereby submitted.
