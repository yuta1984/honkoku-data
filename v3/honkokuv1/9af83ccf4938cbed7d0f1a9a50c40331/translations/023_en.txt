　　　　　　　　(From a Shinto priest)
　　　　　　　　Makino Ōsumi [written as "Firewood-field, Great Charcoal"]

Taxes (unjo) pour one after another into Tanuma's storehouse. For those above (the authorities and powerful), this is most convenient; for those below (the common people), it is a great hardship. Under the guise of honorable purposes, money is collected and collected, rice prices rise, and money is spent away. It is said that through the eight leaders of the mountain ascetics (yamabushi), this was brought to the ears of those above. Unlawful statutes, one's fortunes and home used as collateral.

The foregoing refers to the time of Lord Tanuma Tonomo-no-kami's tenure in office, when various petitions and tax matters were approved. It is said that Lord Makino Ōsumi-no-kami, who was the town magistrate (machi-bugyō) at the time, composed [or arranged] these words in the manner described above.
