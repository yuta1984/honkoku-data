…there are no other particular issues to report. In truth, this is an earthquake rarely seen in all of history. Fields and paddies alike cracked open, and in some places enormous quantities of water gushed forth from the ground. As for the construction site at Shiozaki Village, the embankments and stoneworks have largely collapsed. The Chikuma River has risen two to three *jō* (approximately 6–9 meters) above its normal level, but provided no further unusual developments occur, we do not expect there to be any particularly severe flooding beyond this. The situation is not limited to Shiozaki Village; it appears that throughout the surrounding area there are many collapsed houses and injured persons. At the time of the great earthquake, fires broke out in all directions near and far, and in particular, the Inariyama post-town and the area of Zenkōji Temple-town could still be seen smoldering as of this morning. From last night until just now, the earth has rumbled and shaken dozens of times throughout the day and night, with some tremors so violent that even walls have crumbled. Furthermore, regarding the Sai River in Shinano Province, it appears that a mountain has collapsed from upstream, and since the great earthquake, not even a drop of water has flowed through it. For this reason, it is feared that within a day or two a sudden great flood will occur somewhere, and accordingly the people living along the river, as well as those in low-lying areas, are evacuating one after another. With regard to these extraordinary events, we hereby

The following is a written record of the accounts heard directly from these two men, a copy of which is provided below.

[This is] the direct verbal account given by Construction Magistrates Takasaki Heihachi and Nishimura Kakunai — who had been stationed at the riverbank construction site (*kawayoke fushinjo*) three *ri* from Zenkōji Temple in Shinano Province, within the domain of Matsudaira Hida-no-Kami — given in addition to (*or: following*) their official written report. 

Heihachi (recounts): Having entered the night and finished writing up his daily log, he lit a hand-candle and went to the privy (*shōyōjo*). While standing at the edge of the veranda (*en*), without any warning he found himself tumbling and falling off the veranda. Once momentarily thrown into a panic, after a brief moment he became aware that it was an earthquake, and attempted to stand up, but [the text is unclear at this point — several characters illegible]. As he happened to be in a patch of *ryūnohige* (#1) grass, he grabbed hold of its roots and crawled forward to safety, whereupon the house immediately collapsed behind him. His colleague (Nishimura Kakunai) had been sitting inside behind the *shōji* screens; the *shōji* were wrenched from their frames, the rain shutters (*amado*) snapped and fell under the veranda, and he found himself pinned beneath those rain shutters, having to push aside fallen roof tiles to free himself.
