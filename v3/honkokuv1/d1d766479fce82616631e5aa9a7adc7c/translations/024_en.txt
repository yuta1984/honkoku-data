There are impediments to the supply of drinking water as well, and since this is a critical season for irrigation of the rice paddies, neither matter can be left unattended. Unless repairs are carried out, there will be widespread difficulties not only during the seedbed (sanawashiro) period but also in planting the undamaged fields. The scale of destruction is vast, and it is utterly beyond the capacity of the villages to address through their own means. This situation is entirely different in nature from fire damage: houses, fields, and mountain forests have all sustained enormous harm from the earthquake. In particular, the two districts of Mizuchi and Takai suffered the most severe damage from the great earthquake, and if left as they are, there are many villages that face total ruin. This puts human lives at stake and will result in the loss of future tax revenues and national benefit. This is not a matter to be taken lightly, and without Your Excellency's relief, there is simply no way for us to manage.

Furthermore, as a result of the great earthquake, at a point approximately two and a half ri up the river from the Tabashima Village ferry crossing on the Hokuriku Highway — within the domain of Sanada Shinano-no-Kami, at a place called Kokūzōyama in Hirabayashi Village, some twenty chō in extent — a mountainside gave way and collapsed, pushing out into the Sai River and blocking the watercourse. This dammed up the flow and caused the water to pool. At present, water is being released toward the upstream villages, but should the pooling reach full capacity, the natural force of the impounded water will press against and break through the blockage,

**[Left folio]**

and it is deeply worrying to consider what kind of flooding might result at that point. We have been approached by retainers of Sanada Shinano-no-Kami regarding the need to issue warnings to the villages along the Chikuma River within our jurisdiction, so that they may be prepared. With respect to that location, the Chikuma River is currently seven to eight shaku below its normal water level; should the blockage give way, and should the water that has been pooling for several days be released all at once, yet another flood disaster would be likely to occur — and the anxiety among the people is extraordinarily high, causing us great concern.

As described in the foregoing account, I humbly request that Your Excellency give thorough and wise consideration to the circumstances of the extreme disaster and hardship that have befallen us, and that an emergency loan (haishan) of 2,500 ryō in gold be granted to the villages listed in the accompanying documents, to be applied toward plans for recovery (sōzoku) as well as to the extensive damage to the irrigation channels that fall under the category of self-funded repair works (jifushin-sho). Without such assistance, there are simply no means whatsoever to achieve recovery, and should the people be pressed further into hardship and fall into a state of misguided unrest — with the populace becoming agitated — this would be a deeply troubling and fearful prospect.

The villagers within our jurisdiction have, since last year, in a manner not seen in other jurisdictions within the same province, recognized the benevolence of the domain and have faithfully paid their stipulated additional rice levies (sadame-mashumai)…
