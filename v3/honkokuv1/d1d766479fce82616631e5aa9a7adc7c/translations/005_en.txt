Even up until the time we departed from that location, it is said that the dead continued to be dug out one after another from beneath the collapsed buildings. As for travelers passing through, it was difficult to determine their numbers quickly. Our own lodging also collapsed in the earthquake, and though we barely escaped with our lives, our luggage and other belongings were buried under the house, and it was only upon digging them out the following morning when it grew light that we were able to retrieve them. As for Shiozaki Village, fires broke out only to a small extent, with no more than two or three houses burned. At the time of the great earthquake, fires broke out simultaneously in all directions, near and far, with great columns of smoke rising all at once; amid this, the mountains rumbled and the earth shook dozens of times, people's voices rose in an enormous clamor, the roaring sound of rocks crashing down was intense, and in the fields, paddies, and roads alike, the earth split open both lengthwise and crosswise — cracks ranging from two or three shaku to four or five shaku in width — from which water spurted forth, and in some places mud and soil were ejected, making it feel as though the end of the world had come. The places where the earth had cracked were displaced by steps of one or two shaku, and the places from which mud was ejected had a very foul odor — perhaps it was the smell of sulfur. However, the places from which water spouted had no odor at all.

One item: From the 10th of last month onward, owing to the unveiling ceremony (kaicho) at Zenkoji Temple, great numbers of pilgrims from all over the country had been lodging in the area; at the most crowded time, one single inn lodged 1,240 people, of whom 1,200 paid the lodging fee, while the remaining 40 were put up free of charge. It was during this great gathering of people that the earthquake struck, and Zenkoji-machi was almost entirely shaken to rubble; on top of that, a great conflagration broke out, causing deaths too numerous to count. Approximately 3,000 or more pilgrims who had taken refuge inside the Hall of the Nyorai (the main hall of Zenkoji) were able to evacuate safely; however, their clothing and traveling funds were all lost to the fire. Those from distant provinces were said to be in particularly dire straits. We have heard that only the Hall of the Nyorai and the Sanmon gate survived, and that everything else throughout the town was completely destroyed by fire. As for the number of dead, it is utterly impossible to determine with any certainty; by rumor, there may be as many as twenty to thirty thousand people killed outright in Zenkoji-machi alone.
