(The mountains along the Sai River) had collapsed and fallen into the river; downstream, the water was completely blocked, and that water was being pushed out toward the Matsushiro area. At the bridge over some valley — the name of which escapes the writer — even at normal water levels (*hiramizu*) the depth was considerable; now the valley stream had swollen so greatly that the bridge was submerged and invisible beneath the water. The low-lying areas had become entirely like a lake, with no outlet for the water. The water was rising one to two *jō* per day. At this rate, there was no telling how severe the flooding would become in the direction of Matsushiro. The local residents had retreated to higher ground. As the area is surrounded on all sides by high mountains, it is unclear where the water might find an outlet; but if it were to flow through the mountain valleys and empty into Lake Suwa, it would cause catastrophic flooding on the Tenryū River, and when the water emptied into the lake, there was no telling how much of the surrounding province might end up underwater. Truly, this was a great calamity unprecedented in ancient or modern times. As the official station (*goyō-sho*) was in the condition described above, (those present) concluded there was no remaining at that location, and (they) departed two days later to return to Edo. As far as Usui Pass, there were many collapsed houses; beyond that, it was all just talk of the great earthquake — there were no collapsed houses, and the (intensity of the) shaking appeared to have gradually diminished.

The aforementioned Tōjirō, taking Heizō along as his attendant, departed on the thirteenth day of the second month of this year, made a pilgrimage to the Ise Grand Shrine, thereafter sightseeing in Kyoto, Osaka, and various other places, and was on his return journey via the Nakasendō highway. Having heard in advance of the *kaichō* (special public display of sacred images) at Zenkōji Temple in Mizuchi District, Shinano Province, he had set out intending to pay his respects. On the twenty-fourth day of last month, (he lodged) at the inn of Wataya Nizaemon in Zenkōji Temple-town.
