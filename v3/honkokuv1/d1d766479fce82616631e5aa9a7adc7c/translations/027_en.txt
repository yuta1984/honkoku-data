…(Suzaka,) likewise Inariyama, likewise the area of Shinone-Oiwake (#1) also suffered destruction, and there were collapsed buildings in the surrounding areas as well. Above Tanbajima, in the upper reaches of Mizuchi District, a great mountain collapsed, completely blocking the flow of water downstream. For this reason, the households of Hoshi and Komori today were unharmed, but everyone in this area has vacated their homes and moved without exception to reside in the mountains. I had been concerned that today the dammed-up waters of Mizuchi (caused by the landslide) might break through and surge downstream, but contrary to that expectation, the people around Kawanakajima have all moved their dwellings into the mountains, and as of this evening the water has still not receded. Accordingly, from Mizuchi District and further upstream, the area has been inundated for more than five *ri* (approximately 20 kilometers), and all the people in this vicinity have climbed up into the mountains to take shelter. With regard to the foregoing, there are those who say the number of dead reaches 60,000 — in any case, it is a catastrophic event. My own relatives have suffered no injuries whatsoever. I write this brief notice to inform you. I myself departed on the 27th of the Third Month to visit Zenkoji Temple, stayed one night, and fled taking only a few belongings. I humbly request that you please convey this to our relatives. I sincerely hope that all members of your esteemed household will come out to [meet us]…

**Report submitted by two Construction Magistrates (*fushin-yaku*) who had traveled to Shinano Province for the Saigawa (#2) public works project:**

Last night — the 24th — at around the fourth hour of the night (approximately 10 p.m.), a great earthquake struck. In the villages of Shiozaki, Sarashina District, Shinano Province, which fall under the domain of Matsudaira Hida-no-Kami, with a registered yield of over 2,900 *koku*, there were approximately 1,400 collapsed houses, a large number of injured persons, and approximately 60 people killed outright. Our own lodgings were also destroyed by the earthquake, but we ourselves, along with our two servants (*komono*), were able to evacuate safely. As we were able to take the post-horse travel permit (*tenma goshōmon*) with us when we fled, there are no other particular issues to report…
