A written report submitted in rough outline regarding what was observed in connection with the great earthquake, while conducting inspections and preparatory work for the state corvée construction project (kokueki fushin) along the Chikuma River at Shiozaki Village, Shinshū.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Inspector of Construction Works (Go-fushin-yaku)
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Nishimura Kakunai

While I was stationed at Shiozaki Village along the Chikuma River in Sarashina District, Shinshū — which is the domain of Lord Matsudaira Hidanosuke — together with my colleague Takahashi Heihachi, for the purpose of conducting inspections and preparatory work for the state corvée construction project, around the hour of the fourth watch (approximately 10 p.m.) on the night of the 24th of last month, an earthquake of a magnitude the likes of which had never been heard of before struck. In Shiozaki Village, which has an assessed yield (kokudaka) of over 2,900 koku and a total of over 600 households, the number of structures counted as collapsed — including main buildings, earthen storehouses, storage sheds, detached quarters, and all others — amounted to 1,400 buildings. The local domain officials (jitō yakunin) reported upon their rough investigation that approximately six or seven tenths of all structures had collapsed.
