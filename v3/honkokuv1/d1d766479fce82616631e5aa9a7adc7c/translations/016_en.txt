The earth has continued to shake repeatedly. However, it is not as severe as at the beginning; yet we cannot bring ourselves to go back inside, and everyone has been living in the garden up until today. As for the domain's [outlying] areas, many roads are still blocked and we cannot get news through, so there is much we do not know. We have been told that Komatsubara Village and Yashiro post-station and the like have suffered many collapsed houses. In the area of Zenkōji's Ōmon-machi, a place called Ishidō caught fire — this too was a fire that broke out from houses that had been crushed by the quake — and there were additional outbreaks of fire in various other places. Even by this morning, the fires have still not been extinguished. It is now just before the Hour of the Rooster [around 2 p.m.], yet I am told they are still burning. The main hall of Zenkōji itself has survived, but all the other halls, shrines, and the Daikanjin temple have been completely destroyed by fire. As there were exceptionally many travelers in town for the kaichō [public display of the sacred image], I understand that a great many of them have died. The Sai River, too — perhaps because of a landslide somewhere in the mountains — has stopped flowing. I am told that people are crossing it where the water only comes up to the top of the ankle. Everyone is afraid that a great flood will break out at any moment, and those living nearby are said to be fleeing up into the mountains. Unlike an ordinary earthquake, sounds have been coming from the mountains from time to time, and I wonder whether it might be an eruption of Mt. Asama. □ It is still unknown. Today again we have been out in the garden. Inariyama too, all [burned] —

**[Left folio]**

— is said to have burned down. Fortunately there was no fire [here], and for the time being I am relieved. I have been worrying about your situation — I do not believe the earthquake reached as far as Edo. Here, everyone has come through safely, and there is no particular damage to the castle, so please set your mind at ease. I do not know how things are around Ueda; there are also rumors that Iiyama has had fires. This is altogether an unprecedented great earthquake — one I have never experienced before. Until today it has felt like a dream. Nevertheless, I am in no discomfort, so please do not worry about me. I am also worried about things where you are. [The rest omitted.]

　　　25th day of the third month

P.S. I expect the earthquakes will gradually diminish from here on. Having heard of the Kyoto earthquake [as a precedent], I suppose it may take about seven days; everyone is already taking precautions, so I believe all will remain safe to the end. The weather has cleared, which is fortunate — had it been rainy, everyone would have been in great difficulty. Even as I was writing this, there was yet another great earthquake — one more tremor. I find it most vexing. Thus ends this letter.
