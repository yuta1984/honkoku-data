The following is a copy of a letter sent by the third express messenger (*hikyaku*) dispatched from the home village of Jihei, landlord (*iemoto*) of Akasakaomotedenmachō First Block, who is a native of Matsushiro in Shinano Province. The messenger departed on the last day of the third month of this year and arrived at the fourth hour of the night (*yotsu-doki*, approximately 10 p.m.) on the fourth day of (the fourth month).

Item: In the castle town of Matsushiro and its surrounding villages, the death toll is said to amount to approximately 3,500 persons or more, as investigated by the domain lord.

Item: At Zenkōji Temple, the Hall of the Buddha (*Nyorai-dō*) and the main gate (*sanmon*) survived, but all other halls, temples, and townhouses were shaken and collapsed. Furthermore, fires broke out, and driven by fierce southerly winds, more than one *ri* (approximately 4 kilometers) of the highway continuing from that area toward Echigo Province was consumed by fire spreading from the initial blaze. The fire, which began around the middle of the Hour of the Dog (*inu no nakakoku*, approximately 8 p.m.) on the night of the twenty-fourth, was extinguished at the Hour of the Hare (*u no koku*, approximately 6 a.m.) on the twenty-sixth.

Item: Regarding the Tabashima River: its proper name is the Sai River (*Saigawa*), and its headwaters begin in the mountains entered from Narai-juku below Torii Pass on the Kisō road of the Nakasendō highway. It flows from the northwest of Mount Komagatake and, below Matsumoto in the same province (Shinano), joins in a single current with the Azusa River (*Azusagawa*), which flows from Hida Province. At this particular time the river was running especially high, when in the village of Aniwa (*Aniwa-mura*) in Mochi District (*Mochi-gun*), downstream from Kumejibashi Bridge, a mass of rock approximately twenty *chō* (roughly 2.2 kilometers) in length fell into the Sai River from the earthquake, completely stopping all flow downstream from that point. As a result, at the Tabashima ferry

crossing, by the dawn of the twenty-fifth, the water had completely dried up (*kansui*), and word came in an urgent report from the river officials (*kawayakunin*) that it had become possible to cross on foot. River officials (*kawakata yakunin*) set out to investigate, and confirmed without doubt that the mountains upstream had collapsed on a tremendous scale; appropriate measures were then taken.

Item: Around the Hour of the Hare (*myōrokuji*, approximately 6 a.m.) on the twenty-ninth, sounds like thunder rumbled several times in the direction to the north of the castle town of Matsushiro and its surrounding villages, and there occurred approximately eighty aftershocks in total. The third shock was exceptionally strong. A great number of large boulders fell from an rocky mountain to the east called Mount Azakazari (*Azakazariyama*), causing widespread loss of life among people, horses, and destruction of dwellings in the villages below.

Item: Because the landslide debris at Aniwa village on the Sai River (mentioned above) is damming the current, the water has overflowed into river channels and valleys over a range of approximately ten *ri* (roughly 40 kilometers) upstream, damaging an incalculable number of dwellings; moreover, many homes have been submerged. Should the floodwater continue to rise and the dam suddenly burst all at once, there is grave danger of a water disaster (*suinan*) throughout the river corridor from Kawanakajima all the way to Echigo Province.
