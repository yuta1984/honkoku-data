Official notification submitted by Sanada Shinano-no-kami on the morning of the 6th day of the 4th month

Regarding the great earthquake that struck Matsushiro in Shinshū, my home domain, from around the Hour of the Boar (approximately 10 p.m.) on the night of the 24th of last month, I have already submitted a preliminary report as stated previously. Since that time, the tremors have continued without cessation, shaking occurring at various intervals throughout the day and night without any definite count; furthermore, on the morning of the 29th and the evening of the 30th (the last day of the month), there were three strong earthquakes on those two days. Regarding distant villages, we have been unable to ascertain the full details, but within the castle town there have been yet further cases of collapsed houses, and enormous quantities of boulders and rocks have tumbled down from the mountains in the surrounding area. Furthermore, concerning the area along the upper reaches of the Sai River where a blockage has formed — as I had previously reported — at the vicinity of Yasuniwamura and Yamahirabayashi Village in Sarashina District, a high mountain called Iwakurayama has collapsed at both its ends, with debris pushing into the river over a stretch of approximately thirty chō at one point and approximately five chō at another, burying certain villages in the vicinity beneath the debris. However, as the blockage consists predominantly of massive boulders and rocks, it appears impossible for the force of the water alone to break through it. Depending on how events unfold, the water backed up behind the dam could potentially rise to approximately seven or eight jō above normal water level. As a result, several villages have become submerged in water,

**[Left folio]**

and the area has come to resemble a lake. Naturally, we have issued instructions for various relief measures, but as it involves a great mountain and specifically an inrush of massive boulders and rock, it is feared that human effort alone will be wholly insufficient to address it. Moreover, the people of Kawanakajima and Hirabayashi Village live in terror, unable to predict from which direction the pent-up waters may suddenly burst forth all at once, and have evacuated to higher ground in the mountains. Tanbajima-juku and other post stations are in a similar situation, and the relay of people and horses for transportation has become impossible. We have issued the most thorough instructions possible for relief measures, but for now submit this report. Further details will be submitted in due course. Respectfully submitted.

　　　　　　1st day of the 4th month　　　　　Sanada Shinano-no-kami
