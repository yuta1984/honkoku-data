(Whether Heizō was alive or dead) remained unclear, but thinking that he had perhaps fled in the direction of Oiwake, (Tōjirō) departed from that place and made his way to Tabashima. There, at the ferry crossing, the mountains upstream had collapsed and were blocking the Tabashima River (*Chikuma River* at this point), and as the ferry landing had been swept away by the current, he crossed on foot through the shallows. Word was spreading that floodwaters swelling from upstream would soon come rushing down, so he hurried away, and that same night arrived in Ueda. There too, however, the castle-town streets had been shaken and collapsed, and there was no possibility of finding lodging, so he proceeded to Komoro, where he stayed the night. (Tōjirō) returned to Edo yesterday (the last day of the month). However, since the fate of Heizō, whom he had brought along, remained unknown, upon the request of Tōsuke's household, Heizō's father Eikichi was sent off together (to search for Heizō) immediately upon (Tōjirō's) return.

Item: Of the approximately eighteen *chō* (roughly 2 kilometers) of the Zenkōji temple town, those areas where post-town inns (*hatagoya*) stood were twisted and toppled; the rest were either damaged and leaning, or in some cases unharmed.

Item: In the castle town of Matsushiro in the same province (Shinano), the greater part was crushed and destroyed. The highway was cracked open in various places with mud and sand forced up through the fissures. Both Matsushiro and the castle town of Ueda showed no sign of fires in that vicinity.

Item: The riverside townhouses (*kawamachiya*) at Tabashima were undamaged. It is, however, unclear whether the waters of the Tabashima River (*Chikuma River*) had risen.

Item: The earthquake was severe over a range of approximately seven *ri* (roughly 28 kilometers) in all directions. It first struck around the Hour of the Boar (*yotsu-doki*, approximately 10 p.m.) on the night of the twenty-fourth, and the subsequent events are as reported above.
