The above concerns the disaster that struck the villages of Takai District and Mizuuchi District in Shinano Province, which fall under my magistrate's jurisdiction and temporary administration, as a result of the great earthquake on the night of the 24th day of the 3rd month of the current year. Having already submitted an urgent initial report, I promptly dispatched my assistants and clerks, and personally conducted an inspection tour of the affected villages. What I witnessed was a catastrophe truly beyond words.

**[Left folio]**

It was so terrifying that one could scarcely bear to look. The ground had split open in linear fissures, ranging from seven or eight sun to over five or six shaku in width, extending for several ken at a stretch, and from the cracks in the rocks, enormous quantities of muddy water of black and reddish hues gushed forth, rendering many places impassable on foot. Furthermore, landslides occurred in numerous locations, with earth, sand, and snowmelt rushing down, boulders tumbling, and farmland completely transformed in topography, with extensive areas of lost agricultural land visible everywhere. Many villages found their irrigation water supply completely cut off. In valleys and streams, boulders and earth were pushed out, and areas were buried or collapsed by the seismic forces, causing severe damage; waterways were blocked, and muddy water overflowed across the terrain, sweeping everything away. As for the collapsed houses, every one of them was crushed flat as if pressed down evenly across entire rows — the main beams, crossbeams, tenon joints, and threshold timbers, along with all fittings and fixtures, were completely shattered; household goods and belongings were entirely destroyed; stocks of grain and bales of goods that each household had stored were crushed and scattered, drenched in the mud water that had gushed up, and in some cases buried under earth and sand. When I first made my rounds of inspection, the common villagers (shōmae) — to say nothing of the village officials themselves — had lost their senses entirely, with no capacity whatsoever to think about what needed to be done next, and each stood [dumbfounded] in front of their collapsed home...
