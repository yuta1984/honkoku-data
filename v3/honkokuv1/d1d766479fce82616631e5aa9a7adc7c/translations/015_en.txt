**[Left folio]**

A letter in the hand of Sanada Shinano-no-Kami to his heir — arrived on the last day of the third month.

On the night of the twenty-fourth, just before the Hour of the Ox [around 2 a.m.], I was painting a picture of Daikoku and it was mostly finished; I still had my brush in hand when the great earthquake began to shake. As I was not particularly alarmed, I continued to hold the brush. However, the shaking suddenly grew violent, and the inner and outer sliding screens all fell over at once, so I went out to the veranda as I was. The ladies-in-waiting, Kubo Kukurō [Kyūjūrō], and others nearby, alarmed by the event, came through the lock-gate and went out into the inner garden, where tatami mats were laid on the ground and people sat upon them. The chief elder [karō] and the three magistrates also came, and we met there. Terauchi and Tamiya emerged as well, and it was found that my son Tamon, who had been lying down, was in a place where the residence had collapsed and he had ended up under a beam — but he managed to push aside the roof tiles and other debris and get out, and though he suffered minor injuries, nothing serious had happened. Kubo Kyūjūrō's son Gokujin also sustained minor injuries, and Onda Tanomo's son was reportedly pulled out from beneath a beam. The rest of the household escaped without harm. In the town as well, the area around Nakamachi was mostly destroyed, with some eighteen dead and an uncountable number of injured. After that as well —
