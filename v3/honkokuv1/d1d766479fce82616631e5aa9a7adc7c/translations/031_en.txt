**[Right folio]**

(Having lodged at Wataya Nizaemon's inn,) pilgrims from all provinces had gathered in extraordinarily large numbers, and there were some two hundred fifty or sixty people lodging together at Nizaemon's establishment. Tōjirō and Heizō were placed together with eighteen other guests in a twelve-mat room on the second floor at the front of the inn, and as it was extremely crowded, their baggage and belongings were each left in their respective rooms while they all lay pressed against one another. At what seemed to be shortly past the Hour of the Boar (*yotsu-doki*, around 10 p.m.) that same night, Tōjirō, having fallen asleep completely unawares, had something fall upon him and was buried beneath it, unable to move his body. Amid the confusion, voices — apparently those of the fellow guests — were heard crying and screaming. He thought: was it an earthquake? It felt as though the building had been crushed. In any case, something calamitous had occurred, and he resolved that he must first get out. Just then, he noticed that the area above his head appeared slightly lighter, so he smashed through the debris and finally crawled out onto the roof. He called out repeatedly for Heizō, and it seemed as though a reply was coming back, so he climbed up onto the roof and began breaking through what seemed a likely spot. Together with about seven others who had also escaped, they joined forces and managed to pull out another six or seven people from where they had broken through — but of Heizō, there was no sign.

**[Left folio]**

After that, though he called out, no reply came. He was determined to pull Heizō out no matter what, but as fires broke out here and there, and the earthquake still had not stopped, and he himself was in increasing danger, all those present fled together to the precincts of Zenkōji Temple. However, the Niōmon gate had collapsed, and along the roughly four *chō* (approximately 440 meters) between that gate and the main hall (*hondō*), the paving stones — more than three *ken* (approximately 5.4 meters) wide — along with dozens of stone lanterns had been twisted apart and wrecked, the ground torn up as though it had been dug over. The fifty-seven *bō* (priests' lodging quarters and pilgrim inns) lining both sides of that approach had been entirely destroyed, and here too fires had broken out in numerous places. The main hall (*hondō*) itself was unharmed, but it appeared as though it might topple at any moment, and not a single person was to be found within. Proceeding to the rear of the main hall, they found some one hundred fifty or sixty persons — monks, men and women alike — who had escaped unharmed, gathered there. They passed the night in that place. The following day, the twenty-fifth, around midday, (Tōjirō) went to search again for Heizō, combing through the charred ruins of the town streets, but he could not be found. It appeared that more than half of the pilgrims and local residents had perished, and even the few who had barely survived were mostly wounded or burned, and the turmoil was immense. The fate of Heizō — whether he was alive or dead —（remained unknown...）
