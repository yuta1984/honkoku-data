We gradually reasoned with the people and explained the arrangements for relief measures, and they eventually dispersed. Given these circumstances, it was by no means possible to quickly undertake and complete the necessary construction and repairs, and our capacity to address the situation was limited. As there were no lodgings available, we spent two days and two nights sleeping outdoors before finally departing the area. It is our intention that once the local domain lord has arranged temporary shelters and other relief measures, and once the people's spirits have settled, word should be sent to us promptly so that we may return to commence the repair work — it was with this understanding that we made our return journey.

One: Regarding the extent of this great earthquake, the people there were saying it covered a range of ten ri in all directions, but it appears that the breadth from north to south was approximately five to six ri, and the extent from north to south was limited to about twelve to thirteen ri, and beyond that there likely was nothing of particular note. We departed Shiozaki Village, crossed the Chikuma River, and after traveling one ri arrived at Yashiro-juku in Matsushiro domain, where the number of collapsed houses was relatively small — approximately thirty or so — and the number of immediate deaths was said to be twelve or thirteen. Compared to Shiozaki Village, the ground fissures were narrower,

**[Left folio]**

and damage appeared somewhat lighter. Proceeding further to Sakaki-juku, which falls under the jurisdiction of the magistrate Kawakami Kingo-suke, there were no collapsed houses or deaths, though walls and fixtures had been damaged by the shaking, and tremors continued dozens of times day and night, with everyone sleeping outdoors. Traveling three ri further to Ueda Town, the damage was yet another degree lighter, though people there too appeared to be sleeping outdoors day and night. Proceeding two and a half ri to Komoro Town, then three more ri to Oiwake-juku on the Nakasendō highway, there was no rumbling of mountains whatsoever; after the great earthquake there were only occasional slight tremors from time to time. Crossing the Usui Pass toward Edo, there had only been a fairly strong earthquake on the night of the 24th, and after that there were apparently no further earthquakes at all.

The above is a general account of the natural disaster I witnessed and heard about during the course of my official duties. I humbly submit this summary as stated above, and intend to investigate the details further and report them in due course from the relevant localities. Respectfully submitted.
