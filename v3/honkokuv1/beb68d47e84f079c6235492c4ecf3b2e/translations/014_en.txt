(Continued from ㉕) Ushi Tenjin-shita, Suwa-machi, and Suwa Myōjin in Koishikawa all suffered major damage. In the same area, approximately half a chō burned. Along the Edo River (Edogawa), many buildings collapsed.

Note: Within the approximately 3,700 chō of town districts spanning a four-ri radius around Edo, more than 535,780 storehouses (dozō) collapsed. Furthermore, the total number of burned and collapsed houses is beyond counting. The number of deaths from collapsed and burned buildings amounted to approximately 85,900 people or more, and the number of injured was beyond reckoning. Along the Tōkaidō highway, many suffered injury and death in Kawasaki and Kanagawa. The damage extended as far as Hakone. To the east, from Kasai through Funabori, Horie, Gyōtoku, and Funabashi — these areas in particular suffered widespread collapse and many deaths. Sakura, Narita, Kinoshita, and Genna (possibly Fusa/Abiko area) were especially strongly (shaken). Kazusa and Bōshū (Chiba region) suffered minor damage. Along the Nikkō Kaidō highway, damage extended as far as Satte. In Shimōsa Province, damage was severe in various locations, though the degree varied from place to place. Along the Nakasendō highway, damage extended as far as Kumagaya. Notably, the embankment at Kumagaya cracked open and expelled mud, sand, and gravel. Along the Mito Kaidō highway, the area around Toride was particularly severely (shaken). In the vicinity of Mount Tsukuba, everything collapsed. In Hitachi Province, the shaking and collapse were extremely severe. Beyond these areas, the earthquake reached many provinces, but this is beyond what brush and paper can fully describe.

By the benevolent grace and compassion of the authorities (the Shogunate), relief rice and emergency shelter huts (o-sukui-goya) were provided to those in great distress. House-owning townspeople (chōnin) also distributed money, rice, and other goods, and the people at large were at last able to find peace of mind and rejoice in the restoration of tranquility.
