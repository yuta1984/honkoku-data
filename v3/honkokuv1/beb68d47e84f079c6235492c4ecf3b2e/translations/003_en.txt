[Annotation ㊀ – Shin-Yoshiwara and the Asakusa area (continued)]

In Shin-Yoshiwara, all the districts within the licensed quarter—Edo-machi, Kyō-machi, Kado-machi, Ageyamachi, Fushimi-machi, and Naka-no-machi—were completely destroyed by the earthquake. Moreover, fires then broke out upon the ruins, resulting in the deaths of approximately 2,300 or more people. The number of injured is beyond counting. The official notice board (Gokōsatsu) outside the Ōmon gate, as well as the canal on this side, survived the fire. The right (eastern) side burned, and the ground along Nihon Tsutsumi (the embankment) cracked open. Not a single storehouse within the quarter survived—all burned. A fire broke out from Tamachi, spreading through the 1st and 2nd blocks, past Sodesuri Inari Shrine and the sedge-hat tea houses below the embankment. The northern areas survived, though all buildings there had collapsed. To the west, the two towns in front of the gate of Tennō-ji Temple in Yanaka, Yamakawa-machi, the Batō Kannon, and two temples including Saihō-ji survived the fire. To the south, fire spread into Umamichi and Kitajin-machi, and Henshō-in temple, as well as all three theaters in Saruwaka-machi 3-chōme, were completely burned. The area north of the actors' quarter (Shindō) and the residence of Morita Kanya was spared from the fire. The area within the bamboo grove (Yabu-no-uchi) collapsed entirely, with nothing left standing or unburned. The area across from Kuhonji Temple in Yamanoshuku survived. About seven-tenths of the Tozawa tenement houses in Hanakawado-machi burned before the fire stopped. On the western side of Umamichi, Kichijō-in, Tokuō-in, Enmei-in, Seishin-in, Mudō-in, and Kyōzen-in all burned, from the north side of Umamichi around to the corner of the south side of Umamichi. On the eastern side of the same area, Senryō-in, Senzō-in, Shuzen-in, Myōtoku-in, Iō-in, Mandara-dō, Kongō-in, Kakuzen-in, Hōzen-in, Myōon-in, and Kenmatsu-in—all of these had numerous iroha tenement houses between them, and all burned completely.
