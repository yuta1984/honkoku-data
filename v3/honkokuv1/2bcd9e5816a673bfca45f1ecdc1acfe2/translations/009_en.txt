**[Upper Section]**

○ At Kinryū-zan Senso-ji temple, the main hall (hondō), the Nembutsu-dō, the Kyō-dō (sutra hall), the Shōrō-dō (belfry), the Sanmon (main gate), and the Zuijin-mon (guardian deity gate) were undamaged. However, the Raijin (thunder deity) statue at the Raijin-mon gate toppled over, the nine-ringed finial (kurin) atop the five-story pagoda was bent out of shape, and the various halls within the temple precinct suffered major damage. At Denbō-in sub-temple, the main gate, entrance hall, and reception hall (shoin) were undamaged, but the kitchen quarters (kuri) and other buildings were heavily damaged. The temples within the precinct and the tenement houses in the temple district were all completely collapsed, and there were many casualties.

○ The principal image of Senso-ji, the Kannon Bodhisattva (Kanzeon), was temporarily enshrined in the Hanayashiki garden within the temple grounds.
　　Note: After the earthquake had passed, when they attempted to carry the Bodhisattva back into the main hall, the sacred cabinet (o-zushi) was so heavy that it could not be lifted. People interpreted this as a divine sign indicating that a great aftershock was forthcoming, and so the image was left as it was in the garden for approximately seven or eight days.

**[Lower Section]**

○ A fire broke out in the vicinity of Asakusa Tamachi, and only a small area below the embankment survived. The following areas burned: the substitute district of the temple-front town of Yanaka Tennō-ji, Yamakawa-chō, both sides of Take-mon-dōri, Sodesuri Inari (a small shrine), Umamichi-dōri, Kita-Shin-machi, and approximately twenty houses to the east of Henshō-in. Yamanoshuku, all three theaters of Saruwaka-chō, the actors' back-lane (Gakuya-shindō), the area called Yabu-no-uchi, the lodging grounds of Senso-ji (mochi-bō precinct), the Iroha-nagaya tenement, and the precinct of Iō-in were burned. The fire stopped on both sides at the edge of the Zuijin-mon (guardian deity gate). On the south side, the fire spread as far as the corner of Minami-Shindō. In Hanakawado, the fire was stopped at around the middle of the Tozawa tenement block.
