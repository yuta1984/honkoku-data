○ With each repeated aftershock, people feared that yet another great earthquake might strike. They spread tatami mats out on the main streets, piled up folding screens, doors, shoji screens, and the belongings they had carried out for safekeeping, fashioning makeshift enclosures in which to sleep. This became the common practice for everyone. After seven or eight days had passed, as the earthquakes gradually subsided, fewer and fewer people remained camping outdoors. On the fourteenth day, rain fell throughout the entire day, and it was at this point that the outdoor encampments finally ceased.
