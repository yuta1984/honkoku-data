## The Second Year of Ansei (1855), Year of the Hare, Tenth Month

On the night of the second day, at the Hour of the Pig (around 10 o'clock in the evening), a great earthquake struck. As the shaking began, storehouses tilted and houses collapsed in great numbers, and the death toll among men and women, young and old, was beyond reckoning. At this moment, fierce flames blazed up from all eight directions, scorching the very sky. The fires initially broke out at thirty-eight locations, but as nearby fires merged and spread into one another, the count fell to thirty-two, then to twenty-seven. The fires continued to spread further, and only at the Hour of the Horse (around noon) on the following day, the third, were they finally and completely extinguished.

Moreover, the loss of human life caused by this disaster was immense: the number of residents within the city limits of Edo who lost their lives in a single instant reached tens of thousands — truly an event without precedent in living memory.
