**[Upper Section]**

○ At Higashi-Monzeki, the main hall (hondō) was undamaged. However, approximately three temples within the precinct had their main halls collapse, and both the east gate and north gate fell. The drainage quarters (mizo-beya) and the guard post (rinban-jo) suffered major damage.

○ At the same location, Sōfuku-ji, Daisho-ji, and Seikō-ji all suffered major damage. In Tawara-machi, Naka-machi, and along Morishita-machi-dōri, there were many collapsed houses.

○ At Seigan-ji temple, the main hall was undamaged. Several temples within the precinct suffered major damage. There were many collapsed houses in the temple-front district.

○ At Nichirin-ji temple, the main hall was undamaged. The student quarters (gakuryō), the kitchen quarters (kuri), the temples within the precinct, and the temple-front town were all completely destroyed.

○ At Kōryū-ji temple, the main hall was undamaged. The kitchen quarters (kuri) and the temples within the precinct suffered major damage.

**[Lower Section]**

○ Starting from the corner in front of a restaurant tea house called "Hatsufuji" in Asakusa Komagata-chō, fires burned along both sides of the street, through Suwa-chō and Kurofune-chō, reaching as far as Kaya-dera. To the east, the fire spread as far as Omiya-Kaishi (the shogunate's horse stables riverbank) and Miyoshi-chō. In this area, not a single storehouse (kura) survived.

○ Along Okura-mae-dōri, there was no particularly severe structural damage; however, the tiled roofs of all the storehouses (dozō) were damaged without exception.

○ The stone wall of Asakusa-Gomon (Asakusa Gate) bulged outward slightly.

○ In Fukui-chō, the rental road (kashi-dōri) belonging to Lord Sakai Saemon-no-jō and the area around the Kanda River all suffered major damage.
