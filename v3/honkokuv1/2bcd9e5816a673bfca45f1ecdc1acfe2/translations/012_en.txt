○ No sooner had the earthquake begun to rumble than the pleasure houses of Shin-Yoshiwara's five districts (Gochōmachi) collapsed in their entirety, flames erupted from all directions, and the entire quarter was engulfed in fire. The drawbridges at the rear of the quarter could not be lowered in time, and even when someone attempted to lower one, the bridges were too damaged to be crossed. As the Great Gate (Ōmon) was the only exit, people perished — overwhelmed by smoke, consumed by flames, crushed under collapsed buildings, or trapped beneath beams they had tried to dodge. Even those fortunate enough to be alive had no one to tear through rooftops or break down walls to rescue them, and so they had no choice but to await the approaching flames and burn to death. The number of casualties and fatalities was enormous. For four or five days afterward, the dead lay scattered here and there throughout the quarter; people searched desperately for their parents, for their children — it was a sight too dreadful to behold. The number of courtesans who perished was 831; clients and others who had been visiting the quarter numbered more than 450; teahouse workers, merchants, entertainers, and others throughout the quarter totaled more than 1,400 — bringing the grand total of the dead to more than 2,700, it is said. Not a single storehouse (dozō) survived; a small portion of the western riverbank (Nishi-gashi) remained, along with one side of the fifty-ken frontage.
