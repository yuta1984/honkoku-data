It is the natural order for [the descending energy] to sink and enter into the space left behind. However, take spring as an example: Yang energy should emerge first, and Yin energy should settle into the space left in its wake — yet when the descending Yin energy advances ahead and presses down into the earth, the Yang energy that ought to rise is suppressed by it and cannot emerge. Thus, if spring remains cold for a prolonged period and becomes an irregular, baleful energy (jaki), it harms people as well as plants and trees — people fall ill, and plants and trees wither and die. In the ordinary course of things, Yang energy should have completely risen to the surface by the summer solstice in the fifth month; yet because this Yin energy has pressed forward and suppressed it, Yang energy remains trapped within the earth even through the end of the fourth month, unable to float upward. The Yang energy then accumulates to an unbearable degree within the earth and at last bursts through the Yin energy that has been suppressing it, erupting outward. At this moment it reverberates through the earth, causing the ground to tremble and shake tremendously. It is, so to speak, like a person who has long harbored a grievance and endured it patiently, but who at last, unable to bear it any longer, gives vent to their anger in an outburst.

Now, in autumn, it is the natural order for Yin energy to emerge first, with Yang energy settling into the space left behind — yet the descending Yang energy advances ahead and presses down into the earth, so that the Yin energy that ought to rise is suppressed by it and cannot float upward.
