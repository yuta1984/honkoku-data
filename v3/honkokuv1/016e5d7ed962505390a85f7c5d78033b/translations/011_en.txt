As the proverb says, "A melon vine does not bear eggplants" — and yet people do not realize that by sowing seeds of evil, no good fruit will grow. One must simply and solely sow seeds of goodness, so that a harvest of goodness may be obtained.

○ In the first place, the reason such natural disasters occur is that, fundamentally, people's hearts are not upright and honest, and so Heaven sends down calamities to admonish and correct them. Wang Chong's *Lùnhéng* states: "In an age of great peace, the wind blows once every five days, and the rain falls once every ten days. The wind does not rattle the branches; the rain does not break apart clods of earth." In a sagely age of ideal governance, it is said, the wind comes once every five days and the rain once every ten days, and all is naturally gentle and mild.

And yet, although those above (i.e., the rulers) are pure, administering benevolent governance and showing compassion for all the people, those below are turbid and murky, and the spirit of the people is not good. Having grown proud and complacent after more than two hundred years of peace, they have lost the Way of benevolence and righteousness (*jingi*).
