(Continued from previous page) ...when one practices hidden virtue, even if there exists a karmic fate ordained by Heaven, it will surely be transformed, and one will be able to escape calamity.

In general, what one finds delicious, others find delicious as well; what is bitter is bitter for all, regardless of rank or station. What brings joy brings joy to all, high and low alike; what brings sorrow brings sorrow to all, high and low alike. Thus the character *jo* (恕, "empathy" or "benevolence") is written as "like the heart," conveying the meaning of consideration for others — the teaching that "what you yourself find disagreeable, do not inflict upon others." As the proverb says, "Pinch your own flesh and know another's pain." What one desires, others desire as well; this does not change regardless of whether one is high-born or low-born. This is what is meant by human feeling (*ninjō*).

Yet those who lack this human feeling — who care not whether others are hot or cold, and think only that it is enough for themselves alone to be comfortable and to eat good food — will find that neither their own household nor others will submit to them willingly. Such a person will suffer great losses, and will surely incur the punishment of Heaven.

Make compassion (*jihi*) your foremost priority; constantly practice hidden virtue; show reverence to those above you and show compassion to those below; repay what you have borrowed promptly; do not take the life of living creatures; do not drive oxen and horses cruelly; do not beat dogs that have committed no offense; and do not pit roosters against one another for the amusement of your desires. Such things must absolutely not be done.
