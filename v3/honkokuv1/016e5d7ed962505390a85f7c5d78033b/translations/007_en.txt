Yin energy ought to have finished emerging by the winter solstice in the eleventh month; yet if it is suppressed by this Yang energy and remains unable to emerge right through to the end of the tenth month, it can no longer be contained within the earth, and so it bursts through the Yang energy that has been suppressing it. As it emerges and reverberates through the earth, the ground shakes and trembles repeatedly. This is precisely the cause of the great disaster of this occasion. When Yin and Yang clash in the sky, they become thunder and resound far and wide, yet the ground does not move. The reason for this is that the heavens are a great void (taikyo) in which there is no obstruction. When Yin and Yang rub against each other within the earth, however, the reverberation strikes the soil, causing it to tremble and shake. Thus, while earthquakes may occur at any time, they are certain to occur when the world remains unseasonably cold around the fourth and fifth months; likewise, one should bear in mind that earthquakes are extremely likely to occur when the weather is particularly warm around the tenth and eleventh months. It should be noted that when an earthquake first strikes, it is fierce and violent — like the snapping of one's rope of patience. Therefore, the subsequent tremors are gentler. Although shaking may continue for months thereafter, all of it is but the lingering remnant energy (yoki), growing fainter with each occurrence; nothing about it resembles the initial shock in the slightest.

As the proverb says:
　　*The seedling [is most critical] at the start, water in the middle, and wind at the end.*
