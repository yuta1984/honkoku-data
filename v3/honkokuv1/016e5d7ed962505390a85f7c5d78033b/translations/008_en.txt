*The roar of thunder knows no bounds.*

The word *nawe* (なゑ) refers to an earthquake and is a colloquial Japanese expression. It is thus a saying that teaches us that an earthquake is fierce at first and then gradually becomes gentler. A flood steadily grows from the moment it begins, reaching its peak at its midpoint, and thereafter gradually recedes — therefore, it is said that the midpoint is the greatest moment of a flood. As for wind, the saying notes that it grows stronger after it first begins to blow. Thunder is unpredictable and knows no limits.

Furthermore, there is a distinction between a flood (*kōzui*) and a tsunami (*kyotō*/great wave). A flood occurs when a great volume of water flows down from upstream, breaking through embankments and damaging fields and farmland.

【Lower section】
A tsunami (*kyotō*) is caused by the sea being shaken and surging inland. At the time of a great earthquake, the mud along the water's edge at the shore wells up, and black waves rise and surge like mountains. This is called a tsunami. There are also cases in which violent rainstorms cause the sea and mountains to tremble and shake, and great gales churn the ground, sending furious waves surging in reverse. Both are called tsunamis. The people living in houses along the coast fear these above all else.
