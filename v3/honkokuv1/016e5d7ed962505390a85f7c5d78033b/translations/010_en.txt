That said, it is only natural for women and children who lack discernment to panic and fall into confusion. However, for grown men to similarly lose their composure, running to places such as Kōzu and Ikutama, and fleeing toward the Uemachi and Tennōji areas — this must be called the height of foolishness. Even if the river were to rise and flood the land, how could it be so difficult to protect oneself against this? In the first place, even if the water rises to a dangerous level, it will soon recede. One must understand that the wave that comes in will also go out. Furthermore, regarding the unexpected deaths (*ōshi*) that occurred on this occasion, these were not cases of people drowning because the tsunami overflowed into the streets of the city; rather, people fled onto boats in an attempt to escape the dangers of the earthquake, and were then drowned by the tsunami. In the end, this was a heaven-ordained fate not unlike that of the moth that flies into the flame. Let not those of future generations transmit and misunderstand this point.

That said, those who die by natural disaster because their livelihood keeps them aboard ships are in the same situation as sailors and shipmasters caught in a violent storm — this is something for which one should always be mentally prepared.
