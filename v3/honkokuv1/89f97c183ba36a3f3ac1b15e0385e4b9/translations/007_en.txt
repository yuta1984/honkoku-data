(Continued from previous page) The losses, and the deaths of people young and old, men and women, were beyond measure, and cannot be recorded one by one in their entirety. By the evening of the following third day, the fires finally subsided, and people at last felt a sense of relief. After that, the ground continued to shake from time to time, but no further great damage was caused. In order to inform relatives and acquaintances throughout the various provinces of these events, we have investigated and compiled a summary of this occurrence — brought about by the cyclic workings of heaven and earth — and hereby present it as a general overview.

　　●　Number of town districts: over 5,370　　●　Shrines and temples: over 38,600
　　●　Estates: over 5,870　　●　Earthen storehouses (dozō): over 230,590

Although such a calamity arising from this extraordinary turn of events has befallen us, how deeply grateful we must be for the benevolent governance of the Bakufu (the Shogunal government): immediately, temporary shelters were erected outside the Saiwai-bashi Gate, in Fukagawa Umibe-Daiku-chō, and at Asakusa Hirokōji, and the destitute were received and relieved therein. In addition, various other relief measures and provisions were extended, and the depth of this gracious beneficence is so great that words are wholly inadequate to express it. Even in the midst of sleep and meals, one does not forget it; with the utmost reverence, one can only offer one's most humble and heartfelt gratitude.
