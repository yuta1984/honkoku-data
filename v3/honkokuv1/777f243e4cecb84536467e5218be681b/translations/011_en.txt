**[Right folio]**

[Continued from the previous folio:] The enclosures and the various government offices [of the imperial palace] collapsed and crumbled, and a great many houses throughout the capital fell down. The Hasshōin (Hall of the Eight Ministries), the Burakuin (Ceremonial Hall), Tōji, Saiji, Gokurakuji, Kiyomizudera, and Enkakuji, among others, were destroyed. It was said to be an earthquake without precedent. The Emperor and the Empress had curtains hung in the garden to serve as their residence. At Kiyomizudera, fifty people — monks and laypeople alike — were crushed to death. On the following day, the nineteenth, the earth shook fourteen times. The rear offices of the Saemon Guard station, the corridor of the Horikawa-in, the west wing (*tainoya*) of the Kan'in Palace, and three buildings of the Ministry of Popular Affairs all collapsed. As preparations were being made for the Emperor to move to the residence of the Grand Minister of State (*Daijō Daijin*) Fujiwara no Kaneie at Horikawa — with several hundred carpenters dispatched to make repairs — the earthen walls on all four sides suddenly collapsed, killing more than thirty carpenters. Eighteen of them were dug out [alive]. The young attendants (*chigo*) of the monks performing sutra readings were also crushed to death. The south side of the Hokke Hall of Sōfukuji collapsed into the valley below, killing the temple warden monk Senshō, and the bell tower also collapsed. A great boulder fell on the Miroku Hall, damaging its northwest corner. Furthermore, at the Kokubunji of Ōmi Province, the great gate collapsed and the Two Guardian Kings (*Niō*) were shattered; more than thirty buildings of the provincial government offices (*kokufu*) toppled; and at Sekidera, the great Buddha statue was shattered and lost from the waist up.

**[Left folio]**

On the twentieth day there were eleven tremors; on the twenty-first, thirteen; on the twenty-second, twelve; on the twenty-third, ten; on the twenty-sixth, eight; on the twenty-ninth, five; on the last day of the month, eight; on the eleventh day of the seventh month, six; and on the twelfth day, four earthquakes. On account of this disaster, the era name was changed from Ten'en (fourth year) to Jōgen. (Pardoning of criminals and the like followed, as was customary.) Yet the aftershocks still did not cease: the earth shook twice on the fourteenth, shook greatly on the eighteenth and twentieth, shook three times on the twenty-first, and continued without pause until the twenty-third.

On the twenty-third day of the ninth month of the same year, the earth shook greatly again with severe rumbling. On the eleventh day of the tenth month, sacred treasures (*jinpō*) were offered to various shrines and prayers were performed.

The above is based on the *Nihon Kiryaku* and the *Fusō Ryakki*.

○ In the second year [of Jōgen] (977 CE), on the fourth day of the second month, there was an earthquake at the Hour of the Snake (around 10 a.m.). On the ninth day, at the Hour of the Snake, the earth shook greatly.

During the reign of Emperor Kazan, on the eighth day of the eleventh month of the second year of Jōgan (985 CE), the earth shook greatly.

During the reign of Emperor Ichijō, on the twenty-fourth day of the tenth month of the fifth year of Shōryaku (994 CE), there was likewise a divination (*onuranai*) performed.

During the reign of Emperor Sanjō, on the sixth day of the eleventh month of the fourth year of Chōwa (1015 CE), the earth shook greatly.

The above is based on the *Nihon Kiryaku*.

During the reign of Emperor Go-Ichijō, in the second year of Jian (1022 CE), likewise [an earthquake and divination].

○ In the first year of Manju (1024 CE), on the eleventh day of the third month, there was an earthquake at the Hour of the Dog (around 8 p.m.). On the eighteenth day, at the Hour of the Hare (around 6 a.m.), there was another earthquake. The above is based on the *Fusō Ryakki*.

In the second year of the same era (1025 CE), in the twelfth month, there was an earthquake and heavy snowfall.

○ In the fourth year of the same era (1027 CE), on the second day of the third month, at the Hour of the Monkey (around 4 p.m.), there was a great earthquake.
