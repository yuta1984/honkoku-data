*Great Japan, from the Age of the Gods*　　《Box: Sale Prohibited》
《Title: Chronicle of Earthquakes》
浪花 (Osaka)　Published by Bunreidō

**[Left folio]**

This year, on the second day of the tenth month of Ansei 2 (1855), the Year of the Hare, as the night grew late, a most extraordinary great earthquake struck. Storehouses and warehouses were damaged without exception, and there were countless injuries among people and livestock, so that high and low alike, noble and common, lost their senses in terror. And yet, it is wondrous and most sacred that nine out of ten of the famous shrines and temples came through unscathed. How could we not look up to them in reverence? How could we not pay our respects?

The *Ainōshō* (Volume 14) contains a method for determining the auspiciousness or inauspiciousness of earthquakes. According to this text, the second day of the tenth month corresponded to the lunar mansion "Xu" (虚宿, the Void) among the Twenty-Eight Lunar Mansions, and the hour was the Hour of the Boar (approximately 10 p.m.). According to Buddhist teaching, an earthquake occurring on this day at this hour is called *Taishaku-yuri* (the Shaking of Indra), and its portent is recorded as: "Peace throughout the realm, an abundance of the five grains, good fortune for the Emperor, blessings for the ministers, and peace for all people."

The *Daichidoron* (Mahāprajñāpāramitā Śāstra) states that there are four types of earthquakes: *Kōjin-yuri* (the Shaking of the Fire God), *Ryūjin-yuri* (the Shaking of the Dragon God), *Konjichō-yuri* (the Shaking of the Garuda), and *Taishaku-yuri* (the Shaking of Indra). Of these, all but the Shaking of Indra are ill omens. What is said to be the work of the great catfish (*namazu*) likely belongs to the category of *Konjichō-yuri*. Be that as it may, this is a most auspicious "world renewal" (*yonaoshi*).

This small volume has been compiled by selecting and excerpting accounts of earthquakes from ancient times to the present from various classical texts. There have been dozens of earthquakes far greater in scale than the present one, yet the realm did not decline — on the contrary, it flourished ever more abundantly. It is with the wish to inform the people that our land of the Reed Plains (Japan) has enjoyed and shall continue to enjoy everlasting peace, as endless as heaven and earth themselves, that this work was written.

*Anonymous* (Mumei-shi, "One of No Name")
