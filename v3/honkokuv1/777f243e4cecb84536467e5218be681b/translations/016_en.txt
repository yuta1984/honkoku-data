**[Meiō]** 2nd year (1493), 10th month: likewise (great earthquake). **[Zoku Shiryaku]** 3rd year (1494), 7th day of the 5th month: likewise (great earthquake). **[Gōunzu]** 4th year (1495), 15th day of the 8th month: the Great Buddha of Kamakura was damaged by an earthquake. 7th year (1498), 11th day of the 6th month: a great earthquake struck the whole realm simultaneously. **[Emperor Gokashiwabara / Eishō]** 7th year (1510), 7th day of the 8th month: a great earthquake struck and would not cease. The plaque on the stone torii gate of Tennōji fell, halls and pagodas throughout the Go-Kinai (Five Home Provinces) were destroyed, and many people died. **[Honchō Nendaiki]** On the 8th day, a great earthquake struck; on the 28th, the sea of Tōtōmi overflowed, and the lowlands were submerged and became sea. This is the origin of the Imagire Ferry crossing. **[Zoku Shiryaku]** 9th year (1512), 6th month: great earthquake. **[Gōunzu]** 11th year (1514), 8th day of the 4th month: likewise.

**[Emperor Gonara / Kōji]** 2nd year (1556), 2nd month: great earthquake. 3rd year (1557), 7th month: likewise. **[Kugyō Bunin]**

**[Emperor Ōgimachi / Tenshō]** 13th year (1585), 19th day of the 11th month (the Gōunzu records this as the 29th day): the capital (Kyoto) shook violently and continued trembling for more than one hundred days. **[Zoku Shiryaku]**

**[Emperor Goyōzei / Tenshō]** 17th year (1589), 2nd month: a great earthquake struck the two provinces of Suruga and Tōtōmi, destroying the homes of the common people. **[Butoku Taisei]**

**[Keichō]** 1st year (1596), 12th day of the intercalary 7th month: a great earthquake. The ground split open and water gushed forth. Castles, residences, and commoner dwellings in Kyoto and Fushimi collapsed and were destroyed; the number of dead was beyond counting. The Great Buddha Hall collapsed, and the Buddha statue was also destroyed. The Taikō (Toyotomi Hideyoshi) went to inspect the damage, and turning to face the Buddha, he rebuked it, declaring: "The reason the Buddha image was enshrined here was for the peace and security of the nation. And yet it was unable to stop this great earthquake, causing suffering to all the people, and could not even preserve itself—shattering and splitting apart. What a pathetic thing this is! Of what use is it, after all?" With that, he drew his bow and shot arrows at it. He then had the Buddha image from Zenkōji in Shinano Province brought and installed in the Hall. (Later, this image was returned, and a separate golden Buddha image was cast.) Furthermore, since most of the ladies-in-waiting at his side had been crushed to death, and there was no one immediately available to replace them, several hundred courtesans from Kyoto, Fushimi, Osaka, and Sakai were summoned and employed as ladies-in-waiting. **[Toyotomi Fu]**

**[Emperor Gomizunoo / Keichō]** 19th year (1614), 25th day of the 10th month: great earthquake. **[Gōunzu]**

**[Kan'ei]** 4th year (1627), 11th day of the 1st month: (great earthquake). 5th year (1628): likewise. **[Honchō Nendaiki]**

**[Emperor Meishō / Kan'ei]** 10th year (1633), 20th day of the 1st month: likewise (great earthquake). In Odawara, Sagami Province, people and horses perished.
