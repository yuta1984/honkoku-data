The above is based on the *Montoku Jitsuroku* (Chronicle of Emperor Montoku).

During the reign of Emperor Seiwa, on the seventeenth day of the sixth month of the fifth year of Jōgan (863 CE), a great earthquake struck the provinces of Etchū and Echigo. Hills became valleys and valleys became hills; water gushed forth, destroying dwellings, and many people were crushed to death. From that time on, tremors occurred every day.

○ On the twelfth day of the tenth month of the sixth year (864 CE), there was a great tremor.

○ On the eighth day of the seventh month of the tenth year (868 CE), the earth shook and walls and houses inside and outside the capital collapsed in many places. On the same day, the province of Harima also suffered a great earthquake, and the government offices and temples throughout the various districts all collapsed. Earthquakes also occurred on the ninth, twelfth, thirteenth, sixteenth, and twentieth days of the seventh month; the tenth, twelfth, fourteenth, sixteenth, and twenty-ninth days of the eighth month; and the seventh day of the ninth month.

○ On the twenty-sixth day of the fifth month of the eleventh year (869 CE), a great earthquake struck the province of Mutsu. A light appeared, making it as dark as daytime shade. People cried out but could not stand up. Houses collapsed and crushed people to death; others were swallowed up as the earth split open. Horses and cattle panicked and trampled one another. Castles, gates, watchtowers, fences, and walls collapsed and toppled in numbers beyond reckoning. At sea, great tsunami waves surged and reached as far as the castle town; even in areas separated from the sea, open fields and roads became a vast ocean. People could neither board boats nor climb mountains, and the number of those who drowned reached approximately one thousand. Stored provisions and crops were almost entirely lost. On the thirteenth day of the tenth month, relief measures were granted by the court, broadly similar to those taken on previous occasions.

○ On the fourteenth day of the fourth month of the fifteenth year (873 CE), there was a great tremor.

○ On the twenty-ninth day of the twelfth month of the sixteenth year (874 CE), at the Hour of the Rooster (around 6 p.m.), there was a great tremor.

During the reign of Emperor Yōzei: on the eleventh day of the third month of the nineteenth year of Jōgan (877 CE), at the fourth quarter of the Hour of the Dragon (around 9 a.m.), there was a great earthquake.

○ On the seventeenth day of the tenth month of the first year of Gangyō (877 CE), there was a great earthquake.

○ On the twenty-ninth day of the ninth month of the second year (878 CE), a great earthquake struck the various provinces of the Kantō region, splitting the ground open. The provinces of Sagami and Musashi were the most severely affected; for five or six days thereafter, the tremors did not cease, and there was not a single public or private dwelling left intact. In some places, the ground sank and caved in, blocking roads; the number of commoners who were crushed to death was beyond counting.

○ On the twenty-second day of the third month of the third year (879 CE), there was a great tremor at the first quarter of the Hour of the Horse (around noon), and again at the fourth quarter of the same hour.

○ On the second day of the fourth month of the fourth year (880 CE), there was a great earthquake.

○ On the night of the first day of the tenth month of the same year, there was an earthquake; on the second day there was another tremor; on the third day there was yet another; and on the fourteenth day there was a great tremor. On that same day, in the province of Izumo, shrines, Buddhist temples, government offices, and private dwellings either collapsed outright or were damaged by tilting, and many people were injured. Until the twenty-second day, tremors of small magnitude continued two or three times during the day and three or four times at night, and even by the twenty-seventh day they had not yet ceased.

○ On the night of the fourth day of the twelfth month of the same year, the earth shook greatly five or six times before subsiding.
