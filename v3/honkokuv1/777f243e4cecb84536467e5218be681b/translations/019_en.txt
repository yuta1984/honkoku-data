On the 25th day of the 4th month of the same year (Hōreki 1, 1751), great earthquake in Takada, Echigo Province. From the hour of the Rooster (around 6 p.m.) until the hour of the Ox (around 2 a.m.), more than thirty shocks occurred; mountains collapsed, dwellings fell, and the number of dead is said to have reached approximately 16,300 people.
○ The 6th year of the same era (1756), last day of the 7th month: Great earthquake in Ōmi Province.
◆ During the reign of Emperor Gosakunomachi, Meiwa 3 (1766), 28th day of the 1st month: In the Tsugaru and Aomori area of Ōshū (Mutsu Province), heavy snowfall, a great earthquake, and a great fire caused many casualties.
○ The 8th year of the same era (1771), 2nd day of the 5th month: Earthquake in Edo. On the 2nd day of the 6th month of the same year: Great earthquake in Edo.
◆ During the reign of Emperor Kōkaku, Tenmei 2 (1782), 14th day of the 7th month, at the hour of the Ox (around 2 a.m.): Earthquake in Edo as before. On the 15th, another great shock struck, collapsing dwellings. Through the following morning, fifteen or sixteen tremors occurred. At this time, Odawara in Sagami Province suffered especially severely—the stone walls of Mt. Hakone and of Odawara Castle crumbled, and many dwellings were damaged.
○ The 3rd year of the same era (1783), 2nd day of the 2nd month, at the hour of the Ox (around 2 a.m.): Great earthquake in Edo.
◆ Kansei 4 (1792), intercalary 2nd month: During this month, a mountain in the Shimabara domain of Hizen Province erupted, and hot water welled up and flowed forth, killing many people. The entire province

◆ Kyōwa 3 (1803), 15th day of the 11th month: Great earthquake in Sado Province. The shaking continued until the 6th month of the following year (Bunka 1, 1804). The above is according to *Jishin-kō*.
◆ Bunka 1 (1804): Earthquake in Dewa Province; the mountain at Kisakata collapsed and the ground sank, damaging a scenic landscape of rare beauty under heaven. (According to *Aru Nikki* ["A Certain Diary"])
The 6th year of the same era (1809), from the 21st to the 24th day of the 2nd month: Rumbling occurred in the mountains of Azumi District, Matsumoto domain, Shinshū (Shinano Province). In the evening of the 24th, over an area more than 500 *ken* (approx. 900 m) from north to south and more than 900 *ken* (approx. 1,600 m) from east to west, the earth cracked and split open, and boulders broke loose and tumbled. Four villages including Nakatani Village, six branch hamlets, twenty-seven households, and both rice paddies and dry fields subsided and collapsed—yet there were no casualties among people or horses.
○ The 7th year of the same era (1810), New Year's Day: Great earthquake in Sado Province. Continued without pause day after day.
○ The 9th year of the same era (1812), 4th day of the 11th month: Great earthquake in Edo and nearby provinces. The area around Kanagawa and Hodogaya was especially severe, with dwellings broken and collapsed.
◆ During the reign of Emperor Ninkō, Bunsei 2 (1819), 12th day of the 6th month: Great earthquake in Kyoto and the areas of Ise and Mino.
○ The 5th year of the same era (1822), from the 16th to the 19th day of the intercalary 1st month: Great earthquake in Ōshū (Mutsu) and Ezo (Hokkaido), more than 150 shocks.
○ The 9th year of the same era (1826), this autumn: Several earthquakes in Edo.
