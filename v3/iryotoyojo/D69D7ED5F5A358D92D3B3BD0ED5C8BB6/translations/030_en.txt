Using colored ink to mark the map: areas completely devastated by flooding were marked in green (indigo), areas half-devastated were marked in blue (indigo), and villages unaffected by flooding were marked in yellow. This map was kept at hand, and the village headmen were ordered to create maps of their respective villages using the same method, coloring them with the same three colors of blue, green, and yellow, and bring them in. When these maps were compared with the official map, the extent of damage could be understood without actually inspecting the flooded areas, making it very efficient and practical to determine tax obligations and arrange relief measures.

When Li Li of the Song dynasty was the magistrate (governor) of Biling, and the people were suffering from famine, he divided the degree of disaster damage into four categories using the characters for Benevolence (仁 jin), Righteousness (義 gi), Propriety (禮 rei), and Wisdom (智 chi). The character 仁 (Benevolence) designated households with assets, businesses, and property. The character 義 (Righteousness) designated middle and lower-class people who had some assets but had lost their income due to disaster damage. The character 禮 (Propriety) designated the lowest class of small farmers and tenant farmers who had only meager livelihoods and could not seek work due to the famine. The character 智 (Wisdom) designated orphans, widows, the poor, the sick, the disabled, and beggars. Those designated 仁 received no relief rice; those designated 義 were helped by being sold rice at reduced prices; those designated 禮 received half their rice as relief and half at reduced prices; and those designated 智 received full relief. In all cases, vouchers (exchange tickets) were distributed and the number of recipients was counted in the usual manner. However, regarding relief rice, notices were posted in advance, and every ten days, the distribution was entrusted to officials. The people praised this method for generations to come.

In the year of Hinoto-U (丁卯), when Poyang suffered a drought,義倉 (community grain reserves) rice was used to set up many distribution points within the castle town every day, selling it at reduced prices to first relieve the people inside and outside the castle walls. The proceeds were then calculated in proportion to the rice price, and allocated once a month according to population count to relieve the people in rural villages.
