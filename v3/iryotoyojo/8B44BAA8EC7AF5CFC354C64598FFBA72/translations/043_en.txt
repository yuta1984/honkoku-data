(Continued from the previous page) The idea that three colors generate five colors, and five colors generate seven colors—just as three musical tones generate five tones, which in turn become seven tones—and the insistence on settling this through precise measurement (ratios) was articulated more than twenty years before Martin's writings arrived in our country by ship. The fact that, without any prior arrangement, minds separated by ten thousand leagues arrived at the same conclusion demonstrates that this is an established theory grounded in empirical measurement, and it must not be spoken of in the same breath as the decayed tales of the Five Elements correspondence system.

That said, the character of the people of the Western barbarian nations (Europe) is such that they are gifted with intelligence, cunning, and sharp-witted shrewdness; their desire for profit and gain knows no bounds. Their craftsmanship is minute and skillful—all of this being innate to the nature of their land and climate. Therefore, even in something like their medicine, they take as their primary duty such things as dissecting the human body to examine in detail the structure of the intestines, stomach, and blood vessels, and analyzing medicinal substances to thoroughly ascertain their properties, taste, and effects. When one hears all of this, it seems to have a certain logic to it; yet their disposition is not gentle and harmonious, and because they do not understand the Great Way of Nature, they are ignorant of the fact that the human body possesses mysterious and unfathomable spiritual responsiveness (kan'nō).
