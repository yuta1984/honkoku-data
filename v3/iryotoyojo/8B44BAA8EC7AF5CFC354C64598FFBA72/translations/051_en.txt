(Interlinear note: As for what was recorded in the *Yōjō Yōryaku* [Essential Summary of Nurturing Life], some of it has been abbreviated here, so one should cross-reference the two texts together in order to arrive at a full understanding.)

"Sympathetic resonance" (*kan'ō* 感応) may be illustrated as follows: it is like the reflection of the moon appearing upon the water. The moon is the "stimulus" (*kan* 感), and the reflection is the "response" (*ō* 応). When the breath enters through the nose, that is the stimulus; when it exits, that is the response. When joy, anger, sorrow, or delight arise and are expressed, that is the stimulus; when they hit the proper measure, that is the response. When a lord shows compassion and benevolence toward those below, that is the stimulus; when those below feel gratitude for his kindness, that is the response. When one feels cold, that is the stimulus; when one puts on clothing, that is the response. In all things, when something bends and contracts, that is the stimulus; when it extends and expands, that is the response. This "sympathetic resonance" is the wondrous working (*myōyō* 妙用) of Heaven and Earth, which naturally push and move against each other. When Confucius said in the *Analects*, "How it flows on — like this! — never ceasing, day or night!" — this, too, is nothing other than

[Left folio]

precisely this same principle. In every single thing and matter, this sympathetic resonance is present. For example, it is like how when qi disperses, wind arises, and when the wind ceases, qi gathers once more. Therefore, Cheng Mingdao (Cheng Hao) declared: "Between Heaven and Earth, there is only this one thing — stimulus and response. What else is there?"

When one thoroughly illuminates this principle, one can attain a complete understanding of the workings of the Great Qi, leaving nothing out.

(Interlinear note: In the *Abhidharmakośa* [*Kusharon* 倶舎論], there is the concept of *taihō* 対法 — which, in the language of our country, may be rendered as *tsuriai* 釣り合い, "balance" or "equilibrium" — and this arises from the very same sympathetic resonance. Everything that exists between Heaven and Earth — above: the sun, moon, stars, and constellations; cold and heat; thunder and lightning; rain, snow, frost, and dew; and below: mountains, rivers, and the great earth; humans, animals, and plants; down to every last thing, however infinitesimally small — all are mutually sustained through this *taihō*. The fortune and misfortune, the auspiciousness and inauspiciousness, the gains and losses, and the illness and suffering of the human body are without exception unable to escape this *taihō*, and this is precisely where the workings of this qi are operative. However, since attempting to expound upon all of this would become excessively unwieldy, the present discussion will not venture that far.)
