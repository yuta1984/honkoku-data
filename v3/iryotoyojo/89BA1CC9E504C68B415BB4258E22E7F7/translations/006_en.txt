**A Record of Gratitude to the Great Divine Lord Tōshō (Tokugawa Ieyasu)**

In the days of ancient turmoil, the suffering of the people was beyond all description. This was because, amid the chaos that spread across the provinces, there were in the western provinces the clans of Mōri and Ōtomo; in the Chūgoku region, the Amago clan; in the Gokkinai, the Miyoshi and Hosokawa; in Kōshū (Kai Province), the Takeda; in Sunshū (Suruga Province), the Imagawa; in Bishū (Owari Province), the Oda; in the northern provinces, the Azai and Asakura; in Sōshū (Sagami Province), the Hōjō; and in Echigo, Kenshin (Uesugi Kenshin)—fierce generals and brave warriors who flaunted their power and competed for dominance, throwing all four classes of people into chaos and turmoil, the extent of which one can only imagine.

And yet, reverently, the Divine Lord Tōshō, with his boundless and impartial great compassion and benevolence, and his righteous will to relieve all beings from suffering, set forth his sacred vow. By so doing, the protecting eyes of the gods and buddhas were bestowed upon him, and through more than one hundred and twenty battles in his lifetime—never once removing his armor—he endured great hardships. As a result, all those who had been called fierce generals and brave warriors came to self-destruction, as it were, and thus this era of great peace was established. Not only Japan, but all the lands beyond its borders, wherever the sun and moon shine, there is no place where his divine virtue does not illuminate. Truly, this realm, protected by the gods and buddhas, has become an age of ten thousand generations of unshakeable peace—there is nothing else to which it can be compared.

To be born into such a blessed age, to receive such immeasurable grace and benevolence, and yet to have no awareness of repaying even one ten-thousandth of that grace—this is to be frighteningly ignorant of one's blessings. If humans are said to be the most spiritually endowed of all living things, then:

> To forget, even for a moment, the grace of Lord Nikkō (Tōshō Daigongen) is to be less than human—less than human—less than human.

**Yōgaku Shokubutsu Nōdoku: Preface**

In food, there are things that act as medicine, which drive away the illnesses of a lifetime; and in food, there are things that act as poison, which plant the seeds of a lifetime's illness. To know that medicine benefits people as medicine, while not knowing that food also benefits people as medicine, is truly ignorant. It is written in ancient medical texts that one should first examine the root of illness and treat it with food; only when this cannot be achieved should one cure it with medicine.

If a person wishes to preserve a long life, the first and foremost priority is to be careful about one's diet. The ancients said: "Plain, simple food brings benefit; rich, delicious food brings harm." Believing that illness enters through the mouth, one must learn to distinguish between what is beneficial (nō) and what is harmful (doku) in one's daily meals. One cannot afford to be ignorant of the nobility of dietary therapy (shokuryō).
