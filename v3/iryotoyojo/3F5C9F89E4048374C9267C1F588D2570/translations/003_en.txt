These [stories] have long served as a stepping-stone to guide children through the teaching of encouraging virtue and chastising evil. Indeed, if one wishes to crush the horns of the devil in one's heart and obtain the precious treasure of loyalty and filial piety, then using the wisdom of the pheasant's cry — that faithful companion [of Momotarō] — as a model, [this tale of] the "Battle of Mochi and Sake," in which [characters] are lured in by their appetite and made to bite, has taken the old device of the grandmothers' washing at the riverbank and recast it. The old man [i.e., the author], under the new imprint of Yamadaya,

has retold and rewritten [the story], raising it up like a cherry tree blooming on withered wood. Should this happen to catch the eye of young readers, and should many copies be purchased, then in good time — swift as a rabbit — the author's achievement will be recognized and the bookseller too will prosper for a lifetime, or so [we] greedily declare.
