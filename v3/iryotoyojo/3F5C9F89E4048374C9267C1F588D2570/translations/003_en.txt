This work has served as a bridge to guide children through the teaching of encouraging good and punishing evil. Indeed, if one wishes to seize the demon lurking in the heart — that horned creature — and obtain the auspicious treasure of loyalty and filial piety, then take as a model the wisdom of the pheasant's cry, which accompanied Momotarō, and exchange it for worldly temptation. From that enticing first bite, we present "The Battle of Mochi and Sake" — a tale that draws readers in. Taking the old theme of the grandmothers washing at the riverbank and reworking it, the old man now presents it as a new publication from Yamada(ya)...

[Left Page]
...newly composed and bound, allowing even a withered tree to blossom and climb like a cherry tree in bloom. Should this catch the eye of young lords and ladies, and should many copies be purchased, the credit would go to the author — swift as a rabbit — and the bookseller too prays, with some greed, to prosper for a lifetime.

　Spring of the Year Kinoe-Tatsu (1844)　　Written playfully by Gesaku-sha Kishō
