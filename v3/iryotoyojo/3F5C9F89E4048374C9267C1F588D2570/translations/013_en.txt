The various rice cakes were gathered by each party upon their trays. "Splendid — we have scattered the enemy into *chirashi-meshi* (scattered rice)!" they all cried out in high spirits, shouting "Kiri-meshi (cut rice)!" — and without delay they came rushing forth. The clan of dumplings (*dango*) and the united forces of the rice-cake shops (*mochiya*): the celebrated establishments, blossoming like flowers of great Edo — Torikai Izumi, Beni-ya, Echigo-ya Suzuki and their rice-cake troops — each vying to be first, hiding themselves packed tightly into folded wooden boxes (*orizume*). Then following them came Ryōgoku Ikuyo-no-suke Amatari, hoisting at the very front the great banner proclaiming "Number One in Edo," setting their formations upon great round trays (*marubōn*), their force numbering roughly fifteen-thousand (■), launching a great bargain-sale offensive.

【Left page】

And so they struck. Next came Kame-ya Mannensai Mochiyoshi; from Setomono-chō, Shiratama-Shiroko-rō Neriyoshi; from Ukiyo-kōji, Dango-no-suke Tsukinuki; from Kurofune-chō, Hanayori Yachiyo; Mochiyoshi of Kinryūzan; Kihachi Dango-yasuri of Kaya-chō; Daifuku Tarō of Mikawa-chō; at Shiba Shinmei, Daidai-mochi Dayū; and Botamochi Saemon of Ōkido — and such were they. Now, among those gathered from various provinces, who was there? From Kawachi, the *dōmyōji* dried-rice cake (*hoshii-mochi*); from Nissaka, Warabi-no-ko Jirō (Young Bracken-starch); from Sayo-no-Nakayama, the brothers Kosodatarō (Child-rearing Tarō) — in sweetness of flavour, they were without peer.
