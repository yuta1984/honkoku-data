Resting at a shop
selling sakura mochi
along the Sumida River

(Poem)
The seedlings sown
in the paddies along the embankment
where cherry blossoms fall —
might they become rice cakes
fragrant with the scent of flowers?

Author's Preface

Saruskue, who followed Momotarō, gained fame through his good service, aided by the millet used for dumplings (dango). Meanwhile, Saruzō, who had deceived a crab, burned his cheek by eating a single morsel of food, and ultimately lost his life over a bowl of rice. In the same spirit, Sarumaru Dayū of the deep mountains, too, becomes good or evil depending on his conduct. How could one not be cautious? And so, the old-fashioned kusazoshi picture books — even the "red books" (akahon) about the red bottoms of monkeys — are read with care and tenderness, page by page...
