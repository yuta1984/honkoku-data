Kajitori (helmsman) Sangoro was summoned and questioned in detail. He recounted the following: that in Etorofu (Iturup Island), all their possessions had been confiscated, leaving them virtually naked with nothing remaining; that they had not been given food; that they had repeatedly been beaten; and that the whereabouts of Nagaemon of Yamakawa were unknown. He stated each of these matters one by one. The content of this account was conveyed from Asazaemon to Shirozaemon. Their voices could be heard through the partition. Upon hearing this, Asaemon remarked, "In such circumstances, this is truly a most pitiful situation," and his voice was heard in the room. Shirozaemon came out and said, "Since it is a lengthy matter, you may not remember it well, so please write it down and submit it." Accordingly, it was written out in detail in mixed kana script, and when the ship's captain submitted it, he was immediately called in, as Asaemon would hear the matter directly. The captain held nothing back in his account. Asaemon then, with evident displeasure, remarked: "Etorofu is a remote island, and our jurisdiction does not fully extend there—while it differs from areas connected to Matsumae by land, such conduct should not be permitted." Observing Asaemon's expression and demeanor, Kajitori Sangoro said:

【Left folio】

"No, it was not quite like that. Even on the island, we received a degree of care. As for the ship, it was discarded along with its cargo out at sea. As for Nagaemon of Yamakawa, I understand that he passed away due to illness." Upon hearing this, Asaemon replied, "Indeed, that would be the case." After this, at Okabe Shirozaemon's office, documents addressed to them were handed over. Furthermore, an official draft was provided for them to write and submit their own written statement; following the draft, they wrote it out, affixed their thumbprints, and handed it over. Subsequently, it was stated: "We should arrange for you to be sent to Tsugaru, but unfortunately no ship is presently available. Fortunately, there is a messenger vessel—would you be willing to travel on that?" They replied, "Please send us on the available ship." They were then placed on two ships, and the entire group received ten ryō in gold coins (koban) as travel money, along with seven tan of cotton cloth for use in making clothing. They immediately asked their innkeeper to have it sewn. The innkeeper's ship captain also said, "You will surely need these on board," and gave each person a hand towel for rewrapping belongings. In addition, the innkeeper provided a small amount of tobacco. And so, from here, they were to return directly to their home domain—
