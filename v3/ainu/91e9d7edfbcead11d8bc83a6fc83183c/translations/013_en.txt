　Fire is called "abe"; water is called "tai."
　Firewood is "tsukune"; not knowing something is "iramishikare."
　Food is "amamo"; fermented rice malt (kōji) is "kamitachi."
　(The following terms were) brought over from Matsumae:
　Morohaku (refined sake) is called "abura-sake"; clothing is called "kimono."
　Japanese people are called "mata," (marginal note: also "tokuri" or "shamo"); women are called "menoko."
　Expressing gratitude is "yaiyaikeri"; last month is "oshiyuku tsuki."
　An elder or a person of high standing is "ottsutena."
　Those of lower standing are "ottsukaihō." Above "ottsutena" and "ottsukaihō,"

When women give birth, they go to the mountains to deliver, then immediately wrap the infant in dog skin, tie the front and back with rope to form a bundle like a tsuto (a straw-wrapped package), and carry it hanging as they walk — yet the baby does not cry. The newborn has a line of black hair running along its back. The woman who has given birth immediately goes to collect firewood, works, and then returns home. Children's clothing is mostly made of dog skin. Even on ships traveling from the islands toward Matsumae, there are women who give birth; they deliver with remarkable ease and leave the infant bundled in the manner described above.
