They were harmed because they did not hand over [the cargo] freely to those people. Now, upon coming ashore, the man called Chōuemon of Yamakawa-ya was nowhere to be seen, so we inquired in all directions as to where he might have gone, and searched within the main vessel as well, but could not find him. Thinking he might have fled into the mountains, six or seven men split up and followed his footprints through the snow to search for him; two local people also accompanied them as guides, and they searched here and there, but his whereabouts remained unknown. Since he carried no bladed weapon, there was no way for him to take his own life, and in his overwhelming terror and helplessness, we think he may have hanged himself. Or perhaps he leapt into the sea and drowned — in any case, we do not know. It does not seem likely that the local people killed him. The fourteen men

**[Left folio]**
were left without food and without shelter, and could only grieve. All the goods on the main vessel were divided up and taken by the local people. There were more than two hundred *koku* of rice on board. Three hundred *monme* of silver that the ship's captain kept for immediate expenses was thrown into the sea, saying it was an offering to the Dragon Palace. In addition, there was more than ten *kanme* of silver kept as emergency funds. The ship was smashed apart and its metal fittings were taken; the small boat was burned and its metal fittings were taken as well. Since it had been decided that anyone who did not comply with these acts, or showed even the slightest resistance, would be killed outright, no one offered any resistance whatsoever. Everything worn on their bodies, down to their loincloths, was stripped away. It was clear that to resist meant death, as half-bows and spears were deployed in great numbers, with men lying in wait in the grasslands nearby.
