It was because we had not willingly handed over [the goods] to them that they became enraged. Now, when we came ashore, a man named Chōuemon from Yamakawa was nowhere to be seen, so we searched everywhere asking where he might have gone, and even searched inside the main vessel, but he was not to be found. Thinking he might have fled into the mountains, six or seven men split up and followed footprints in the snow to search for him. Two of the local people also joined as guides, and they searched here and there, but his whereabouts remained unknown. Since he carried no blade, there was no way he could have committed suicide; it is thought that, overcome with unbearable terror and having no recourse, he may have hanged himself. Or perhaps he leaped into the sea and drowned—in any case, his fate is unknown. It does not seem likely that the local people killed him. The fourteen men

**[Left folio]**
were without food, without shelter, and could only lament their situation. All the goods aboard the main vessel were divided up and taken by the local people. There were over two hundred *koku* of rice on board. The three hundred *monme* of silver that the ship's captain had kept for immediate expenses was thrown into the sea, [the locals] saying it was an offering to Ryūgū [the Dragon Palace]. Additionally, there was over ten *kanme* of reserve silver. The ship itself was smashed to pieces, and the metal fittings were taken; the small boat was burned and its metal fittings taken as well. Since it had been decided that anyone who did not comply with all of this, or who showed even the slightest resistance, would be killed outright, we did not defy them in the least. Everything worn on our bodies, down to our loincloths, was stripped from us. It was plain that we would be killed if we resisted, as a large number of people with half-bows and spears lay concealed in the grasslands around the area.
