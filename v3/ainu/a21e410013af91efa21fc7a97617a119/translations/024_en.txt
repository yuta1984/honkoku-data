**[Right folio]**
This region is connected by land from Matsumae. However, as it is a difficult terrain with inconvenient travel, trade and commerce are conducted by boat. Nevertheless, it is said that if a road were built, even this difficult terrain could become easier to pass through than Hakone, and there are those who privately harbor such ambitions. Keikoku, Osarubutsu, Kayamanbe, Ususan, Kiitatsufou, Nochisafu — how many hundreds of ri these places stretch is unknown, and the distances are impossible to estimate. There are said to be considerable gold, silver, copper, iron, and tin mines there. Japanese rice is highly valued. Fields frequented by bears exist in the direction of the interior (Oku) Ezo, but in the direction of outer (Kuchi) Ezo, within a range of nine hundred ri, travel and movement are relatively free and easy.

**[Left folio]**
It is rumored that textiles, even brocades (nishiki), are woven there, but in reality they are said to be goods brought over from North Korea (Kita Kōrai — referring to Manchuria/the Maritime Province region). North Korea, from the direction of inner Ezo, is said to be closer than Japan. Considerable trade has been commissioned by the lord of Matsumae, which is said to benefit his domain's finances. Regarding the gold, silver, and copper mines, it is certain that considerable output could be expected if they were developed — this has been handed down from old times and knowledgeable people say the same — however, out of deference to the Shogunate (the honorable public authority), no mining has been carried out. Even so, the local people extract small amounts of alluvial gold as their livelihood, and in inner Ezo especially, it seems a considerable amount is being mined, as gold thread is woven into their textiles. In the outer Ezo region as well, there are places where vast flatlands continue for hundreds of ri; if converted to farmland, it could yield hundreds of thousands of koku, but this too (remains undeveloped for similar reasons).
