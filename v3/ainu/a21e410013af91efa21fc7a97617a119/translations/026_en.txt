There were also vast quantities of whale oil; whales were sent up to the lord from Ezo. Toward the end of September, great numbers of whales would gather. After catching the whales, they would be cut up, placed on stones, and fires lit beneath them to extract the oil. There were also various large trees. There was a tree about ten ken (approximately 18 meters) in length, resembling bamboo. When rolled up tightly and then released, it would spring back perfectly straight, just as it had been before. This was called the "shiuri tree." It also appears to have been referred to as the "shibari tree."

One item: Not only in our case, but throughout the year, people from ships that had drifted ashore would come and go, conducting trade and returning home. Not only in Matsumae, but in the Tsugaru and Nanbu areas as well, among the Ezo people,

One item: On the twenty-third day, we were summoned by what appeared to be the town magistrate's office. Led by Handayū, we appeared before a man called Okabe Shirōemon, at a place resembling a kitchen. There, the captain of the vessel we had encountered, Chōemon, and the helmsman Sangōrō also appeared. We were questioned in detail, and so we explained everything one by one: how on Etorofu Island, everything had been seized from us in great detail, leaving us completely stripped bare with nothing remaining; how we had been given no food; the circumstances of being progressively beaten; and how Nagaemon of the Yamakawa [vessel] had gone missing.
