Those who had found food would pass word along — "Go to such-and-such a place; now is a good time" — and in this way they barely managed to sustain their lives. At first, while among the Ainu, even when the broth and washing-water from boiled fish got into their beards and on their bodies, they did not wash it off, and the stench was so terrible that every breath felt as though it might ruin their stomachs. But as they gradually grew accustomed to it, it ceased to bother them much at all. Indeed, the boiled fish they received to eat tasted remarkably flavorful to them. As they grew accustomed to living among (the Ainu), the latter began asking "What is this called? What is this called?" — thinking it must be a matter of names, the castaways answered, "I am Naniemon, and this one is called Nanibee," whereupon thereafter the Ainu

called each of them by their respective names.

One: When the Ainu agreed to hear out the request that they be sent back to Matsumae, the castaways asked through gestures and expression, "About when will you send us?" The Ainu drew the shape of a full moon and a half-moon, indicating the approximate month and day, and then drew the shape of the island in the hearth ashes, and blew on the palm of their hand with their mouth — "fuu" — conveying the manner in which they would travel by wind.
