Throughout their lives, they never settle in a fixed place; the dwellings they maintain along beaches stretching dozens of *ri* are all temporary huts, and they move to wherever hunting and gathering yields are plentiful, building new temporary huts to live in. Their entire lives are spent in this manner. This is because they do not know the productivity of agriculture, and live instead on the abundant yields of hunting and fishing.

　　　　　On Fishing and Hunting

One entry: In the sixth year of Tenmei, the year of Hinoe-Uma (1786), on official duty to survey the borders of Ezo, the vessel *Jintsū-maru* — owned by a man named Tomaya Kyūbei of Edo Honchō, with Taihei serving as the offshore ship's captain (*okifunagashira*) — arrived at Matsumae under strict orders, and from there crossed the sea to Nishihetsu in eastern Ezo. Someone well-acquainted with the sea routes along the eastern coast of the Ezo lands was

　　selected by the Matsumae domain, and a man named Jirōbei from Karatsunaichō in Matsumae, serving as a route pilot (*michisaki-yaku*), joined the crew. They arrived at the mouth of the large river called Nishihetsu. Each year, as the autumnal equinox approaches, salmon migrate upstream in great numbers toward the headwaters of this Nishihetsu River. As I was the official in charge, I gave instructions to the men, and on the seventeenth day of the eighth month of that year, around the hour of the seventh *koku* at midday (approximately 2:00 p.m.), the net they hauled in caught some twenty or thirty salmon. Then on the following morning of the eighteenth, at the sixth hour (*roku-ji*, approximately 6:00 a.m.), the net they hauled in yielded a large catch; after discarding the poor specimens and selecting the good ones, there were ninety-seven bundles. One bundle consists of twenty fish, making a total of one thousand nine hundred and forty fish. "Discarding the poor specimens" refers to those that are exceptionally small or exceptionally large; medium-sized, uniformly-sized fish are considered the best quality.
