**Wansei (碗青 / Azurite-type Pigment)**: Brought from Shemen Island, it is a stone of rare quality. After careful appraisal and deliberation among connoisseurs, it was given the name *Butsutōkō* ("Buddha-head incense/pigment"). It is a pigment used for painting patterns on earthenware before firing in the kiln.

**Inkstone (Suzuri-ishi)**: Found throughout the area known as "Shiroi Beach" (White Beach) in Ishizaki Village, beyond Hakodate. There are also deposits along the riverbed of the Surui River on the shaded side of the mountain. I had some carved by craftsmen in Edo and keep pieces in my possession. Transportation to Japan proper would be easy.

**Stalactites (Shōnyūseki)**: Said to be found in a cave at the cape of Ōta Mountain in Western Ezo, where a stone Jizō statue is enshrined.

**Coal**: Found in Hetsushafu Village, within the Kusuri trading post territory.

**Sea Pine (Miru / *Codium* seaweed)**: Produced along the coastline of Matsumae from all directions. Its color is red, resembling the leaves of the Japanese cypress (*hinoki*). Near Matsumae, it is used as a decorative display in the *tokonoma* (alcove).

**[Left Page]**

**Shiogori (汐凝)**: Commonly called "Ezo Coral." It resembles branching coral, is crimson in color, and is an extremely beautiful object. It is also used as a decorative display in the *tokonoma*.

**Alum (Myōban)**: Found in great abundance at Esashi. As the method of refining it is not yet known, the local people (*dojin*) leave it discarded and unused.

**Hot Springs (Onsen)**: Shiriuke, Yunokawa, Otobe, Miichi, Hiratanai, Yunotai, Ōsawa, Katsukushi, Shikabe, Tome-no-yu, Esashi, Yama-no-yu, Shibetsu, Seseki — and others throughout the various islands.

**Black-Flowered Lily**: Found in various places in the interior beyond the Akkeshi area.

**White-Flowered Spring Chrysanthemum (Shungiku)**: This spring chrysanthemum is found in various locations throughout Eastern Ezo.

**Autumn Bush Clover (Aki-hagi)**: Found in the vicinity of Monashibe Village and Yamokishinai Village. Some specimens have a trunk circumference exceeding four *sun* (approximately 12 cm).
