**Illustration of Bow (Ku), Arrow (Ai), and Quiver (Ikaetsupu)**

The arrowheads are made from the base of the kumazasa bamboo grass. Monkshood (aconite/Aconitum, *Uzu*) is pounded into a paste and applied as shown in the illustration. When shot into the flesh of fish or animals, they fall dead immediately.

The *Honzō* (Materia Medica) entry on monkshood states that the Tartars (Dattan) use poisoned arrows. On reflection, this technique may have been transmitted from Karafuto (Sakhalin) Island.

- **Osoru** (bow)
- Bowstring: *Tekinoshike* / *Masumu*
- **Shyapa** (also called *Penashi*) (part of the bow)
- **Runomu** (arrow)
- **Makani** — made from deer antler
- **Kanshi** (arrowhead)
- Wrapped with cherry bark (*Karinba*)

The quiver is referred to as a treasured possession of women.

*Sōchūshō* states:
"How dreadful — the poisoned arrows made by the Ezo of Chishima (the Kuril Islands), which have gaps [between them]."

*Kengen* states: "Poisoned arrows" refers to the practice of the Ezo of the far interior, who coat the quill of a bird's feather with the poison called *Bushi* (aconite/monkshood) and are said to aim for gaps in armor. What is called a *Bushi-ya* (aconite arrow) is precisely this.

The Ainu name [for the bow wood] is *Raruma*; in the vicinity of Futahakodate, it is called *Okono-ki*. It is the material used for making bows. In Hida Province, it is called the *Ichii* (Japanese yew). It is also fashioned into *shaku* (ceremonial baton). Another name is *Araraki*.
