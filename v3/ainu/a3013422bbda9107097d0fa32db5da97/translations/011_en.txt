**Illustration of Chashi**

"Chashi" is the name for a fortification. The chieftains of each locality all construct these. Upon examining the etymology of the name "Chashi," it would appear to be a corrupted form of "Sashi." In the *Nihon Shoki* (Chronicles of Japan), under the section on Empress Okinaga-tarashi-hime (Empress Jingū), it is written "the Sōra fortress of Silla (Shiragi)." Furthermore, in other passages, fortresses such as "Soryu-jō (Sashi)" and "Hi-jō (Sashi)" — all fortifications of Silla and Koma (Goryeo) — are referred to as "Sashi." Additionally, the term "Kanasashi-no-miya" (Palace of Kanasashi) would appear to be a name expressing the solidity and strength of an imperial palace.

In ancient times, the reading of "shiro" (castle/fortress) as a Japanese word likely derived from the meaning of planting wooden stakes to repel soldiers.
