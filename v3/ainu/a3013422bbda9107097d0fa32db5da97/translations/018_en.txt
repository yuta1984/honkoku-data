On the Ainu method of catching eagles.

A small hut is built beside the rushing waters of a deep mountain valley. Under cover of night, one enters the hut and places a fish tied up inside, as shown in the illustration. When the eagle comes to seize the leaping fish, one uses a hooked pole of approximately five shaku (about 150 cm) in length to catch the eagle by its talons and capture it. Unless one conceals oneself in complete silence, it is very difficult to make the catch.

A waka poem by Gon-no-Sōjō Kōchō:

*Michinoku no / ezo ga Chishima no / washi no hane ni / myō naru nori no / moji mo arikeri*

"Even on the feathers of the eagles of the islands of Ezo in Michinoku, the wondrous characters of the Buddhist Law are to be found."
