【On the Method of Catching Eagles】

For the Ezo (Ainu) people to catch eagles, they build a small hut beside a torrent in a deep mountain valley, and as shown in the illustration, they enter it during the night. They watch for a fish they have tied (as bait) to splash about, and when an eagle comes down to seize it, they use a pole of about five shaku (approximately 150 cm) with a hook attached to its tip to snag the eagle by its talons and capture it. Unless one remains completely still and silent, it is very difficult to catch one.

【Waka Poem】

By Gon no Sōjō Kinchō (a Buddhist ecclesiastical official):

On the wings of eagles from the Ezo lands and Chishima (Kuril Islands) of far Michinoku,
one can discern the wondrous written characters of the Buddhist Law.
