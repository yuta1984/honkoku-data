**[Scholarly Discussion on Ottosei (Fur Seal / Ossified Navel, 膃肭臍)]**

[Xiao (斆) states:] There are many counterfeits of *ottosei* (膃肭臍). In the sea there is a creature called the "Water-Bird Dragon" (水鳥竜), and fishermen (sea-people) take its kidneys (testes) and sell them as *ottosei*, but this substance is entirely different from the genuine article. The genuine specimen comes as a pair: two rounded pieces wrapped in thin skin, upon which there is naturally yellow, flesh-colored hair, with two orifices and three stems. When stored in a vessel, it remains moist year after year as if freshly obtained. Alternatively, if it is placed upon the head of a sleeping person and that person suddenly startles and leaps up as if in a frenzy, then it is genuine.

[Shizhen (Li Shizhen, 李時珍) states:] Upon investigation, the *Tang Shu* (唐書, History of the Tang Dynasty) records that "the *kottsu* beast (骨𧰹獣) is found in Liaoxi, Yingzhou, and the land of Jiegu." The *Yitong Zhi* (一統志, Comprehensive Gazetteer) records: "Ottosei (膃肭臍) is produced in Jurchen (女直) and the land of Srivijaya (三仏斉国). The animal resembles a fox, its legs are as high as an arrow, and it runs as if flying. Its kidneys are soaked in clear oil and called *ottosei*." From this, the claim that it resembles a fox is not without basis. In general, the comparisons to a fox or deer refer to the color of its fur; the comparison to a dog refers to the shape of its feet; and the comparison to a fish refers to the shape of its tail. Though the part used in medicine is the external kidney (testes), it is called "navel" (*sei*/臍) because the navel is taken along with it.

Furthermore, the *Yiwu Zhi* (異物志, Record of Strange Things) states: "The *da* (豽) beast is found in Korea (朝鮮), resembles a badger (*tanuki*/貍), is dark bluish-black in color, lacks both front legs, and is adept at catching mice." Guo Pu (郭璞) states: "During the Jin dynasty, in Zhaofu Yi County, one beast was captured that resembled a dog with the markings of a leopard (*bào*/豿), had horns, and had two legs." Based on this, there appear to be two varieties of *da* — aquatic and terrestrial — and what Zang Qi (蔵器, Chen Zangqi) described as "resembling a fox with a long tail" may well belong to this category.
