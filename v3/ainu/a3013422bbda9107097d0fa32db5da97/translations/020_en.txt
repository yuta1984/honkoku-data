## A Study on Onkotsusei (膃肭臍, Medicinal Seal Genitalia)

**[Kou (斆) states:]** There are many counterfeits of *onkotsusei*. In the sea there is a creature called "suichōryū" (water-bird dragon), and sea people (fishermen/maritime folk) take its kidneys (testes) and pass them off as *onkotsusei*. However, these are entirely different from the genuine article. The genuine item comes in a pair, wrapped in two layers of thin skin in a rounded form; on the surface of the skin there are naturally flesh, yellow fur, two openings, and three stems. When stored in a vessel, it remains warm and moist year after year, as if new. Alternatively, if placed upon the head of a sleeping person, that person will suddenly be startled and jump up, moving as if in a frenzy — this is the proof of authenticity.

**[Shizhen (Li Shizhen) states:]** Examining the *Tang Shu* (History of the Tang Dynasty), it states: "The *kotsuten* beast comes from Liaoxi, Yingzhou, and the land of Jiegǔ." The *Yitongzhi* (Comprehensive Gazetteer) states: "Onkotsusei comes from Jurchen territory and the kingdom of Srivijaya (Sanfoji); the beast resembles a fox, its legs are as tall as an arrow, and it runs as if flying. Its kidneys are taken and soaked in clear oil, and this is called *onkotsusei*." Considering this, the claim that it resembles a fox is not entirely without basis. Indeed, its resemblance to a fox or deer refers to its fur color; its resemblance to a dog refers to the shape of its feet; and its resemblance to a fish refers to the shape of its tail. Although the part used medicinally is the external kidneys (testes), it is called "*sei*" (navel/umbilicus) because it is removed together with the navel.

Furthermore, the *Izumonshi* (*Yiwuzhi*, Records of Strange Things) states: "The *datsu* beast comes from Korea; it resembles a carp, is dark bluish-black in color, lacks both front legs, and is skilled at catching mice." Guo Pu states: "In the Jin dynasty, in Fuyi County of Zhaosa, one beast was captured that resembled a dog with spotted markings, had horns, and had two legs." Based on all this, it appears that the *datsu* has two varieties — aquatic and terrestrial — and what Zangqi (Chen Zangqi) described as "resembling a fox with a long tail" likely belongs to this category.
