**Figure of Ikema**

This is a climbing vine plant. It belongs to the same family as *ramo* (gagaimo, *Metaplexis japonica*, a type of swallowwort), though it is a distinct species; the flowers, fruit, leaves, and vines closely resemble those of *ramo*. The fruit is three-ridged in shape and forms clusters of three to four fruits. It has less silky floss than *ramo*. The root is as depicted in the illustration.

When roasted and eaten, it has a sweet and delicious flavor. When dried and ground into powder and applied to sword wounds (金瘡, *kinsō*), it has a remarkably effective ability to stop bleeding. According to local tradition passed down in the Matsumae region, the plant has been used effectively for treating horses' ailments, and it is said that this is the origin of its name, "Ikema" (生馬, literally "living horse").
