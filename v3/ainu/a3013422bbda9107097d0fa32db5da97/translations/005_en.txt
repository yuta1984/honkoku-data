"Inaho" refers to the central element of the nusa (ritual offerings), which is presented to the gods. Since there are no cloth or paper in Ezo (Hokkaido), wood is shaved into curling shavings (kezurikake) to serve as a substitute for ritual offerings. Perhaps this was also the practice in ancient Japan. Even today, the custom of decorating with "kezuri-hana" (shaved flower ornaments) on the fifteenth day of the New Year may be a remnant of this tradition.

　Upon reflection, "Inaho" likely derives from the Japanese word for "rice ear" (稲穂, inaho). The term "o-hatsuhо̄" (first fruits offering) carries the same meaning. There is a tree called "Inahoni" which is used to make these in some areas, while willow is also used in others. There are various methods of production, and during funeral rites, they are made using wood placed in an inverted (upside-down) manner.
