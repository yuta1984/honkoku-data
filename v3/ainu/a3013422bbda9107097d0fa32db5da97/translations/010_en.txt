"Ayotsupe" is the name of Ainu armor. Upon reflection, this likely means "a dangerous instrument" in its reading. It is made by combining wood and hide. As for helmets, no Ainu-made examples have been seen; Japanese-made ones are used instead. It is said that there are proper protocols during warfare, and their forces are called "Tomi," which is likely an abbreviation of the reading of the word meaning "to challenge" (idamu).

Item: The chieftain Shirimekishyu of Tōbetsu in the northern Ezo lands possesses more than ten suits of armor. Among them is one suit of foreign-style armor, which upon inspection appears to be a hi-odoshi (red-laced) type. Its construction is of an ancient style and of incomparable fine workmanship. In recent years, the painter Haruta Seihō showed me a copy — dated to the fifth year of Shōō (1292) — of a diagram of a contemporary kakekake-style armor (hanging armor), saying: "This is a diagram of kakekake armor recorded in the Engi Hyōgorō records, and it is something that can no longer be found today." Although the aforementioned unusual armor has sustained much damage due to the passage of years, it is indeed an ancient kakekake-style armor. It appears that Ainu armor was made by the Ainu people, transmitting the form of the kakekake armor.
