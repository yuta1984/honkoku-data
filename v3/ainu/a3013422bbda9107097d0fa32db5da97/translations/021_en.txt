## Sea Otter Hunting in Ezo (Hokkaido)

In the deep blue sea off Oshamambe in Ezo (Hokkaido), fur seals (*onkotsu-jū*) appear. From around the tenth month of winter through the third and fourth months of spring, the Ainu people are directed to hunt them. Those who are skilled at this fishing are called "*Teha Ezo*."

As the season approaches, the hunters purify themselves and their boats, prepare *inau* (wooden prayer sticks) and sacred sake, and perform rituals to venerate the gods of the sea and of boats.

In the local Ainu language, the male fur seal is called "*Uneo*" and the female "*Homatsup*." They measure over three to four *shaku* in size. There is also another variety called "*Ōkotsup*," ranging from about five *shaku* to six or seven *shaku* in length. Both varieties have small tusks, with the upper teeth being lateral teeth. They are also found in the western region of Ezo at Okushiri, though those specimens have something resembling a few scales on their tails. The hunters wait for a calm day with gentle waves before setting out. After the hunters have departed, it is strictly forbidden to make any noise in the household; the women remaining at home must even eat their meals in silence. It is believed that if they do not observe this prohibition, the *Uneo* will be startled and wake up.

When the hunters return with their catch, they bring it in through the window. Even the fishing equipment must be taken in and out through the window in the same manner — for it is said that failure to observe this custom will result in an empty catch.
