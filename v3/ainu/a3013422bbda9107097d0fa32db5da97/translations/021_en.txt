## Seal Hunting in Ezo (Hokkaido)

In the blue sea off Oshamambe (長万部) on Ezo Island, fur seals (*onkotsu-jū*) are found. From the tenth month of winter through spring, around the third and fourth months, the Ainu people are directed to capture them. Those skilled in this fishing are called "*Teha Ezo*."

When that season arrives, various ritual purifications are observed: boats are cleansed, and *inau* (木幣, wooden ritual shavings) and sacred sake are prepared to worship the god of the sea and the god of boats.

In the local (Ainu) language, the male seal is called "*Unewo*" and the female "*Homatsup*." They measure over three to four shaku in size. There is also another variety called "*Wonetsup*," measuring from about five shaku up to six or seven shaku. Both varieties have tusks, with the upper teeth being branched. Their appearance as they float on the waves is as shown in the illustration.

Those found in the western region of Wokoshiri (Okushiri Island) have slightly [remarkable/spiritual?] tail fins as well. Hunters wait for calm, peaceful days when the sea is quiet before setting out. After the hunters depart, all noise is strictly forbidden; even the women of the household must eat their meals in silence, for it is said that if they do not, the "*Unewo*" will be startled and wake up. When the hunters return with their catch, everything — the catch itself as well as all fishing equipment — must be brought in and out through the window. It is said that if this is not done, the hunt will be unsuccessful.
