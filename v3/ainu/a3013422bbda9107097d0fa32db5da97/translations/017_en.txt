This concerns the salmon (*shihe/shake*). They are found in particularly great numbers in the interior regions. From early autumn, they ascend the rivers, and upon reaching the riverbanks, the female fish sweep the riverbed with their tails. When they release their eggs, the male fish coat them with milt in such a way that the roe becomes viscous and adheres to the riverbed, preventing it from being carried away by the current. There is no river, no matter how fierce the rapids, that the salmon do not ascend. Consequently, in some locations, such enormous numbers of fish migrate upstream that flooding can occur. The following spring, when the snow melts, the fish become fry and travel with the waters out to the open ocean. It is said that fish born in a particular river will never ascend any other river.
