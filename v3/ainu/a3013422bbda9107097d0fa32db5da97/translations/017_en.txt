*Shiihe* (salmon) refers to the salmon fish. They are found in particularly great numbers in the interior regions. From early autumn, they ascend the rivers, and upon reaching the riverbanks, the river water becomes turbid. The female fish sweeps the riverbed with her tail and releases her eggs, whereupon the male fish deposits his milt over them; the eggs, being viscous in nature, adhere to the riverbed and are not carried away by the current. No matter how fierce the rapids, there is no river that the fish cannot ascend. For this reason, in some locations, such enormous numbers of fish ascend the rivers that flooding can occur. Come the following spring, together with the snowmelt waters, the eggs rapidly develop into fry and make their way down to the open sea. It is said that fish born in a particular river will never ascend any other river.
