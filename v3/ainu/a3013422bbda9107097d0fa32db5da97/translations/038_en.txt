**[Quotation from the *Nihon Shoki*: The Prohibition of Burial Sacrifice and the Origin of Haniwa]**

The *Nihon Shoki* states: In the twenty-eighth year of Emperor Suinin's reign, in the winter, on the day of Kōgo in the tenth month (which began on Hinoe-Tora), the Emperor's younger brother on his mother's side, Prince Yamato-hiko, passed away. In the eleventh month, on the day of Hinoto-Tori (which began on Hinoe-Saru), Prince Yamato-hiko was buried at Musashi no Tsukisaka. On this occasion, those who had served in close attendance to the prince were gathered together, and all were buried alive around the funeral mound. They did not die for several days, and day and night they wept and groaned. When they finally perished and began to rot and putrefy, dogs and crows gathered to feed upon them. The Emperor, hearing the sound of their weeping and groaning, felt grief in his heart, and issued a decree to his assembled ministers: "To force those whom one loves in life to follow in death — this is a most grievous thing. Though it be an ancient custom, it is by no means a good one. From this time forward, let this matter be deliberated upon and the practice of burial sacrifice brought to an end."

In the same thirty-second year, in the autumn, on the day of Tsuchinoto-U in the seventh month (which began on Kinoe-Inu), the Empress Hibasu-hime no Mikoto (also called Hibasu-ne no Mikoto in one account) passed away. As the day of the burial approached, the Emperor issued a decree to his assembled ministers: "The way of burial sacrifice — I previously commanded it knowing full well it was wrong. What shall we do for this funeral?" At this point, Nomi no Sukune stepped forward and said: "To bury living people in the funeral mound of a royal lord is truly not a good thing. How can it be handed down to posterity? I beg leave to deliberate upon this matter and submit a proposal." He then sent messengers to summon one hundred Haji-be (clay workers) from the province of Izumo, and personally led the Haji-be in taking clay to fashion figures of people, horses, and various other objects, which he then presented to the Emperor, saying: "From this day forward, let these clay objects be substituted for living people, and let them be set up at funeral mounds as the law for future generations." The Emperor was greatly pleased and decreed to Nomi no Sukune: "Your proposal truly accords with Our heart." The clay objects were first erected at the tomb of Empress Hibasu-hime no Mikoto, and the Emperor declared: "Let these clay objects henceforth be called *haniwa* (埴輪; clay rings/cylinders). They are also called *tatemono* (立物; standing objects)." He further issued an order: "Henceforth, these clay objects shall always be erected at funeral mounds, and people shall not be harmed." The Emperor rewarded Nomi no Sukune handsomely for his achievement, and also granted him lands for ironworking/smithing, appointing him to the office of Haji-be. Thus his original family name was changed, and he is regarded as the founding ancestor of the Haji-be no Muraji clan.
