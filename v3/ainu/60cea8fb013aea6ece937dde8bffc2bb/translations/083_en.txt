## (Right folio — main text)

…harboring this underlying intention, they set sail from Kunashiri Island and pushed on as far as a place called "Chikushui," within the trading post of Akkeshi, among the islands where Matsumae held sway. From Kunashiri Island to this place is approximately forty *ri*. It is said that they surveyed and corrected the distances, bearings, and maps along this route as they went. As charts showing the bearings, distances, and geography of Japan and its dependent islands had already been transmitted to Russia separately, they presumably had prior knowledge of the area, and this surveying was likely no more than a matter of cross-referencing and verification.

In any event, they anchored at this Chikushui and waited for the Japanese officials to come across. Here, in accordance with the promise made the previous year, three Matsumae retainers — Asari Kōbē, Matsui Mōbē, and Niida Daihachi — crossed to Chikushui and, the two parties going ashore on both sides, proceeded to a

## (Left folio — main text)

meeting. When the audience came to an end, the Russians, breathlessly awaiting word of whether permission for trade had been granted, watched with bated breath and changing expressions. At that point, the three officials delivered their official order (conveying the Shogunate's directive). Their formal statement read as follows:

"As for the place of trade with foreign countries, in Japan it is limited to a single location known as Nagasaki; all other locations are strictly prohibited under national law. No matter how earnestly you may desire it, the matter cannot be accommodated. We therefore inform you that henceforth any voyages here for such purposes are to be considered unnecessary."

As a provision for the return journey of those who had traveled so great a distance, fifteen bales of rice (each containing slightly more than four *to*), along with some sake, tobacco, and pipes, were presented to them.

"Should you strongly desire trade, you are directed to come to Nagasaki to conduct it."
