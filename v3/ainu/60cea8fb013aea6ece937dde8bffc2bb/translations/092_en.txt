The ship's captain was Tomoemon. On the seventh day of the second month of Hōreki 6, the year of Hinoe-Ne (1756), the ship departed from the port of Uraga inIzu Province, encountered a violent storm, and on the seventeenth day of the fifth month of the same year drifted ashore at this place of "Moyoro." This was prior to the sixth year of Tenmei, the year of Hinoe-Uma (1786). Since no Japanese people had crossed to this island after that incident, there were many Ainu who had never yet seen a Japanese person. When I (Mogami Tokunai) passed through this place in the fifth month of Tenmei Hinoe-Uma year, it was the first time Japanese people had come and gone through here, and so word was passed from one neighboring village to the next. A great crowd of natives gathered, and they all came out to the beach, waiting eagerly for me to pass. Even the women and children came out to the sandy shore to greet me, bowing reverently and lining up respectfully.

Furthermore, from around this area a type of kelp called "Share" is harvested.

[Left folio]

Like young shoots, its leaves are thin, cut in various shapes resembling the painted tail feathers of a phoenix, and also similar to the leaves of a banana plant (bashō). The width of the leaves ranges from about one shaku to two shaku. Running along the center of each leaf is a vein resembling a stem, which is like a thin piece of bamboo, hollow inside, yet fibrous. The leaves, when short, measure two to three shaku, and the longer ones can reach approximately twenty ken in length. It is truly the finest variety of kelp. Proceeding from there northward along the rocky coastline, after rowing approximately twenty-odd ri, there is a tall mountain called "Asanohori." In this vicinity, there is a tree called "Kuna." It resembles the tree known in Japan as "umimatu" (sea pine), and has five leaves.
