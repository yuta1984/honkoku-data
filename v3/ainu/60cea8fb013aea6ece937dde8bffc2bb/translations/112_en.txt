(Continued from previous page) Two rivers converge, and the three streams thus combined become one great river. This great river is named "Eruete" (the Yenisei River), flowing through the land of "Torohanasukoi" (Tobolsk?) before emptying into the Northern Sea (Arctic Ocean) of "Seriueru" (unknown). In the country called "Chiruchianasukoi" (unknown), there are two great rivers called "Nerucha" (Nercha River) and "Shirika" (Shilka River). These flow through the land of "Kasan" (Kazan?) before emptying into the Northern High Sea (Arctic Ocean). To the north of "Tohorisukoi" (Tobolsk), there are two rivers called "Ira" (Irtysh River) and "Shitoho" (unknown), which also empty into the Northern High Sea (Arctic Ocean). Furthermore, within Russian territory, at the terminus of this river's flow, on the border with Chinese territory, there is a great river called "Amoru" (the Amur River). Reaching the end of this river's flow, it enters Russian territory and then converges with two streams called "Torohansukousha" (Tunguska River?) and "Re-

As for the open waters of the Northern High Sea (Arctic Ocean) mentioned in the preceding chapter, it is said that, being seas at latitudes of 66 to 67 degrees or above from the North Pole, they are frozen seas even in summer, making navigation impossible. What is referred to as [missing text] in the preceding chapter exists within the territory south of Russia and north of Persia, India, and China, and because there is no flow of seawater into it, it is a lake. As it lies at approximately forty-some degrees of northern latitude, while ice may form in winter, the summers are warm, navigation is possible, and it is convenient for transport.
