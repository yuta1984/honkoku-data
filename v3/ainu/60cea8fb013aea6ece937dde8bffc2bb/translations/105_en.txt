(Continued from previous page) ...and so Ikotoi and the Russians exchanged the usual greetings. I had a rough field shelter built on the shore, had a meal prepared, and invited the Russians to dine with us in order to become better acquainted and to cultivate friendly relations. They praised the meal greatly and were very pleased. The following day, I sent word that they should come, as I had instructions to convey and matters to inquire into. They came immediately. At this, I spoke up and said: "By order [of my superiors], I have matters to investigate, and I therefore request this meeting. For what reason have you come into Japanese territory?" The Russians replied: "During the summer of the past Year of the Serpent [*Mi-doshi*], we crossed the sea to 'Urutsufu' (Urup) Island for the purpose of hunting, but we became embroiled in a violent quarrel with our fellow crew members — a dispute so serious it threatened our very lives — and so

**[Left folio]**
we fled into the deep mountains of that island and went into hiding. In the meantime, the ship bound for our home country had already set sail and departed." The full meaning of this account is quite profound; a separate document exists concerning it, and it is therefore omitted here. For reasons of my own, I took two of the three Russians with me and returned to "Kunashiri" (Kunashir) Island. Upon arriving at the trading post (*un'jōya*) on "Kunashiri" Island, after the officials had concluded their deliberations, a retainer of the Matsumae [domain] conveyed the official decree to the Russians: "It is strictly forbidden for foreigners to enter the territory of Japan. You are therefore hereby ordered to return to your home country with all haste." The home countries and names of these two men are as follows:
