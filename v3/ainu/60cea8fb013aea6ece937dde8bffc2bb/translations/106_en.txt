**[Right folio]**
A man from the great port of Okhotsk:
　　　Ivan Wen Yuin Yu — Sasnosukoi — [Year of the Horse] Age: 28

"Ishiyoha" appeared to be a man of senior rank, and "Sasnosukoi" a man of secondary rank. Upon hearing the terms of the command described in the preceding chapter, both men received the directive that they must return to their home country. When they were about to depart for "Etorou" (Iturup) Island, the fact that we had lived together for three months and formed a close bond made the parting deeply sorrowful for all. In early August, as the Russians were setting out, I performed the Japanese farewell courtesy to bid them goodbye, whereupon the two Russians also observed the Russian parting ceremony. Placing me in the center, the two Russians knelt to my left and right, pressed their palms against mine, and with one hand slipped their palm beneath my arm

**[Left folio]**
from either side, drawing me toward them — left and right alike — as both wept with deep emotion, trembling and shaking, and said: "Even if the distance is great, let us meet again in our home country. Let us part in hope of that reunion." Waiting for these words to end, one of them then said: "Truly, it is a far country — and since there is no prospect of meeting again, this seems a parting for the rest of our lives." When each had finished his phrase in turn, left and right, they grew all the more longing and grieving; and afterward they cried out in loud voices, wailing and weeping without cease. I, being quite at a loss for words, had nothing to say in return, and merely sat in silence, moved to tears myself, choking with sobs.
