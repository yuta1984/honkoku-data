【Right Folio】
(The Ainu sailors) said: "The sea looks completely white—it feels eerie and foreboding." Without yet knowing the reason why, when the night passed and they looked out upon the sea the following morning, the distant offshore waters had become an endless mountain range of ice. The thickness of the ice was five or six *ken* [approximately 9–11 meters], or even ten-odd *ken*, and in some places perhaps twenty or thirty *ken*, floating five or six *shaku* [approximately 1.5–1.8 meters] above the surface of the sea. One could not know how deeply the ice extended below the waterline—it had truly become a mountain of solid ice. This ice had all been driven in by the winds from the northern sea (*hokkai*), and there were truly no waves upon the great ocean. As it was therefore impossible to pass through by boat, they were obliged to remain and wait. The local Ainu people leapt from floe to floe of ice, venturing far out into the open sea, and caught great numbers of seals (*azarashi*) and sea lions (*ashika*) alive. Day by day, the solid ice gradually

【Left Folio】
began to break up and scatter; around that time, they ceased sea hunting and shifted to mountain hunting, going deep into the mountains to make their living. It was the headman "Ikotoi" who discovered the den of a brown bear (*higuma*), and he ordered his attendants, *utare* (※meaning "retainers" or "followers"), to shoot and bring down the bear. They skinned it, took the flesh, extracted the gallbladder (*kimo*), discarded the bones, prepared everything into provisions, and loaded it onto the *utare* to carry. Furthermore, three bear cubs found at the same spot were captured alive, and the party—master, retainers, and all—descended from the deep mountains to the shore. Their appearance was as follows: they divided the skin and flesh of the parent bear among themselves to carry on their backs, led along the three live captured bear cubs, and—in keeping with Ainu custom—while still far off in the distance from the overnight shelter, raised their voices and called out loudly, shouting "hō hō hō!" all the while laughing cheerfully, rejoicing and returning in high spirits...
