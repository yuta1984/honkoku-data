## (Right folio — main text)

In the fourth month of Tenmei *Hinoe-uma* (1786), a Russian vessel came sailing across and entered Uchiura Bay, to the southwest of Matsumae. It dropped anchor and lay at a distance of two or three *ri* off the rocky shores near a place called "Ejima-tei-mura," situated to the northwest of Matsumae. The Ainu natives found this remarkable: the ship bore three masts — something not seen on Japanese vessels — and numerous sails, flags, and streamers could be seen flying from it. The natives deliberated among themselves and concluded that it was a foreign ship. As the western shore of this inner sea is the land of Sandan (Manchuria), they also debated whether it might be a Sandan vessel. Given that the lands to the east of Korea are Japan, Ezochi, and so forth, no drifting vessel of such a character had ever been seen in all of recorded history. The natives' suspicion and bewilderment were entirely understandable. At Matsumae there was no watchman assigned to foreign vessels, and so there was no one to report the matter to the Matsumae authorities. Before long, the vessel weighed anchor and sailed off toward the northwest.

## (Left folio — main text)

On the same island, at a place called "Maki-mura," the vessel again anchored two or three *ri* off the rocky shore. Some Ainu, riding in their hunting boats, rowed up close to get a look at the great ship. From the ship, foreigners emerged and beckoned to them. Drawing up alongside for a closer look, the natives could see beyond any doubt that these were indeed foreigners. As there was no common language between them, the Ainu felt uneasy and wary, and would not draw any nearer. The Russians beckoned repeatedly, while the natives shook their heads in refusal, over and over. Just as the natives were about to turn back, the Russians hung from the tip of a pole a *furasuko* (flask) filled with a measure of spirits, and a package of *manreu* (mantou/steamed buns), and presented these to the Ainu. After this, the vessel set sail from that place and sped away toward the northwest.
