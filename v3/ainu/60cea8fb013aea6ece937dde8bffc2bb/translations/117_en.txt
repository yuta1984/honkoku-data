Now, "Khaselov" (Khaseróf) and "Ausu" had sailed together aboard the same vessel from the Japanese dependency of Ezo (Hokkaido), and had subsequently crossed the Japanese seas (the waters surrounding Japan) — this was a stratagem devised to survey and reconnoiter the sea routes, distances, geographical measures, and bearings. That they had conducted surveys at various places along the way is also evident from examinations of the "fūsetsu-kō" (reviews of intelligence reports on foreign affairs). When I inquired further of "Iyo" with even greater care, the account again matched perfectly, like a tally-stick. All of this is owing to the exceptional talent and brilliance of "Khaseróf" and "Ausu." After that, it appears that the Russians came to know the various islands of the East and the lands of Ezo, along with Japan's sea routes, distances, geographic positions, and bearings, in ever-

When I examined the accurate copy of a map sent from "Noite-koku" (Sweden?) by the Dutch ship's captain who makes his annual voyage to Nagasaki, I found that it recorded all four seas surrounding Japan in their entirety, even marking the location of the rising smoke of the volcanic mountain at "Esashi" in Matsumae (Hokkaido). I could not but think this a truly fearsome matter.
