## (Left folio — main text)

…he told me, saying that he had come to learn in detail the circumstances of Okhotsk (*Ōhotsuka*).

This is a matter of great significance, and it is difficult for my humble understanding to fully grasp. All I can say is that the shoreline there is treacherous. There are various theories about this, and as yet no well-founded account is known. In any case, it seems to me to be a matter of utmost importance. Even if the opportunity presents itself, without the right timing, no meaningful debate about its merits can be had.

It is my earnest hope that I might once more cross the sea to the island of "Etorou" (*Iturup*), and — having heard that the one called "Ishieyo" still remains there — meet with him directly to inquire about the circumstances of five years prior, and also to learn whether the fate of those nine great vessels that reportedly sailed to North and South America had indeed been communicated to "Ishieyo" by the officials of Okhotsk…
