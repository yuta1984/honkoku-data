At this place, many Ezo natives dwell, and it is said that people from Sandan (inhabitants from the continental mainland) also cross the sea and come here to take up temporary residence. From "Nayoro" to this "Ratsukeshi," the journey by boat is approximately ten days, covering roughly one hundred ri or more by sea. From here, traveling one day's journey by boat to the northwest, there is a place called "Nayoro" [a different Nayoro]. This place is the crossing point to the land of Sandan, with approximately ten ri of open sea in between. During the winter months, thick ice forms across this strait, making it like solid land; when this season comes, people travel across it by driving large sleds. The area from "Shiranushi" to hereabouts is called "Western Karafuto." To the east of "Shiranushi," at a place called "Uru," there is a large river, and upstream of this river there is a lake,

by which the natives cross by Ezo-style boat to carry out transport and trade. To the northeast of this "Shikama-nai," there is a place called "Shiretō." At this location, a mountain promontory juts out from the coastline into the sea. From the vicinity of "Notoro," in the district of "Shiranushi," to this area, many Ezo natives are said to travel about and devote themselves to their livelihoods. Furthermore, the place called "Taraikhai" in the western and northwestern part of the same island is the foremost thriving and prosperous location in the northwest. Beyond this, deeper into the mountains, there is a place called "Ōkukata." Here there is a large village where many Ezo natives reside. That there should be village inhabitants in the mountains—rare for Ezo territory—is known to be due to the excellent sunlight, good local produce, and favorable land. From this "Taraikhai," proceeding westward, one arrives at "Ōfukeshi."
