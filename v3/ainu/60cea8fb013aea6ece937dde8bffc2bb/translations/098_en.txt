A "harbor" refers to a place where ships can anchor. In the ten years prior to the year Tenmei 6 (1786), the year of Hinoe-Uma, when the Russians crossed the sea, there was a great tsunami. The massive waves cast one of their large ships ashore, and it became lodged in a mountain valley. Though the Russians attempted to retrieve it, they had no means to do so, and the ship was left abandoned in the mountains — this I witnessed with my own eyes. The Russians have renamed this harbor "Rehakin." There are five or six temporary Russian dwellings there. Furthermore, to the south of "Funnawa," connected by land yet at a great distance, there is a place called "Nohi." Throughout this area, sea otters are plentiful. The Russians have renamed this place "Yurosin." It is truly an island abundant in valuable products. Yet the Russians cross the sea in great numbers and

**[Left folio]**
seize the various products in enormous quantities. As the Ainu people are simple-minded, there is nothing to be done about it. The sea otter is one of Japan's prized products, and in former times they were sent to Nagasaki in Hizen Province for trade with Chinese merchants, but this had long since ceased. In recent years, however, the Russians have been harvesting sea otters and other prized products, presenting them as Russian goods, sending them to Beijing in China, trading them, and reportedly making great profits. All of this will naturally become a source of disadvantage for the Japanese islands. There is more to be said on this matter, but it is omitted here.
