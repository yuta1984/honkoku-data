**On Japanese Castaways Washed Ashore on Karafuto Island**

Upon examining the old records of the Matsumae domain and the written registers of inquiries into castaway vessels, it is found that a ship captain named Tokugorō from Nishinomiya in Sesshū Province (present-day Hyogo Prefecture) encountered a violent storm, drifted at sea, and was washed ashore on Karafuto Island. This occurred on the twenty-first day of the sixth month of the twelfth year of Hōreki (1762). Captain Tokugorō, having no idea where he was, could only stand in bewilderment gazing up at the vast sky. During the several days he remained there, he observed the flight of birds and noticed that birds resembling wild ducks were flying in the direction of the *tatsumi* (southeast). Since birds gather southward every autumn, he reasoned that Japan must lie in the southeast direction. From that point, after eighteen days of travel, he reached a human settlement. By this time,

**[Left folio]**
it was the eighteenth day of the ninth month. This place was a village in Ezo territory called "Shiwonushi." By that time, it appears snow had already fallen. Upon quiet reflection, I believe he must have come ashore somewhere between "Taraika" and "Iwotchishi" on Karafuto Island. From these circumstances, one ought to appreciate the inexperience of Japanese navigators, and reflect upon how vast the island truly is.

Furthermore, in the summer of the year *Hinoe-Uma* of the Tenmei era (Tenmei 6, 1786), a man named Ōishi Itsupei crossed to Karafuto Island to survey the breadth and extent of its territories, its various natural products, and its inhabitants. Proceeding gradually along the coastline on his inspection tour, he arrived at the village of "Nayoro." The *otona* (village headman) of that place was called "Yaenkoroi."
