## (Right folio — main text)

…such is the method. Moreover, there exists a rope used for measuring sea distances: this rope is paid out over the water while the vessel sails under sail. On one's first ocean crossing, distances are invariably measured in this way. Alternatively, to determine bearings and angles, and to measure the altitude of the sun, moon, and stars, they possess compasses, quadrants, and other instruments, by means of which they ascertain their vessel's position and make navigational decisions. This is truly a realm in which Japan falls far short.

Among the retainers of the Matsumae domain, there exists an official post called *uwanori* (supercargo). Those holding this post sail aboard the vessels of the contract traders (*basho ukeoi shōnin*) in order to collect tribute goods — sea otter pelts, eagle feathers, and various other products — and travel to each trading post to collect the local produce. A retainer by the name of Niida Daihachi was assigned to this *uwanori* post and crossed over to Kunashiri Island.

## (Left folio — main text)

At that time, the Russians also came over to Kunashiri Island and repeatedly petitioned for the establishment of permanent trade between Japan and Russia. The official Niida Daihachi heard their request, but as the matter was not one he could decide on his own authority, he returned to the home domain and reported the matter to his lord. His lord, in turn, submitted the matter to the higher authorities (the Shogunate) for deliberation, as such a matter could not be approved without their sanction. He conveyed the reply that the decision would have to await the following year, and that they should arrange to meet again on the same island the next year. The Russians acknowledged this, and in the summer of the following year — Anei 8 (the Year of the Boar) — they returned once more to Kunashiri Island and waited for the answer. However, as the *uwanori* official failed to make the crossing, the Russians, growing suspicious at the absence of any reception, sailed deep into Japanese territory with the apparent intention of surveying the local geography and distances…
