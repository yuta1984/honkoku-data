## On Local Products (continued)

**Sea Lion** *(海鹿 / アシカ)* *(continued from previous page)*
In Matsumae, they are given the name "*ashika*" and are also called "*todo*."

**Harbor Seal / Spotted Seal** *(海豹 / アザラシ)*
In the Ainu language, there are thirteen varieties: "*Shirotokari*," "*Asarashi*," "*Ōutanne*," "*Hekatosuma*," "*Ureho*," "*Kikube*," "*Ketsuho*," "*Yumaruо*," "*Amushihe*," "*Makui*," "*Itankо*," "*Hohiri*," "*Oshiyō*," and "*Yaitokari*" — all thirteen are seals (*azarashi*).

**Dolphin / Porpoise** *(イルカ)*
In the Ainu language, there are nine varieties: "*Okon*," "*Tereкechiro*," "*Nnоfu*," "*Refunkamoy*," "*Tohayuku*," "*Yushinfu*," "*Ikorakamoy*," "*Itemuke*," "*Nehai*," "*Yuikikamoy*," "*Funbe*," and "*Yunoki*" — all nine are dolphins (*iruka*).

**Whale** *(鯨)*
In the Ainu language, there are approximately nine varieties: "*Noyurutonai*," "*Furenbe*," "*Tannehe*," "*Yurinhe*," "*Oriki*," "*Tenbe*," "*Itochikín*," and "*Ōayaun*" — all are whales.

**Brocade** *(錦 / にしき)*
In Matsumae, it is called "*jittoku*" and also "*koromо*." There are two types: those with tapestry weaving (*tsuzure*) and those with embroidery (*nuitori*). All are used garments from foreign countries, said to be official robes of the Manchu (Qing) dynasty.

**Fabric Remnants / Cut Lengths of Cloth** *(段切れ)*
These arrive rolled up as bolts of cloth. They include varieties of brocade (*nishiki*), damask (*donsu*), and satin (*shusu*). All are of foreign origin.

---

**Blue/Green Beads** *(青玉 / あおたま)*
The large ones are pale blue-green (*asagi*) in color. The medium and small beads come in a variety of colors.

**Kuntsura** *(クンツラ)* [Ainu term]
A woven felt (*mōsen*) with various patterns. Most arrive as old, well-used items.

**Pipe** *(煙筒)*
Made of white copper (*hakudō*) with carved decorations. The craftsmanship involves inlaid glass, comparable to Japanese *shippō* (cloisonné) work. Some pieces bear engraved inscriptions or maker's marks.

---

Beyond these, Ezo produces many other goods such as "*ikema*" (a medicinal plant), *efuriko*, and scallops (*hotategai*), but as these are recorded in various other writings, they are omitted here. As for imported trade goods (*watarimono*), items such as tanned leather (*nameshi*), gold, silver, coinage, rapeseed, woolen cloth (*rasha*), and scarlet cloth (*shōjōhi*) are also said to appear, but as these are irregular trade items, they are omitted here as well. Among things washed ashore along the coastline and rocky shores, items such as large bamboo, driftwood, and ship fittings occasionally include rare and unusual objects, but since these too are recorded in various other writings, they are omitted here.
