## (Right folio — main text)

The Russians had no choice but to set sail and return to their homeland. The goods received from Japan — fifteen bales of rice — were presented to the Russian tsar with a detailed explanation to the effect that they were "gifts sent from the Matsumae house, retainers of the Japanese sovereign." However, it is said that a Russian official, through his own diplomatic incompetence (*fuchōhō*), had accepted these goods, while the gifts that Russia had brought to offer were returned by Japan. On account of this blunder, that official was reportedly put to punishment. So "Ireyuyo" told me (the author).

### On the Number of *Ri* per Degree of Celestial Arc

When I look at the theories of earlier scholars of our country, it appears that one degree of celestial arc, when applied to the surface of the globe, corresponds to roughly forty-odd *ri*. Furthermore, in medieval times there was a man called Shimaya Ichizaemon who, upon receiving a strict command, crossed to foreign lands for the purpose of trade and conducted commerce there. This man had a distinguished reputation for his accomplishments at sea. He authored a work called the *Anshin-sho* [Book of Navigation], in which he recorded matters pertaining to crossing the great ocean. According to that work, the distance of one degree of arc amounts to 43 *ri* and 7.5 *bu*. Moreover, in the revised world atlas (*Yochizu*) compiled in recent times, the distance of one degree is shown as approximately 32 *ri*. These are matters that one who would be a navigator (*hakushi*) must know in detail.

Ships are, under heaven, the most important of instruments, and serve the greatest needs of the realm and state. Even in years when our land is struck by famine, if the transport of goods by ship proceeds without interruption, the people are spared the misery of hunger and thirst. This is the meritorious achievement of navigators. Here I secretly reflect: throughout our country's customs, the way of astronomy and mathematical reckoning is held in high esteem and trusted by the world…
