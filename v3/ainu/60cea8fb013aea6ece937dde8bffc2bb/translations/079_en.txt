## (Right folio — main text)

…Coming upon the Ainu natives who had remained behind on the island and questioning them, the natives replied in a flustered manner to the Russians: "A man called 'Hatsuha-ai no,' who serves as the *otona* (headman) of Etorofu Island, had crossed over to this island for the purpose of hunting and was working and staying at this place called '*Akutsutoi*.' During his stay, a foreign vessel drifted ashore. However, the ship was found to be empty of any living people, with only a single corpse aboard; and since there was no owner of the vessel to be found, he seized the cargo and fled." They gave a full and clear account of the affair.

Upon hearing this, the Russians were greatly angered and declared: "This island has long been a working ground where both Russians and Ainu enter and ply their trades. When some incident occurs, the two parties ought immediately to come to one another's aid. That this was not done, and that such an act of dishonesty was committed instead, is something beyond words."

---

## (Left folio — main text)

When I myself crossed over in the summer of Tenmei *Hinoe-uma* (1786), the Russians gave me a full account of the particulars of the events described in the preceding chapter. I replied: "There is nothing to be done about it — it is precisely because this land is so remote, and the reach of governance does not extend here, that such dishonest natives come to behave as they do." I offered this by way of response, but as the argument could not be sustained from any angle, I was left, to my great chagrin, with nothing more to say.

The Russians declared: "A country with a proper lord has governance, and throughout the world there is no nation wherein wrongful conduct and unlawful acts go unchecked. The absence of governance is proof that this is an island with no master. Therefore, it is difficult to regard this island as a dependency of Japan under Japanese administration. Accordingly, those who have established industries here are the true masters of this island." — Such was their argument.
