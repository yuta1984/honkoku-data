The islands of the Eastern Ezo lands total twenty-one in number, and from time immemorial they have been dependencies of the island where Matsumae is situated [Hokkaidō mainland]. Since the Ezo people — who are of Japanese stock — reside there, it is without doubt that these islands fall within the boundaries of Japan. Yet in recent years, the Russians have been crossing over in ever-increasing numbers, seizing control of all twenty-one islands, claiming them as their own territory, nurturing and instructing the natives, harvesting various products and sending them back to their home country, treating these twenty-one islands as a source of tribute. I crossed the sea alone as a pioneer, fulfilling the role of inspector of these islands. Departing Matsumae in Tenmei

**[Left folio]**
Six [1786], the year of Hinoe-Uma [the Fire Horse], in the middle of the first month [mid-January], setting out through the snow in great haste — passing by the island where Matsumae is situated, I made landfall on "Kunashiri" (Kunashir) Island. Sailing eastward along the southern shore of this island, I then made landfall on "Etorou" (Etorofu/Iturup) Island. Proceeding northward along the western shore of this island, I arrived at the village of Naibo on that same island on the fourth day of the fifth month of the same year. The following morning, the fifth day, at the hour of six [around 6 a.m.], I departed from this place, and on the same day at the hour of eight in the afternoon [around 2 p.m.], I made landfall at "Sharusham" on the same island. At this time, the three Russians — master and retinue — who had been staying here came out to the shore and waited to greet me. Because I had sent word from village to village that I would be crossing the sea aboard the boat of "Ikotoi," the headman (*otona*) of "Akkeshi" (Akkeshi), the Russians had heard the news passed along and came out to meet me.
