One. In the first month of the second year of An'ei [1773], the year of the Snake, he had applied himself diligently to his current duties, and accordingly was granted an increase (kajō) and adjustment of his stipend allowance (yakuryō) to fifty koku.

One. In the twelfth month of the third year of An'ei [1774], the year of the Horse, as there were to be established new barrier stations (shinseki) and checkpoints for regulating passage (okikaeri no basho) in Ushū [Dewa Province], and as there was a confidential directive (go-naii) from the Bakufu to the effect that peasants should be provided by this domain, he traveled up to Edo on this official business, returning in the fourth month of the following year of the Sheep [An'ei 4, 1775].

One. In the sixth month of the fourth year of An'ei [1775], the year of the Sheep, it was commended that the previous winter he had traveled to Edo on official business concerning Ushū, made his rounds among the relevant officials, applied himself with diligence, and fulfilled his duties admirably.

One. In the first month of the fifth year of An'ei [1776], the year of the Monkey, as he had requested, he was granted release from his official post and was immediately ordered to retire (inkyo). In recognition of his decades of devoted service across various duties, he was granted three pieces of silver (ginshi sanmai) as a reward for distinguished service.

One. In the fourth month of the first year of Kansei [1789], the year of the Rooster, he died of illness at the age of eighty-four. His posthumous Buddhist name (hōmyō) was Junri-in Honshō Nikkan Koji. His place of burial is the same as previously recorded.
