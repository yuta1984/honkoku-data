1. In the 11th month of the same 3rd year, Year of the Boar (Kansei 3, 1791): His son Ukon had become a wanted person (*o-tazunemon*) sought by the shogunate (*kōgi*). He had been instructed that if he had any knowledge of Ukon's whereabouts, he should report it. However, upon hearing from a certain man called Ōmachi Chūemon that he had encountered Ukon in Shinshū (Shinano Province) during the 4th month, he failed to report this as he should have. Furthermore, he wrote a letter to a man called Seiemon of Zaimokuchō (Timber Town), asking him: if he should happen to meet Ukon while traveling to Echigo Province during the 4th month, to tell Ukon either to go to Kōya (Mount Kōya) and take Buddhist vows, or — if his heart was truly corrupt and dishonest — to commit ritual suicide (*seppuku*). He then sent this letter via the said Seiemon. While such actions may be understood as arising from fatherly affection, they were absolutely impermissible. Moreover, during the formal inquiry (*tadashi*), he had Ukon's whereabouts guided to the examination seat,

**[Left folio]**

...and presented him (or information leading to him) before the authorities. Although there was no misunderstanding of what was being asked in the inquiry, he claimed to be hard of hearing, and midway through the examination recklessly advanced from his seat and came forward — an act showing no deference to the solemn occasion. For these various improprieties and inappropriate conducts, he was dismissed from his unit assignment (*kumitsuki*) and placed under house confinement (*heimon*).

1. In the 11th month of the same 4th year, Year of the Rat (Kansei 4, 1792): He was appointed to the *go-karō*-attached *tozama* (outer retainer) unit and placed in the 2nd unit, where he performed castle guard duty.

1. In the 6th month of the same 7th year, Year of the Hare (Kansei 7, 1795): In accordance with his petition, his son Sukekurō was appointed as his guard replacement (*go-bandai*).

1. In the 11th month of the same 8th year, Year of the Dragon (Kansei 8, 1796): In accordance with his petition, he was granted permission to retire (*inkyo*).
