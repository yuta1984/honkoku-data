【Right folio】
(The ancestors resided in Shizuyama-Moto-no-gō in Echizen Province.) It is said that Gondayū's father, Sakyōnoshin, served Asakura Yoshikage and married Yoshikage's daughter, fathering five sons. Gondayū was reportedly the fifth son. In the seventeenth year of Tenshō (1589), he went down to Higo Province and entered the service of Katō Kiyomasa, receiving a fief of over 2,100 koku within Kōshi and Yamamoto Districts, and being entrusted with five yoriki horsemen and fifty dōshin foot soldiers. In that same year, when Konishi Yukinaga attacked Shiki Castle, he went to render assistance. He also participated in both Korean campaigns, going to the peninsula each time and distinguishing himself in battle. In the tenth month of Keichō five (1600),

【Left folio】
he was appointed castellan (jōdai) of Yatsushiro. In Kan'ei nine (1632), when the Katō domain was dissolved, he had already become elderly. Having relatives among the retainers of the Inaba clan of Bungo Usuki, he traveled there, but died of illness in the eleventh month of that same year at the age of sixty-one. His son, Gon'emon, had previously been taken into the service of the Katō clan, but due to illness, he stayed behind for a time to recuperate, and when he set out for Aizu in Kan'ei twelve (1635), his illness relapsed along the way, and he died shortly after arriving.
