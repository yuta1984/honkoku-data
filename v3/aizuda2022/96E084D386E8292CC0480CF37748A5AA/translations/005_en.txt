(He had been residing in Shizuyama Moto-no-gō in Echizen Province.) The father of Gondayū, Sakyōnoshin, became attached to Asakura Yoshikage, married Yoshikage's daughter, and fathered five sons. Gondayū is said to have been the fifth son. In the seventeenth year of Tenshō (1589), he went down to Higo Province and entered the service of Katō Kiyomasa, receiving over 2,100 koku from within the two districts of Gōshi and Yamamoto, and was entrusted with five yoriki cavalry and fifty dōshin foot soldiers. In the same year, when Konishi Yukinaga attacked Shiki Castle, he went to assist in the campaign, and participated in both Korean expeditions, each time traveling to that land and distinguishing himself in battle. In the tenth month of the fifth year of Keichō (1600),

he was appointed castle keeper (jōdai) of Yatsushiro. In the ninth year of Kan'ei (1632), when the Katō clan was dissolved, he had become elderly. Having relatives among the retainers of Lord Inaba of Usuki in Bungo Province, he traveled there, but died of illness in the eleventh month of that same year at the age of sixty-one. His son, Gon'emon, had previously been summoned into the service of the Katō clan, but due to illness, he stayed for a time to recuperate.
