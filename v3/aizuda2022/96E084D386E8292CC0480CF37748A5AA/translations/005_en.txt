(He had been residing in Shizuyama-Moto-no-Sato in Echizen Province,) and it is said that Gondayū's father, Sakyōnoshin, served Asakura Yoshikage, married Yoshikage's daughter, and had five sons. Gondayū is said to have been the fifth son. In the seventeenth year of Tenshō (1589), he went down to Higo Province and entered the service of Katō Kiyomasa, receiving over 2,100 koku within the two districts of Kōshi and Yamamoto, and was entrusted with five yoriki cavalry and fifty dōshin foot soldiers. In the same year, when Konishi Yukinaga attacked Shiki Castle, he went to provide reinforcements. He also participated in both Korean campaigns, going to the peninsula on each occasion and distinguished himself in battle. In the tenth month of the fifth year of Keichō (1600),

【Left folio】
he was appointed as the castellan (jōdai) of Yatsushiro. In the ninth year of Kan'ei (1632), when the Katō clan was dissolved, he had grown old, and as he had relatives among the retainers of the Inaba clan of Usuki in Bungo Province, he traveled there, but died of illness in the eleventh month of that same year at the age of sixty-one. His son, Gon'emon, had previously been summoned into service by the Katō clan, but due to illness, he stayed for a while to recuperate. When he set out to travel down to Aizu in the twelfth year of Kan'ei (1635), his illness recurred along the way, and he died of illness shortly after arriving.
