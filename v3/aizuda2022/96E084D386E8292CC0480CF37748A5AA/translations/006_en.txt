《Marginal note: It is unknown whose grave markers these are.》 His son, Shozaemon, was still a young child at that time and had been living as a rōnin (masterless samurai), when in the twentieth year of Kan'ei (1643), Lord Hoshina [Masayuki] (土津様) entered the domain. In the eleventh month of that same year, Shozaemon was granted ten koku and a stipend for two attendants, and was called into service as an o-kachi (foot soldier/attendant). In the second year of Kan'an (1649), he was granted an additional two koku as a role stipend (yakunryō). In the second year of Jōō (1653), while on duty rotation (kinban) in Edo, on the eighteenth day of the fifth month while in garrison, he killed Kawahara Bunzaemon in a quarrel, and on the following nineteenth day was ordered to commit ritual suicide (seppuku). His posthumous Buddhist name (hōmyō) was: Teijuin Ton'a Dōmi Koji. His place of burial: Jutokuzan Jōnen-ji temple [continued on next page].
