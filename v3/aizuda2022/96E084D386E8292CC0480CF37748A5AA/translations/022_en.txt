【Right page】

　　Wife: Daughter of Aritake Gorōzaemon Taka.《interlinear note: Died of illness on the sixth day of the ninth month of Kansei 3, the year of the Boar (1791); posthumous Buddhist name: Eimyōin Kyōichi Jukō Daishi; place of burial: Shōshūji Temple at Azabu Hakudō in Edo.》

One item: In the first month of Kan'en 1, the year of the Dragon (1748), he was granted the standard stipend of eight ryō and four-man rations (yonin-fuchi), and was summoned into service as an o-koshō (page/personal attendant to the lord).

One item: In the fourth month of the same year, he traveled up [to Edo] as advance guard (o-saki-ban) in conjunction with the lord's attendance [at the shogunate (sankō-fu)], and in the fourth month of the following year of the Snake (1749), he returned [to the domain] as advance guard in conjunction with the lord's return journey.

One item: In the fourth month of Kan'en 3, the year of the Horse (1750), he traveled up [to Edo] as advance guard in conjunction with the lord's attendance [at the shogunate].

One item: In the sixth month of the same year, in accordance with his petition, he was granted a half coming-of-age ceremony (han-genpuku).

【Left page】

One item: In the twelfth month of the same year, due to the passing of Lord Tsuchijō [Tsuchijō-sama], he was granted various remaining personal effects as mementos (o-katami).

One item: In the same month, since [the lord] was to remain permanently in Edo (o-jōfu), all members of the Nakano-ma were ordered to serve on permanent duty; it was proclaimed that they would be granted leave of approximately one hundred days each, on a rotation of two to three years, for rest and recuperation.

One item: In the fifth month of Hōreki 1, the year of the Sheep (1751), in accordance with his petition, he was granted the coming-of-age ceremony (genpuku), and it was proclaimed that he should continue serving as o-koshō as before.
