Regarding this Yajirōemon: in the fifth month of the second year of Jōō, the year of the Snake (1653), his father Shozaemon was ordered to commit seppuku, and Yajirōemon was born on the first day of the eighth month of that same year. Out of deference [to his father's disgrace], he refrained from residing within the castle town, and instead borrowed a small hermitage at Banshōzan Teinei-ji temple, where he was raised by his mother. On the thirteenth day of the eleventh month of the sixth year of Kanbun (1666), when he turned fourteen years old, his mother died of illness. In the tenth year of Kanbun (1670), having reached the age of eighteen, he took his mother's family name and went to Edo, where he was taken into service by Lord Kuze Yamato-no-kami. For reasons unstated, he left that service and was subsequently taken on by Lord Ōkubo Kaga-no-kami, under whom he continued to serve. In the spring of the eighth year of Enpō (1680),

there were those who quietly sent word that he should be recalled [to Aizu], so he took his leave and returned to Aizu. On the fifteenth day of the third month, he was granted a standard provisional stipend (gigi-saku) and called into service as an o-kachi (foot soldier/attendant). However, since his father Shozaemon had quarreled with Kawahara Mataemon twenty-eight years prior and had been ordered to commit seppuku, it was said that there was no precedent for calling the son of such a person into service in the present day, and he was dismissed the following day. Nevertheless, on the twenty-fourth day of the intercalary eighth month of that same year, he was once again granted a standard provisional stipend and called into service as an o-kachi.
