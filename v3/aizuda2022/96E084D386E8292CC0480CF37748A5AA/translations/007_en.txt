Regarding the aforementioned Yajirōemon: in the second year of Jōō, the year of the Snake (1653), in the fifth month, his father Shozaemon was ordered to commit ritual suicide (seppuku), and Yajirōemon was born on the first day of the eighth month of that same year. Out of deference, he refrained from residing in the castle town, instead borrowing a small hermitage at Banshōzan Tennei-ji temple, where he was raised by his mother. On the thirteenth day of the eleventh month of the sixth year of Kanbun (1666), when he turned fourteen years of age, his mother died of illness. In the tenth year of the same era (1670), having turned eighteen, he took his mother's family name (Yokoyama) and went to Edo, where he was taken into service by Lord Kuze Yamato-no-kami. For reasons [unstated], he took his leave from that position and was subsequently taken into service by Lord Ōkubo Kaga-no-kami, where he continued to serve, until in the spring of the eighth year of Enpō (1680),

【Left page】
word was sent to him privately by certain individuals that he should return to Aizu, and so he took his leave and went down to Aizu. On the fifteenth day of the third month, he was granted the standard provisional stipend (nami no ogisaku) and was called into service as an o-kachi (foot soldier/attendant). However, as his father Shozaemon had committed seppuku twenty-eight years prior following a quarrel with Kawahara Matazaemon, it was said there was no precedent for calling into service the son of such a person in the present day, and so on the following day he was dismissed. Nevertheless, on the twenty-fourth day of the intercalary eighth month of the same year, he was once again granted the standard provisional stipend and called into service as an o-kachi.
