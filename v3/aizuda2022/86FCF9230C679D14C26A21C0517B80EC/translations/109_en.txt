...reports were to be made to the [nearest] garrison, and after consultation, the matter was to be reported to the authorities (*kōbe*, i.e., the shogunate); it was likewise established that both parties would inform each other accordingly, and the details of the arrangements agreed upon between the two domains were to be duly submitted as a report. As it was determined that, upon the arrival of a foreign ship (*ikoku-sen*), [the domain forces] would sortie into open waters to engage in naval battle, a Ship Magistrate (*fune-bugyō*) was appointed; several warships (*gunsen*) as well as vessels of various sizes were newly constructed, and additionally, in the event of an emergency, arrangements were made to requisition vessels (*yosebune*) from the coastal areas of the domain's territories. Since it was stipulated by regulation that the ship's ensign (*o-fune-jirushi*) bearing the hollyhock crest (*aoi go-mon*) could only be used by the shogunate (*kōgi*), and as there was nothing equivalent to the shogunate's official insignia that was suited to the nature of this special duty, an authorization was issued to use, exclusively on emergency occasions, a large streamer (*ō-fukinuki*) dyed with a white crest on a red ground and two white horizontal stripes below it, which was to be raised on the vessel of the commanding officer (*shōchō*). A man by the name of Miyake Kojūrō was engaged as the chief helmsman (*o-sendō*), and several others serving as ship's officers (*o-funakata*), navigating officers (*okinori*), and sailors (*kako*) were taken on as stipended retainers (*o-fuchi-nin*). From among those skilled in maritime affairs (*sengyo*) within the domain's territories, selections were made, and over fifty provisional helmsmen (*kari-sendō*) and over five hundred and fifty sailors (*suifu*) were engaged as hired personnel (*o-yatoi*), with the privileges of surname (*myōji*), the right to wear swords (*taitō*), and exemption from foot-service (*ashiyaku*) granted [as incentives]. They were to be thoroughly briefed in advance on conduct and procedures, and arrangements were made to ensure that maritime affairs would proceed without hindrance in the event of an emergency. When a [foreign vessel's] arrival was signaled from a shore battery (*daiba*) by means of a signal rocket (*noroshi*), the next shore battery was to relay the signal in succession; and throughout the villages of the domain's territories, wooden clappers (*ita-gi*) were to be hung in advance at the village headmen's (*nanushi*) residences, so that upon receiving the signal from the shore battery, they would be promptly struck and [the alarm spread] from village to village...
