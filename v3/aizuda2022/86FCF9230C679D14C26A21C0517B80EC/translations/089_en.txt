Regarding the domain's internal financial affairs (*o-naishō-kata*): the previous lord had exerted great efforts, strictly curtailing personal household expenditures and earnestly promoting frugality and economy; however, as the situation now stood, the financial difficulties had grown ever more severe, resulting in the strained financial circumstances described above. The present moment required exceptional measures of oversight and control, with further expenditures anticipated ahead. Now that he had been appointed Head of the Senior Attendants (*Goyōnin Headmaster*), he was entrusted with carrying out duties related to economies and simplifications (*shōryaku*). Regarding matters pertaining to revenue (*irikata*): he was to make thorough inquiries not only into established sources of income, but also into other channels and parties, broadening his knowledge, and even in matters directly pertaining to the lord's household, he was furthermore to communicate ideas and proposals to various parties that might otherwise be inclined to hold back, so as to achieve further exceptional reductions in expenditures, and to take appropriate measures accordingly, as was officially proclaimed.

Item: In the seventh month of the same year, it was officially proclaimed that, in preparation for any unexpected contingencies (*fuji*) that might arise in Edo, ten men serving as mounted escorts (*yoriki*) and twenty-two men including foot-soldier unit leaders (*ashigaru kogashira*) were to be attached to him.

Item: In the eighth month of the same year, as the inquiry to the shogunate (*kōbe*) regarding a re-inspection (*mi-kenbun*) of the defense installations (*o-sonaeba*) in Sagami Province (*Sōshū*) had been concluded, it was officially proclaimed that he was to be dispatched to that location for the re-inspection at this time. It was also proclaimed that Kawase Jūjirō was likewise to be dispatched along with the group for the re-inspection, to examine the planning and construction (*o-fushin tatekata*) of the said location, and that all parties were to consult with one another thoroughly on all matters.
