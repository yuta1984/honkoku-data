It was conveyed that prior consultations had taken place regarding this matter, and while [the response] was indeed reasonable, as for [the author] himself, having received and acknowledged the orders in Edo to cross to Sōya and proceed overland,
　　　upon arriving [at the posting], since this year's initial operations were connected to the mood of the Ainu people,
　　　and causing them hardship was greatly to be avoided, [the author] was resolved to proceed by direct sail.
　　　Should [the troops] land at Sōya and proceed overland, the number of people involved would not be small,
　　　and it would be unavoidable that this would cause hardship to the Ainu people.
　　　It would not be possible for [the other party] to go ashore while only [the author] proceeded by direct sail.
　　　Therefore, the matter of the orders already issued should be communicated to Sōya,
　　　and [the author] would also send word to the officials stationed at Sōya.
　　　Upon doing so, even if prior consultations had taken place, there should be no difficulty.
　　　Having been told this, and given that official orders had been issued, there was no room to argue further;
　　　however, beyond the circumstances already described,
　　　it was certain that [the garrison] at Sōya would remain stationed there until around the ninth month,
　　　and if, while the troops were assembled and remaining there, foreign vessels were to appear,
　　　that would be the most desirable outcome, and [the author] very much wished to cross to Sōya.
　　　However, having been informed that it would cause hardship to the Ainu people and that there were various other difficulties,
　　　there was no way to press the matter further.
　　　Therefore, since this concerned the withdrawal route rather than the advance route,
　　　even if the troops under [the author's] command were released and sent ahead first,
　　　there should be no objection whatsoever.
