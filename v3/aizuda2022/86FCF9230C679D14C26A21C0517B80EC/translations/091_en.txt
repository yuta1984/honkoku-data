Were the battery emplacements (*daiba*) to be increased in number and assigned to us in such a manner, it would not be a temporary matter but something that would continue in perpetuity, bearing upon the honor and disgrace of our military house. At this time, when our lord is still of young age, it would be all the more likely that the retainers as a whole could not sincerely submit and would remain harboring dissatisfaction—this was by no means an acceptable state of affairs. It was therefore communicated confidentially to the appropriate officials that the assignment should be made in accordance with our domain's assessed rice yield (*ryōchidaka*) and our domain's status (*ieгaku*). On the 15th day of the 7th month, it was decided that Sagami Province would be accepted as our assigned area. On the same day, Lord Aoyama Shimotsuke-no-Kami issued an order assigning our defensive positions, and declared that if we had any thoughts or proposals regarding the coastal batteries or other matters, we should submit them. Furthermore, the same communication was conveyed from the *Kanjō-bugyō* (Finance Magistrates) via the same lord. We were additionally ordered to re-examine in detail the locations of the field headquarters (*jin'ya*), guard huts (*ikogoya*), and other facilities. Accordingly, while we ought to have investigated the required troop numbers for establishing the field headquarters, the calculation of troop strength is fundamentally a matter of strategic planning (*byōsan*) that depends first and foremost on the enemy's disposition, relative numbers, and strength or weakness, as well as the ease or difficulty of the terrain at the battlefield. However, since foreign affairs are broad in scope, and it is impossible to know from which country, under what circumstances, and in what manner they might come, it is not possible to calculate with what number of troops one could hold them off.
