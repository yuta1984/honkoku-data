Note: As the domain lands (*go-ryōchi*) in Sagami Province were being handed over in succession at this time, [he and his party] departed together with Tanomo and arrived at Ura-no-gō, where they investigated the official matters at hand. From the twenty-sixth day of the same month, Tanomo's entire party conducted a thorough inspection (*junken*) of all the defensive positions (*go-sonae-ba*), the villages throughout the domain territory (*go-ryōbun*), and as far as the boundary markers (*go-sakaime*). Along the inspection route, word had been put out (*ai-fure*) that elderly persons aged ninety and above should present themselves, and indeed they came forth, whereupon [he] performed the appropriate ceremonial greetings (*tori-kata*). Furthermore, as the shrines and temples (*jisha*) within the domain territory came out to the roadside — including those of the *go-shūin-chi* (temple and shrine lands held under shogunal seal) — [he] exchanged appropriate courtesies (*e-shaku*) with each as [the party] passed along the route. Regarding the defensive positions, just as had been investigated and reported the previous year, orders (*go-gechi*) were issued, and [construction work commenced on] the building of three battery emplacements (*daiba*) and watchtower guard posts (*miharibanjo*), long-house residential quarters (*o-nagaya*) at both garrison headquarters (*go-jin'ya*), and permanent family quarters (*kainai jōjū*). In particular, given the nature of the location, in order to maintain strict control of the gate regulations (*go-monsei*), the structures were designed to be somewhat more spacious than the Edo long-houses (*o-nagaya*) — smaller in number of units yet more generous in width. Since the previous winter, the timber had been cut and pre-assembled (*kirikumi*) on Tsukishima in Edo, transported by ship (*funemaわし*), and construction was gradually undertaken. The ridge-raising ceremony (*mune-age no shiki*) was completed at Kannonzaki on the nineteenth day of the ninth month, and at Misaki on the sixth day of the tenth month. Upon formally taking charge of the domain lands, it was not possible to delay the deployment of the defensive garrison (*go-sonae haichi*). Although the full completion of all construction had not yet been achieved, as the long-house quarters were being progressively completed, from the third day of the eighth month, families (*kainai*) were dispatched from Aizu in succession and moved in with their households. Accordingly, the various officials (*yaku-yaku*) took oaths and signed in blood (*seiyaku keppan*) following the precedent established for the Ezo expedition (*Ezochi shussin*), the regulations for conduct during travel (*dōchū okite*) were drawn up, and the code of conduct was made known to all. At a wide, flat location outside both garrison headquarters, a training ground (*kyōjō*) was established, [for the practice of] archery, gunnery, flag-bearing, drum [signaling], and mounted warrior (*kōshi*) foot drills...
