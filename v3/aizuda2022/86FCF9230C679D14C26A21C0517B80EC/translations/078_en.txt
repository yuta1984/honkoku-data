...they petitioned accordingly, and the captains of the two red vessels further stated that they wished to be granted permission to wait for favorable winds from the 20th, and that if that date were to pass, it would be difficult to predict the wind conditions, and therefore requested to be given provisions for wintering over. Because of this forceful petition, Arai Heibei and Mogami Tokunai were further instructed, and arrangements were made to persuade them earnestly that they should remain through the current month at all costs. However, they were entirely unconvinced and replied: "If we are told that the departure will be postponed, we will accept that for the time being, but since we also have our professional duties, we must insist on this matter. As you can see in detail, since the great storm the other day, the sea conditions change day by day, and hereafter the wind and waves will become rougher with each passing day. Predicting favorable winds will be extremely difficult, and if we miss the right opportunity even once, it will become exceedingly difficult to make a safe crossing. In terms of our professional responsibilities, we cannot accept this with peace of mind. Furthermore, if we sail directly, a delay of two or three days would be acceptable, but if we are to put in at Sōya, we must be even more precise about the timing." They pressed their case insistently. While it was an unavoidable situation, since it was greatly to the benefit of all parties to remain here even one day longer, and since the 22nd would mark the 220th day of the year, after much deliberation, we decided to extend the stay by three days and settled on waiting for favorable winds from the 23rd. This differed from what we had originally resolved, and it was regrettable, but it was also the first time for me in this particular year, and since the men with specialized responsibilities were unanimously and repeatedly urging this course, it was not entirely contrary to my own judgment, and in the event that we might miss a favorable wind and be unable to make the crossing...
