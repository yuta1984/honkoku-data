I have been ordered to oversee the withdrawal of personnel, and since we cannot report to the shogunate unless all the personnel withdraw together, if you proceed to land at Sōya, I will also go ashore and remain there as long as you stay in that place. However, since there is no official business at Sōya, I believe it would be better for next year's preparations if we return to the capital quickly and report on the situation here. Therefore, I would like you to consent to the direct sailing plan so that my intentions may be fulfilled. Nevertheless, it would not be good if you have any lingering dissatisfaction, so please feel free to express your thoughts without reservation. Up until now, since the time of Uneme-dono, we have discussed matters without concealment and things have proceeded smoothly, so it would be a shame if only the matter of the return voyage were left unresolved. He repeatedly urged that we think the matter over carefully. Following this, on the 16th of the same month, the person who formally came forward stated that, while he had originally intended to remain past the 10th of next month and endure the pressure, he had resolved not to press the matter forcefully from around the 5th or 6th of next month. However, when the sailors had been requesting permission to wait for favorable winds since around the 13th and 14th of this month and had been entirely ignored, the captain of the Shōkichimaru came forward with a petition, requesting that they be granted permission to wait for favorable winds starting from the 17th. He stated that if this permission were not granted from said date onward, it would become impossible to make a safe crossing thereafter, and therefore requested leave to depart.
