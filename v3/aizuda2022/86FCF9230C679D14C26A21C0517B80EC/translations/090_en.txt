[The writer and] Kawase Jūjirō jointly inspected the land and its acreage for the proposed site, and returned to the capital on the 13th day of the 9th month.

Note: When we visited that summer to inspect the defensive positions (*sonaeба*), we surmised that the official sent by the Shirakawa domain might praise Kazusa/Shimōsa (Bōsō region) favorably, citing the villages of Sagami, their miscellaneous taxes, levies, and local products in detail. However, should the Bōsō region be assigned to us, first and foremost, the number of coastal battery emplacements (*daiba*) would fall short by one position—an outcome unacceptable for a military house. Furthermore, as a confidential matter, the Bōsō region is separated by a sea route, meaning it would be inconvenient to dispatch troops from Edo in the event of an urgent emergency. If winds and waves were rough, arrangements could easily fall behind schedule. The nature of the land also appears considerably inferior compared to Sagami. Sagami borders the Tōkaidō highway, and while it is interspersed with public domains (*kōryō*) such as Uraga and Misaki, leaving something to be desired in terms of the disposition of the local population, above all, it is well-suited for the rapid dispatch of troops in an emergency, and the land itself is favorable. It was therefore communicated confidentially that we wished, by all means, to be assigned Sagami. Furthermore, since our domain's assessed rice yield (*ryōchidaka*) differs from that of the Shirakawa domain by more than double, it goes without saying that the domain with the larger yield should be assigned more battery emplacements. However, should there be any chance that we might be assigned to a location in the Bōsō region that is not even strategically important…[the text continues on the next page]
