...and [various martial arts] were also practiced. As for gunnery (*hōjutsu*), since it was of the utmost importance at the defensive positions (*go-sonae-ba*), it was especially encouraged and diligently practiced. In accordance with the progress of training, gunpowder and lead were distributed; and as not only regular firing of large cannon (*ōzutsu*) on the flatlands but also aimed cannon fire at sea targets from the battery emplacements (*daiba*), as well as the handling of large and small firearms — including primed shooting (*shikake*) and percussion firing (*hakuda*) — maneuvering freely fore and aft, port and starboard aboard ships, and the procedures of star-load firing (*hoshi-iri*) and curtain-load firing (*maku-iri*) and the like, would be of no practical use unless thoroughly drilled, [he] reported to the shogunate (*kōhen*) and, upon receiving approval, arranged for training sessions to be held on fixed days each month throughout all four seasons. In addition, training facilities for both civil and military arts — a riding ground (*umaba*), archery range (*kakuba*), and target range (*matoba*) — were each established, and with regard to instructors (*shihan*) and administrative management (*shimari-kata*), they were ordered in accordance with the model of the Aizu domain school (*gakkō*), and the pursuit of training was diligently promoted. Furthermore, since the entire body of personnel being unfamiliar with nautical affairs would render them unfit for practical service, he took great care in progressively arranging training in swimming (*yūei*) and the practice of stern-handling and rowing (*tomote*), so that in time they would be able to travel without impediment — without the assistance of professional boatmen (*funate no mono*) and within set hours — not only to the Bōsō region but also to and from the domain residence (*o-yashiki*) in Edo. As had previously been ascertained through inspection and investigation, the headlands (*dezaki*) of Nagai, Matsūwa, Nobi, and Yokosuka within the domain territory were all locations well-suited for gunnery depending on circumstances, but since they were remote from the garrison headquarters (*go-jin'ya*), arrangements had been made in advance to position large cannon there, assign gun crews, and dispatch a portion of the personnel accordingly. Regarding the Uraga checkpoint (*go-bansho*), every passing vessel and traveler was subject to inspection, and in particular the inspection of weapons was strictly enforced; yet in cases of emergency, [personnel would] embark from the garrison headquarters...
