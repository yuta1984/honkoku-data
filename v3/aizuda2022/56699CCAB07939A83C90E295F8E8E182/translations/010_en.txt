(Continued from previous page) ...an increase was added to the existing stipend (御役料) of fifty koku, and it was proclaimed that his rank would be on par with Saka Jūzaemon and Takemoto Shinzaemon.

Item: In the 3rd year, the year of the Rooster (酉, 1753), 10th month: He was appointed as an attendant (御附) to Rikōin-sama, and his court seat (席) was placed above that of the御聞番 (Okikiban, Auditing Officers).

Item: In the 6th year, the year of the Tiger (寅, 1756), 12th month: Having faithfully served for many decades,

Item: And having been entrusted with various duties since the time of the previous lord (御先代様), and having diligently performed the duties related to His Lordship's calligraphy (御手跡御用), as a reward,

[Left folio]
...ten pieces of silver (銀子十枚) were granted.

Item: [Year and month unknown]
He was ordered to present calligraphy models (御手本) to Kyōtei-sama and Rikōin-sama; in connection with these duties, he was repeatedly summoned to appear before Kyōtei-sama, received gracious words of appreciation (御懇之御意), and was bestowed with various gifts.

Item: In the 7th year, the year of the Ox (丑, 1757), 7th month: His request for retirement (隠居) was granted. In recognition of his many decades of diligent service, it was proclaimed that his son Jūzō Tsuneharu would be appointed to the Tozama-gumitsuke (外様組付). Furthermore, it was stated that when calligraphy instruction duties (御手習御用) arose, he should continue to appear and serve as before...
