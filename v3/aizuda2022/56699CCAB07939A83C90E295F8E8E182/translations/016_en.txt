　　[Continued from the previous page:] He traveled to Nikkō to serve as ceremonial dresser (o-shōzoku) and duly performed the duties of the ceremonial dress department (o-shōzoku-kata).

Item:　　In the 6th month of the same year [Meiwa 2, 1765], it was officially proclaimed that he had diligently performed his duties without any absences.

Item:　　In the 10th month of the same [Meiwa] 3, the year of the Boar (1766), it was officially proclaimed that he had diligently performed his duties without any absences.

Item:　　In the 12th month of the same year, he was appointed to the formal post of ceremonial dresser (o-shōzoku nin-yaku).

Item:　　In the same month, he was appointed as a gate guard (o-to-ban).

Item:　　In the 4th month of Anei 3, the year of the Horse (1774), [it was noted that] over a number of years he had diligently performed his duties,

[Left folio]

　　and furthermore had also served in the ceremonial dress department (o-shōzoku-kata); accordingly, he was informed by proclamation that he would be presented with one used lined kimono (o-meshifuru o-awase) from [the lord's] wardrobe; it was further noted that the actual item would be presented before [the lord] at a later date.

Item:　　In the 10th month of the same year, he was appointed as an acting inspector (o-metsuke kari-yaku), and performed this duty until the 3rd month of the following year of the Ox [Anei 5, 1776].

Item:　　In the 6th month of the same [Anei] 9, the year of the Rat (1780), it was officially proclaimed that he had diligently performed his duties accompanying Lady Shundō-in (春洞院様).

Item:　　In the 4th month of Tenmei 1, the year of the Ox (1781), he was appointed as Chief Wardrobe Officer (Ō-o-nando-yaku).
