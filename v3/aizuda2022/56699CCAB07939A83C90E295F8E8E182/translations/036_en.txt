**[Right folio]**

- (Continued from the tenth month of the same year): it was proclaimed that, in recognition of his exceptional service, he was to be raised to the rank of *jō-geika* [the standard upper-and-lower stipend grade].

- In the first month of the fourteenth year, the Year of the Ox [Bunsei 14 / Tenpō 1, 1831]: during the previous spring, when a foreign vessel had drifted ashore and there were those who had observed and reported upon it, he had applied himself diligently to his duties stationed at Hirane-yama [Hiraneyama post] and to his duties in the Armaments Office [bugu-kata go-yō]; furthermore, having been ordered to carry out guard duties against the foreign vessel and dispatched to Shimoda in Izu Province, he mobilized with all urgency, and at that location devoted himself fully and administered all matters with sound judgment; as he had endured particular hardship amid the cold of winter, he was presented with a formal meal [go-ryōri] as a mark of recognition.

- In the sixth month of the first year of Bunsei, the Year of the Tiger [1818]: as foreign vessels and the like had been sighted off the coast of Bōshū [Awa Province, present-day Chiba Prefecture] the previous autumn,

**[Left folio]**

...he had properly managed and coordinated the arrangements for defensive position duties [o-sonaeba go-yō tebaikkata] and various other official matters, and had rendered meritorious service; accordingly, he was presented with sake [o-sake] as a mark of recognition.

- In the third month of the third year of the same era [Bunsei 3], the Year of the Rabbit [1820]: following the assumption of responsibility for Sagami Province [Sōshū o-ukewatashi], foreign vessels had drifted through the area on numerous occasions, and in the previous spring, for the first time, they had come within the offshore waters of the domain's territory; as he had applied himself diligently to both his primary and concurrent official duties, had put out to sea, and had performed guard duties [keigo go-yō] for a number of days, rendering distinguished service, he was awarded five bundles of cotton [wata go-wa] as a mark of commendation; it was further proclaimed that he had also applied himself diligently to emergency official duties [rinji no go-yō].
