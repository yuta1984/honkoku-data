## Genealogy of Yoshimura Jūuemon Tomotsugu (continued)

**[Right folio]**

One entry: (Continued from Hōei 3rd year, 3rd month)
　　His stipend (fuchi-kata) was reduced by half, and he was assigned to ko-bushin (small construction/maintenance duty — a status for unemployed or reduced-rank retainers).

One entry: 4th month of the same (Hōei) 4th year, Year of the Boar (1707)
　　On the occasion of his lord's arrival at the castle (o-chakujō), he was granted an audience (omemie).

One entry: 25th day of the 10th month of the same year
　　He died of illness at the age of 53. He received a Confucian-style funeral. Posthumous Buddhist/Confucian name: Hikkensai. Place of burial: Ōkuboyama.

## Genealogy of Yoshimura Jūuemon Tomoyoshi

《Initial name: Tomonosuke; also known as Heihachi; posthumous/retirement name: Yōsai.
Born on the 27th day of the 12th month of Jōkyō 4, Year of the Rabbit (1687), at Akashi in Banshū (Harima Province).》

　　Mother: Daughter of the Kanbe clan

**[Left folio]**

　　Wife: Daughter of Nakayama Jizaemon Kiyoai
　　《Died of illness on the 28th day of the 8th month of Enkyō 4, Year of the Rabbit (1747), at the age of 50.
　　Buddhist posthumous name: Tsukimasa Ryōtei Shinnyo. Place of burial: Edo, Kaizuka, Banpitsuzan Shōshōji [possibly an error for Shōshōji (青松寺)], Chūginsōin.》

One entry: 12th month of Hōei (4th year), Year of the Boar (1707)
　　He was granted a stipend of 5 nin-fuchi and called into service as a retainer outside the regular unit structure (kumi-gai no shi).

One entry: 7th month of Shōtoku 1, Year of the Rabbit (1711)
　　He was assigned as a trainee secretary/scribe (oyūhitsu minarai), and his seating rank was designated as next after the full-rank secretaries (o-yūhitsu).

One entry: 7th month of the same (Shōtoku) 2nd year, Year of the Dragon (1712)
　　His previous stipend was revised to 15 koku and 3 nin-fuchi, and he was formally appointed as secretary/scribe (yūhitsu).
