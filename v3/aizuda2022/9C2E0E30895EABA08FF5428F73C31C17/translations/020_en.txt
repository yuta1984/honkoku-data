　　For meritorious service, was granted two *bu* of silver.

- In the first month of the 2nd year (Year of the Tiger), was appointed to the role of overseeing official rice (*goyōmai*).
- In the sixth month of the same year, served in the role of airing the lord's treasures (*otakara-mono mushibo*shi).
- In the twelfth month of the same year, regarding the various matters of the previous year-end receipts, having applied himself diligently in careful deliberation, consulting thoroughly with the relevant parties, and handling the reduction of expenses appropriately, was granted two *ryō* of silver as a reward.
- In the first month of the 3rd year (Year of the Rabbit), served in the role of overseeing official baggage (*onimotsu shihai*).
- In the first month of the 4th year (Year of the Dragon), served in the role of overseeing official payments (*okane-barai*).
- In the first month of the 5th year (Year of the Snake), served in the role of castle official duties (*oshiro goyō*).
　　Note: He continued in this role until the 7th year (Year of the Sheep), and during this period also served in the role of overseeing the lord's garments (*omeshimono kata*),

[Left folio]
　　for which he received, privately through the intermediary of the senior officials (*goyōnin*), one *bu* of gold per year.

- In the ninth month of the same year, upon the occasion of an unexpected journey to Edo (*fuji no gosanpu*), as the number of servants (*chūgen*) and porters (*fumaru*) accompanying their masters amounted to nearly one hundred people, making arrangements difficult, he coordinated matters appropriately across all relevant parties and, through extraordinary management, was able to complete the arrangements without the need to hire additional men. He received commendation (*shōbi*) for this.

- In the sixth month of the 6th year (Year of the Horse), during the lord's journey down from Edo, amidst the busy preparations for the wedding ceremony in Edo, the retinue of servants (*chūgen*) and porters (*fumaru*) was rotated, leaving few people available with no days off. He worked day and night without incurring additional hiring costs, and was commended for his diligent service.

- In the twelfth month of the 7th year (Year of the Sheep), he had continuously served in the role of castle official duties for three full years.
