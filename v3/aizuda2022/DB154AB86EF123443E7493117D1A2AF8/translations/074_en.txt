One. In the fourth month of the same year, he traveled down (to Aizu) as part of the lord's retinue.

One. In the sixth month of Bunsei 3, the Year of the Dragon (1820), as he had on each occasion of the lord's residence in Edo served in the rotation of Nakama attendants accompanying the lord, and had exerted himself diligently in his duties during extraordinary occasions, he was awarded one piece of silver as a commendation.

One. In the twelfth month of the same year, he had remained stationed throughout the lord's illness, performing his service duties; upon the lord's full recovery and rising from his sickbed, he was given one bundle of cotton (wata hitotaba) as a congratulatory gift.

One. In the same month, as his illness had grown increasingly severe and he had no male heir, he petitioned to adopt his younger brother Satasuke as his son and heir. However, before the lord's approval could be granted, he died of illness on the fifteenth day of that month at the age of twenty-three. After his death, it was proclaimed to his relatives that his petition would be granted posthumously. His posthumous Buddhist name (hōmyō) was Tennyo-in Honryū Jikaku Koji. His place of burial was Chōkoku-ji (Hasé-dera) Temple in Shibuya, Musashi Province.
