One. In the second month of the same third Year of the Boar (Kyōwa 3, 1803), the hereditary stipend of 150 koku was granted without alteration; he was appointed as a daikan (local magistrate/land administrator), and his seat/rank was lowered to that of Okinju Ichinoyoriai (a specific rank within the domain's hierarchical seating system).

One. In the ninth month of the same year, during the domain's annual tori-kari (bird-hunting ceremony), as the Ōkawa River had flooded, there arose the pressing matter of constructing a pontoon bridge from scratch. He went to that location and managed the situation skillfully; the bridge was completed promptly. He was commended for his diligent service in this urgent duty.

One. In the third month of the second Year of the Ox of the Bunka era (1805), his request to arrange a mutual exchange of residences with Noguchi Kurōemon was granted as requested.

One. In the fourth month of the same year, he was appointed as assistant to the magistrate (soeyaku) of the kurairi-gun (domain-controlled district). His seat was placed above that of the Kanayama Magistrate, and he was appointed to a permanent post at the Tajima garrison headquarters (jinya).

One. In the same month, his request to designate his elder brother Takenosuke's son Kakunoshin as his (lineal) heir was granted as requested.
