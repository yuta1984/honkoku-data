Item: In the fifth month of the same year (Enkyō 1, 1744), [his] request to take Kohara Gorōemon's third son, Heikichi, as a son-in-law and adopted heir (muko-yōshi) was granted as requested.

Item: In the ninth month of the same year, [he] traveled up to Edo for duty rotation (kinban), and returned in the third month of the following year of the Ox (1745).

Item: In the tenth month of the second year of [Enkyō], the year of the Ox (1745), when the copying of official documents (go-shomotsu) was ordered to be carried out by the sons of retainer families, [he] was appointed to oversee the management of said documents.

Item: In the third month of the fourth year [of Enkyō], the year of the Rabbit (1747), [he] traveled up to Edo for duty rotation, and returned in the ninth month of the same year.

Item: In the first month of the fifth year [of Enkyō], the year of the Dragon (1748), [his] request was granted and [he] was relieved of his duties (go-yaku go-men), assigned to the Tozama-gumi (the outer/affiliated retainer group), placed under the command of Numazawa Gonbei's unit, and performed castle guard duty (go-shiro go-ban).

Item: In the fourth month of the second year of Kan'en, the year of the Snake (1749), [his] request was granted and his son Heikichi was appointed as his replacement (go-bandai) on duty.

Item: On the thirteenth day of the eleventh month of the third year of Hōreki, the year of the Rooster (1753), [he] died of illness at the age of fifty-nine. His posthumous Buddhist name (hōmyō) was Hon'yū Jōshin Koji. His place of burial is Shōgakuzan Jōganji Temple.
