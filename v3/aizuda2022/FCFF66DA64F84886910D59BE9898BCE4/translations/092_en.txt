(Continued from previous page) One: (At the time of his retirement in the 7th year of Hōreki [1757], in recognition of his years of diligent service as a bannerman commander [bantō],) he was granted fifteen koku and four-person stipend [yonin-fuchi], and was summoned to serve as a personal escort guard [otomo-ban].

One: In the tenth month of the same year, he traveled up to Edo to serve as a duty-rotation guard [kinban], completed one tour of duty, and returned.

One: In the fourth month of the tenth year of Hōreki [1760, the year of the Dragon], he traveled up to Edo to serve as a duty-rotation guard, completed one tour of duty, and returned.

One: In the fourth month of the eleventh year of Hōreki [1761, the year of the Snake], he traveled up to Edo to return in order to serve as a welcoming escort, performed escort duties, and returned.

One: In the tenth month of the first year of Meiwa [1764, the year of the Monkey], he traveled up to Edo to serve as a duty-rotation guard, completed one tour of duty, and returned.

One: In the third month of the fourth year of Meiwa [1767, the year of the Boar], he was appointed as a resident Confucian scholar [jōzume jusha], his rank placed above that of the honorable scribes [o-yūhitsu], and it was declared that he would receive five ryō in gold each year.

One: In the fourth month of the same year, he relocated (to Edo) and traveled up.

One: In the tenth month of the same year, he was appointed to serve as a reading companion for Lady Senmyō-in (attending to her books and literary pursuits).

One: In the same month, succeeding Koike Saburōkurō, he was appointed custodian of the Mita Library [Mita Obunko].
　　Note: After this, he was appointed custodian on three further occasions.
