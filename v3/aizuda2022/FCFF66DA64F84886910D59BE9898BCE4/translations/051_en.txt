(Continued from previous page) ...he was satisfactorily served as o-daikan, and accordingly, his stipend, which had until then been recorded as a provisional assessment (gigigsaku), was rectified and fixed at one hundred koku of assessed domain land (o-chigyo hyakukoku).

Item: In the twelfth month of the same sixth year, the Year of the Ox (1769), as he had applied himself diligently and satisfactorily handled the procurement of rice and gold/money during a period in recent years when there were pressing financial expenditures (o-irikata), he was granted two bundles of cotton (wata nihata).

Item: In the fifth month of the same seventh year, the Year of the Tiger (1770), his request to take Kyūjūrō, the younger brother of Tōyama Jinzaburō, as his son-in-law and adopted heir (muko-yōshi), was granted.
　　Note: The date of the dissolution of this relationship is not known.

Item: In the second month of Anei 2 (1773), the Year of the Snake, his request to change his name to Koemon was granted.

Item: In the third month of the same third year, the Year of the Horse (1774), he was appointed to the post of o-metsuke (inspector/censor).

Item: In the fourth month of the same year, he was granted a role stipend (o-yakuryō) of fifty koku.

Item: In the ninth month of the same year, he traveled up to Edo for duty rotation (kinban), and returned in the fourth month of the following year, the Year of the Ram (1775), as part of the lord's entourage.
　　Note: Although it was his first such posting (shojin), it was stated that he had applied himself diligently and performed his duties admirably.
