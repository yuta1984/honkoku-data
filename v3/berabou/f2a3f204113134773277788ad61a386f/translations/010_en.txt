One must never be off one's guard. If one traces the headwaters of this river called "love," it amounts to no more than a knothole barely an inch square — yet since ancient times, those who have through this hole brought ruin to countries, toppled castles, destroyed households, and laid waste to themselves are beyond counting in both Japan and China. The pit of Bāo Sì, who brought down the house of Zhou, and the pit of the Shima-□ riverbank into which a single wave-coin was dropped — neither, it is said, has ever been filled. The words of the Qing scholar Man'ō, who called it "the treacherous ford of the emerald sea," are by no means untrue. Had it been decided back in the age of primordial chaos, before heaven and earth were separated — when children might just as well have been born through the ear or the nose — that the dynastic shrines and altars of state be perpetuated, and that everyone unanimously agree never to open that knothole, then such delusions as these would never have arisen. In every matter, what a troublesome drinking-spout it is! A terrible wind-blowing hole — a suspicious and dreadful mystic cavern indeed.
