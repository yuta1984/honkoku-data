There was a place of great tranquility. Like the sedge hat of one who has forsaken the world, the place had come to be called by the name Minowa — perhaps because the sound of *mino* (a straw raincoat) carries with it the notion of being close to *nure* (wetness, or ruin through love). The establishment known as the Minowa Retreat was well known to people in the area; its conspicuous enclosure was the villa of Tsuchiya Jiemon, proprietor of a brothel in Shinmachi. One of his kept courtesans, an *oiran* named Umekawa, had been languishing with a lingering illness, and so it was decided that this quiet retreat would be most suitable for her convalescence. The head attendant Umeharu accompanied her for nursing duties, and together they had been residing there for some time. It happened to be autumn: the cold dew lay heavy, chrysanthemums had been ravaged by tobacco worms, pampas grasses had been bundled into charcoal sacks, and the garden plants had faded. Even the faint, threadlike cry of the bell cricket was enough to startle the ear of one mired in the bitter sea of the pleasure trade — might it not be a bell tolling its nightly warning?
