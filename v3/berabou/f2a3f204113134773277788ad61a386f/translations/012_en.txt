With the winter drizzle falling, even the toll of a distant temple bell seemed dampened and heavy with moisture, and in the melancholy hour of lamp-lighting, Umekawa had wasted away to a state resembling the death-like exhaustion of the famous Fukusei — as though buffeted by a ghostly sea breeze — and lay slumped against her bedding. Beside her, the junior attendant Umeharu had been reading a book partway through, trying to lift her spirits.

"Oh, my lady — how are you feeling in your bed today?"
"Yes, today I feel quite a bit better."
"Oh, wonderful! Then you shall get better and better, little by little. Do be careful to keep out of drafts. Now then, let me warm your medicine for you."

With such light-footed eagerness — though younger in years, she called the *oiran* her elder sister — the depth of Umeharu's devotion was a true model for junior courtesans to follow. Umekawa, her eyes brimming with tears, said:

"Truly, your care extends to every last thing, a kindness that not even one's own kin could match. Even in death, I shall never forget it."
