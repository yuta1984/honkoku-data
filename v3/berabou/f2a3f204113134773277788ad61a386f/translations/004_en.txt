Having reached that critical moment, one plays a "two-pawn" move (an illegal move, breaking the rules), and though one tries to make excuses and stall for time, the debts from credit purchases cut off all means of escape, and one ultimately ends up in a "privy-corner checkmate" (setchin-zume). For one who plays shogi with a courtesan, not even the next move can be foreseen. It is for this reason that I compose "Shōgi Kinuburu" (Courtesans as a Silk Sieve / Shogi). As for those matters beyond the reach of my clumsy shogi, I ask only for the guidance of a dan-ranked shogi player.

Written on a thawing day in the third year of Kansei, the year of the Metal Boar (1791), at the Kikukatei.
　　　　Santō Kyōden
