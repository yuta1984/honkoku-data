To plunge one's house and storehouse into a courtesan's lap, ransom her out, and make her one's wife — the feeling of that grows stale within three days, and there are any number of such cases in the world. The knowing fellow of Narabioka who said "Is it only the cherry blossoms at their peak and the moon without a cloud that are worth seeing?" — he was indeed a clever monk. If one reasons from this principle, the infatuation with women should surely be dispelled. "Ah yes, quite right!" one says with a self-satisfied air of enlightenment, mouth stretched wide — and yet, what is hard to cast away is precisely this very infatuation. From the highest single person (the Shogun) down to the chapter head of a confraternity of ten thousand, even an old man who dons a toupée of rabbit-fur wisps and sets out in a fake sedge hat — when one thinks back to the old days, these are all fellows who were once hoodwinked in the crumbling cottage behind the forest of Shinoda. That senryū selection verse, "Mother's name lies withered on father's arm," is remarkably apt. "As for me, I'm like this — (continues)"
