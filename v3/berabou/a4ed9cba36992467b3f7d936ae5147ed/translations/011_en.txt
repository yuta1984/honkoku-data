Continuing further along, he climbed over great rocks and leapt across ravines, yet found nothing resembling a road. Then he came upon a single rocky cave path. As there was a fearsome growth of dwarf bamboo, he peered in to find a large cave like the interior of a storehouse. Looking carefully at two glittering things deep within, he saw that they were the eyes of a great bear, glaring as it smoked tobacco. Startled, he thought that this was just the sort of place one would put on a rain cape and hat — but not knowing the road was worse, so despite his fear, he timidly addressed it: "Excuse me, I must ask a small favor. I am one who has lost his way. How might I make my way toward [Odawara]?" he said with extraordinary politeness. The bear replied: "That's an unfortunate mistake — you're going the wrong way. Today, given the weather, I have walked some twenty leagues through the mountains. There's no way you can return today. Come on in here. You must be terribly hungry, but there is no cooked rice — so please lick this instead," and extended its paw, upon which ants had been smeared. Saying that this was a store of provisions laid up through the winter and offering it as a gift, Shinemon licked it, and found it quite impossible to describe how far superior it was to a formal two-tray meal; moreover, his stomach settled splendidly. Just as people say, he had heard that ants are bear food, but licking them was a first for him.

"My place has nothing else in the way of hospitality, and it is not a particularly entertaining spot either, but tomorrow I shall see you as far as the road," said the bear — and that it should take more care of him than a country relative would was indeed a remarkable and admirable thing.

A monkey and a rabbit go as the bear's messengers to call a wolf.

This is quite an extraordinary affair!
