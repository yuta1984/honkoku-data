Shinemon hurried home briskly, and the joy of his household was beyond measure. He bathed and finished his meal, whereupon Daikoku-ten, who appeared to have been waiting a little impatiently, came waddling grandly into the sitting room and appeared before them.

"Well then, Shinemon — this time you've witnessed and heard some instructive things, have you not? It all comes down to the same principle. I shall explain it to you, so listen without reserve, and let your household hear it too.

〽Now, this may sound a bit self-important, but — people are the treasured gift of Heaven, the most spiritually endowed beings under heaven and earth. Therefore, they are the chief among all living things that sustain their lives. But handle that poorly, and there are those who fall far below the beasts of the field. Even the gods feel pity for such people, but since they have placed their heart in the wrong direction, there is no principle left for them to follow. Among people, someone like you, who faithfully upholds the laws of the land, values your family, and recognizes the achievements of your ancestors — that is certainly a fine thing, and that is why we deities feel so at ease with you. Think carefully about this: among birds and beasts, there is not one that is idle or dishonest. Each and every one, faithful to its nature, thinks only of loving its own life, and so runs about in search of what to put in its mouth. That is what serves as their 'Way.' People, on the other hand, are apt to say, 'I am an honest person.' But honesty is simply human nature — what is there to boast about? It is precisely because people compare honesty to something special that they lose their way. You yourself apparently don't read much, and since you've managed on honesty alone, that is fine. But birds and beasts are honest in the way I have just described — if humans are to be no more than that, then what is the point of being human? By all means, have your son read widely and teach him that there is a Way for humanity. People often say that if you read books you become arrogant or a know-it-all, but that sort of person has only a superficial education of the mouth. At the same time, reading widely and spending all one's time in abstract inquiry is worse than not reading at all. To improve one's conduct in actual practice — that is what makes even a single book worthwhile to have read. Though there are distinctions of rank among people, in the heart there is no high or low. Even among the common people, those who faithfully keep to the Way are what we call true human beings. Those whose conduct is poor are no different from those who go 'woof woof' and 'meow meow.' This is, well, the gist of the matter. In any case, one must learn — now, I have never in all my time said such things as this. But your intention in calling upon me in hopes of making your son into a fine person is in accord with the Way of a parent, and so, despite my being unaccustomed to such lengthy speech, I have said all of this at length — but mind, mind, not a word of this to the outside world. Not a word, not a word."

[Shinemon prostrates himself:]
"I am deeply, deeply grateful."
[To Daikoku-ten:]
"Again, I thank you most humbly."

[Daikoku-ten:]
"In any case, make sure he reads. There is no treasure greater than reading — even I hold none. Gold and silver can be distributed among people; it is a little difficult to distribute them, but first let it be distributed. If it proves not to work out, well then — however that may be, such is the nature of things!"
