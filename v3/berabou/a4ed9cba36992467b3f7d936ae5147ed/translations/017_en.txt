Following the words of Daikoku-ten, they had their son read the Xiaojing (Classic of Filial Piety) and the Daigaku (Great Learning) from the age of seven, and Shinemon himself studied alongside him in the learning of commerce. Whereas until now he had thought only of his own well-being, he came to think of benefiting others before himself. Until now he had thought that without gold and silver one could not get through the world, and had considered it a virtue to constantly think only of accumulating money and looking after his own hearth — but the Great Shrine (Daijingū) had warned that such a way of thinking brings only short-term profit and ultimately leads to ruin, and the sages too had written the teaching: "When treasure is flourishing, it flourishes by flowing out." What a fine thing this was! Shinemon's son was not yet very accustomed to learning, so books such as fans inscribed with verses from the "Tōshisen" (Tang Poetry Anthology) were beyond his reading, yet the boy grasped the teaching of self-cultivation, and moreover came to understand even the natural instincts of dogs, cats, kites, and crows — so much so that the world around them recognized him as a worthy successor to the retired head of the Shimaya household. As the son continued his studies and modeled himself upon these lessons, it seemed he would grow into a person of the highest character, and the joy of the husband and wife — regarding this too as a divine message from Daikoku-sama — was such that they welcomed the New Year with even greater celebration than ever before.
