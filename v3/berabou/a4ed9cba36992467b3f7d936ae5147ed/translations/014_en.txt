Shinemon had seen most of the Dragon Palace, and thinking "even if I don't eat, on days of abstinence it's torment for the eyes," he set out to leave. Peeking into a latticed building along the way, it appeared to be a doctor's office. Among the great number of patients who had come for treatment, nine out of ten showed injuries to the inside of their mouths. These were all creatures that had been caught on fishing hooks and swallowed them. Long ago, when the deity Hikohohodemi-no-Mikoto had his fishhook taken by a fish called Akame, he came to this very place to retrieve it and returned — but now there seemed to be no one left who remembered this. If the fish knew about it, not a single one would ever be caught on a hook, or so one might think. It is only natural that they assume they've been harmed by something sharp they ate. Also, a whale attacked by a shark looks just like a picture with a plaster applied to it. Furthermore, small fish scales have been found transforming along the banks of the Asakusa River. Could it be then that the river water is the Dragon Palace's hot spring resort? Since it would be rather difficult for whales to take a hot spring cure, it makes sense that they are treated with medicinal plasters instead.
