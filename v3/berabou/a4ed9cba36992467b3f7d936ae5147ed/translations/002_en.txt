Preface

As the saying goes, "Even insects that eat knotweed have their preferences" — thinking the whole business foolish, I had set it aside for two or three years, when Tsutajū (Tsutaya Jūzaburō) came to me with a request. "I am fully aware," said I — playing along with the publisher's little wordplay on the tiresome matter of moral instruction and advice — yet I would not be swayed: "Not so fast!" Rather than taking the grand, showy approach like a tiger's pelt that runs a thousand leagues, I dug in my heels with my usual stubbornness, going a full ten measures beyond my usual self. My one sure plan, my greatest strength: a book that grandparents can bring as a souvenir for their grandchildren — something with no objectionable or forbidden content to worry about. So speaks Tsūshō himself.
