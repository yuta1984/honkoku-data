"—I use no bow and arrow nor sword or spear, but strike only with thrown stones, and have the skill to hit my mark one hundred times out of one hundred. When I take aim and throw within a range of about eight chō, there is not a swift bird nor a fierce beast that I fail to bring down. For that reason, people have given me the nickname 'Hatchō-tsubute no Kiheiji-dayū'—'Kiheiji the Eight-Chō Slinger.' Though I now live in such a remote village as this, I am not entirely without ambitions reaching toward high office. I had heard tell that you are accomplished in both the literary and martial arts and that you care broadly for your people, and I yearned greatly to meet you; yet being of low station, I had no means of presenting myself before you. How fortunate it is, then, that I should unexpectedly behold your honored countenance here in these deep mountains—what greater good fortune could there be?" So he spoke.

Tametomo, hearing this, thought to himself: "So then, the reason these wolves were afraid and could not advance was because of Kiheiji's presence. This tells me that his skill with the thrown stone must be extraordinary." Deeply impressed within his heart, he answered Kiheiji courteously and told him everything—how he had lost his way, and the matter of the wolves. Kiheiji praised him without reserve, saying: "That your virtue has already reached even to birds and beasts—how truly wonderful and wise you are!

You must have been hungry, having been lost on the road all day long. My home is at the foot of this mountain. If you do not object to my humble thatched dwelling, please stop in and rest." This was difficult to refuse, and they went together toward the foot of the mountain. The two wolves also followed behind and came all the way to the gate.

Kiheiji gently pushed open the old double-hinged door and ushered Tametomo inside; he called his wife and told her all that had happened. His wife, being as sincere of heart as her husband, served Tametomo millet rice with plain-grilled ayu trout. Kiheiji, too, took off his straw sandals and came inside, saying, "This is my wife; her name is Yatsushiro. Please grant her a glance." Tametomo expressed how grateful he was for that day's kindness. Kiheiji then brought out a jug of wine and offered it to Tametomo. When Tametomo drank it, it resembled grape wine but had a different flavor. "From what is this brewed?" he asked. Kiheiji replied: "This is made from something rarely found in the mountains, and is called saru-zake—'monkey wine.' Toward the end of autumn, the monkeys gather nuts and berries——"
