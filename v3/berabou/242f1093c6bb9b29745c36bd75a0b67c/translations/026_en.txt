"...to store them up, I collected many of them and kept them hidden in the hollows of old trees and in the crevices of rocks. Over the months, the fruits all became crushed and naturally turned into something like sake. Even those who make the mountains their home rarely come across such a thing, but I recently discovered it, drew some out, and brought it back with me," he said. Tametomo, hearing this, clapped his hands and said, "I had heard that such things exist in the mountains, but having been born and raised in the capital, I had never seen one myself. Had I not wandered to this place, how could I ever have encountered such hospitality?" At these words, Kiheiji was greatly amused and continued to offer the drink again and again. Then, as if suddenly remembering, he muttered to himself, "I've forgotten something. Those fellows must be terribly hungry," and went out toward the gate, where he called the two wolves and threw them cuts of venison from the deer's haunch. His wife Yatushiro, seeing this, was greatly startled and frightened, but Kiheiji laughed and told her the story of how it came to be, and she gradually settled her heart. Kiheiji then returned again to where they had been sitting, and in the course of wide-ranging conversation, they debated tactics and the art of war (eboshi-heihō). Everything that Tametomo spoke of came from knowledge Kiheiji had never heard before, and his talent seemed immeasurable, so Kiheiji was moved to deep admiration and in the end

they concluded a covenant of lord and retainer. Time passed in this storytelling, and the day had already grown dark, so Tametomo announced his departure and was about to take his leave, when his foster-brother Sudō Kurō Shigezue, growing anxious at his lord's late return, came searching here and there with a torch held aloft, and at length arrived at this very place. Tametomo summoned Shigezue and explained to him the matters concerning Kiheiji and the wolves, and Shigezue, too, was glad to hear of the couple's warm-hearted devotion. As the lord and his retainer departed together, those wolves still followed behind and would not go back no matter how many times they were chased away. From that evening on, they never left the area around the room where Tametomo dwelt, and Tametomo, taking pity on them, named one "Yamao" (Mountain Male) and the other "Nokaze" (Field Wind), and kept them just as one would keep a pair of domestic dogs.
