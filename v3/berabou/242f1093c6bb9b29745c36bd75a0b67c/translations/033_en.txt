【Right Page】

"And yet, he was not someone I had thought of as particularly brave before, but even as he was struck by the thunderbolt, he managed to preserve the jewel intact. He surpasses even the loyalty of Lin Xiangru of old. And yet, what a pointless thing I did," he lamented with regret, and his heart found no ease at all. Resolving then that he would not let Sudō's devotion go to waste, he took up the jewel to examine it, and found it gleaming brilliantly like a full moon — a precious jewel without equal in the world.

Just then, from the cliff path ahead, someone came along soaking wet. Looking to see who it was, it turned out to be Kihēji of the "Eight-Town Sling." Seeing that this was the young lord (Tametomo), he came running up in haste. Upon witnessing the circumstances of Sudō's death by lightning, the fate of Yamao, and the great serpent, he was greatly shocked, and first asked what had happened. Tametomo, with a sorrowful expression, holding back tears, recounted everything from beginning to end. Kihēji was even more astonished, mourned deeply the death of Shigezue, and lamented bitterly over Yamao as well. Together they dug out the roots of a camphor tree and buried Shigezue's remains, and buried Yamao beside him. When it came to deciding on what to use as a grave marker, Tametomo himself effortlessly carried two large stones and set them as grave monuments.

【Left Page】

Chanting "May the departed soul ascend to heaven, be freed from suffering, and attain joy (亡魂生天脱苦与楽)," he descended the mountain together with Kihēji. As they went, from the branch of a pine tree protruding from the peak across a narrow mountain stream, an aged crane had become entangled and, though trying to fly away, could not manage it, and was merely flapping its wings in distress. Tametomo, observing from afar, said, "That bird must have a thread or something attached to its leg, and the end of it has become caught in the branch, preventing it from flying." Kihēji nodded in agreement and said, "In that case, I shall knock it down with my sling," and was moving in to take aim when Tametomo quickly stopped him. "If you strike it with a sling stone, its life will end on the spot. Come, watch me shoot it without wounding it, and we shall perform a release-of-living-creatures ceremony in memorial of Shigezue and the others." Before he had even finished speaking, he took aim and loosed an arrow with a sharp twang. The crane instantly let go of the branch and fell into the ravine stream, but was not able to fly up immediately. Kihēji, seeing this, grasped at the bear grass, clung to the wisteria vines, and with great difficulty made his way to the bottom of the ravine, took the crane into his arms, and stood back on the original cliff side…
