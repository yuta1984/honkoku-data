"— and to ascertain the truth of the matter, I deliberately prolonged my stay today, and instructed the teahouse attendant to make a show of my departure, and taking advantage of a moment when no one was present, I concealed myself inside this long chest (*nagamochi*) here, listened carefully to the tender exchanges within the screen, and having seen through to the true feelings of Lady Yūgiri — I am now prepared to call upon the proprietor of this establishment, Yoshidaya Kizaemon, before the end of this very day, and settle the arrangements for her ransom (*miuke*). Here, in the meantime, is the deposit toward that payment.' So saying, he drew a purse from within his breast and placed it in Izaemon's hands.

Izaemon received it and looked inside — a striped purse filled to the brim — nay, this was in fact a brocade (*nishiki*) purse, stuffed to the very ears with five hundred *ryō* in gold — and accompanying the money, a single letter. Puzzled, he opened and read it: 'Whereas it has come to our hearing that you, for your part, have reformed your heart and conduct, from this day forth your disownment (*kandō*) is hereby pardoned.' The hand was unmistakably that of his father.

"Ah — how gracious! How thankful I am!" — and Izaemon and Yūgiri together pressed their palms together in gratitude — *(continued)*
