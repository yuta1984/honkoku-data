**(Main text, continued)**

*(This appears to be the preparations for tonight [the evening's business].)*

*At this moment, the laundry-stretcher [arai-hari-ya] arrives from the direction of the bolts of cloth; the lending-library man [kashi-hon-ya] pesters her about a missing book; the crockery seller shows his wares; the errand-runner [tokkkai] receives an address note; and the charcoal merchant is nursing his backside, burned by a popping coal — and just then —*

**[Yu-ban / 湯番 (Bath attendant)]** "The bath is closing — the bath is closing!"

*(He announces this throughout the second floor.)*

*Meanwhile, over in the room of —*

**[Yūgiri / 夕霧]** — the female hairdresser —

**[O-Kichi / お吉]** — arrives and begins dressing her hair.

**[Yūgiri / 夕霧]** *(reading aloud from a fortune-paper [hakanatsuki / tōjisen] while having her hair done)*

"The great way is straight and true, like hair; spring days bring abundant auspicious air..."

"Oh my, what a guess — now, this one, make it stick on well, won't you."

**[O-Kichi / お吉]** "Oiran, are you still abstaining from salt [shio-dachi]?"

**[Yūgiri / 夕霧]** "The day I set for it ran out the day before yesterday."

**[O-Kichi / お吉]** "Well, that's a relief!"

*(Just as she says this, a maid [gejo] from the teahouse arrives, wiping her eyes with a scrap of crepe fabric.)*

**[Maid / 下女]** "Excuse me — the oiran, regarding the fan she was so kind as to write [a poem/inscription] on the other day — a guest has come to collect it again and again."

**[Yūgiri / 夕霧]** "Is there something particular [the guest] would like on it?"

**[Maid / 下女]** "As long as it has your name on it, that will do."

**[Yūgiri / 夕霧]** "Oh, anything will do then, will it — very well, I'll write something on it."

**[Maid / 下女]** "I am most grateful."

*(She leaves.)*

**[O-Kichi / お吉]** "Come to think of it, *shinobu* [a swept-back, modest hairstyle] suits you far better than *tegara* [a more elaborate, proud style], you know."

**[Yūgiri / 夕霧]** "But whenever I have it done in the *shinobu* style, I always end up grinding tea [i.e., sitting idle without customers], so..."
