For in ancient times, when Empress Jingū subjugated the Three Korean Kingdoms, she prayed to the Heavenly Deities and Earthly Spirits, and the eight million deities assembled in answer. By their grace, the divine being called Isora, dwelling at the bottom of the inner sea, unable to contain his deep emotion at the sacred music (kagura), came forth onto the sacred ground of divine revelry. Summoned as a divine messenger, he was commanded to retrieve from the Dragon Palace the two tide jewels—the Flood Tide Jewel and the Ebb Tide Jewel—by means of which the Three Korean Kingdoms were effortlessly subjugated. How much more so, then, that in these latter days, the conquest of a great kingdom with such ease was achieved through the divine protection of all the gods and buddhas. The four-cornered radiant moon signified the four provinces of Satsuma, Ōsumi, Hyūga, and Ryūkyū, and it was a portent of becoming the lord of so valiant and mighty a domain. That this interpretation came to mind only afterward was related by a certain person, and it too accords with the same principle.
