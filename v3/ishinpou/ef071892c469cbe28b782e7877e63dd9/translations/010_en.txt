## From the *Shinkyū-kyō* (Classic of Acupuncture and Moxibustion) and the *Imon-hō* (Medical Prescriptions) — Sweating Therapies and Formulas

**[From the *Shinkyū-kyō* 針灸経]**

The *Shinkyū-kyō* says: Children of ten years of age and elderly persons of seventy years of age must not receive acupuncture needling. It is appropriate to use moxibustion and sweet-flavored medicines (*kanyaku*).

---

**[From the *Imon-hō* 医門方 — The General Rule for Inducing Sweating]**

The *Imon-hō* says: As a general rule (*ōmune*), in spring and summer it is appropriate to induce sweating.

When administering herbal decoctions (*tōyaku*) to induce sweating, if sweating [is brought about and] strikes (*ataru*) the disease [effectively], stop [the administration of medicine] immediately. It is not necessary to exhaust an entire preparation (*zai*, a full dosage).

When inducing sweating, it is best to have the fluids of the body (*shin'eki*, body fluids, i.e., *jin ye* 津液) flow through to the extremities of the hands and feet (*shūhen*, comprehensively throughout) *[phonetic gloss: "蟄" (*chitsu*) — fanqie reading 直立反, meaning "to store / conceal"]*, circulate throughout the body, and moisten [it] all at once — that is the superior result. However, one must not allow the sweat to pour off in streams (flowing out of the pores) like a sudden rainstorm. Apply powdered [medicine], grinding and rubbing it onto the body, and do not expose [the patient] to wind or cold.

When a great sweat has broken out and then the pulse again becomes flooding and large (*kōdai*), and the patient's condition resembles a malarial attack (*okori*), recurring twice in a single day — if sweating occurs the condition is promptly resolved. Administer *Keishi-maō-tō* (Cinnamon Twig and Ephedra Decoction, 桂枝麻黄湯) once again.

**[Formula: *Keishi-maō-tō* 桂枝麻黄湯]**

> Ephedra (*maō*, 麻黄) — *kure-no-hashikami*, also known as *amana* or *katsunekusa* — 4 *ryō* [remove the nodes]
> Cinnamon heartwood (*keishin*, 桂心) — 2 *ryō*
> Licorice root (*kanzo*, 甘草) — 1 *ryō* [dry-roasted]
> Apricot kernel (*kyōnin*, 杏仁; *karamomo no sane*) — 80 pieces [remove the pointed tips]

Using 7 *shō* of water, first boil the ephedra until three full rolling boils have occurred, skim off the foam (*撩去沫*), add all the other medicines, boil down to obtain 2½ *shō*, divide into three portions, and take [one portion] at a time. Each dose should be spaced approximately the time it takes to walk seven or eight *ri* apart. Cover [the patient with bedding] to induce sweating. There is no need to sip gruel (*kayu*). For the remainder [of the method], follow the protocol for Cinnamon Twig Decoction (*Keishi-hō*).

---

**[Formula for Persistent Leaking Sweat (*rōkan*) After Sweating Treatment]**

When, after inducing sweating, the sweat continues to leak and does not stop, and the patient has an aversion to wind, difficulty urinating, mild cramping in the four limbs, and difficulty bending and extending [the limbs], *Keishi-ka-bushi-tō* (Cinnamon Twig Decoction Plus Aconite, 桂枝加附子湯) governs this condition.

**[Formula: *Keishi-ka-bushi-tō* 桂枝加附子湯]**

> Cinnamon heartwood (*keishin*, 桂心) — 3 *ryō*
> Peony (*shakuyaku*, 芍薬; *ebisukusuri*) — 3 *ryō*
> Licorice root (*kanzo*, 甘草) — 2 *ryō* [dry-roasted]
> Fresh ginger (*shōkyō*, 生姜) — 3 *ryō*
> Large jujube (*taiso*, 大棗) — 12 pieces [cut into pieces / torn apart]

Using 7 *shō* of water, simmer over a gentle flame, reduce to obtain 3 small *shō*, remove the dregs (*kasu*), and take warm in three divided doses. After taking the decoction, one should then sip one *shō* of hot rice gruel to assist the efficacy of the medicine. Cover [the patient] warmly to promote sweating. If sweating appears [and then stops], take [the medicine] again as before. For those who do not sweat, after about half a day [they will] find that recovery has been achieved.
