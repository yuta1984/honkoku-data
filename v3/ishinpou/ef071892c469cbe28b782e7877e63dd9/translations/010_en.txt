## [From the *Shinkyū-kyō* and *Imon-hō* — Age-based Restrictions on Needling, Methods of Inducing Perspiration, and Prescriptions]

### From the *Shinkyū-kyō* (Classic of Acupuncture and Moxibustion)

The *Shinkyū-kyō* says: Children of ten years of age and the elderly of seventy years of age must not receive acupuncture needling. Moxibustion (*kyū*) and sweet medicines (*kanyaku*) are appropriate for them.

### From the *Imon-hō* (Medical Formulary)

The *Imon-hō* says: As a general principle (*ōmune*), in spring and summer it is appropriate to induce perspiration.

In general, when taking an herbal decoction (*tōyaku*) [to promote sweating]: if the sweating is induced and hits the mark [i.e., effectively addresses the illness], stop [taking the medicine] at once. It is not necessarily required to finish the entire prescribed dose (*sai*, one full course of the formula).

In general, when inducing perspiration, it is best to have the fluids and humours (*shin'eki*, i.e., body fluids) circulate throughout, reaching the hands and feet evenly, moistening the entire body all at once. However, it is not necessary for the sweat to pour forth like a streaming rainstorm. Apply powder by rubbing it onto the body, and do not expose [the body] to wind or cold.

In general, after a great sweat has broken out and then subsequently the pulse becomes *kōdai* (surging and large), and the appearance [of the condition] resembles that of malaria (*okori*), recurring twice in one day — when sweat breaks out [again], the condition will resolve. [In this case,] administer *Keishi Maō-tō* (Cinnamon Twig and Ephedra Decoction) once more.

---

**Keishi Maō-tō 桂枝麻黄湯 (Cinnamon Twig and Ephedra Decoction)**

- Ephedra (*maō*) [vernacular names: *kure no hashikami*, or *amana*, or *katsunekusa*]: 4 *ryō* (nodes removed)
- Cinnamon heartwood (*keishin*): 2 *ryō*
- Licorice (*kanzō*) [lit. "sweet herb"]: 1 *ryō* (roasted/processed)
- Apricot kernels (*kyōnin*) [lit. "seeds of the Chinese apricot"]: 80 pieces (tips removed)

Using 7 *shō* of water, first boil the ephedra until it has come to three boils; skim off and discard the foam; add the remaining medicines; boil down and obtain 2½ *shō*. Divide into three doses and take. Allow seven to eight *ri* [of travel time] between each dose. [Cover the body to keep warm.] [This will induce] perspiration. It is not necessary to sip (*susuru*) rice gruel (*kayu*). For the remainder [of the treatment], follow the method of *Keishi* (Cinnamon Twig Decoction).

---

In general, after perspiration has been induced and the sweat subsequently continues to leak out without stopping — and the patient avers to wind (*kaze o nikumu*), finds urination difficult, has slight stiffness (*kyū*) in the four limbs, and finds it difficult to flex and extend them — *Keishi-ka-Bushi-tō* (Cinnamon Twig Decoction plus Aconite) governs this condition.

---

**Keishi-ka-Bushi-tō 桂枝加附子湯 (Cinnamon Twig Decoction with Aconite)**

- Cinnamon heartwood (*keishin*): 3 *ryō*
- Peony (*shakuyaku*) [vernacular: *ebisu-kusuri*]: 3 *ryō*
- Licorice (*kanzō*): 2 *ryō* (roasted/processed)
- Fresh ginger (*shōkyō*): 3 *ryō*
- Jujube (*taisō*): 12 pieces (crushed and sliced)

Using 7 *shō* of water, cook over a gentle flame (*toro-bi*) and obtain 3 small *shō*; remove the dregs (*kasu*). Take warm, divided into three doses. After taking the decoction, it is necessary to further sip one *shō* of hot rice gruel to help augment the power of the medicine. Cover warmly [to keep the body warm] and allow [the patient] to perspire gently. If [the sweating] does not appear, take [the medicine] again as before. For those in whom sweating does not occur, from about half a day's interval [after taking the medicine], one will begin to feel recovery (*iyuru*). Observe the illness the following morning (*myōshin*)…
