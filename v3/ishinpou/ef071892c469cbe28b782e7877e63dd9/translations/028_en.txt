It also states: When taking medicine, if the formula contains *shōshi* (松脂, pine resin), one must not eat any of the five meats, fish, vegetables, salt, or *hishio* (醤, fermented soybean paste / sauce). One is permitted only to drink water, and may have a small amount of wine (*sake*) and *hoshi-niku* (脯, dried/jerked meat).

It also states: When taking medicine, if the formula contains *tenmon-tō* (天門冬, *Asparagus cochinchinensis* — also called *sumarokusa*), one must avoid (*imu*) carp (*koi*, 鯉魚).

It also states: When taking medicine, if the formula contains *ōsei* (黄精, *Polygonatum* sp., Solomon's seal), one must avoid eating plum (*ume*, 梅).

The *Chinchū-hō* (枕中方, *Prescriptions from the Pillow Box*) states: In general, when taking medicinal substances, one should not desire to eat garlic (*ninniku*, 蒜), pomegranate (*zakuro*, 石榴), or pig liver (*buta no kimo*, 猪肝). Abstaining entirely from sexual intercourse (*bōshitsu*, 房室) is the highest (ideal). When taking divine medicines (*shinyaku*, 神薬), one must not face the north — this is a great taboo.

It also states (the formula states): In general, when eating, one must avoid the flavour of blood (*chi no aji*, 血の味 — foods containing blood). Eating such things prevents the *sanshi* (三尸, the Three Corpses / Three Worms) from being expelled.

The *Senkin-hō* (千金方, *Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold* / *Bèijí Qiānjīn Yàofāng*) states: In general, a person who takes medicine (*kusuri wo kurau*) must not consume deer (*kano*, 鹿) meat. If one takes medicine (while eating deer meat), one will certainly not obtain the medicine's efficacy. The reason this is so is that deer constantly feed on grasses that counteract poisons (*gedoku no kusa*, 解毒之草), and therefore deer meat is able to neutralise and disperse (*seisan suru*, 制散する) all medicines.

It also states: In general, when taking medicine, one must abstain entirely from: raw (*namamono*, 生) things, cold (*hiyaka*) things, sour (*su*, 酢) things, slippery (*nameramono*, 滑) things, pork/boar (*inoshishi*, 豕), chicken (*niwatori*, 鶏), fish (*sakana*, 魚), oil (*abura*, 油), wheat-flour foods (*mukiko*, 麺), and garlic (*hiru*, 蒜). For great tonifying (*taihо̄*, 大補) pills and powders, one must also be cautious of anything old and malodorous (*furuku kusaki mono*, 陳臭).

It also states: When taking medicine, if the formula contains *byakushi* (柏子, *Platycladus orientalis* seeds, Chinese arborvitae seeds), one must avoid (*imu*) eating wheat-flour foods (*mukiko*, 麺), the five meats, fish, and vegetables.

The medicine formula of *Ēnichiji* (慧日寺, a temple) states: When taking *kei* (桂, *Cinnamomum* sp., cinnamon / cassia bark), one must not eat carp (*koi*, 鯉魚) — it is harmful to the person. [Note upon examination: The *Yōjō-yōshū* states: Causing a person to eat *asatsuki* (葱, spring onions/scallions) together with cinnamon (*kei*, 桂) would injure the person, and must not be permitted.]

The *Shokkyō* (食経, *Classic of Food*) of *Bakan* (馬琬, Ma Wan) states: When taking *kyōnin* (杏仁, *Prunus armeniaca* seeds, apricot kernels), one must avoid (*imu*) eating pork or boar (*inoshishi*, 猪) flesh — it kills the person.

The *Yakushō-kyō* (薬像教, *Teachings on Medicinal Images / Symbols*) states: When taking *kaijitsu* (槐実, *Sophora japonica* fruits, Japanese pagoda tree seeds), one must avoid (*imu*) pork.

## [Section Five: Prescriptions for Being Poisoned by Medicine]

The *Kasshi-hō* (葛氏方, *Prescriptions of Ge* [Ge Hong]) states: Treatment for the case of having taken medicine in excess of the proper dose (*sai wo sugoshi*, 過剤) or having been poisoned by medicine (*doku ni atarite*, 中毒), (causing the patient to be) greatly afflicted with anguish (*hanmon*, 煩悶) and on the verge of death.
