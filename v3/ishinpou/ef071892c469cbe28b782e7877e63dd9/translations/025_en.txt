## [Section Three: The Proper Measure of Taking Medicine, Continued]

[Continuing from the previous page:] …Those who are about to eat should first be given a single mouthful of cold vinegared rice (*sakuhan*, 酢飯), and only then proceed with the meal. This is the better practice.

It also states: In general, pills (*gan'yaku*, 丸薬) should all be made to the size of the seeds of the Chinese parasol tree (*kiri no ko*, 梧子 — *Firmiana simplex*). When taking tonifying medicines (*ho-yaku*, 補薬), begin with ten pills, and gradually increase the number, taking forty pills as the upper limit, never exceeding this. As for persons of weak constitution (*kyojin*, 虚人), they should take the medicine three times a day. The desired approach is to accumulate the medicinal energy (*yakki*, 薬気) over many days and a long period of time without interruption, gently fumigating and steaming (*kunjō*, 薫蒸) the five viscera (*gozō*, 五蔵 — the five organ systems), and to continue this over a prolonged period. It is not considered good to take the medicine hurriedly and exhaust the supply quickly. To squander a fine medicine in vain results in very little benefit obtained.

It also states: In general, when taking purgative pills (*sha-gan*, 瀉丸), take purging (*geri*, 下痢) as the measure (*do*, 度) and do not exceed it. Take great care not to cause a person to suffer diarrhea without limit. If one exceeds the proper measure, it will greatly harm the person.

It also states: In general, when a person suddenly encounters the onset of a wind affliction (*fū*, 風) and their body and mind suddenly deteriorate severely — in some cases to the point where they are unable to speak — such a person should take decoctions (*tō*, 湯) such as the Greater and Lesser Life-Prolonging Decoctions (*Daishō Zokumeito*, 大小続命湯), the Xīzhōu Life-Prolonging Decoction (*Saishū Zokumeito*, 西州続命湯), the Peach Wind Decoction (*Tōfūtō*, 桃風湯), the Yuè Bì Decoction (*Eppitō*, 越婢湯), and similar preparations. This should be done inside a sealed chamber (*misshitsu*, 密室) from which wind is excluded, four times — or ideally five times — throughout the day and night, without concern for the number of doses, and without worrying about the patient's underlying weakness (*kyo*, 虚). The ideal state is to maintain a continuous perspiration emanating from the head, face, hands, feet, abdomen, and back without ceasing. When the decoction has been digested, immediately eat porridge (*kayu*, 粥); when the porridge has been digested, immediately take the decoction again. It is also acceptable to supplement the patient's strength with a small amount of lamb broth (*atsumono*, 臛 — a thick soup or broth made from meat).

If the wind affliction is very severe, continue taking the decoction without interruption for five days and five nights. After two days have passed [following the five-day course], discontinue the decoction and allow the body to restore and recover of its own accord through nourishing broths (*bi-kōkaku*, 美羹臛 — fine, high-quality meat broth), giving rest to the four limbs and body (*shitai*, 四体). If the condition has improved somewhat, immediately discontinue the medicine and gradually enter a period of recuperation (*shōsoku*, 将息). If the condition has not improved, one should again take the decoction to assail (*osamu*, 攻) the affliction, taking recovery (*iyuru*, 差) as the limit.
