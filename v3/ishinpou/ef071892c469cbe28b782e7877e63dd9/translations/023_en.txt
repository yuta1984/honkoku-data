## [Section Three: The Proper Measure of Taking Medicine, Continued]

[Continuing from the previous page:] [For conditions such as] lingering wind (*tai-fū*, 滞風) and *dō*-depletion (*dō-kyo*, 洞虚), one must take the medicine for a prolonged period — several tens of courses, and even up to a hundred or more courses — before a cure can be achieved. Nevertheless, one should adjust and modify [the prescription] as appropriate (*zōson*, 増損 — increasing or decreasing the components), following the condition of the person's body with regard to cold, warmth, dampness, and purgation (*kan-on-shitsu-ri*, 寒温湿利).

The *Qianjin Fang* (千金方; "Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold Pieces") states: "In general, for a person aged forty or below who has an illness, it is appropriate to take purgative medicines (*sha-yaku*, 瀉薬), and there is no great need to take restorative medicines (*ho-yaku*, 補薬). Of course, if there is necessarily some deficiency, this rule does not apply. For those aged forty and above, one should not take purgative medicines; one must take restorative medicines. For those aged fifty and above, restorative medicines should not be lacking throughout all four seasons. By doing so, one may extend one's years and attain the art of nourishing life (*yōjō no jutsu*, 養生の術)."

It also states: "If there is necessarily an accumulation (*shaku-jū*, 積聚 — a mass or accumulation of pathogenic *qi*, blood, or food in the organs) in the viscera and bowels (*zō-fu*, 蔵府), regardless of whether the person is young or old, one must purge. If there is necessarily a deficiency and depletion (*kyo-son*, 虚損), regardless of whether the person is young or old, one must supplement. Think carefully and deliberate (*shōryō*, 商量) before applying [the treatment]."

It also states: "Every spring and every autumn, one should administer a purging medicine (*ten-sha-yaku*, 転瀉薬 — a medicine that flushes out the bowels) once. If one does so, one will not be struck by the seasonal epidemic *qi* (*ji-ki*, 時気) that Heaven causes to circulate."

It also states: "In general, when using medicines, one must follow what is appropriate to the land and region (*tochi no yoshiki*, 土地の宜き). In Jiangnan and Lingbiao (江南・嶺表; the regions south of the Yangtze River and south of the Nanling Mountains), the climate is hot, the skin of the inhabitants is thin and delicate (*usuku moroku*, 薄く脆く), and the interstices of the flesh (*sō-ri*, 腠理 — the pores and texture of the skin and underlying tissue) are open and loose. Therefore, medicines should be used in lighter and smaller quantities (*kei-sei*, 軽省). In Guanzhong and Hebei (関中・河北; the Wei River basin region and the area north of the Yellow River), the land is hard and dry (*kō-sō*, 堽燥), and the people there have skin that is firm and hard (*ken-kō*, 堅硬), with interstices that are closed and solid (*heijitsu*, 閉実). Therefore, medicines must be used in heavier and repeated doses (*jū-fuku*, 重複)."

It also states: "When taking a purgative decoction (*ri-tō*, 痢湯), one desires that [the prior medicine's] effect be [just slightly] encroaching (*okasu koto*, 侵すこと) …" [The text appears to have a lacuna here.]

"When taking a decoction, one should take care to heat it properly in accordance with the method, and drink it while it is hot. If done so, it will easily dissolve and move downward, and one will not vomit. If it has cooled, then one will vomit and it will not descend. If it is excessively hot, however, it will injure the person's throat and gullet (*in-kō*, 咽喉). One must take great care and apply deliberate attention when using it. The decoction must necessarily be clear and limpid (*chō-sei*, 澄清). If it is turbid, it will cause the person distress and the illness will not resolve.

Between [successive] doses, one should allow an interval of approximately the time it takes to walk ten *ri* (*jū-ri*, 十里 — a traditional unit of distance, here used as a measure of time). If the interval is exceedingly short and the doses are taken too frequently, the previous decoction will not yet have been digested when [the next is taken] …"
