## [Continuation of Keishi-ka-Bushi-tō; Contraindications for Inducing Perspiration]

*(Continued from the previous page)* …If the signs (*shirushi*, symptoms/indications) are genuinely present, administer this decoction again. Even if there are cases in which the patient is simply unwilling to sweat, after taking two or three preparations (*sān liǎng jì*), the condition will resolve. What is called "Keishi-ka-Bushi-tō" (Cinnamon Twig Decoction Plus Aconite) is [the base] Cinnamon Twig Decoction (*tō*) with one piece of aconite (*bushi*, *Aconitum*) added. This is it.

---

It further says: In winter, warming medicines (*netsuyaku*) are appropriate; one must not induce perspiration. If sweat is produced, sores (*kasa*) will emerge within the mouth, and the patient will vomit and have diarrhea.

> **Marginal note:** *Chiku* (衂): read [with the fanqie] *nǚjú* 女鞠 反 — meaning blood issuing from the nose.

As a general rule, in households where there is *chiku* (nosebleed, epistaxis) — [i.e., persons prone to nosebleeds] — one must not induce perspiration. If sweat is produced, the patient will immediately develop a fixed forward gaze (*chokushi*, staring eyes that cannot move), and will be unable to sleep.

As a general rule, in households where there is *rin* (淋, urinary difficulty, strangury) — [i.e., persons with urinary disorders] — one must not induce perspiration. If sweat is produced, the patient will immediately have blood in the stool (*benketsu*).

As a general rule, in persons whose throat is obstructed and whose throat is dry, one must not induce perspiration in them.

As a general rule, if perspiration is induced after a major purgation, the patient will immediately develop abdominal distension and fullness (*chōman*, bloating).

As a general rule, after perspiration has been induced, a person who has an aversion to cold (*okurimono wa kyōsuru*) is so because [their vital energy] is deficient (*kyō*). A person who has no aversion to cold but only fever is in a state of excess (*jitsu*). [In the latter case,] one should harmonize (*yawaragu*) the qi of that person's stomach.

As a general rule, in persons with sores (*kasa*) — [i.e., those with skin wounds or ulcers] — even if the body aches and is in pain, one must not attack (*semu*) the exterior (*omote*, surface of the body). If sweat is produced, it will certainly bring about rigidity/tetanic spasm (*sukumi*, convulsion or stiffening).

> **Marginal note:** 𤷁 — *chōngzhì fǎn* (充至反), *è bìng fǎn* (悪病反) [phonetic glosses].

As a general rule, those who have recently undergone major purgation, suffered nosebleed (*chikaketsu*, epistaxis with blood loss), lost blood, or received blows and injuries (*ōken*), and women who have recently suffered injury in childbirth (*shōsan*) — all of these must not be made to sweat.

As a general rule, if a person has a cough with free urination (*shōbenri*), or is incontinent of urine (*shitsu shōben*), one must not induce perspiration. If sweat is produced, the patient will immediately suffer from vomiting and counterflow (*ōgyaku*) and become cold in the extremities (*ketsurei*, jué-lěng, reversal cold of the limbs).

As a general rule, when after perspiration has been induced the patient drinks much water, that person will certainly pant (*aegu*, dyspnea, wheezing). If water is poured over that person, they will also pant.

As a general rule, after perspiration has been induced and completed, if the area below the patient's navel palpitates (*kokorobashiri*, "the heart runs" — a fluttering/throbbing sensation), and the patient is about to experience *Honton-ki* (奔豚気, "Running Piglet Qi," a paroxysmal condition of surging energy), then:

> **The decoction of Poria (*matsuhodo*, *Poria cocos*), Cinnamon Heart, Licorice, and Jujubes** governs (*tsukasadoru*) this.

As a general rule, after perspiration has been induced and completed, if the abdomen becomes distended and full (*chōman*, bloating), then:

> **The decoction of Magnolia Bark (*kōboku*, *Magnolia officinalis*), Fresh Ginger, Pinellia (*hosokuā*, *Pinellia ternata*), Licorice, and Ginseng (*Panax ginseng*)** governs this.
