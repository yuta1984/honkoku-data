**[Continuing from the *Taisu Jing* (Grand Basis Classic)]**

Each [method] has obtained what is most suitable [for the situation]. Therefore, although the treatments differ, the reason all diseases are cured is that [the physician] has grasped the true nature (*qíng*) of the disease and knows the essentials (*dàtǐ*) of treatment.

It is also said: When examining a disease, one must always ask whether [the patient] was once of high rank and later fell to low station. Even if they have not been struck by external pathogenic influences (*wàixié*), if disease arises from within, this is called "loss of glory" (*tuō róng* 脱栄). When someone was once rich and has since become poor, this is called "loss of essence" (*shī jīng* 失精).

Yang Shangshan (*Yáng Shàngshàn*) says: One who was first of high rank and later became lowly suffers the injury of "loss of glory" (*tuō róng*). One who was first wealthy and later became poor suffers the damage of "loss of essence" (*shī jīng*). Loss of glory is an injury, arising from the humiliation of low and base station. Loss of essence is also an injury, arising from the [hardship and suffering] of poverty and destitution. Even without the attack of external pathogenic influences, these are conditions brought about by the suffering of both the physical body (*xíng*) and the spirit (*shén*).

**[From the *Suvarṇaprabhāsa Sūtra* (最勝王経, *Saishōōkyō*)]**

*[Marginal note: The passage from the Suvarṇaprabhāsa Sūtra is absent in the Uji manuscript but present in the physician's manuscript.]*

Disease has four distinct types, namely: wind (*fēng*), heat (*rè*), phlegm-humor (*tán yǐn* / 痰癊), and combined accumulation (*sōng jí* / 惣集). One should know the time of their arising and activity:

In spring, phlegm-humor (*tán yǐn*) becomes active. In summer, wind-disease (*fēng bìng*) arises. In autumn, yellow-heat (*huáng rè*) increases. In winter, all three arise together.

In spring, eat astringent (*shè*), heating (*rè*), and pungent (*xīn*) [foods]. In summer, [eat] rich/oily (*nì*), heating, salty (*xián*), and sour (*cù*) [foods]. In autumn, [eat] cooling (*lěng*), sweet (*tián*), and rich/oily [foods]. In winter, [eat] sour, astringent, rich/oily, and sweet [foods].

Within these four seasons, if in taking medicine and in [choosing] food and drink one follows the flavors appropriate to each season as described above, there will be no reason for the many diseases to arise.

Disease after eating arises from *yǐn* (phlegm-humor/癊). When food is being digested, [disease] arises from heat. After digestion is complete, [disease] arising is due to wind. In accordance with the season, it is necessary to identify the disease.

Once the source of the disease has been identified, simply follow the disease and prescribe medicine accordingly. Even if the symptoms and condition of the ailment vary, one must first treat its root (*běn*, the fundamental cause).

For wind disease, take oily and fatty [foods/medicines]. For heat disorders, using [purgative] drainage is best. For phlegm-humor disease, one should change the diet (flavors). For combined accumulation (*sōng jí*), the three medicines (*sān yào*) are necessary.

When wind, heat, and phlegm-humor are all present together, this is called "combined accumulation" (*sōng jí* / 惣集). Even when one knows the time at which the disease arises, one should also observe the patient's fundamental constitution (*běn xìng*).

Having thus observed and understood [all these things], one administers medicine in accordance with the season; if there is no error in [prescribing] food, drink, and medicine, such a person is called a "good physician" (*shàn yī* / 善医).

Furthermore, [a good physician] should know the Eight Arts (*bā shù* / 八術). By comprehensively mastering the methods of all [branches of] medicine, if one is thoroughly conversant with these [Eight Arts], one can treat the diseases of all living beings.
