**[Continuation of the preceding passage—Each obtains what is appropriate]**

Each [region's method] obtained what was appropriate to it. Therefore, even though the treatments differ, the fact that all diseases are cured [by these methods] comes from having grasped the true nature (*qíng*) of the disease. This is what it means to know the fundamental principles (*dàtǐ*) of treatment.

It is also said: When diagnosing a disease, one must always ask whether [the patient] was formerly of high status and later became of low status. Even without being struck by an external pathogenic influence (*wài xié*), disease can arise from within [the body]. This is called "loss of glory" (*tuō róng*). [A case where someone] was formerly wealthy and later became poor is called "loss of essence" (*shī jīng*).

Yáng Shàngshàn says: One who was first noble and later became humble suffers the injury of "loss of glory" (*tuō róng*). One who was first wealthy and later became poor suffers the detriment of "loss of essence" (*shī jīng*). "Loss of glory" is an injury—it arises from the humiliation of being reduced to low and base status. "Loss of essence" is likewise an injury—it arises from the [suffering of] poverty and destitution. Even without being struck by an external pathogenic influence, these [conditions] are brought about by the affliction of both the physical form and the spirit (*shén*).

**[From the *Suvarṇaprabhāsottama-sūtra* (最勝王経 / *Saishōōkyō*)]**

*[Marginal annotation: This passage from the Saishōōkyō is absent in the Uji manuscript but present in the Hinoshōshi manuscript.]*

Diseases are distinguished into four kinds: namely, wind (*fū*), heat (*netsu*), phlegm-rheum (*tan'on*), and "total confluence" (*sōshū*—the disease in which all three converge). One should know the time at which [each] arises and moves.

- In **spring**, phlegm-rheum (*tan'on*) becomes active.
- In **summer**, wind-disease (*fūbyō*) arises.
- In **autumn**, yellow-heat (*ōnetsu*) increases.
- In **winter**, all three arise together.

- **Spring**: eat astringent (*shibu*), heating (*netsu*), and pungent (*karai*) [foods].
- **Summer**: [eat] fatty (*neri*), heating, salty (*shiokarai*), and sour (*suppai*) [foods].
- **Autumn**: [eat] cooling (*hiyayaka*), sweet (*amai*), and fatty [foods].
- **Winter**: [eat] sour, astringent, fatty, and sweet [foods].

When taking medicine and consuming food and drink during these four seasons, if one follows the appropriate flavors as described above, there will be no means by which the multitude of diseases can arise.

Disease after eating arises from [phlegm-]rheum. When food is being digested, [disease arises] from heat. After digestion is complete [and disease arises], it is due to wind. According to the time [of occurrence], one must discern the disease [accordingly].

Once the source of the disease has been identified, one simply prescribes medicine according to the disease. Even if the symptoms presenting are varied, one must first treat the fundamental root [of the condition].

- For **wind** disease: administer oily, fatty [preparations].
- For **heat** [disease]: effecting purgation/discharge is considered best.
- For **phlegm-rheum** disease: one should alter the [dietary] flavors.
- For **total confluence** (*sōshū*): medicines for all three [wind, heat, and phlegm-rheum] are required.

When wind, heat, and phlegm-rheum are all present together, this is called "total confluence" (*sōshū*). Even if one knows the time at which the disease arose, one should also observe the patient's fundamental constitution (*honshō*—innate nature/constitution).

Having thus observed and understood [the condition], one should administer medicine in accordance with the season. If there is no error in the food, drink, and medicine [prescribed], such a person is called a "skilled physician" (*zen'i*).

Furthermore, one should know the "Eight Arts" (*hachijutsu*). [One should] comprehensively master the methods of all the various [branches of] medicine. If one is thoroughly versed in these, one will be able to treat the diseases of all living beings.
