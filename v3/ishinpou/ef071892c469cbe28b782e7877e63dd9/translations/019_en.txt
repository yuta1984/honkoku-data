## [Section Three: The Proper Measure of Taking Medicine, Continued]

[Continuing from the previous page:] These two kinds of illness are as they are precisely because the manner in which the *fu* (腑, bowels/yang organs) and *zang* (蔵/臓, viscera/yin organs) receive and take in illness differs from one another. In general, diseases of the *zang* (yin viscera) all take many years to form, while diseases of the *fu* (yang bowels) quickly become severe within a short time of onset. The Five Viscera (*go-zō*, 五蔵) are classified as *yin*, and the Six Bowels (*roku-fu*, 六府) are classified as *yang*. Yin diseases are difficult to treat; yang diseases are easy to treat. For the two categories of yin and yang disease, the nature of the medicines used is not the same. For *yin* [diseases], it is necessary that the *sovereign medicine* (*kun-yaku*, 君薬) predominate; for *yang* [diseases], it is necessary that the *minister medicines* (*shin-yaku*, 臣薬) predominate. For sudden invasion by pathogenic *qi* (*sotsu-ja*, 卒邪) and acute violent illness (*bō-byō*, 暴病), the *courier medicines* (*shi-yaku*, 使薬) should be made more numerous.

It also states: In spring and autumn, take powdered medicines (*san*, 散); in summer, take decoctions (*tō*, 湯); in winter, take pills (*gan*, 丸). These are the medicinal formulations that accord with the season. The reasoning is as follows:

**On taking decoctions in summer:** In summer, the human body's *qi* (*jinki*, 人気) circulates through the skin and within the *ying* and *wei* (栄衛, the nutritive and defensive *qi*). If a person contracts a pathogenic influence (*ja*, 邪) in summer, the illness at its onset is superficial and does not go deep. Therefore, it is sufficient simply to take a decoction to expel the initial pathogenic influence.

**On taking pills in winter:** In winter, the cold causes the body's *qi* to penetrate deeply, circulating within the five viscera, six bowels, and bone marrow. If one initially contracts a pathogenic influence, it in turn penetrates deeply and travels together alongside the body's *qi*. If one takes a decoction, its *qi* disperses, and before it can reach the location of the illness, its *qi* is already exhausted. Therefore, one makes pills and takes them. [In the case of powdered medicines (*san*,] one takes them slowly over the course of days; those who do not allow [the medicine] to go to waste will see the illness cured without squandering [the medicine's potency]. Hence, in winter one takes pills.

**On taking powdered medicines in spring and autumn:** In the two seasons of spring and autumn, day and night are equal in cold and warmth — the temperature is balanced — and the body's *qi* circulates in the space between the skin and flesh (*hiniku*, 皮宍), neither deep nor shallow. Therefore, one uses powdered medicines dissolved in wine and takes them. Wine is capable of carrying the medicinal *qi* (*yakuki*) and conducting it into the flesh of the body, thereby expelling the pathogenic influence. Hence, in spring and autumn one takes powdered medicines.

It also states: In spring and summer, one should not combine and consume hot medicines (*netsuyaku*). In autumn and winter, one should not combine and consume cold medicines (*reikyoku/hiyakusuri*). However, [in all cases] one must observe the patient's condition — whether they are of a cold or hot constitution.
