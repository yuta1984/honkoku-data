## [Section Two: Signs of Incurable Diseases, Continued]

As a general rule, whether a person is ill or not, one should routinely turn back the upper eyelid and examine the inside: if there are red vessels (*suji*, veins/streaks) running downward from above, appearing as though they are about to penetrate the pupil (*manako*) — one such vessel means death within one year; two such vessels mean death within two years. If [the streaks] have not yet reached [the pupil], the patient can still be treated.

If a person suddenly and abnormally gains weight, and exhibits *kwai-kwai* (憒々, mental confusion, agitation of the heart) and *tō-tō* (恫々, frustration, a sense of things not going as intended), such a person will die within a few dozen days.

If a person's face suddenly displays a red colour, emerging on the cheekbones (*kyōkotsu*) to a size as large as a finger, they will die suddenly. If [the redness] extends to the tip of the nose, they will also die suddenly.

If a red or black colour suddenly arises from the upper forehead and descends to the tip of the nose, the person will also die suddenly. If black colour suddenly appears on the forehead in a patch the size of a finger, they will die suddenly, even without any apparent illness.

If black colour suddenly appears lying horizontally across the nose, or extending as far as beneath the eyebrows, and does not disappear within a month — the person will die suddenly.

If the face suddenly displays a green-blue (*ao*) colour, as though a broom were hanging [across it], the person will soon die.

If, from the nose up to the eyebrows and forehead, colour suddenly appears resembling a horse's liver (*uma no kimo*) — appearing bluish-green (*ao*) when viewed from a distance, but appearing black (*kuro*) when one approaches — the person will die within a hundred days.

If the facial colour suddenly loses its lustre and moisture (*uroi*, sheen), as though one had just fallen newly ill, that person will ultimately die.

If the face suddenly appears hollow and puffy, fat yet pallid white, and devoid of blood-colour and vitality (*chi no kotowari*, the proper complexion of blood), that person is on the verge of death and will not last long.

If the area above the pupil (*hitomi*) within the eye turns white, as if half a grain of rice [were lodged there], the person will die.

If the face suddenly turns blue-green, and the eyes become dark [and dull], or red — that person will also die.

If the face suddenly turns white and the eyes become dark, that person will also die.

If the facial colour becomes dark and the eyes turn white, the person will die.

If the face suddenly turns red and the eyes turn white, or the eyes turn blue-green, the person will die on the spot (*tachidokoro ni*).

If the eyes suddenly lose any fixed point of focus — unable to settle on what to see (*miru tokoro nashi*, gaze unfixed, wandering) — the person will die.

If a sick person's body emits a foul odour (*kusaki*, abnormal smell) that is different from the ordinary, that person will die.

If a sick person suddenly weeps in grief, that person will die.

If a sick person's body suddenly ceases to feel pain or itching, their spirit becomes dulled and confused (*konkai*, stuporous, clouded consciousness), and yet their eating and drinking remain as before — that person will die.

If a sick person stares blankly with fixed, unmoving eyes (*sorame*, a vacant, fixed gaze; the pupils not rotating/moving), and their breathing (*iki*) is high and coarse (*takaki*), that person will die.
