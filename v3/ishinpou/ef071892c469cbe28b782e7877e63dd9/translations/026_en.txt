## [Section Three: The Proper Measure of Taking Medicine, Continued / Section Four: Foods and Substances Prohibited When Taking Medicine]

[Continuing from the previous page:] …[prescriptions for all wind disorders] all contain ephedra (*maō*, 麻黄; *Ephedra sinica*). [They range] all the way up to prescriptions such as the Western Province Life-Prolonging Decoction (*Seishū Zokumei-tō*, 西州続命湯), which uses eight *ryō* (両), while the Maidservant's Decoction from Beyond the Pass (*Eppi-tō*, 越婢湯) uses six *ryō*, and the Greater and Lesser Life-Prolonging Decoctions (*Daishō Zokumei-tō*, 大小続命湯) use either one, three, or four *ryō*. From this one can understand that without inducing perspiration, the wind [disorder] will not be cured. And so, the treatment of wind [disorders] cannot be done properly without a draught-free sealed room (*misshitsu*, 密室). To carelessly administer decoctions and medicines [without this] merely leads to harming the patient through one's own error. [Using the sealed-room method] only increases [the effect]; there has never been an instance of the effect being diminished or reduced.

The *Kashi-hō* (葛氏方; *Formulary of Master Ge*) states: When taking medicines, in any case where [the instructions] do not specify "before eating" or "after eating," it should be understood that [the medicine is to be taken] before eating. Those that should be taken after eating will each individually state so.

When taking decoctions where [the instructions] say to "divide into three doses" or "take again [in a second dose]," one must first observe the signs and symptoms (*byōgen no shirushi*, 病源候) of the disease, and whether to administer [doses] at wider or narrower intervals should be [determined so as to ensure] that the medicinal strength (*seiryoku*, 勢力) permeates [the body] adequately. Medicines with strong purgative properties (*doku-ri*, 毒利) should all be taken on an empty stomach (*kūfuku*, 空腹). Between doses of restorative decoctions (*ho-tō*, 補湯), one may of one's own accord take gruel (*kayu*, 粥). In cases where pills and powders (*gansan*, 丸散) are to be taken three times a day, [the doses] should be [distributed across] morning, midday, and evening. In cases of four or five doses, [the doses] should be carefully measured and equally distributed throughout the day.

When taking pills and powders, if [the instructions] do not specify [the use of] "wine," "water," or "[some] drink [to wash them down]," such is the nature of the original prescription. When a separate [instruction] specifies the use of wine or water, it is permissible in either case to drink and take (*inpuku*, 飲服) [the medicine] using water.

The *Sanban-ron* (刪繁論; *Abridged and Simplified Treatise*) states: The rules regarding prohibitions [on food and substances during medicine-taking]: If, in the case of a decoction, there is something [in the prescription] that triggers a prohibition, one must abstain from it for five days after finishing the course of medicine. If, in the case of pills, powders, or wine-based medicines, there is something that conflicts with a prohibition, one must wait until ten days after finishing the medicine before drinking or dispersing [those substances]. If the medicine contains mineral milk (*nyūseki*, 乳石), one must wait a full month thereafter. If one does not follow these rules, not only will the medicine fail to be effective, but it will inversely bring about calamity [to one's health].
