## [Section Two: Signs of Incurable Diseases, Continued]

[Continuing from the previous page:] — cannot drink or eat. Death comes in four days.

If a sick person's flesh beneath the fingernails and toenails is black — death comes in eight days.

If a sick person's eyes lack *seikō* (精光, vital luminosity) and the teeth are black — this is incurable.

If a sick person's *fu* (趺, the dorsum of the foot, the upper surface of the foot) swells up, and both knees become as large as a *to* (斗, a vessel used for measuring grain) — death comes in ten days.

If a sick person's body emits a foul odour — this is incurable.

If a sick person's face is blue-green and the eyes are yellow — death comes in one hundred days.

If a sick person's mouth falls open — death comes in three days.

If a sick person's *fukuri* (陰嚢, scrotum) and *kuki* (茎, the shaft of the penis) swell — that person will die.

If a sick person speaks nonsense and becomes confused (*mōgo sakuran*, delirious and incoherent), or conversely becomes unable to speak at all — this is incurable.

If a sick person's sweat comes out like foam and does not flow — that person will die.

### The *Kashiho* (葛氏方, *Formulary of Master Ge*) states: In general, there are five kinds of swelling (*hare*) that cannot be treated.

If the face is swollen and dark blue-black (*aoguroi*) — this is failure (*hai*, collapse) of the **Liver** (*kan*), and is incurable.

If the palm is swollen, has no lines (*shiwa*, creases), and is tautly full — this is failure of the **Heart** (*shin*), and is incurable.

If the navel (*hoso*) is full, swollen, and protrudes outward (*fukureru*, bloated and distended) — this is failure of the **Spleen** (*hi*), and is incurable.

If the abdomen is full and has no *mon-ri* (文理, surface markings/creases/texture) — this is failure of the **Lung** (*hai*), and is incurable.

If the *in* (陰, genitals/genital region) is swollen and does not become erect — this is failure of the **Kidney** (*jin*), and is incurable.

---

### The *Henjaku-den* (扁鵲伝, *Biography of Bian Que*) states: There are six things in illness that cannot be treated.

One who is arrogant and self-indulgent (*kyōshi*) and will not listen to reason (*dōri*) — this is the **first** incurable condition.

One who holds their body in low regard but prizes their wealth — this is the **second** incurable condition.

One whose clothing and food (*ishoku*, material circumstances) cannot be made suitable (*kanau*) — this is the **third** incurable condition.

One in whom *yin* and *yang*, as well as the visceral *qi* (*zōki*), are unsettled and not stabilised — this is the **fourth** incurable condition.

One whose body (*katachi*) is exhausted and emaciated (*tsukare*, wasted) and cannot take medicine — this is the **fifth** incurable condition.

One who trusts in shamans (*fu*, *kannagi*, spirit-mediums/exorcists) and will not trust in physicians — this is the **sixth** incurable condition.

[Interlinear note: According to my present inquiry, the *Senkin-hō* (千金方, *Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold*) gives "the suitability or unsuitability of the physician" as the third condition.]

---

### The *Honzō-kyō* (本草経, *Classic of Materia Medica*) states:

Master Cāng Gōng (倉公) had the following words: One who, though ill, will not consent to taking medicine — this is the **first** cause of death. One who trusts in shamans (*kannagi*) and will not trust in physicians — this is the **second** cause of death.

## Section Three: The Proper Measure of Taking Medicine (*Fuyaku Setsudo*, 服薬節度第三)

### The *Senkin-hō* (千金方, *Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold*) states:

Bian Que (扁鵲) said: "That upon which human beings depend is the body (*katachi*, the physical form). What disturbs the harmonious *qi* (*waki*, the balanced and harmonised vital energy) is disease. What regulates and resolves the affliction of vexing poison (*handoku*, the toxic pathogenic influence causing distress) is medicine. What rescues life and aids [one] out of calamity is the physician."
