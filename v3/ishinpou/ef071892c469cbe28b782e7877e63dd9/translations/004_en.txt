**[Translation of the current page, continuing Chapter 1: General Principles of Treating Disease]**

…If a patient is suffering in pain, [a physician] must not leave their side even for a brief moment—yet if a physician remains at ease and unmoved, this is something of which both human conscience and the gods are ashamed, and something that no one in a position of responsibility should do.

It is also said: As a principle of practicing medicine, one must not speak excessively, chatter and laugh, engage in idle banter and jesting, make a clamorous din, pronounce judgments on right and wrong, pass judgment on others, flaunt one's own reputation and glory, slander and disparage other physicians, or boast of one's own virtue. When one happens to have cured a single illness, one must not hold one's head high with a self-congratulatory expression, thinking there is no one in the world to match oneself—this is the "gu-huang" [incurable affliction] of the physician.

Laozi said: "If a person performs visible virtuous deeds (*yáng dé*), others will naturally reward them. If a person performs hidden virtuous deeds (*yīn dé*), the spirits and gods will reward them. If a person commits visible wicked acts (*yáng è*), others will naturally repay them [with retribution]. If a person commits hidden wicked acts (*yīn è*), the spirits and gods will repay them." For this reason, a physician must not rely on his own strengths and talents and devote his mind entirely to acquiring wealth. He should only arouse the heart to relieve suffering—[by doing so,] he will naturally accumulate great blessings in the unseen realm. To use rare and precious medicines on a wealthy patient so as to make it difficult for them to procure [such medicines] elsewhere, and thereby to show off one's own skill and ability, is truly not the way of loyalty and empathy (*zhōng shù*).

It is also said: Since ancient times, famous physicians treating illness have often made use of living creatures' lives to rescue those in urgent need. Although it is said that animals are base and humans are noble, when it comes to cherishing life, humans and animals are alike. To harm another [living creature] for one's own benefit is something that all sentient beings share in lamenting—how much more so for a sage! In general, to kill life in order to seek life is to depart from life and move ever further away from it. As for creatures such as horseflies (*meng chong*) and leeches (*shui zhi*), if there are any at the market that have already died [naturally], buy those and use them. As for the single substance of a hen's egg—because it is in a state of undifferentiated chaos (*hundun*), not yet separated [into a fully formed life], it will inevitably be needed in cases of great and urgent necessity; when there is no other choice, bear the pain [of using it] and employ it. One who is entirely unable to use it is being excessively scrupulous—he simply falls short of [what the situation requires].

It is also said: Zhongjing said: "When sweating is not necessary, to forcibly induce sweating…"
