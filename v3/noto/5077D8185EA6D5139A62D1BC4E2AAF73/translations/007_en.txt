Mibayashi Zenshirō (marginal note: He was the deputy official of Rokujō) fought his first battle at the Battle of Kuzuhara.

○Around this time, various commanders held castles in different locations: Kurose Kakudō held Hinodani Castle (marginal note: He was Mibayashi's father-in-law. Hinodani is to the southeast of Daishōji); Fujimaru and Miyakita Shuri held Hasadani (marginal note: Son of Tajiri Tajima, deputy official of Rokujō; allied with the ikki forces); Tokuyama Gohei held Matsuyama (marginal note: Shibata's men); Yanada Dewa held Daishōji; Ōsugi Hikozaemon held Ōsugi above Awazu in; and at Kurotani there was a monk-administrator called Daikō.

○The Battle of Hinodani. The vanguard of Hinodani was Kurose Kakudō. The second-in-command inside was Kōan. The combined forces of the ikki and Mibayashi numbered roughly 130 foot soldiers, with Hori Gohei as the vanguard commander. When Kōan's retainer, a man named Taki Genji, was shot and killed by a firearm, those on Mibayashi's side tried to recover his head. Kōan, determined not to let the head be retrieved, seized a spear and charged up the mountainside, and although his spear was shot and broken by firearms, Genji's head was saved without being taken by the enemy. Here, Hori Saisuke (a retainer of Rokujō), his younger brother Saizō, and Kano no Manzoku performed notable services.

○When the forces of the four districts of Kaga advanced together to Takinohara, a night attack occurred.

○Hori Saisuke. Because of (the rearguard action by) Hori Gohei refusing to retreat, Saisuke's younger brother Saizō was struck by an arrow.

Gohei pulled out the arrow and urged a retreat. At that moment, Hori Gohei took a notable head — the only head taken that day. Whether this rearguard action was at Sakumino or at Suganaminami is unclear.

○The Battle of Ishizaka. At this time, Kakudō had switched to the Osaka (Toyotomi) side. Because Kakudō had become an ally, the forces of Kaga rose with the intention of attacking Daishōji. However, as their own numbers were insufficient to continue, the Kaga forces withdrew that night.

○Kakudō, Mibayashi, and Sugao no Gorōzaemon — these three held Hinodani together. By order of Lord Nobunaga, Hideyoshi's forces besieged Hinodani for eighteen days. By Hideyoshi's stratagem, approximately a thousand men brought up a large cannon, and by drawing it personally with sacred rope decorations of bamboo leaves (shide), they fired it at the castle walls, collapsing them by about two ken (roughly 3.6 meters). After that, over five days they felled large trees and erected a tower (seirō), and when they fired the large cannon again, the barrel burst and the tower fell into the valley along with it. Afterward, Kinoshita Gorōbei (marginal note: Hideyoshi's cousin), a relative of Mibayashi, shot an arrow letter from Hideyoshi's side proposing peace negotiations. Though Gorōbei was in agreement, Mibayashi refused to comply, citing the great favor he had received from Osaka (the Toyotomi). Nevertheless, after mediation proceedings, the hearts of those within the castle turned toward the East (Tokugawa side).
