If a young man imitates them, would he not merely be wasting his days and months to no purpose? Why not redirect that energy instead toward reading books?" From that point on, I reverently heeded this loving admonition, and whenever I had leisure I would read without fail; the desire to roam and climb mountains was at last entirely abandoned.

As I approached the age of thirty, I was unhappily struck twice by the "calamity of the dancing horse" (fire) and once by "the year's rice-cake" (famine). As a result, my family's assets were entirely exhausted, and I had no means of recovering them. I could do no more than eke out a modest livelihood through teaching young students, barely managing to provide for my parents in the most frugal manner.

One day, I happened to visit Motoyoshi, where I encountered Tanka (Denpō/Tankyū), Miyamoto Yō, and Meitō Momo, and upon our first meeting I felt as though I had known them for years. Motoyoshi was not far from my home and was easy to visit. I therefore went back and forth frequently and set up a private school there. The bonds of friendship grew steadily deeper. Tankyū and the others encouraged me to go and study in the capital (Kyoto), and they also assisted me with the necessary funds. It was thus that I was first able to enter the school of Master Kien (淇園), and it was entirely through their efforts that I came to receive his gracious favor. Before long, Tankyū and Botsuyō passed away one after the other, each departing this world. I then moved to Komatsu Castle and set up a school there for students. In the spring of Kōshin (甲辰), the castle's school building was newly completed, and the local magistrate recommended me to serve as instructor. I resided in Komatsu Castle for a total of ten years. Near the castle were beautiful lakes and mountains, more than sufficient for seeking out blossoms and admiring the moon. Yet the anxiety of ageing parents and household poverty never left my breast — I was, as it were, moved to shed tears even at flowers, and grieved in heart even by the birds' song.

Then, unexpectedly, an invitation arrived from Lord Imaeda (今枝君), the chief retainer of Kinshiro (Kanazawa). Rejoicing that my aged mother was still alive to accompany me, I gratefully accepted the summons, and taking my entire household, I moved to Kinshiro (Kanazawa). From that time on, I was never at a loss in providing good food for my mother, and my heart was at last truly at ease. After that, my duties as a lecturer and teacher became, if anything, more demanding than before, and when I imagined the green mountains and misty forests (of my travels), it felt as though they belonged to another lifetime entirely.

In the year Teichū (丁丑), my mother (lit. "the Northern Hall") came to the end of her natural years. Overcome by grief and longing, I could not contain myself, and I immediately wished to return to my home village, till the fields, and sweep clean the family graves.
