The waves of the shore warm the seawater, and immersed in its depths, all the wondrous scenery of Buddhist mystery prostrates itself before the viewer. Pointing to Sōshō-rō (the tower of outstanding victory), it is as if one beholds the very essence of Noto itself. The ancients said, "The splendid scenery of Kyōtei (the capital district) is comparable to the entire breadth of the Twenty-One Histories." Here, to exhaust the splendors of Noto is likewise so. The domain's man of letters, Master Yukiko Naka, traveled through Noto and composed a travel record, entrusting the clarity of its mountains and the beauty of its waters to a single composition, committing it to the tip of his brush. Unfurling it and viewing it, one feels as if sailing directly into that realm itself; vaguely one perceives the spiritual elegance of the mountains through the flow of the prose, and the brushwork grows ever more overwhelming and majestic. Now, the Twenty-One Histories are vast and comprehensive — there is no one who has not read them — yet only those who diligently read them through will surely exhaust and investigate their depths without omission.
