## Nan'endō (South Circular Hall)

The principal image enshrined here is the Fukūkensaku Kannon (Amoghapāśa Avalokiteśvara). The image has three faces and eight arms, stands approximately 4.8 meters tall (jōroku), and has a deer skin draped over the left shoulder. This image is said to be the reason why the Great Deity of Kasuga holds deer in such reverence.

This is the ninth temple on the Saikoku Pilgrimage circuit. It was founded in the fourth year of Kōnin (813 CE) when Fujiwara no Fuyutsugu, lamenting the decline of the Fujiwara clan, joined forces with Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai) and established this hall to pray for the prosperity of the clan. At that time, the Kasuga Deity appeared in the form of an elderly man, mingled among the laborers, and composed a poem:

**[New Kokinshū]**
*On the southern shore of Potalaka, we have built this hall—*
*May the wisteria waves of the north now flourish.*

The Shūchūshō records that this poem was composed by the Ikuta deity as a messenger of the Kasuga Deity. Potalaka (Fudaraku-san) is the Pure Land of Kannon, an eight-sided mountain. Therefore, this hall too was built in an octagonal shape. That mountain is covered with evergreen trees. The children of Lord Ōmi (Fujiwara no Fuhito) comprised four noble houses: the Southern House, the Northern House, the Shiki House, and the Kyō House. Though all were of Fujiwara lineage, this poem foretold the flourishing of the Northern House descended from Fusasaki, the second son.

## Hokuen'dō (North Circular Hall)

The principal image enshrined here is Maitreya (Miroku Butsu). According to the Mizu Kagami, this hall was founded in the eighth month of the fifth year of Yōrō (721 CE), when Emperors Genshō and Genmei united their intentions to commemorate the first anniversary of the death of Lord Ōmi (Fujiwara no Fuhito).

---

## Great Lecture Hall (Daikōdō)

The principal images enshrined here are the Amida Triad. The image of Jōmyō koji (Vimalakīrti) is also placed here. This hall was built to pray for the repose of the soul of a beloved cat belonging to the mother of Ujimaro's daughter of the Southern House and Emi no Oshikatsu (Fujiwara no Nakamaro), among others.

**[Goshūi Wakashū]** — Composed upon visiting the Nirvana Lecture at Sanryōji Temple:
*Even if we were to meet again in that garden of ancient parting,*
*how the tears of today would overflow and overflow.*
— Kōgen Hōshi

**[Composed on the Vimalakirti Assembly]:**
*In the tenth month, with winter rains falling—*
*this teaching lingers in the old capital of Nara, in the sound of chanting voices.*
— New Grand Counselor Akisuke

## Five-Storied Pagoda

The Five Wisdom Buddhas (Gochi Nyorai) are enshrined here. It was built by Empress Kōmyō in the fourth month of the second year of Tenpyō (730 CE). According to the Kanmon Record, the height of the pagoda was originally planned at one chō and four jō, and since Kōfukuji occupies four chō on each side, the pagoda would have stood sixteen jō tall. However, since this would have made it taller than Kasuga Shrine, out of reverence for the divine will, it was reduced to fifteen jō and one shaku.

---

## Kubo Benzaiten Shrine

During the Kōnin era (810–824 CE), the Great Virtuous Master (Kōbō Daishi) secluded himself in prayer at the Benzaiten of Tenkawa, praying for the construction of Nan'endō. The living body of Uga Benzaiten appeared to him, and was subsequently enshrined here.

---

## Hitokoto-nushi Shrine

Commonly called the Shōten-gū (Shrine of the Sacred Elephant Deity). Located in the southwestern corner of the temple grounds.

---

## Hana no I (Flower Well)

According to the Kanmon Record, it is located behind the East Golden Hall and is a famous spring of pure water.

---

## Carved Crucian Carp Stone

Among the foundation stones of the former Lecture Hall, there is a stone with a crucian carp carved upon it. This is probably of the same type as the water plantain (澤潟) carving on the South Great Gate.

---

## Wisteria

According to the Kanmon Record, it is located to the left of Nan'endō and is called the "Left Wisteria" (Sakon no fuji). Repeated fires left it nearly destroyed, and Mizuno Iwami no Kami lamented this, having it replanted in its original location.

---

## Mandarin Orange Tree

The same record states that to the right of the same hall stands a famous tree called the "Right Orange" (Ukon no tachibana).

---

## Great Cauldron

The same record states that it is located near the Gyokuzō-in. The opening is approximately eight shaku in diameter. One is inside a building; the other is buried in the ground outside.

---

## Double Cherry Blossoms (Yaezakura)

According to the Shasekishū (Volume Three), the double cherry blossoms are in front of the East Circular Hall. Some say that during the Enpō era (1673–1681), there were double cherry blossoms at this spot, but now there is only a single wild cherry tree, which is not an ancient tree.

During the reign of Emperor Ichijō, someone presented double cherry blossoms from the old capital of Nara to the court. As the poet was in attendance at the Imperial presence, she was given the flowers and commanded to compose a poem:

**[Shikashū]**
*The double-petaled cherries of the ancient capital of Nara—*
*today they bloom in eightfold glory at the ninefold imperial court.*
— Ise no Taiyū

In the sixth year of Kenkyū (1195 CE), during the imperial procession for the dedication ceremony of Tōdaiji, when the double cherry blossoms of Kōfukuji were at their peak, (a poem was tied to a branch):

**[New Kokinshū]**
*Do not think of this as merely an old, familiar place—*
*cherry blossoms: this is a world that witnesses such an imperial visit.*
— Anonymous

---

The Shasekishū states:
There was an Empress known as Jōtōmon'in who wished to have the double cherry blossoms brought to the capital. However, the monks of Kōfukuji found this most disagreeable, and declared, "Even if it should cost us our lives, we cannot dig up these cherry trees and offer them." There were apparently instances of rather forceful resistance. When the Empress heard of this, she said, "I had thought the monks of Nara to be heartless, but truly their feelings for these blossoms run deep," and she did not request the orange tree.
