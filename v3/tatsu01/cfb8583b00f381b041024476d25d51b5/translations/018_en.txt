*(Continued from previous page: Regarding the Fourth Deity, Hime no Ōkami)*
Being a female deity, she is enshrined together in the same hall as Hiraoka Myōjin. The *Shoku Nihongi* states: In the ninth month of the third year of Kashō (850 CE), Counselor Fujiwara no Tasuku was dispatched with an imperial decree. Takemikazuchi no Mikoto and Iwainushi no Mikoto were elevated to Senior First Rank; Amano Koyane no Mikoto to Junior First Rank; and Hime no Kami to Senior Fourth Rank, Upper Grade. In light of these court ranks, the theory that she is a consort-deity may well be plausible.

**Middle Precinct (Chūin): Six Small Shrines**
(The area outside the sacred fence [mizugaki] is called the Chūin.)
- **Iwamoto Shrine** (Located to the southwest of the main shrine; enshrines Sumiyoshi Myōjin.)
- **Shingo-ji** (Located to the east.)
- **Aosaka-ki Shrine** (Located to the south of Shingo-ji; associated with blue offerings [aonigite].)
- **Karasaka-ki Shrine** (Located to the south of Aosaka-ki; associated with white offerings [shironigite].)
- **Anakuri Shrine** (Located to the south of Karasaka-ki; enshrines Anatsugu no Kami.)
- **Ikuri Shrine** (Located to the south of Anakuri; enshrines Takamusubi no Mikoto.)

**Inner Precinct (Naiin): Two Small Shrines**
(The area inside the sacred fence [mizugaki] is called the Naiin.)
- **Tajikarao no Kami** (One seat to the south.)
- **Hirai Tenjin** (One seat to the north; Amenominakanushi no Mikoto.)

**The Naoe-dono (Direct Meeting Hall)** is the place where the ceremonies of the imperial envoy (chokushi) and the senior courtier (shōkei) are conducted. It is also called the Hakkō-ya (Eight Lectures Hall), a name derived from the performance of the Lotus Sutra Eight Lectures ceremony held there. (The Eight Lectures were first held twice a year beginning in the first year of Tenryaku [947 CE] during the reign of Emperor Murakami. The senior patron was Teishin-kō, and the superintendent was the Bishop Hyōgen. The reference in the *Kokinshū* to "Ōnaoi-hi" also refers to this place.)

**The Heiden (Offering Hall)** (Located alongside the Naoe-dono; the place where the imperial envoy presents offerings on festival days.)

**The Maidono (Dance Hall)** (Located adjacent to the Naoe-dono; constructed in the first year of Jōgan [859 CE].)

**The Shishi Hashiri (Deer Run)** (*Kanbuniki* states:* Located within the corridor. Every year on the night of the fifth day of the second month, officials serving as annual deputies [toshiyori] from the three theatrical troupes of Konparu, Kongō, and Hōshō attend and perform the *Okina* ritual. This is called the "Deer Run." At this time, sacred sake and offerings are brought forth from the third shrine.)

**The Ringo-no-Niwa (Apple Garden)** (Located in front of the Heiden; the place where offerings are presented on the day of the Kasuga Festival.)

**The Mitarashi River** (Refers to the small stream running through the corridors. On the day of the Kasuga Festival, the imperial envoy performs ritual hand-washing here.)

**The First-Rank Bridge (Ichinoi-hashi)** (Refers to the bridge inside the two-storied gate [rōmon].)

**The Second-Rank Bridge (Ninoi-hashi)** (Refers to the bridge in front of the rōmon.)

From the *Kasuga Koki*, a poem composed about the First-Rank Bridge:
　　　Pray, all people — for in the midst of this transient world's glory, may the path of health, long life, and happiness be yours.

**The First Torii** (*Hōeiki* states:* This gate is called the Nirvana Gate [Nehan-mon]. It is also called the Purple Gate [Shimon].)

**The Seat of the Holy Man (Hijiri no Toko)** (Located within the corridor. *Kanbuniki* states:* This is the place where biwa-playing monks seclude themselves to recite the Jishin-kyō [Earth Deity Sutra].)

**The Utsushi-dono (Transfer Hall)** (Located to the north of the Hakkō-ya. *Kanbuniki* states:* This is the place for the temporary enshrinement [ke-sengū] during shrine reconstruction.)

**The South Gate** (Called the Rōmon. *Kanbuniki* states:* In ancient times it was a torii bearing a plaque, but in the summer of the third year of Hōki [772 CE], it was struck by lightning and burned. Thereafter, it became a two-storied gate. It is also said to have been built by Fujiwara no Mitsuyori.)

**The Influence Stone (Yōgō-seki)** (Located to the south of the South Gate; the stone upon which the Red Boy [Aka Dōji] manifested.)

**The Wish-Granting Stone (Nyoi-seki)** (Located to the south of the Influence Stone. In the fourth year of Kōhō [967 CE], a bottomless pit opened in this place. The chief priest Suketo deposited there a complete set of the Great Prajñā Sutra and the purification prayers of ten thousand seats — this is said to be that very spot.)

**The Fushō Bridge** (Also called the Oai Bridge [Oai-ohashi].)

From the *Kasuga Koki*:
　　　How wondrous! Crossing over the Fushō Bridge, to hear the sacred kagura offered in prayer — what joy it brings!

**The Sake Hall (Saka-dono)** (Located to the west of the corridors; the place where ceremonial sake is brewed. Contains two sub-shrines: the Saka-dono Shrine and the Kamado-dono Shrine.)

**The Offering Preparation Hall (Goku-sho)** (Located below the Naishi-mon gate, to the west. Constructed in the first year of Jōgan [859 CE].)

**The Shunki Cherry Tree (Shunki-zakura)** (Located between the Naishi-mon gate and the Goku-sho. *Kanbuniki* states:* This is said to be an unparalleled famous tree without equal under heaven.)

**Kasuga Wakamiya** enshrines Amano Oshikumo no Mikoto, child of Amano Koyane, as recorded in the *Myōhō Yōshū*. (That is, the Wakamiya of Hiraoka Myōjin in Kawachi Province is Amano Oshikumo no Mikoto.) Another record states that according to the records of the Yoshida house, the deity is Ninigi no Mikoto. However, this is the secret teaching known only to the one family of Wakamiya shrine priests and is not disclosed to others. (*Kasuga Shaki*.)

As for the enshrinement of the Wakamiya: on the third day of the third month of the fifth year of Chōhō (1003 CE), the deity manifested between the second and third shrine buildings. Nakatomi no Muraji Koretada, the fifth-generation descendant of Tokikaze, moved the deity to the third shrine and presented the ritual prayers (norito). One hundred and thirty years thereafter, on the twenty-seventh day of the fourth month of the fourth year of Chōshō (1135 CE), Sukefusa, the eighth-generation descendant of Tokikaze, separately constructed a shrine hall and performed the formal enshrinement ceremony. This is the Wakamiya Daimyōjin worshipped today.
