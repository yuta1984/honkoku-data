*[Furthermore, the Great Buddha Hall was not rebuilt, and for approximately one hundred and thirty years the Buddha stood without a hall. At that time, the Venerable Kōkei of Ryūshōin at Tōdai-ji resolved to undertake a restoration and, receiving an imperial decree, solicited donations from people of all stations and rebuilt the Great Buddha Hall. The present Buddha Hall is that building. (The Record of the Venerable Kōkei's Reconstruction of the Great Buddha Hall is noted in a separate account.)]*

To the **left** of the Great Buddha stands **Kannon (Avalokiteśvara)** *(jointly carved by Hōgen Fukyō of Nara and Hokkyō Jōkaku of the capital; donated by Utsunomiya Saemon-no-kami Tomоtsuna)*; to the **right** stands **Kokūzō (Ākāśagarbha)** *(jointly carved by Hōin Kōkei and Hōgen Unkei of Nara; donated by Chikayoshi, Administrator of the Grain Storehouse)*. **Zōchō-ten** *(carved by Hōgen Fukyō of Nara; donated by Hatakeyama Shigetada)*; **Kōmoku-ten** *(carved by Kōkei; donated by Kajiwara Kagetoki)*; **Tamon-ten** *(carved by Jōkaku; donated by Takeda Nobuyoshi)*; **Jikoku-ten** *(carved by Unkei; donated by Ogasawara Nagakiyo)*. The two flanking attendant figures and the Four Heavenly Kings were constructed during the Jishō era [1177–81] and were destroyed during the Eiroku era [1558–70]. The images currently enshrined in the hall are new works.

**Site of the Old Man Saba's Staff** (*Saba-ō tsue no ato*) — Located in front of the eastern corridor. At the time of the founding of the temple complex, there was an old man who sold mackerel (*saba*). Emperor Shōmu summoned him to serve as the lecturer at a great assembly. As he carried eighty mackerel on a pole, the mackerel transformed into the eighty [fascicles of] the *Avatamsaka Sūtra* (Kegon-kyō), and the staff, standing in the ground, suddenly put forth branches and leaves and became a cypress tree (*byakushin*). It was destroyed in the great fire of the Jishō era. The site remains today.

**Bell Tower** (*Shōrō*) — First cast in Tenpyō 4 [732]. Height: 1 *jō* 3 *shaku* 6 *sun*; mouth diameter: 9 *shaku* 3 *bu*; thickness: 8 *sun*. Materials used: 52,680 *kin* of refined copper (*jukudō*), and 2,300 *kin* of refined tin (*hakushaku*). *(As recorded in the Chōya Gunzai.)*

*[The Kanbun-ki states: According to a proverb of Nara: "For grandeur, Tōdai-ji; for form, Byōdō-in; for voice [of its bell], Onjō-ji."]*

**Ranjatai** — This is a sealed imperial treasure of Tōdai-ji. According to the *Nobunaga-ki*: On the twenty-third day of the third month of Tenshō 1 [1573], Lord Nobunaga conveyed to the court his wish to have the Ranjatai. The Dainagon Hino and the Dainagon Asukai were dispatched as imperial envoys to Nanto [Nara]. Lord Nobunaga arrived in Nara on the twenty-seventh day of the same month and took up residence at Tamon. The following day, following established precedent, he had a piece of one *sun* and eight *bu* cut from the Ranjatai. He took two-thirds for himself, keeping one-third, and distributed the remainder among the lords great and small of the various provinces, down to the outer-domain samurai.

**Shunjō-dō** (Hall of Shunjōbō Chōgen) — The Venerable Shunjōbō Chōgen was of the Ki clan by birth, the third son of Takiguchi Sama-no-jō Sueshige, bearing the title Gyōbu Saemon-no-jō. He renounced the world, and in Nin'an 2 [1167] crossed to Song China, ascended Mount Tiantai, and venerated the arhats (*rakan*). At Mingzhou he witnessed a miraculous light emanating from the Buddha's relics (*shari*). After three years he returned to Japan, became a disciple of the Venerable Genku of Kurodani [i.e., Hōnen], and thereafter undertook the fundraising (*kanjin*) campaign for the reconstruction of Tōdai-ji. *(Shakusho.)* The Venerable Chōgen's staff, wooden clogs (*bokuri*), and hat are preserved within the hall. In front of the hall stands a renowned weeping cherry tree (*ito-zakura*).

**Great Iron Cauldron** — Located to the west of the Shunjō-dō. According to tradition, this was the cauldron used to prepare warm water offered to visitors (*settai*) during the Venerable Shunjō's reconstruction of the Great Buddha Hall.

**Nenbutsu-dō** (Nembutsu Hall) — The principal image is Jizō Bosatsu (Kṣitigarbha). Within its abdomen is enshrined a Jizō image one *shaku* in length. This was formerly the personal devotional image of a *yūjo* (courtesan) from Settsu Province named Itojo, who presented it to the Venerable Shunjō. It is also known as the *Yonaki Jizō* ("Night-Crying Jizō").

**Ryōben Sugi** (Ryōben's Cedar) — This is the site where the young Bishop Ryōben once resided. Originally it was a *ichiï* tree (Japanese yew); it fell of its own accord in Tennei 2 [1111], during the reign of Retired Emperor Toba. A cedar grew up in its place, and so it has been colloquially called "Ryōben's Cedar" ever since. *(御順礼記, Gojunrei-ki.)*

The story of Bishop Ryōben is as follows: He was a native of Shiga District in Ōmi Province. His mother prayed to Kannon and gave birth to a son. When the boy was two years old, his mother went out to gather mulberry leaves and left the child in the shade of a tree. Suddenly a great eagle came and seized the child and flew away. She ran after it in alarm, but the wings were swift, and before her eyes the eagle disappeared into the clouds. The mother grieved deeply, but to no avail. Around that time, there lived in Nanto a monk called Bishop Gien, who had come to worship at the Kasuga Myōjin shrine. He found an eagle playing with a small child; startled perhaps by the sound of a human, the eagle abandoned the child in the field and flew away.
