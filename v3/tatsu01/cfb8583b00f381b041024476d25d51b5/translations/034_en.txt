Furthermore, since the Great Buddha Hall had not been rebuilt and the state of having a Buddha but no hall continued for approximately one hundred and thirty years, the Venerable Kōkei [Koukei] of Ryūshōin at Tōdai-ji resolved to undertake a restoration. Receiving an imperial decree, he solicited donations from people of all social ranks and rebuilt the Great Buddha Hall. The current Buddha Hall is the result of his efforts. [The Record of the Venerable Kōkei's Rebuilding of the Great Buddha Hall is recorded separately.]

**Attendant Figures (Wakiji) of the Great Buddha**
- Left: Kanzeon (Avalokiteśvara/Kannon) — [Created jointly by Hōgen Fukyō of Nara and Hōkkyō Jōkaku of the capital. Donated by Utsunomiya Saemon no Kami Tomotsuna.]
- Right: Kokūzō (Ākāśagarbha) — [Created jointly by Hōin Kōkei of Nara and Hōgen Unkei. Donated by the Director of the Imperial Granary, Chikayoshi.]

**Four Heavenly Kings (Shitennō)**
- Zōchōten (Virūḍhaka) — [Created by Hōgen Fukyō of Nara. Donated by Hatakeyama Shigetada.]
- Kōmokuten (Virūpākṣa) — [Created by Kōkei. Donated by Kajiwara Kagetoki.]
- Tamonten (Vaiśravaṇa) — [Created by Jōkaku. Donated by Takeda Nobuyoshi.]
- Jikokuten (Dhṛtarāṣṭra) — [Created by Unkei. Donated by Ogasawara Nagakiyo.]
※ The two attendant figures on the right and the Four Heavenly Kings were originally erected in the Jishō era [1177–1181] and were destroyed in the Eiroku era [1558–1570]. The images currently enshrined in the hall are newly made works.

**Saba-Ō Tsue no Ato (Traces of the Mackerel-Seller Elder's Staff)**
Located in front of the eastern corridor. At the beginning of the construction of the temple complex, there was an old man who sold mackerel. Emperor Shōmu summoned him to serve as the preacher for the great assembly. As the old man was carrying eighty mackerel with his staff, the mackerel transformed into the eighty volumes of the Avatamsaka Sutra (Kegon-kyō), and his staff, planted in the ground, immediately sprouted branches and leaves and became a white cedar tree. It was destroyed in the fire of the Jishō era. The site remains to this day.

**The Bell Tower (Shōrō)**
The bell was first cast in Tenpyō 4 [732]. Height: 1 jō 3 shaku 6 sun; diameter of mouth: 9 shaku 3 bu; thickness: 8 sun. Materials used: 52,680 kin of refined copper and 2,200 kin of white tin. [As recorded in the *Chōya Gunzai*.]

[The *Kanbun-ki* states: A proverb of Nara says, "For grandeur, Tōdai-ji; for form, Byōdō-in; for the sound of its bell, Onjō-ji."]

**Ranjatai (蘭奢待)**
This is a treasure of Tōdai-ji under imperial seal. According to the *Nobunaga-ki*: On the twenty-second day of the third month of Tenshō 1 [1573], when Lord Nobunaga expressed his desire to obtain the Ranjatai, the Major Counselor Hino and the Major Counselor Asukai were sent as imperial envoys to Nanto [Nara]. Lord Nobunaga arrived in Nara on the twenty-seventh of the same month and lodged at Tamon. The following day, the Ranjatai was cut according to the established tradition—one sun and eight bu was cut; two-thirds was kept by Nobunaga himself, and the remainder was distributed among great and lesser lords of various provinces as well as outside vassals.

**Shunjōdō Hall**

The Venerable Chōgen Shunjōbō's family name was Ki. He was the third son of Takiguchi Sama no Jō Sueshige and came from a family of Gyōbu Saemon [officials]. In Nin'an 2 [1167], he traveled to Song-dynasty China, where at Mount Tiantai he paid reverence to an Arhat and received a divine revelation, witnessing the miraculous light of sacred relics. After three years he returned to Japan and became a disciple of Genkū Shōnin [Hōnen] of Kurodani. Subsequently, he undertook the campaign to solicit donations for the rebuilding of Tōdai-ji. [*Shaku-sho*] The Venerable Chōgen's staff, wooden clogs, sedge hat, and other personal effects are kept in the hall. Before the hall stands a celebrated weeping cherry tree (ito-zakura).

**The Great Iron Cauldron**
Located to the west of Shunjōdō Hall. According to tradition, this was the cauldron used for the ritual of offering hospitality (settai) during the Venerable Chōgen's rebuilding of the Great Buddha Hall.

**Nembutsu-dō (Hall of Nembutsu)**
The principal object of worship is Jizō Bodhisattva (Kṣitigarbha). Within the body of this image resides a Jizō figure of one shaku in length. This was originally the personal devotional image of a courtesan in Settsu Province named Itome, who offered it to the Venerable Chōgen. It is also known as Yaon-ya-zō.

**Rōben Sugi (Rōben Cedar)**

This is the site where Bishop Rōben resided as a young child. Originally there was a sawtooth oak (kunugi) tree here, but in Taiei 2 [1522] during the reign of the Toba In, it fell of its own accord. A cedar tree grew up in its place, and so it became commonly known as the "Rōben Cedar." [*Go-junrei-ki*]

Now, Bishop Rōben was a native of Shiga District, Ōmi Province. His mother prayed to Kannon and gave birth to a son. When the boy was two years old, his mother left him beneath a tree while she went to gather mulberry leaves. Suddenly a great eagle swooped down, seized the child, and flew away. She cried out in alarm and ran after it, but she was no match for its wings and, before her eyes, it disappeared into the clouds. The mother grieved deeply but to no avail. At that time in Nanto [Nara], there was a monk called Bishop Gien [Gien Sōjō]. When he went to pay his respects at the Kasuga Myōjin [shrine], he saw an eagle playing with a young child. Startled perhaps by the sound of human voices, the eagle left the child in the field and fled. Though the monk was greatly puzzled, he took the child in and raised him.
