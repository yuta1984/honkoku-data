**Tōnomine** (located in Toichi District; the peak rises high, lush with verdant trees and grasses, a place of deep seclusion and mystery)

It has also been written as: Tafunomne 〔Nihon Shoki〕, Ōmu 〔Nihon Shoki〕, Danpō 〔Engi〕, Tamu 〔Hokke Genki〕, and Danbu 〔Zōga Muki〕.

〔Man'yōshū〕
> Breaking off a branch—
> the mountain mists of Tafu lie thick,
> how deeply they gather!
> At the Hosokawa shallows
> the waves stir and surge.
>
> — Prince Toneri

Tōnomine is recorded in the *Shakusho* as "Wutai Mountain" (Five Terrace Mountain). To the east lies the high mountains of Ise, to the west Kongōzan, to the south Kinpusen, to the north Miwa Mountain, and at the center stands Danpō — truly a sacred grotto of the immortals, no different from the Five Sacred Peaks (*Wuyue*) of China 〔Kasei-ki〕.

There are three routes to Tōnomine: to the east, Kurahashi is fifty-odd *chō* away; to the west, Hosokawa is thirty-seven *chō*; to the north, Kitayama is forty-nine *chō*. The northern route through Kitayama is said in the *Ryakki* to have fallen out of use by now.

**Danzan Myōraku-ji Gokoku-in** was founded by the monk Jōe. At the center of the main hall stands 〔lacuna〕 the Great Weaver's Cap (Daishokkwan) Lord Kamatari; to the left is the monk Jōe, and to the right is Lord Tankai (Fujiwara no Fuhito). There are also a lecture hall, a *jōgyō* hall (constant-practice hall), a *kanjō* hall (initiation hall), a *hongan* hall (vow hall), a *goma* hall (fire ritual hall), a bell tower, a *rinzō* (rotating sutra repository), a treasure storehouse, and forty-two monastic quarters. The shrine dedicated to the posthumously appointed Grand Chancellor of State, Senior First Rank, Fujiwara no Ason Kamatari-kō stands to the east of the main hall, with a worship hall, a tower gate, and eight small shrines.

Now, Danzanis so named because Prince Naka no Ōe 〔Emperor Tenji〕 and Nakatomi no Kamako no Muraji joined forces to devise a stratagem to slay Kurakuri 〔the minister Iruka〕 and bring peace to the realm.

【Left page】

Leading the prince up to the peak of Kurahashi Mountain, east of Shiki, beneath the wisteria blossoms, they discussed their plan to quell the disorder and restore order. The prince was greatly pleased and declared: "If I ascend to the throne, I shall change your surname to Fujiwara." Because this was the place where they held this fateful discussion (*katarahi*), it came to be called *Kataraino-mine* (the Peak of Dialogue). It is also said that the name *Tafunomne* likewise came into use at this time 〔*Gojunreiki*〕.

The name "Ryūgaku" (Dragon Peak) derives from the fact that when this mountain's shape is viewed from Otowa-no-mine, it appears like a dragon rearing up and turning its head back 〔lacuna〕, with its head lying low while its tail and feet seem to move — hence this name 〔lacuna〕.

The **Thirteen-Story Pagoda** was first built by the monk Jōe and is the very origin of this temple. The bones of the Great Weaver's Cap (Lord Kamatari) are enshrined beneath the foundation of the pagoda. As for the history of the original pagoda: in the fourth year of Hakuchi (653), the monk Jōe crossed to Tang China not only to pursue his studies but also to replicate the thirteen-story pagoda of Seiryōzan Hōchi-in. He had it loaded onto his homeward-bound ship; however, as the pagoda materials were so numerous, one story had to be left behind in China. In the seventh year of Hakuhō (679), in the ninth month, he arrived on Japanese shores and met with his younger brother Fuhito. Fuhito told him that their father, the Great Weaver's Cap, had passed away while Jōe was in Tang, and that he had been buried at Awi Mountain in Settsu Province. The monk said: "I had secretly heard my late father's words — that the peak of Katarahi in Yamato Province is a sacred…"
