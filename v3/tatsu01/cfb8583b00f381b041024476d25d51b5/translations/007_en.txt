Editorial Notes (Hanrei)

· The fifteen districts (gun) within the province have varying territorial extents. Larger districts span two volumes per district, while smaller districts may have five districts consolidated into a single volume. The boundaries of each district are noted in small writing above the border rules as a guide.

· Many of the great temple complexes depicted in this work have histories of over a thousand years since their founding. As times have changed, and during periods of unrest within provinces and districts, many have fallen into ruin or been destroyed by fire. For this reason, the illustrations depict the current scenic appearance of these sites, while their histories and origins are recorded based on old documents and records. The cases of Kōfukuji and Yakushiji temples are examples of this.

· Among the illustrations, there are large figure paintings inserted at intervals. Those depicting the spirit of classical poetry (waka) are intended to convey the scenery and atmosphere of each locale, while those depicting actual events are provided as an aid so that children and beginners may easily understand. The illustrations of Kasugano and Ryōben's Cedar are examples of this.

· Newly constructed halls and newly erected stone monuments and inscriptions of that kind have been omitted here; only those of remarkable elegance and refined taste have been selected for inclusion.

A thousand years have passed, yet the fragrant grasses are ever new.
Wind and mist preserve the magnificent scenery,
And long shall this place draw poets and wanderers to its shores.
