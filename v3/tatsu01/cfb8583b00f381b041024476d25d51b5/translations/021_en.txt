**Inner Precincts Small Shrines** (These are the inner precincts of the Wakamiya Shrine; there are two shrines.)

- **Tajikarao-no-Kami Shrine** (Enshrined to the south.)
- **Tsūkō-no-Kami Shrine** (This is the spirit of Nakatomi no Sukefusa Ason. After transferring the Wakamiya Shrine to this location, Sukefusa passed away on the 24th day of the 12th month of Ninpei 2 [1152]. Twenty-seven years later, in Jishō 2 [1178], following a divine oracle, he came to be venerated as Tsūkō-no-Kami ["the God of Communication/Mediation"]. Also at this shrine is something called the "Kasuga Mandala," a picture based on a dream vision of Lord Motomichi of Fukenji-dono during the Juei era [1182–1185].)

---

**Outer Precincts Small Shrines** (These are the outer precincts of the Wakamiya Shrine.)

- **Hirose Shrine** (Popularly known as the Kishimojin ["Demon Mother Goddess"] shrine.)
- **Kakehashi Shrine** (Enshrining a deity of Katsuragi.)
- **Sanjūhasshō Shrine** (Enshrining Kannyamato Iwarehiko no Mikoto [Emperor Jimmu], Izanagi no Mikoto, and Izanami no Mikoto.)
- **Saraki Shrine** (Enshrining Hiruko-no-Kami.)
- **Kii Shrine** (Located to the south of the outer precincts; four deities enshrined: Hi no Mae no Kami, Isotakeru no Kami, Ōyatsuhime no Kami, and Kotsuhime no Kami.)
- **Benzaiten Shrine** (According to the Hōei records, this Benten was petitioned [kanjō] from Tenkawa [Heavenly River Shrine] by Kōbō Daishi [Kūkai]. To the east of this is a place where the Great Master performed goma [fire rituals].)

---

**The Resting Stone** (Located to the south of the Sanjūhasshō Shrine. It is said to be the spot where the Eminent Monk Gedatsu paid reverence to the deity. Some also say it was the Eminent Monk Myōe. When Gedatsu Shōnin invited the deity to his hermitage at Kasagi, the deity appeared in the form of a child and came before the monk, bestowing a divine poem. This account appears in the *Shasekishū*.)

> *Wa yukan / yuki te mamoran / Hannyādai / Shaka no mi-nori no / aran kagiri wa*
> ("I shall go forth and guard the Platform of Prajñā, for as long as the sacred Dharma of Śākyamuni shall endure.")

---

**Worship Hall (Haiden)** (According to the Kanbun records, on the 14th day of the 3rd month of Hōan 3 [1122], during the reign of Retired Emperor Toba, kagura music was performed in this hall for the first time. Also, in Chōshō 1 [1132], during the reign of Emperor Sutoku, the gallery of the worship hall was first constructed. A poem composed at that time reads:)

> *Mezurashiya / kyō no Kasuga no / yaotome wo / kami mo ureshito / omowazarameya*
> ("How wondrous! Even the gods must surely rejoice to see the eight sacred maidens of Kasuga today." — Fujiwara no Tadafusa)

(The portrait-poems of the poetic immortals [kasen] in the Haiden were brushed by Retired Emperor Gokōmyō himself, with the robes and garments painted in color; the poems themselves were written by each respective household. These were quietly placed on the paving stones of the Great Shrine. The official clerk on duty at the time found this suspicious and reported it to the imperial court. The Emperor then issued a decree that these were offerings to the Four Deities [Shishō Myōjin], and so they were placed in this worship hall. The folding screens were donated by Takimoto-bō of Yawata.)

---

**The O-rō (Sacred Gallery)** (According to the Kanbun records, the area to the north of the Haiden is called the O-rō. Buddhist relics [busshari] are kept inside, symbolizing the dual-aspect Shinto [Ryōbu Shintō]. The space between this gallery and the Haiden is called the *Hosodono* ["Narrow Hall"].)

**Hai-no-Ya (Hall of Worship)** (According to the Kanbun records, this is located to the east of the Haiden. The shrine officials of the Wakamiya take turns on duty here. This structure was built in Jishō 2 [1178], during the reign of Retired Emperor Takakura. Each year, the *Takigi Nō* [firelight Noh theater] is performed for two days at the South Great Gate, and on the third day, two days of Noh are performed on this stone stage. When the Kongō Tayū is on duty, sacred sake [miki] is offered from the Wakamiya Sanctuary at the Haiden. When other Tayū are on duty,)

**The Five Buildings** (The New Building, the Tile-Roofed Building, the Upper Building, the Western Building, and the Original Lecture Building — these five. Adding the Chūin Building makes six buildings in total.)

- **New Building [Shinzō-ya]** (According to the Kanbun records, this is located to the west of the Original Lecture Building. It is also popularly called the "Kasuga Divine Manifestation Building [Kasuga Suijaku no Ya]." The principal Buddha image is Amida Nyorai, said to be the lifetime work of An'ami. There is also a Daikokuten image, also by An'ami, which was a possession of Matsura Chōja; it is said that on the bottom of its feet is the seal of Shinran Shōnin.)

- **Tile-Roofed Building [Kawara-no-Ya]** (According to the Kanbun records, a Śākyamuni Buddha image is enshrined here.)

- **Upper Building [Kami-no-Ya]** (According to the Kanbun records, the Sixteen Benevolent Deities of the Dharma [Gohō Jūroku Zenjin] were petitioned here.)

- **Western Building [Nishi-no-Ya]** (A Jizō Bodhisattva image is placed here.)

- **Original Lecture Building [Moto-Dangi-ya]** (According to the Kanbun records, the principal object of worship here is the ten-volume *Vijñaptimātratā-siddhi-śāstra* [Yuishiki-ron] composed by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva of Busshōkoku, used as the body of the Buddha. In the treasure repository to the east are numerous precious artifacts; every year on the 15th day of the 7th month these are brought out for all people to venerate. The Chūzan Ikō [senior scholarly monk Chūzan] was bestowed a fan in the Emperor's own hand. This fan dates from the 3rd year of Ōwa [963], during the reign of Emperor Murakami, when a Lotus Sutra lecture [Hokkekō] was held in the Seiryōden. Twenty-five eminent monks from the Southern Capital [Nara] and Northern Ridge [Mount Hiei] engaged in five days and five nights of debate. The monk Chūzan's words bloomed with eloquence and revealed the true fruit of doctrine, and the Emperor, in deep admiration, bestowed this fan. It bears a poem in the Emperor's own hand:)

> *Kimi ga yo wa / Ama no Hagoromo / mare ni kite / natsu tomo tsukinu / iwaho naru ran*
> ("Your Majesty's reign — like the Celestial Feather Robe, rarely donned, yet never worn out even in summer — must be enduring as a great rock.")

> *Shiratama ka / onore ga iro wa / sorenagara / momiji ni okeba / kurenai no tsuyu*
> ("Is it a white jewel? Its color unchanged, yet when placed upon the autumn leaves, it becomes a crimson drop of dew.")

(Further treasures include: the *Lotus Sutra* hand-copied by Kōbō Daishi in fine characters, eight volumes on one sheet of paper; the "One-Character Incense Flower Triple Homage Five Great Mahāyāna Sutras" by Lord Nijō Nobufusa [also seen in the *Taiheiki*], together with the *Vijñaptimātratā-siddhi-śāstra* in the same hand; a five-toned armor with lacing by Minamoto no Yoshiie, together with a crimson-laced cuirass and front chest plate; armor [donated by Minamoto no Yoshitsune]; a votive prayer document by Taira no Munemori; armor [donated by Taira no Koremochi]; armor of Kusunoki Masashige; a summoning bell [said to have been brought from the Palace of Enma the King of Hell by Monk Hōzō Sōzu]; and a folding screen painted by Sumiyoshi Hōgen depicting the horse races at Kamo.)

- **Matsu-no-Ya (Pine Building)** (This is the building of the Kubō household [Ashikaga shogunate].)
- **Tajima-no-Ya (Tajima Building)** (This is the building of the Konoe family. These two buildings today survive only as their former sites.)
- **Funato-no-Ya (Harbor/Crossroads Building)** (This is the building of the Kujō family. According to the Kanbun records, Lord Tsukimichi Kanezane first had it built in the 1st month of Kenkyū 1 [1190]. There is a Funato Shrine here, which protects those at sea.)
- **Sugi-no-Ya (Cedar Building)** (This is the building of the Ichijōin Monzeki [the chief abbot of Ichijōin temple].)
- **Tsubaki-no-Ya (Camellia Building)** (This is the building of the Head Priest of the Great Shrine.)
