*[Furthermore, as gold was being collected to gild the great image of Vairocana in resplendent gold, there was at that time no gold to be found in our land. The Emperor prayed to Kongōzaō [the Diamond Storehouse King] of Mount Kinpusen in Yamato Province, [hoping] to obtain gold from that mountain to assist in the gilding. Bishop Rōben [Ryōben], having received the imperial edict, prayed with utmost sincerity, and received a divine oracle from Zaō. Following that miraculous sign, he traveled to Seta-no-sato in Ōmi Province, where he found an old man fishing upon a rock. The bishop asked the old man, "Who are you?" The old man replied, "I am the deity Hira Myōjin. This place is a sacred site of Kannon [Avalokiteśvara]," and as soon as he had spoken these words, he vanished as though dissolved into thin air. Heeding the divine promise, [Rōben] built a hermitage and fashioned an image of Nyoirin [Cintāmaṇicakra] Kannon, offering prayers. This is the Kannon of the present-day Ishiyama-dera Temple. (Shakusho)]*

*[Before long, in the second month of Tenpyō 21 [749], gold was for the first time presented from Mutsu Province [in the northeast], at which the Emperor was deeply moved and delighted. In the fourth month of the same year, the era name was changed, and it was styled Tenpyō Kampō 1. Later, the character "Kan [感]" was changed to "Shō [勝]." (Shoku Nihongi; poem composed at this time:)]*

**Man'yōshū:**

> *Suberaki no / miyo sakae n to / azumanaru / Michinoku yama ni / kogane hana saku*
> ("So that the Emperor's reign may flourish — upon the mountains of far Michinoku in the East, flowers of gold are blooming.")
> — Yakamochi [Ōtomo no Yakamochi]

*[Furthermore, an additional nine hundred ryō of gold was presented from Mutsu Province. (Shoku Nihongi) For this achievement, the Governor of Mutsu Province, Keifuku [Miyake no Keifuku], was granted the court rank of Ginsei Kōroku Taifu [a Chinese-style honorific rank]. (Shakusho)]*

*[◦ One hundred and seven years after the original construction, on the twenty-third day of the fifth month of Saikō 5 [858], the head of the great Vairocana image fell of its own accord. This is recorded in the Teio Hennen-ki [Imperial Chronology], Chōmei's Hōjōki [An Account of My Hut], the Heike Monogatari [The Tale of the Heike], and other works.]*

*[◦ The second reconstruction: On the twenty-eighth day of the twelfth month of Jishō 4 [1180], in the reign of the Retired Emperor Takakura, [the temple] was consumed in the flames of the military attack launched by Lord Taira no Shigehira, and the entire temple compound was reduced to ashes. Even the image of Vairocana — peerless throughout the three countries [of India, China, and Japan] — had its head melt and fall away. (Heike Monogatari) With the conviction that the temple absolutely must be restored, the Retired Emperor Go-Shirakawa issued edicts to the Right General Lord Yoritomo [Minamoto no Yoritomo] and to the Venerable Chōgen of Shunjōbō [Tōdai-ji's restoration master] of Daigo, and the restoration was accomplished. On the twelfth day of the third month of Kenkyū 6 [1195], a dedication ceremony for the Great Buddha was held. The sovereign proceeded in imperial procession, and Lord Minamoto no Yoritomo attended in worship, donating ten thousand koku of grain, ten thousand ryō of gold, and one thousand bolts of fine silk — all of which is recorded in the Azuma Kagami [Mirror of the East].]*

*[◦ The third reconstruction: On the tenth day of the tenth month of Eiroku 10 [1567], in the reign of the Retired Emperor Ōgimachi, [the temple] was caught in the flames of a military attack by Matsunaga Danjō no Chū Hisahide, the lord of Shigi Castle in this province, and the Great Buddha Hall was consumed in smoke. The head of the Vairocana Buddha also burned and fell. After that, as the world was in great turmoil, there were no craftsmen willing or able to undertake the restoration, and many years passed by. **The Humorous Account (Kokkei-dan) states:**

The *manzai* [a traditional comic performance] [tradition] of the southern capital [Nara] — at a distance of some three *ri* to the southwest — there are two villages: Kubota and Kinuo [Choo]. Because [the manzai performers] come from these villages, there are two schools: the Kubota school and the Kinuo school.

**[Haiku]**

> *Manzai ya / chie naki kao to / miete yoshi*
> ("The manzai performers — they appear to have no wisdom at all — and that is precisely what makes them good!")
> — Shōseki
