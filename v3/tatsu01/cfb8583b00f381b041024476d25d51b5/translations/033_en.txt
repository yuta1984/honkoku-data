*[Furthermore, as gold was being gathered to gild the great image of Vairocana (Śākyamuni) in gold, there was as yet no gold to be found in Japan. The Emperor prayed to Kongō Zaō [the principal deity] of Mount Kinpusen in Yamato Province, hoping to obtain gold from that mountain to assist with the gilding. He issued an imperial decree to Bishop Ryōben, bidding him pray with all sincerity. Ryōben prayed devoutly, whereupon an oracle was received from Zaō. Following the divine omen, he proceeded to Seta-no-sato in Ōmi Province, where he found an old man fishing on a rock. The bishop asked: "Who might you be, old one?" The old man replied: "I am the deity Hira Myōjin. This place is a sacred land of Kannon," and with those words, he vanished as if dissolved. Following the divine pledge, [Ryōben] built a hermitage and fashioned an image of Nyoirin [Kannon] and offered prayers. This is the Kannon of the present-day Ishiyama-dera Temple. *(Shakusho)* Before long, in the second month of Tenpyō 21 [749], gold was first sent as tribute from Ōshū (Mutsu Province). The Emperor was greatly moved, and in the fourth month of the same year, the era name was changed to Tenpyō Kampō 1. Later, the character *kan* (感) was changed to *shō* (勝). *(Shoku Nihongi. The following poem was composed at this time)*]*

**Man'yōshū**

> *Suberaki no / miyo sakaen to / azuma naru / Michinoku-yama ni / kogane hana saku*
> ("So that our Sovereign's reign may flourish — in the eastern mountains of Michinoku, golden flowers bloom.")
> — [Ōtomo no] Yakamochi

*[Furthermore, an additional nine hundred ryō of gold was sent as tribute from Ōshū. *(Shoku Nihongi)* In recognition of this achievement, Kyōfuku, the Governor of Mutsu Province, was awarded the court rank of Ginsei Kōroku Taifu. *(Shakusho)*]*

- One hundred and seven years after the initial construction, on the twenty-third day of the fifth month of Saikō 5 [858], the head of the great Vairocana image fell of its own accord. This is recorded in the *Teio Hennenki*, the *Hōjōki*, the *Heike Monogatari*, and other texts.

- The second reconstruction: On the twenty-eighth day of the twelfth month of Jishō 4 [1180], during the reign of the Retired Emperor Takakura, the temple complex was consumed in the conflagration set by Lord Taira no Shigehira. It was reduced to ashes. Even the Vairocana image — matchless across the three countries [Japan, China, and India] — had its head melted and fallen away. *(Heike Monogatari)* Declaring that there must surely be a restoration, Retired Emperor Go-Shirakawa issued an imperial decree to the Right Grand General Yoritomo and to the Venerable Chōgen of Shunjōbō of Daigo, ordering a reconstruction. On the twelfth day of the third month of Kenkyū 6 [1195], a great dedication ceremony for the Great Buddha was held. The reigning emperor made an imperial visit, and Minamoto no Yoritomo came in worship, donating ten thousand *koku* of rice [*hachimoku*], ten thousand *ryō* of gold, and one thousand bolts of finest silk — as recorded in the *Azuma Kagami*.

- The third reconstruction: On the tenth day of the tenth month of Eiroku 10 [1567], in what is recorded in the *Ōginin* [records], the Great Buddha Hall was reduced to smoke in the conflagration set by Matsunaga Danjo-no-chū Hisahide, lord of Shigi Castle in this province [Yamato]. The head of the Vairocana Buddha was also burned and fell. After that, the world fell into disorder, and there was no craftsman to take up the task of restoration. The years and months piled up, but a warrior of this province named Yamada Dōan lamented this state of affairs and threw open his personal wealth to undertake the repairs. **According to the *Kokkei-dan* [Book of Humorous Tales]:**

*Manzai* [comic performers of the New Year] come from two villages called Kubota and Hashio, located approximately three *ri* [about twelve kilometers] to the southwest of Nanto [Nara]. Because they originate from these two villages, there are two lineages: the Kubota school and the Hashio school.

> *Manzai ya / chie naki kao to / mie te yoshi*
> ("Manzai performers — though they appear to have no wit at all, that is precisely their charm!")
> — Shōseki
