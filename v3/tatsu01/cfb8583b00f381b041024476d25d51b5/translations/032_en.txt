In the tenth month of the eighteenth year of the same era [Tenpyō 18, 746], Emperor Shōmu, the Retired Empress Genshō, and Empress Kōmyō proceeded to Konshō-ji Temple for the worship of the great image. More than 15,700 votive lamps were lit before and behind the Buddha. *(Shoku Nihongi)* It is recorded that the presiding officiant (dōshi) was the Brahman Bishop [Bodhisena], and that the prayer-vow officiant (shugon-shi) was Bishop Gyōki. *(Azuma Kagami)* From the very beginning, the casting was repeated eight times in total *(Chōya Gunzai)*, and finally in the tenth month of the first year of Tenpyō Shōhō [749], all the work was brought to full completion. In the fourth month of the fourth year [Tenpyō Shōhō 4, 752], the Eye-Opening Ceremony (kaigen kuyō) was held. *(Shoku Nihongi)* The presiding officiant was Bishop Bodai [Bodhisena], the prayer-vow officiant was Vinaya Master Dōsai, the lecturer was Ryūson, and the reader was Empuku, as is recorded. *(Teio Hennen)*

*[Prior to all this, at the time of the first dedication ceremony, an imperial decree was issued appointing Bishop Gyōki as the presiding officiant. However, the bishop declined, saying: "Such a role as officiant is not fitting for a person of my ordinary clerical standing. I have heard that a monk from a foreign land will soon come to our country — he would be truly worthy to serve as officiant." In Tenpyō 8 [736], when the time of his arrival finally came, [Gyōki] prepared his boat and rowed out from the shores of Naniwa [present-day Osaka] to greet him. The sleeves of his Buddhist robes fluttered in the breeze just as flowers sway in the wind, and the sound of music carried over the waves of the sea. From far beyond the offshore waves, a ship could be seen drawing near — each party must have thought, "Is that not the very one we seek?" — for they rowed and paddled their vessels swiftly toward one another, and before long had drawn close. They smiled at each other, as though reuniting with an old friend. *(Teio Hennen)*]*

> *Reizan no / Shaka no omae ni / chigiri te shi / shinnyo kuchisezu / aimitsuru kana*
> ("On Mount Gridhrakūta, before the Lord Śākyamuni, we made our vow — and that true spirit, undecayed, has brought us to meet at last.")
> — Bishop Gyōki

> *Kabirāvastu ni / tomo ni chigirishi / kai arite / Monju no mikao / aimitsuru kana*
> ("In Kapilavastu we made our pledge together — and with good fruit, I have now beheld the august face of Mañjuśrī [i.e., Gyōki].")
> — Brahman [Bishop Bodhisena]

### Illustration of the Gilt-Bronze Lantern Before the Great Buddha Hall
*(大仏殿前金銅燈炉図, Daibutsuden-mae Kondō Tōro no Zu)*

> *There is an inscription on the pillar.*

This is an octagonal gilt-bronze lantern (金銅燈炉, kondō tōro) cast by Chin Nakei [Chen Hejing] of Song [China]. On four of its faces are images of Buddhas, and on the other four faces are figures of animals (獣形, jūkei). The inscription is found on the bronze pillar and is recorded in a separate note.
