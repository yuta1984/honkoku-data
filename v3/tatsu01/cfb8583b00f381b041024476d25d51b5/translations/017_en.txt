Kasuga Grand Shrine: Four Shrines of the Great Deity (enshrined at the foot of Mount Mikasa; eleven ri from the imperial capital. As recorded in the Engishiki Jinmyōchō: "Kasuga Festival Deities, Four Seats")

《Zoku Kokin Wakashū: Sacred Verse of the Kasuga Great Deity》
　　Know me as I am — the Buddha Śākyamuni emerged into this world to illuminate it as the bright moon illuminates the night.

In the age of the gods long ago, when the Heavenly Prince (Emperor) first entered the Land of Reed Plains (Ashihara no Nakatsukuni), wicked deities barred his path. Therefore, Futsunushi no Mikoto (Katori Myōjin) and Takemikazuchi no Mikoto (Kashima Myōjin) were dispatched as subjugation envoys, bearing the spear of governance. They pushed open the Heavenly Rock Cave, illuminated the eternal darkness, and soothed the sorrows of all people. The deep covenant between Amaterasu Ōmikami and Amenokoyane no Mikoto was made manifest: like the unceasing flow of the Mimosuso River, and the gentle spring breezes of Mount Mikasa, their divine manifestations and benevolent traces brought peace to the land and aided the imperial rule. Looking up at the shrine precinct, one sees a two-storied gate tower, three corridors, the brilliant glow of night lanterns, and the four shrine halls standing in splendid array.

◦The first shrine hall to the east enshrines Takemikazuchi no Kami. (Also known by the names Takeikaduchi no Shin, Tatefutsu no Shin, and Toyofutsu no Shin. This deity was born from the blood that dripped from the sword hilt when Izanagi no Mikoto cut the fire deity Kagatsuchi. Per the Nihon Shoki.) The deity first descended at Shiogama Bay in Mutsu Province, subdued wicked deities, and then appeared at Kashima in Hitachi Province. On the twenty-first day of the sixth month of Jingo Keiun 1 (767 CE), the deity departed Kashima, and on the seventh day of the twelfth month of the same year, arrived at Komou no Nakayama. The attendants in the procession were two individuals, Tokikaze and Hideyuki. (The descendants of Tokikaze became the stewards of the Shrine, and the descendants of Hideyuki became the stewards of shrine construction.) The servant Otonomaru was also brought along. (Ancestor of the Negi priests; Umeki clan.) At that time, when Tokikaze and Hideyuki offered provisions, they presented chestnuts that were nearby,

and the deity was so moved that the deity granted them the surname "Uekuri" (planted chestnut). The descendants of Tokikaze and Hideyuki, bearing the surname Nakatomi, thus trace the origin of the Uekuri clan name. On the ninth day of the first month of the second year (768 CE), the deity moved to Abe Mountain in Yamato Province, and on the ninth day of the eleventh month of the same year, the deity's divine traces came to rest on Mount Mikasa. (The above is the general purport of the Kasuga Koki. There are points of difference from the account in the Kuji Kongen. The Seisuiki states that the deity rode upon a white deer with a sakaki tree set upon the saddle, covered by five-colored clouds, above which five divine mirrors appeared, shining brilliantly, as the deity moved to Mount Mikasa.)

◦The second shrine hall enshrines Futsunushi no Mikoto (also known as Iwainushi no Kami or Iwai no Ushi. This deity was formed from the blood that dripped from the blade of the sword with which Izanagi no Mikoto cut the fire deity. The deity descended at the small shore of Isotasa in Izumo Province and subdued the wicked deities of that land. Per the Nihon Shoki.) This is Katori Myōjin of Shimōsa Province. The deity moved to Mount Mikasa in Jingo Keiun 2 (768 CE). (Per the Kasuga Koki.)

◦The third shrine hall enshrines Amenokoyane no Mikoto (the founding deity of the Nakatomi clan. Also known by the names Tsuhayamusubi no Mikoto, Koichichi Musubi no Mikoto, and Kototama Musubi no Mikoto; also said to be the child of the deity Kotokotomussubi. Per the Nihon Shoki. When Amaterasu Ōmikami shut herself inside the Heavenly Rock Cave, plunging the world into eternal darkness, this deity, together with Futodama no Mikoto, uprooted a sacred sakaki tree of five hundred branches from the Heavenly Mount Kagu, hanging the five-hundred-bead jewel necklace of Yasakani on the upper branches, the eight-span mirror on the middle branches, and the blue and white cloth offerings on the lower branches. Together with all the deities, they prayed in concert, whereupon the cave was opened, the clouds of darkness cleared, and light was restored to distinguish night from day. Per the Nihon Shoki.)

This is Hiraoka Myōjin of Kawachi Province. The enshrinement took place on a day of the Dog (tsuchinoe saru) in the eleventh month of the fourth year of Emperor Kōtoku (the 37th human emperor), preceding the other three deities in moving to Mount Mikasa. (The above appears in the Kasuga Sha-ki. The Kuji Kongen records that all four deities were enshrined together in Jingo Keiun 2.)

The fourth shrine hall enshrines the Princess Great Deity (Hime Ōmikami; also known as Ōhirume no Muchi or Amaterasu Ōhirume no Mikoto). She is the same as Amaterasu Ōmikami of the Inner Shrine at the Isuzu River in Ise Province. (Per the Kasuga Sha-ki. Another theory holds that she is a divided spirit of Amaterasu Ōmikami; yet another theory says that the fourth deity, Hime Ōmikami, is the princess daughter of Takemikazuchi no Mikoto and the wife of Amenokoyane no Mikoto.)
