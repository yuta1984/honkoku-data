**Karika-no-yama (Karika Mountain)** [Also known by another name for Kasuga Mountain. In the *Man'yōshū* it is written as "Kasuga-no-yama" (備香能山).]
*(Utamakura — a poetic place name)*
　At the sound of the wild geese crying, from tomorrow, Karika Mountain must have begun to dye itself in autumn colors.

**Hongū-take (Main Shrine Peak)** [According to the *Kanmon-ki*: It lies to the south of the Wakamiya of Nagao. At its foot is the Ukigumo Shrine. It is the first and foremost sanctuary of Kasuga. The deity Takemikazuchi-no-kami first descended here on the ninth day of the eleventh month of the second year of Jingo-keiun (768), and in the first month of the fourth year of the same era was transferred to the present shrine. For this reason, it is also called Hongū-yama and Hongū-ga-take. The Hongū Shrine and the Mizuya Shrine are located here.]

**Takamato-yama (Takamato Mountain)** [A mountain standing alongside to the south of Mikasa-yama.]
*(New Kokinshū)*
　On the field paths of Takamato, the tips of the bamboo grass rustle and stir — the winter wind must be blowing today.　*Fujiwara no Mototoshi*
*(Shokugosen-shū)*
　On the autumn wind of Takamato Mountain in Yamato, the moonlight rises over the cloudless peak.　*Retired Emperor Go-Toba*
*(Gyokuyō-shū)*
　The white dew, too, spills and glows with fragrance — the autumn bush clover on the fields of Takamato is now at its finest.　*Retired Emperor Go-Saga*
*(Shoku Senzai-shū)*
　The autumn bush clover on the field paths of Takamato has bloomed — its fragrance must be drifting onto the sleeves of travelers passing by.　*Sōjō Gyōi*
*(Shin Shūi-shū)*
　In the direction where the spring haze trails, beneath the evening moon, Takamato Mountain must be lit up clearly and beautifully.　*Akahito*

**Byakugō-ji (Byakugō Temple)** [Located on Takamato-yama.] Built by the wish of Emperor Tenji, with a principal image of Amida Buddha, carved by the Kasuga sculptor.
　The image in the Enma Hall is the work of Chancellor Sugawara (Sugawara no Michizane); the Jizō statue is the work of Ono no Takamura. A disciple of Kōshō Bosatsu of Saidai-ji, a monk named Dōshō, traveled to Tang China and brought back the complete Buddhist canon (*issaikyō*), which was then enshrined in this temple.

**[Left page]**

**Yake-Kasuga (Burned Kasuga)** [According to the *Yamato-shi*: The Ōyake district has already fallen into ruin; it survives in the village of Byakugō-ji. For this reason it is now called Yake-Kasuga. According to temple records: One shrine is dedicated to Kasuga Myōjin, and another is said to be a shrine enshrining Hōmyōbō Ninkaku. According to the *Shunyo Jinki*: The Great Deity descended (*yōgō*) to this location from Nara (Heijō), and thereafter moved to Hongū-take. After the transfer of the shrine, lightning fire fell and burned the shrine, which is why it is also called Yake-Kasuga (Burned Kasuga). According to the shrine record: The rice field before this shrine is called Sugi-machi. When the Great Deity scattered cedar (*sugi*) leaves, they became rice. Even today, it is the custom at the rice-planting ceremony to take pine needles grown in this place in place of cedar, and plant them.]

**Onohe-no-miya (Onohe Shrine)** [Located on Takamato-yama. According to the *Yamatoji-ki*: The site of Onohe-no-miya is in the area above Furuichi. It is also called the Onohe Detached Palace. It is not known to which imperial reign this detached palace belonged. According to the *Shoku Nihongi*: In the ninth month of the first year of Wadō (708), Empress Genmei entered the Kasuga Detached Palace.]
　In the evening dusk, the hem of my sleeve feels cool — the first autumn wind at the Onohe Shrine on Takamato.　*Kamakura Udaijin (Minamoto no Sanetomo)*
*(Shoku Shūi-shū)*
　The traces of Onohe Shrine on Takamato are now gone — no one visits the palace of long ago.　*Jūnii Yukiie*

**Kōsen (Fragrant Mountain)** [According to the *Shaseki-shū*: It is said that Kasuga Myōjin constantly preaches the *Prajñā* (wisdom) sutras here.]
**Narugami-jinja (Thunder Shrine)** [Located on Kōsen. It appears in the *Engishiki* and the *Sandai Jitsuroku*. It is now called Kaminari-no-miya (Thunder Shrine).]
**Takamine (High Peak)** [Located to the south of Hongū-take. According to the *Kanmon-ki*: There are three shrines here. A princess of Prince Toneri took Buddhist vows at this place, established a temple called Kōya-ji, and resided here; the traces of this remain.]
**Hakai-yama** [This probably refers collectively to the three mountains: Mikasa-yama, Takamato-yama, and Wakakusa-yama.]
*(Shin Goshūi-shū)*
　Among the spring colors of Hakai Mountain where waterbirds gather, there stands alone, unmixed, the rock azalea.　*Retired Emperor Juntoku*

**Wakakusa-yama (Young Grass Mountain)** [A mountain standing to the north of Mikasa-yama. According to the *Enpō-ki*: The reason people call this place "Tsuzura Mountain" is perhaps because of the word *tsuzuraori* (winding zigzag path).]
*(Fubokushū)*
　Even now, does a wife lie hidden here? — On the young grass mountain of Kasuga Plain, the warbler sings.　*Prince Nakatsukasa*
*(Utamakura)*
　On Wakakusa Mountain of Kasuga Plain, a pheasant has taken flight — awakened from sleep by the sound of its wings this morning.　*Yoshitada*
