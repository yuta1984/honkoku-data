Regarding why this land is called "Yamato" (大和国): the "Age of the Gods" chapter of the *Nihon Shoki* states "Ōyamatotoyoakitsushima" (割書: Japan is called "Yamato" [耶麻騰]). From the very beginning of the reigns of human emperors, when Emperor Jinmu came to rule the realm, he inherited the legacy of the age of the gods and established his capital at Miyazaki in Hyuga Province. At this time, the realm was in primordial chaos and its borders were not yet fixed. After the Emperor's eastern campaign, he first established his capital at Kashihara Palace in Yamato Province, appointing Utsuhiko as the provincial chieftain (kuni no miyatsuko). For this reason, Yamato Province was the general name for all of Japan and the land where the imperial residence was established; it thus came to be used as a common name for the entire country.

The *Shoku Nihongi* states: "In the ninth year of Emperor Shōmu's Tenpyō era, the name Yamato Province (大倭国) was changed to Ōyamato Province (大養徳国), and in the nineteenth year it was changed back to the old name, Yamato Province (大倭国)" (割書: the *Shūgaishō* states that during the Tenpyō Shōhō era it was again changed to Yamato Province [大和国]). The *Engi Kaidai no Ki* states: "In the primordial beginnings of Yamato Province, there were no dwellings yet, and the people simply lived in the mountains. It is for this reason that it is called 'Yamato' [山戸, mountain dwelling]." The *Shaku Nihongi* states: "At the beginning of the creation of the world, the land was damp and would not dry; as people climbed the mountain paths, their footprints appeared, and so it was called 'Yamato' [山跡, mountain tracks]." The *Zenrin Kokuhōki* states: "The *Hou Han Shu* records that 'the King of Wa resided at Yamato [耶麻堆].' It was presumably because people from this land traveled there and called it 'Yamato' [大倭] that it came to be written thus (and so forth)." The *Nihon Seiki* (by the monk Dōken) states: "It is 'Eastern Court' [東朝, Yamato]. The two characters 大倭 appearing in continuous succession came about because either this court first wrote it and foreign courts followed, or foreign lands named it first and our court adopted it afterward." The *Nihon Shakumyō* (by Kaibara Atsunobu [Ekiken]) states: "When Emperor Jinmu conducted his eastern campaign from Hyuga, he first

traveled up from Naniwa to Hirakata, then crossed Mount Ikoma (Ikomayana) to enter Yamato. Because it was a land beyond (outside) Mount Ikoma, it was called 'Yamato' [山外, outside the mountain]. Because it was a land within the Yodo River, it was called 'Kawachi' [河内, inside the river]. 'Yamato' was thus a name contrasting with Kawachi. Similarly, the northern land behind Mount Ikoma was written as 'Yamashiro Province' [山背国, behind the mountain]. 'Behind' (ushiro) means north."

The *Shoku Nihon Kōki* states: "In the tenth month of the third year of Jōwa, on the己未 day, following the precedent of承前 [the established custom], concerning the order of provinces in the Home Provinces (Kinai), Yamato Province had previously been placed first; however, by imperial decree, following the new regulations, it was changed, and Yamashiro Province was placed first (and so forth)." The *Nihon Shōtō Zu* states: "Yamato Province administers fifteen districts in total; surrounded by mountains, its agricultural yield is ten times [the standard]; there are many famous places and historical sites; it is a province of the highest grade (jōjō)."

Nara belongs to Sōnokami District (添上郡). The *Nihongi* states: "In the tenth year of Emperor Sujin's reign, Takehaniasuhiko and his wife Atahime plotted to overthrow the state and attacked from Yamashiro Province. The imperial forces assembled on Mount Nara (Narayama) and trampled down and leveled (ならした) the vegetation; for this reason the mountain was named Narayama. They also confronted each other in battle across the Wakara River; the people of the time called it the Itomi River (挑河, challenging river) [in waka poetry it is sung as Izumi River; at the time of the construction of Tōdai-ji it was renamed the Kizu River]. The rebel forces were defeated, and the imperial army easily struck down the couple Takehaniasuhiko and Atahime. Here, a sacred vessel (itsube) was reverently enshrined on the Takesuki slope (武鐰坂) at Wani. The itsube is of blue-green ceramic (aoshi) [*Shūchūshō*]. 'Shi' (磁) refers to a sake vessel [*Shirin Saiyōshō*]. It is for this reason that 'aoniyoshi Nara' (青丹よし奈良) and 'aonikitenara' are used as pillow words (makura kotoba) for Nara [there are further esoteric explanations, which should be clarified through the way of poetry]."
