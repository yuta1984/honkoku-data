## Tōdai-ji (東大寺)

*(Adjacent to the north of the Kasuga Shrine. Also known as Daikegonji [Great Huayan Temple], or Kōsetsu Kegonji [according to the Buppō Dentsūki], or Kokubunji [Provincial Temple], or Kōmyōmyō Shitennō Gokoku no Tera [Temple Protecting the Nation by the Four Heavenly Kings of Golden Light], as cited in the Shoku Nihongi.)*

This temple was founded at the behest of Emperor Shōmu, and was completed during the Tenpyō-Shōhō era (749–757). Its doctrinal tradition encompasses all eight Buddhist schools, with the Sanron (Three Treatises) and Kegon (Huayan/Avatamsaka) schools forming its foundation. One might indeed liken it to the Jetavana Garden (Jeta Grove, the famous park donated to the Buddha) — where musk deer slumber among wild pinks and parrots peck at golden peaches.

**West Great Gate** *(According to the Heijō Shiseki Kō, this is the gate to the southwest of Tōdai-ji, located at Kumoi-zaka. It is popularly known as the Kumoi-zaka Gate or the Kokubu Gate. The plaque was brushed by Kōbō Daishi and bears the inscription "Kōmyōmyō Shitennō Gokoku no Tera" [Temple Protecting the Nation by the Four Heavenly Kings of Golden Light]. The border of the plaque displays figures of Brahmā, Indra, and the Four Heavenly Kings; it measures eight shaku in length and six shaku in width. After this gate fell into ruin, the plaque was stored in the Tōdai-ji granary treasure repository. The gate's foundation stones remain to the east of Kumoi-zaka.)*

**South Great Gate** *(The plaque was brushed by Kōbō Daishi. It is said that in former times a certain head administrator of the Tōnan-in precinct declared: "Although this temple embraces all eight Buddhist schools, the Sanron and Kegon are particularly honored — how then can it be limited to Kegon alone?" And with this, he had the plaques of both the west and south gates taken down.)*

**The Great Buddha Hall** *(According to the Chōya Gunzai, the hall stands fifteen jō and six shaku high, is twenty-nine jō east-to-west, and seventeen jō north-to-south. The base platform stands seven shaku high, thirty-two jō and seven shaku east-to-west, and twenty jō and six shaku north-to-south. The inner sanctuary has ninety-six pillars and a ceiling area of three thousand one hundred and twenty units. The surrounding galleries have five hundred and eighty pillars, spanning eighty-five ken east-to-west and one hundred ken north-to-south. These are the dimensions of the original construction carried out during the Tenpyō era.)*

**Principal Icon**: Seated image of Vairocana Buddha (Rushana Butsu). Height: five jō, three shaku, five sun. *(The Shoku Nihongi gives five jō; the Shakusho gives sixteen jō.)* Materials used in casting:熟銅 (refined copper) 73,956 kin; white tin 12,638 kin; refined gold 10,436 ryō; copper (red copper/bronze) 58,620 ryō; charcoal 16,356 koku. These are the materials used in the original casting; for full details, refer to the Chōya Gunzai.

In a former life, the Venerable Rōben had been a monk in China, who, roused by an aspiration to travel to India to practice the Buddha's teachings, journeyed to the River of Flowing Sands (the Liusha River, i.e., the desert streams of Central Asia). Having no funds to pay the ferryman's fare, he could not cross. At that time, the Emperor had been the ferryman, and, taking pity on the monk's sincere aspiration, ferried him across. The monk rejoiced greatly and vowed: "In your next life, you shall surely become a king." Now, the Emperor is the lord of Japan, and the Venerable Rōben is that same monk reborn.

After receiving this dream-vision, the Emperor formed the vow to have the Great Buddha constructed. *(Shakusho)*

Thereupon, the Minister of the Right, Lord Tachibana (Tachibana no Moroe), was dispatched as imperial envoy to the Grand Shrine of Amaterasu at Ise, to pray for the creation of the august image. Furthermore, an imperial envoy was sent to Usa Hachiman Shrine in Buzen Province. *(Shoku Nihongi)* When the divine oracles of both shrines were found to accord with the Emperor's wishes, in the tenth month of Tenpyō 15 (743), at Shigaraki Palace in Ōmi Province, the Emperor resolved to construct the great image of Vairocana. *(Shoku Nihongi)* The Venerable Gyōki received the imperial command and solicited donations from warriors and commoners throughout the land. The text of the founding vow was penned by the Emperor himself with his own august brush. Monks from the four great temples all gathered, music was performed, central pillars were erected to prepare the casting mold for the great image, and the Emperor himself pulled on the ropes. However, the work did not go well, and in the fourth month of the seventeenth year (745), the Emperor moved the capital back to Nara. *(Shoku Nihongi)* Then in the eighth month of the same year, wishing once again to have the great image cast, the Emperor gathered earth in the sleeve of his imperial robe…
