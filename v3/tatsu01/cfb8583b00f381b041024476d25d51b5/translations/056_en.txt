## Sarusawa Pond (猿沢池)
This pond lies on the southern shore of Kōfukuji Temple. It is said to have been modeled after the Mihōchi (獼猴池, "Monkey Pool") of India (Tenjiku), from which it takes its name. The Monkey Mound Pine (猿墳松, Sarutsuka no Matsu) stands by the pond's edge, likely created in association with the name "Sarusawa."

From *The Yamato Monogatari* (Tales of Yamato):

Long ago, there was a court lady (uneme) who served the Emperor of Nara. She was exceedingly beautiful and graceful, and many men sought her hand — including nobles of the court — but she refused them all. The reason she refused was that she had devoted her heart entirely to the Emperor, whom she considered incomparably magnificent. The Emperor too had favored her. But afterward, she was never again summoned to his presence, and she came to find the world unbearably painful. Day and night the Emperor weighed upon her heart; she felt longing and grief beyond endurance. (Excerpt omitted.)
　Unable to bear living in this world any longer, she stole out one night and threw herself into Sarusawa Pond. The Emperor was unaware that she had done this, but someone brought the matter to his attention at an appropriate occasion, and he heard of it. He was deeply moved with sorrow, came in procession to the pond's edge, and had those around him compose poems. Kakinomoto no Hitomaro composed:

> *Wagimo ko ga nekutare kami wo sarusawa no / ike no tama mo to miru zo kanashiki*
> (How sorrowful it is to see the tangled hair of my beloved as the beautiful water-weeds of Sarusawa Pond.)

At that time, the Emperor composed:

> *Sarusawa no ike mo tsurashi na wagimo ko ga / tama mo kazukaba mizo hi namashi*
> (Even Sarusawa Pond is heartless — if my beloved sank among its water-weeds, the waters should have risen to embrace her.)

Thereupon, the Emperor had a grave made for her at the pond's edge and returned to the palace.

## Uneme Shrine (采女祠)
This shrine stands by the shore of Sarusawa Pond. According to the *Genyōki*, it was established at the behest of Kōnan-in Gon-Sōjō Kaiyu. "Uneme" is not a personal name but an official title — these were women who served in the imperial palace during ceremonial banquets (sekkai) and performed duties such as serving food.

## Kinukake Willow (衣掛柳, "Robe-Hanging Willow")
This is in the same location. It is said that when the uneme threw herself into the pond, she hung her robe on the branch of a willow. The original ancient tree has already withered, and later people planted a successor in its place.

From the *Fuboku Wakashō*:

> *Sarusawa no ike no yanagi ya wagimo ko ga / nekutare kami no katachi mi naran*
> (Does the willow by Sarusawa Pond reflect the form of my beloved's tangled, disheveled hair?)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Shōmeimon'in no Saisho

Also from the same anthology:

> *Sarusawa no ike no usurai uchi tokete / tama mo wo yadosu kishi no aoyagi*
> (The thin ice of Sarusawa Pond has melted away, and the green willows on the bank shelter the beautiful water-weeds.)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Shōmyō Hōshi

## Yōkihi Cherry Tree (楊貴妃桜)
This is in the same location. Long ago, there was a monk at Kōfukuji called Gensō Hōshi. Because he loved this cherry tree so dearly, it came to be called by this name (after Yang Guifei, the beloved consort of the Tang Emperor Xuanzong).

## Todoroki Bridge (轟橋)
Located between Tōdaiji and Kōfukuji Temples, to the south of the Ōshiake Gate.

## Jūsan Kane (十三鐘, "Thirteen Bells")
Located to the east of Sarusawa Pond.

From *Nara Hakkei* (Eight Views of Nara):

> *Uchiwataru hitome mo taezu yukikoma no / fumi koso narase todoroki no hashi*
> (A stream of people crosses ceaselessly; the hoofbeats of passing horses resound — thus rings the Todoroki Bridge.)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Tōsō

## Kumoi Slope (雲井坂)
Located to the north of Todoroki Bridge.

## Dragon Lantern Pine (龍燈松) and Konnyaku Bridge (蒟蒻橋)
Both are located near the Jūsan Kane (Thirteen Bells).

From *Nara Hakkei*:

> *Murasame no harema ni koeyo kumoi zaka / Mikasa no yama wa hodo chikaku tomo*
> (Cross Kumoi Slope in the clearing after the passing shower — even though Mount Mikasa is near, the distance of the heart is great.)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Tameshige

## Saho River (佐保川)
There is a stone bridge on the highway leading to what is now Zaike-chō Tengai-suji. The river referred to here is its lower reaches. Its source flows from Uguisu Falls (Uguisu Taki) on Mount Kasuga, passes to the west of Nanto (Nara), and eventually enters Soushimo District (添下郡).

From the *Kokinwakashū*:

> *Chidori naku saho no kawagiri tatinu rashi / yama no konoha mo iro masari yuku*
> (Plovers cry, and mist seems to rise over the Saho River; the leaves on the mountain deepen in color.)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Tadamine (Mibu no Tadamine)

From the *Kin'yō Wakashū*:

> *Kasugayama mine yori izuru tsukikage wa / saho no kawase no kōri narikeri*
> (The moonlight emerging from the peak of Mount Kasuga is like ice upon the shallows of the Saho River.)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Dainagon Tsunenobu (Minamoto no Tsunenobu)

From *Nara Hakkei*:

> *Tobu hotaru kage wo utsushite sahogawa no / asase ni fukaki kokoro wo zo shiru*
> (Flying fireflies cast their reflections upon the Saho River; in its shallow waters, one discerns a depth of feeling.)
> 　　　　　　　　　　　　　転法輪三条前内大臣公忠 (Tenbōrin Sanjō Former Minister of the Interior Kimitada)
