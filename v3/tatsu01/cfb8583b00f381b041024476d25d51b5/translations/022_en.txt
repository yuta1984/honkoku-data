(The Three Great Shrines Hall is) **the hall of the Wakamiya Chief Priest.**

**Watari no Ya (Crossing Hall)** (The hall of the shrine families)

**Take no Ya (Bamboo Hall)** · **Kiku no Ya (Chrysanthemum Hall)** (These two halls are the residences of the *negi* [junior priests])

**Hannya no Ya (Prajñā Hall)** (In former times, monks who lectured on the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra* would seclude themselves here, and thus the name arose)

**Naishi-bō (Ladies' Chamber)** (Also called *Hosokura* [Narrow Repository] or *Akakura* [Red Repository]. This served as the dressing room for the *suke* (senior ladies-in-waiting) and *naishi* (ladies-in-waiting) when the Empress made an imperial visit to Kasuga. In the 3rd month of Eiso 1 [989], when Emperor Ichijō made an imperial visit, the Jōtōmon'in [Empress Shōshi] stayed in this building.)

**Ango no Ya (Summer Retreat Hall)** (According to the *Honchō Gashi* [History of Japanese Painting], this served as the painting studio of Kasuga Shrine)

**Kyōzō (Sutra Repository)** (The complete scriptures (*issaikyō*) written in gold on dark blue paper in the imperial brushwork of the Retired Emperor Shirakawa are stored here. The placard above the entrance is the work of a transformed being [*kejin*].)

**Tsurugi no Sawa (Sword Ravine)** (According to the Kanmon Records: Located about half a *chō* [approximately 55 meters] to the west of the Nishi no Ya)

**Mizuya Shrine** (Located approximately 300 paces to the north of the Great Shrine. A mountain stream encircles the shrine precincts and flows into the village of Noda.)

Deities enshrined: First, Susanoo no Mikoto; Second, Inadahime; Third, Nankai no Shinjo (the Divine Woman of the Southern Sea)

Every year on the 5th day of the 4th month, a Noh performance is held, called the "Mizuya Nō." Its origin (*ranshō*) dates to the reign of Retired Emperor Fushimi, when the land was afflicted by an epidemic. Thinking to appease this shrine's deity, *kagura* sacred music was performed and dances were offered, whereupon divine efficacy immediately manifested, and it became an annual tradition. (According to shrine tradition)

**Ushiishi (Ox Stone)** (According to the Hōei Records: Inside the sacred fence, on the north side, there is a stone in the shape of a deity of misfortune (*yakushin*) leading an ox. The practice of hanging an iron chain on the shrine altar at *Setsubun* [the seasonal turning point] symbolizes tying up this misfortune deity's ox. It is also said to bind together the relationship between husband and wife.)

**Stone Lantern** (According to the Kanmon Records: This stands on the road from Mizuya Shrine to the Great Shrine. It is said to have been made by Kōbō Daishi [Kūkai].)

**Mizuya River** (Located along the northern bank of the shrine)

(From the *Ruijū* [anthology]):
> *Spring of Kasuga — even to where the waters of Mizuya flow — I entrust my life to the care of the gods* — Kinugasa no Ōtodo (Minister Kinugasa)

(From the *Fuboku* [anthology]):
> *The Mizuya River — damming its waters downstream — in the fields of Kasuga — the seedlings of Noda — are being gathered today* — Tameie

**Nagao Shrine** (According to the Kanmon Records: Located on the east side of the road from the Haraido Shrine. To the east of the main road are the Nagao Inner Sanctuary, the Upper Mizuya Shrine, and the Haraihonto Shrine.)

**Hitotsuki-iwa (Sun-Moon Rock) and Himuro Old Site**
(According to the *Heijō Shiseki Kō* [Study of Heijō Palace Ruins]: Located about six *chō* above the Mizuya River, in the area of the eastern mountains. The Hitotsuki-iwa is located about ten *chō* above the Yoshiki River, north of the Kasuga Hon-miya (original shrine), near the source of the Mitarashi River. The images of the sun, moon, and stars — the three luminaries — are carved into the surface of the rock. The local people call this place "Hitotsuki" [Sun and Moon].)

**Mikasayama (Mount Mikasa)** (According to the *Kenchū Mikkan* [a Heian-period poetic commentary]: At Kasugayama there is a gently sloping, small mountain called Mikasayama, and the Kasuga Shrine is situated there. "Kasugayama" is the general name, while "Mikasayama" is the specific name.)

(From the *Man'yōshū*):
> *The great sovereign's — Mount Mikasa serving as a sash — the sound of the narrow valley stream — how clear and bright it is!* — Hitomaro [Kakinomoto no Hitomaro]

(From the *Shūishū*):
> *Only the name exists — the mountain has no "mikasa" [august parasol] — or does it refer to where the morning and evening sun shines?* — Tsurayuki [Ki no Tsurayuki]

(From the *Goshūishū*):
> *Since the god of Mount Mikasa — whom we worship today is present — under the heavens — may you prosper and flourish!* — Fujiwara no Noriyoshi

(From the "Eight Views of the Southern Capital — Snow on Mount Mikasa," a Chinese poem):
> *Boundless white snow conceals the many peaks — traces of *wakō* [the softening of divine light] remain within this mountain*
> *When the great tree [the Shogun] puts forth blossoms — its glorious color tells us anew of the merit of benefiting beings* — Tokudaiji, Commander of the Left Imperial Guard, Sanetoki

(From the same, a waka poem):
> *Trusting in Mount Mikasa — as my sure support — does the god know — the deep sincerity — of the white snow that falls?* — Saionji, Former Minister of the Right, Sanetoshi

**Kasugayama (Mount Kasuga)** (In Jōwa 8 [841] of Emperor Nimmyō's reign, it is recorded in the *Shoku Nihongi* [Chronicles of Japan Continued] that hunting and logging within the sacred mountain of the Kasuga Great Deity were prohibited by imperial command issued to the provincial district officials.)

(From the *Shūishū*):
> *How promising! — even from its two tender leaves — Mount Kasuga — for I know it to be the seed — of a lofty pine* — Yoshinobu [Minamoto no Yoshinobu]

(From the same):
> *Spring haze — drifting and settling — over Mount Kasuga — filled to overflowing — with the sight of people from the capital* — Fujiwara no Tadafusa

(From the *Goshūishū*):
> *The sakaki tree — on the ridge of Mount Kasuga — is evergreen — the light of an eternal reign — shines too in the moon* — Junior Third Rank Yukinō
