### On the Yahagi-gawa (矢作川) — continued

...He heard that the barbarian (Ebisu) warriors were lying in wait for him at Mount Kōseki (高石山), and at that place he made a great many arrows. For this reason, that place was named "Yahagi" (矢作, "arrow-making"), and this name was also given to the river.

### On the Province of Tōtōmi (遠江国)

The reason for writing and reading [this province as] "Tōtōmi" (遠江) is as follows: until the time when the capital was in the Province of Ōmi (近江), this name [Tōtōmi] did not yet exist. After that, this place came to be called "Tōtōmi" (遠江) — meaning "the province far from Ōmi" — and was given that name. This is because the character 江 (e) is read as "Afumi" (近江, Ōmi), and is also read as "Sasana." Therefore, although one could write it as "Ontsu-e" (遠津江), it is written as "On-e" (遠江) and read as "Tōtōmi."

### On the Province of Suruga (駿河国)

As for "Suruga" (駿河) — in ancient times it was written as "洲流河" (Suruga) and read as "suruka." The reason for this is that at the harbor of the Katsuragi-kyūka River (葛絡級河), there were sandbars (su) that drifted and lay flat upon the water, and following the waves that beat against these sandbars, [the sand] would move this way and that. Given this, the name "Suruka" was given because this river is swift (hayai), and the character 駿 (shun, meaning "swift/excellent") was appended.

Rocked and floating in the southern sea of Mount Fuji (富士), when the flatlands were unsettled and unfixed, the waves came washing in and deposited [sand] upon this province — thus it is also named Suruga, meaning "that which is washed ashore."

Furthermore, regarding the reading of the character 駿 (shun) as "suru": the two characters of "Shunma" (駿摩) share a common phonetic root with the character 洲 (su). The character "yuhau" is a rendaku/rensei (連声, phonetic assimilation), and the character "mu" is in rensei with the character "ru." This forms a single horizontal phrase as well.
