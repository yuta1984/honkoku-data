At that time, the troops of Prince Ōtomo's faction lay in wait for Emperor Tenmu at Mount Suzuka. Upon learning of this, the Emperor detoured through Mino Province. During this time, the "Great Lord of Kagami" (Kagami-no-Ōkimi) was slain, and the place where his burial mound was raised came to be called "Ōmi Kagamiyama" (Mirror Mountain of Ōmi).

Furthermore, because the troops of Prince Ōtomo's faction were defeated while the Emperor's forces remained undefeated, that place came to be named "Fuwa-no-Seki" (the Barrier of Fuwa — literally, "the Unbroken Barrier").

After this, upon reaching Mino Province, Emperor Tenmu bathed in a certain river to cleanse his body, and at that time the wound on his back that he had suffered in Yamashiro was healed. That river was then named "Kuise-kawa" (苦醫瀬河, the River That Cures Suffering), because he declared it to be "the river that heals suffering."

### On the Province of Yamato

"Yamato" was originally the general name for all of Japan. It later came to be used as the name of a single province.

As for the word "Yamato" itself: in the time of primordial chaos, when heaven and earth had only just separated and the world had not yet fully formed — when the mud and earth had not yet dried — the people of old used mountains as their dwelling places, and thus many traces of human habitation could be seen upon the mountains. For this reason, Japan as a whole came to be called "Yamato-no-kuni" (山迹国, "the Land of Mountain Traces").

There are also explanations related to "Yamato-uta" (Japanese poetry), "Yamato-kotoba" (the Japanese language), "Shikishima-no-Yamato," and "Soratsu," but to examine all of these in detail here would take too long. The explanations can be found in the commentaries on the Nihongi (Chronicles of Japan).

### On the Province of Kawachi

During the reign of Emperor Jinmu, the Heavenly River (Ama-no-kawa, the Milky Way) was redirected or relocated, and because the waterway in question lay within (uchi) that river, the place came to be called "Kawachi" (河内, "Within the River"). For this reason, a place called "Ama-no-kawa" (Heavenly River) still exists there to this day.
