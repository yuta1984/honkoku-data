### On the Province of Izu (伊豆国)

"Izu" (伊豆) is a name written in kana. In Chinese characters, it should properly be written as "Izu no Na-kuni" (出の名国), meaning "the province that juts out." The reason for this is as follows: to the east lies the Province of Sagami (相模国), to the west lies the Province of Suruga (駿河国), and from the middle between them, the province of Zushū (豆州, i.e., Izu) extends far out into the sea as a mountain cape (saki). Therefore, it is named "the Province of Izu" (出の国, "the protruding province").

As a second meaning (ichigi): there are hot springs (yu) that well up from Mount Izu (伊豆の御山), and the Gongen (権現, the deity) of Izu resides as the tutelary guardian (chinju) of this province. This deity greatly loves hot water, and is said to run and play upon the surface of the flowing hot spring. For this reason, by analogy with the image of "the running hot spring (hashiri-yu) issuing forth (izu)," the province is named "Izu" in kana.

### On the Province of Kai (甲斐国)

As for "Kai" (甲斐): in ancient times, at the foot of Mount Fuji, there lived a man known as the "Bamboo-cutter Elder" (Taketori no Okina), who grew bamboo and traded in it. This old man, in the bamboo grove of his garden, discovered an egg (kaigo) of a bush warbler (uguisu). He kept it warm, and after some time had passed, when he looked upon it, it had transformed into a beautiful and charming princess of graceful countenance. He then adopted her as his own child.

Later on, when the old man was busy working in the fields and had no spare time, his foster-mother (yōbo) complained, saying: "Even at such a busy time as this, why will you not lend a hand? How heartless!" Upon hearing this, the Uguisu-hime (Warbler Princess, 鶯姫) became furious, climbed to the peak of Mount Fuji, and kicked through the rocks, sending hot spring water rushing forth...
