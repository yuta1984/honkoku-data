White snow falling and accumulating made the shape of the mountain appear as if one were looking at a painting of a piebald horse (dappled horse), and so it was said to be a famous mountain in spring during that era. The sacred peak of Komagata is a divine mountain of Komagata, and in the period from late spring to early summer when the snow melts away in patches, the form of a horse (komagata) appears in that snow—and thus the mountain bears that name. Furthermore, in what is now Yamagoto District, Kamitsuno Estate, in Dewa Province, there is also a Mount Komagatake, and this too has a snow horse-shape. For this reason it is also called Kasugemo-dake (originally written "霞毛," meaning dapple-grey), and this name is also used for the village at its foot. As for Mount Komagatake in Shinano Province, it is written in detail in the *Shinchōmonshū* that a sacred horse still resides there; and in fact, the appearance of the snow horse-shape was told to me by a person from Ina District in that province. The area around the foot of the sacred peak of Koma in Mutsu Province was, in both Dewa and Mutsu, commonly called Komagata Estate in former times, and the people at the foot of the mountain make a pilgrimage to this Mount Okoma every year. There was also a text called *Mitake Sōshi* (Tales of the Sacred Peak). Furthermore, the principal consort (Kita no Kata) of Regent Hidetsugu was the daughter of Mogami Yoshiaki Ason and was called O-Koma-no-Mae—perhaps this name too was inspired by this Mount Komagata.
