《Origins of Japan's Kokubunji Temples》

To begin, tracing the origins of the Kokubunji temples across the sixty-odd provinces of our land, they were founded as temples built by imperial vow under the forty-fifth Emperor, Emperor Shōmu, serving as sacred sites for the protection and peace of the nation. Reflecting upon their history: the consort of the Emperor, Empress Kōmyō, was the daughter of Tankai-kō (Fujiwara no Fuhito). Blessed from birth with a beautiful countenance and a radiant, luminous appearance, she came to be called Empress Kōmyō ("Empress of Radiant Light"). Possessing a profound spirit of compassion, she pitied the people and worked to save the world. She established the Hiden'in (Institute for the Poor) to care for the hungry throughout the land; she established the Seyaku-in (Institute for Medicine) to tend to the sick in all provinces; and at Ashiku-ji Temple she built a bathhouse, gathering people of all ranks and both sexes, personally providing offerings of merit to one thousand persons. Because of her deep devotion to the Buddhist teachings, she once urged the Emperor, saying: "In each of the sixty-odd provinces of our land, let us build temples, place monks, enshrine Buddhist images and sutras, and pray for the peace of the realm throughout the land..."
