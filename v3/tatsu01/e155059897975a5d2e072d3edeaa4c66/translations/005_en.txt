The Emperor thereupon, in the third month of Tenpyō 9 (the Year of the Ox), issued an imperial command to Gyōgi Bosatsu, ordering that temples be built in each province, that images of Amida, Śākyamuni, Yakushi, and other Buddhas be enshrined, and that monks be stationed there. He then had six hundred scrolls of the *Daihanya-kyō* (Great Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra) and the *Hokke-kyō* (Lotus Sūtra) copied and deposited in temples throughout the provinces, with prayers offered for the safety of the nation and the longevity of the imperial line. All of these temples were granted the imperial name "Kokubunji." As a result, in the following year, calamities ceased and peace prevailed throughout the realm.

○ In an imperial edict of Tenpyō 10, it was declared: "Because last year Buddhist images and sūtras were enshrined in the Kokubunji temples of all districts throughout the realm and prayers were offered, this year the winds and rains came in proper season and the five grains yielded an abundant harvest. The divine response was as immediate as an echo. Henceforth, Buddhist rites shall be held in high esteem."

○ In the seventh month of the same year, twenty thousand bundles of government rice were granted to the Kokubunji temples throughout the realm.

○ In Tenpyō 11, Kokubuni-ji (provincial nunneries) were additionally established in each of the provinces.

○ In the first month of Tenpyō 13, a grant of three thousand households was allocated to the Kokubunji temples of the various provinces.

an imperial edict declared: "Though We are of little virtue, We have been graciously entrusted with the supreme imperial dignity." Furthermore, as the sūtra states: "If there are those in the land who lecture and recite this sūtra and reverently make offerings to it, we, the Four Heavenly Kings, shall always come and protect them"—as taught in the *Saishōō-kyō* (*Suvarṇaprabhāsa-sūtra*)—images of the Four Heavenly Kings were therefore crafted and enshrined in Kokubunji temples throughout the realm. The *Konkōmyō Saishōō-kyō* (*Golden Light Sūtra of the Most Victorious King*) was copied and distributed to the various provinces. Furthermore, the Emperor personally copied the *Konkōmyō Saishōō-kyō* in gold thread and gold ink, and presented it to the Kokubunji temples.

○ An edict was issued declaring: "Henceforth, the Kokubunji temples of the various provinces shall be called 'Konkōmyō Shitennō Gokoku no Tera' (Temples for the Protection of the Nation by the Golden Light and the Four Heavenly Kings)." Twenty monks of outstanding virtue were selected to reside there, and they were granted a stipend of fifty households and one hundred *se* (畝) of land.

The Kokubuni-ji (nunneries) were named "Hokke Metsuzai no Tera" (Temples of the Lotus Sūtra for the Eradication of Sin).
