By virtue of this, prayers for peace throughout the realm and the eternal prosperity of the imperial line were performed without fail.

○ When Gyōgi Bosatsu was dispatched as the envoy of the good vow (*zensei no chokushi*) to determine the sacred sites for the protection of the nation, it was established that the Yamato Kokubunji, being the foremost of all Kokubunji temples in Japan, had as its principal image a *jōroku* (approximately 4.8 meters) image of Śākyamuni Tathāgata. However, in Tenpyō 15, a great Buddha of sixteen *jō* (approximately 48 meters) was constructed and consecrated, and the temple was renamed Tōdai-ji. The province of Yamashiro, being the highest-ranking province among the Five Home Provinces and Seven Circuits (*Goki Shichidō*), had Amitābha Buddha (*Amida Butsu*) as its principal image — a sacred image carved by Gyōgi himself through the practice of one cut and three bows (*ittō sanrai*). As for the Kokubunji temples of all other provinces, Bhaiṣajyaguru (*Yakushi Nyorai*, the Healing Buddha) was established as the principal image in every case. Thus, because Yamashiro and Yamato were the highest-ranking provinces in Japan, the two honored ones of Amitābha and Śākyamuni were enshrined for the promotion of Buddhism.

Upon reflection, Śākyamuni Tathāgata underwent difficult yet willingly chosen austerities (*nansa nōsa*) for the sake of all sentient beings, and ultimately achieved

the attainment of Buddhahood, gave rise to five hundred great vows, became the Great Teacher of the Three Realms (*sangai*), and preached the various sūtras — both major and minor, provisional and true — thereby guiding all sentient beings with karmic connection to him. Furthermore, for the method of dispelling disasters in this present world, he preached and spread the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra* and various secret methods for eliminating obstacles and calamities; and for the direct path by which ordinary people of the degenerate age may attain Buddhahood and liberation, he expounded the teaching of oral nenbutsu (*shōmyō nenbutsu*) — easy to proceed upon, easy to practice — making him truly the Fundamental Teacher (*honshi*) for both this life and the life to come.

○ Amitābha Tathāgata is the original ground (*honji*) of deities both great and small. For this reason, he was enshrined in Yamashiro province for the protection of the nation. The *Kanmuryōju-kyō* (*Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra*) states: "When the realm is at peace and in harmony, the sun and moon shine bright and clear, the winds and rains come in their proper seasons, and disasters and plagues do not arise." Furthermore, it is stated that there exists a vow to universally save all those in poverty and suffering. For the sake of the ordinary people of the degenerate age to achieve rebirth in the Pure Land of ultimate bliss, Amitābha raised forty-eight great vows, established the Pure Land of nine grades of birth (*kubon*) ten billion lands to the west, and guides those who practice nenbutsu. "With a single recitation of the name of Amida Buddha, immeasurable sins are instantly extinguished" — the benefits for both this life and the next are profound.
