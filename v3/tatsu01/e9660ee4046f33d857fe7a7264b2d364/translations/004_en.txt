Kumano Gongen was moved by the Cloistered Emperor's devout sincerity, and one night appeared to the Emperor in a dream with the following message: "Long ago, in the realm of the dead, the Great King Enma lamented that all manner of sinners were falling into hell. At that time, ten thousand copies of the Lotus Sutra were offered in memorial service for those burdened with grave karmic sins. The officiant of this service was the Venerable Shōkū of Shoshazan in Harima Province. As an offering, the aforementioned Lotus Sutra and mandala were presented to him." The Venerable Shōkū then beseeched: "I humbly request that you reveal a teaching by which the sentient beings of this world may attain Buddhahood in peace." To this, the Great King replied: "In this world, there are thirty-three sacred sites of Kannon. For those who make the pilgrimage even once, from this age until the end of time,

[Left folio]
they shall know peace and tranquility in this life, and in the next life shall attain the fruits of Buddhahood. If this be false, then I and all Ten Kings of Hell shall fall into hell ourselves." He then wrote this pledge upon a stone and presented it. The Venerable Shōkū returned to this world and enshrined it at Nakayamadera temple in Settsu Province. "Therefore, resolve quickly to undertake the pilgrimage. I shall guide you along the way." With these words, the dream came to an end. The Cloistered Emperor's joy was boundless. On the seventeenth day of the third month, he set out from Nachisan as the first temple, and on the first day of the sixth month, he concluded the pilgrimage at Tanigumi in Mino Province. Let none doubt this account — have faith, have faith.
