**Honnōji Temple** is located south of Oshikoji on Kyōgoku-dōri. It belongs to the Hokke (Nichiren) sect, specifically the Shōretsu branch.
Originally it was a sub-temple (tatchū) of Myōkenji Temple, but Nichisai, the fourth-generation successor from the monk Nichizō, promoted and established the Shōretsu branch as a distinct school.
The temple was founded by the monk Nichiryū, and its original site was east of Abura-no-koji, south of Rokkaku Street (the area is now called Honnōji-chō).

The monk Nichiyō, a Provisional Senior Prelate (Gondai Sōzu) who revitalized the temple, authored a work in Japanese called *Tsukuba-shū*.
The gate before the abbot's quarters was relocated here from Jurakudai Castle. Its carvings are ornate and beautiful, and are said to be the work of the master craftsman Hidari Jingorō.
The Shrine of the Thirty Guardian Deities was originally an old shrine of the Atago-yama deity. It has a tiled roof, unlike any other.

**Pagoda of Lord Oda Nobunaga**
Located to the east of the main hall. On the second day of the sixth month of the tenth year of Tenshō (June 21, 1582), Nobunaga was forced to take his own life at the former site of this temple at the hands of Akechi Mitsuhide. The full account can be found in the *Shinchōki* (Chronicle of Nobunaga).

**The Daimoku Mandala**
This is written by the hand of Nichiren, the founding patriarch of the sect. The mounting is of indigo-colored satin (donsu) with a Chinese arabesque (karakusa) ground pattern, and it is known in the world as the "Honnōji-gire" (the Honnōji cutting).

**Monbōzan Chōmyōji Temple** is located three chō north of the eastern end of the Sanjō Bridge. It belongs to the same sect (Hokke/Nichiren), specifically the Chi branch, and was founded by the monk Nichishuku. He held the rank of Gondai Sōzu Hōin, and his family name was Chiba, originally from Chiba District in Shimōsa Province. He passed away on the twelfth day of the fourth month of the tenth year of Eishō (1513) at the age of eighty-seven. The two guardian deity (Nitten) statues at the temple's two-storied gate (rōmon) — Jikokuten to the east and Tamonten to the west — are jointly the works of Unkei and An'amida. Their spiritual power is fresh and potent, and worshippers never cease to visit.
In front of the rōmon gate there is a worship hall (haiden) for the two guardian deities, unlike anything found elsewhere.

Hosokawa Jibu-no-shō Minamoto no Katsu donated the land of Ekiji and named the temple Chōmyōji. Its first site was on Shinmachi-dōri at Takatsukasa Street (present-day Shimocho-jachō). It was later moved to the former site of the Nakamikado Imperial Palace north of Naka-no-mikado Street (present-day Sawaragichō), and at the end of the Tenshō era (around the 1590s), it was relocated again to its present site.
