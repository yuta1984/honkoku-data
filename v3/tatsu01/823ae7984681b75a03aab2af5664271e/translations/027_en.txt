**Shimo-Goryo Shrine** is located south of Kyōgoku-dōri and Kasuga (present-day Marutamachi) Street. The deities enshrined here are the Eight Goryo spirits, the same as those of Kami-Goryo Shrine.
The names of the eight deities are recorded above (on the previous page). The annual festival is held on the same day as Kami-Goryo Shrine.

**Kannon Hall**
Located within the shrine precinct; it is the sixth stop on the Rakuyō Kannon Pilgrimage.

**Gyōganji Temple** is adjacent to the south of Shimo-Goryo. It is also known as **Kōdō (the Leather Hall)**. It belongs to the Tendai sect, and its principal image — an eleven-faced, thousand-armed Kannon (Senju Kannon) — is a standing figure eight shaku tall, carved by the monk Gyōen.
It is the nineteenth stop on the Saigoku Thirty-Three Temple Pilgrimage and the fourth stop on the Rakuyō Kannon Pilgrimage.

**Stone Pagoda of the Kamo Deity**
This is a five-element (gorin) stupa standing over one jō in height. There is a torii gate before the pagoda, which was erected by the monk Gyōen.

The founding monk of this temple, Gyōen, was originally from Chinzei (Kyushu). In the second year of Kankō (1005), he traveled to the capital, wearing a jeweled crown on his head and leather robes on his body. For this reason, the people of the capital called him "Kawa Shōnin" (the Leather Monk). Gyōen constantly recited the Senju Daihii Dharani and prayed to find fine timber from which to carve an image of Kannon. One night, he dreamed that a monk appeared and said he would send sacred timber, and then he awoke. The very next morning, a monk indeed arrived and told him: "Near the Kamo Shrine, there is a moss-covered zelkova tree; on each of the six fasting days (rokusei-nichi), one can hear the sound of the thousand-armed divine mantra being chanted. Long ago, the great deity of Kamo is said to have descended from heaven beneath this very tree." Gyōen then went to seek out the tree and asked the shrine officials for permission to use it. He carved a bodhisattva image, established Gyōganji Temple, and enshrined it there. This became the principal image of the temple. Because Gyōen always wore his leather robes, the temple came to be called "Kōdō" (Leather Hall). Later, Gyōen's disciple, the monk Jinkō, obtained the remaining timber and carved another eight-shaku image, which became the principal image of Ryōmineji Temple on the Western Hills. Originally, the temple was located west of Shinmachi on Ichijō-dōri, and was therefore called "Ichijō Kōdō."

**Kiyo-Kōjin Shrine** is located east of Kyōgoku, at the Kōjinguchi. The deity enshrined is an eight-faced, eight-armed Kōjin (a fierce deity). Originally, the deity was enshrined at Katsuoji Mountain in Settsu Province (present-day Osaka Prefecture). By imperial decree of Emperor Go-Yōzei, it was relocated during the Bunroku era (1592–1596) to the west of Abura-no-Koji on Gojō-bōmon, and later moved to its present location, chosen for its proximity to the northern provinces.
In ancient times, this area was called "Konoe-Kawara," but after the enshrinement of this deity, it came to be called "Kōjinguchi."

**Takada Senshūji Gobō** is located north of Kawaramachi and Nijō. Originally situated at Ichijō-Yanagihara, it was founded by the monk Shinran. It was later moved to Ainocho Takatagawa, and around the Genna era (1615–1624) relocated to its present site. The principal image of Amida Buddha is a work of An'ami; an image painted by Kannon Shōnin (Shinran) at the age of sixty-two is enshrined in a treasure cabinet. [Several characters are illegible.] The main hall's eastern side was formerly the cosmetics chamber (kesho-den) of Lady Kita-no-Mandokoro, the wife of Toyotomi Hideyoshi. The paintings inside the hall are by Kanō Eitoku [characters illegible]. In [a certain] year, a single-halo golden Buddha was donated in prayer for the repose of Emperor Sakuramachi, and a Sanzon Hall (Hall of the Three Honored Ones) was constructed. The Seiryū (Blue Dragon) Water, offered to the monk Mue Ryū Shōnin during the Keian era by a dragon maiden, is located beside the Sanzon Hall.

**Myōtōzan Myōmanji Temple** is located south of Nijō on Kyōgoku-dōri. It belongs to the Hokke (Nichiren) sect, and was founded by the monk Nichijū. It was established in the fifth month of the third year of Eitoku (1383). The original site was west of Horikawa on Ayanokoji Street (present-day Myōmanji-chō).

**The Bell of Dōjōji Temple**
This bell, preserved in this temple, is from Dōjōji Temple in Hidaka, Kii Province (present-day Wakayama Prefecture). It bears an inscription. After the temple buildings were destroyed by fire in the wars, the bell was moved from place to place, and was finally donated in the fifth month of the sixteenth year of Tenshō (1588) from the Nanjio Temple at Shingū in Kii Province. However, there was a crack in the bell and it did not produce a resonant sound. When an attempt was made to melt it down and recast it, the bell trembled greatly and flames shot out from it. The assembled monks were alarmed and abandoned the plan, casting a new bell instead. The original bell has been stored inside the hall. At first there was a crack below the dragon-head mount (ryūzu), but it has gradually healed and is now smooth.

**The Well of Nakagawa**
Located in front of the hall, it is one of the Seven Wells of Rakuyō (Kyoto).
