**Shin-Sumiyoshi Shrine**
Located at the corner of Samegai-dōri and Takatsujī. It enshrines the deity Sumiyoshi Myōjin from Settsu Province (modern Osaka), and is said to have been established at the invitation of Lord Fujiwara no Shunzei.

**Kōjin Shrine**
Located north of Samegai and Takatsujī. It was established during the Bunroku era (1592–1596) by inviting the deity from Katsuo-yama in Settsu Province.
　　　　This is said to be the original site of the Kiyokōjin of Konoe Kawara.

**Kesho-mizu (Cosmetic Water)**
Located south of Nishitōin and Shijō.
　　　　In ancient times, it is said that Ono no Komachi had a small villa at this location. About three ken (roughly 5.5 meters) to the north, there is a drainage stream that flows westward beneath the houses along Shijō-dōri and empties into the Nishitōin River. This is called the "Aisomegawa" (Indigo-dyeing River). It is said that a man who had fallen in love with Ono no Komachi, unable to fulfill his desire, threw himself into this river and drowned. For this reason, wedding processions avoid crossing this bridge.

**The Villa of Komatsu no Naifu Shigemori**
Located on the southwest side of Muromachi and Shijō.
　　　　One account holds that this was also the former site of the estate of Grand Counselor Fujiwara no Kinto. There was once a tall tower here, which was demolished in recent years. The spring water (shimizu) still remains.

**Sugi Ebisu Shrine**
Located north of Inokuma-dōri and Matsubara. It enshrines Hiruko no Kami (Ebisu).
　　　　Among the shrine's treasures is a nine-character nenbutsu inscription in the hand of Saint Shinran. This is displayed on the twentieth day of the tenth month.

**Tendō Shrine**
Located north of Gojō-bōmon and Inokuma. It enshrines the deities of the sun and moon.

**The Tachi-kake Matsu (Sword-hanging Pine)**
Located behind residences at Shijō, Inokuma, west of Shinmachi. In ancient times, this was the founding site of a Tendai Buddhist temple established by Dengyō Daishi (Saichō), called Shiunzan Konpōji. In the spring of Genryaku 1 (1184), Minamoto no Yoshitsune lodged at this temple and hung a sword—received from a certain monk—on the pine tree here, giving rise to the name. The fifty-seventh abbot, Monk Dōchin, became a disciple of Saint Shinran. The temple buildings were destroyed in the fires of the Ōnin War, after which the temple was relocated to the Nishi Rokujō御堂 (御堂, mido/hall) and renamed Konpōji.

**Ishigami Shrine**
Located south of Ishigami-dōri and Sanjō.
　　　　It enshrines Toyoiwamado no Mikoto and Kushiiwamado no Mikoto. In ancient times, this site was the residence of Lord Nakayama Tadachika, and it is therefore also known as "Nakayama Shrine."

**Sarasuzume-dera (Temple of the Sparrows)**
Located west of Shijō-dōri and Ōmiya, this is a temple of the Pure Land (Jōdo) sect, whose principal image—an Amida Buddha—is a work of the Kasuga school of Buddhist sculptors. It is said that Chūjō Fujiwara no Sanekata, having received an imperial command to travel to the eastern provinces to research utamakura (poetic place-names), died in Mutsu Province (modern Tōhoku). His spirit, transformed into a sparrow, came to dwell in the forest, as seen in a dream by the temple's head priest, Kanchi Hōin. For this reason, it is also called "Suzume-mori" (Sparrow Grove).
It is also said that this site was formerly the location of the Kangakuin of the Greater Imperial Palace.
