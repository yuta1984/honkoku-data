**The Yūgao Mound**
The Yūgao Mound is located in the vicinity of Gojō, to the north of present-day Sakai-machi Matsubara.

It is said by tradition that the woman known as Yūgao, who appears in *The Tale of Genji*, once lived in this very place.

　　*From the New Kokinshū* — a poem composed on the theme of *yūgao* (the moonflower):

　　*Shiratsuyu no / nasake wo okikeru / kotonoha ya /
　　honobono mieshi / yūgao no hana*

　　*(How tender — the words that lay like white dew;
　　barely visible in the dimming light, the moonflower blooms)*

　　　　　　Former Grand Minister (Saki no Daijō-daijin)

**The Separated Pond (Hanare-no-ike)**
The Separated Pond refers to a well in front of the hall of Sōsen-ji Temple, to the south of Takakura-Gojō. It once lay within the boundary of the Kawara-no-in estate and is a remnant of that historic site. The temple belongs to the Sōtō sect of Zen Buddhism, and was founded by the monk Tenkō. The plaque on the main hall is said to be in the brushwork of Shōsui.

---

**Aizome River (Indigo-Dyeing River)**
The Aizome River flows south from Ton-no-chō, passing beneath the residences, after passing through Gojō and Takakura. It is a turbid stream. This too is said to be a remnant of the famed Kawara-no-in estate.

---

**Hanasaki Inari Shrine**
The Hanasaki Inari Shrine is located to the west of Matsubara-dōri Takakura. The surrounding neighborhood is known as "Inari-chō." This place was once the residence of the haiku master Matsunaga Teitoku, where he compiled the haiku treatise *Mikasa* (御傘).

　　*(From Teitoku's personal poetry collection:)*

　　　　*On the theme of the New Year's arrival at an old dwelling, composed upon moving to the Hanasaki house on Gojō:*

　　*Onozukara / mugura wo kado no / matsu ni mite /
　　aretaru yado no / haru ni au kana* — Teitoku

　　*(Naturally, I see the wild mugwort as the pine at the gate;
　　how I have come to meet spring at this overgrown, rustic dwelling.)*

　　　　*At a gathering held at the Gojō Hanasaki lodging, composed on the topic of yūgao (moonflower):*

　　*Koguruma no / mukashi no tachi to / na ni shi owaba /
　　sokoso to ware ni / yūgao no hana* — same author

　　*(If it truly bears the name of the old mansion from the tale of the little carriage,
　　then show yourself to me — O moonflower!)*

　　　　*At the Gojō lodging, where the former site of the Musō Inari Shrine existed. A poet, coming beneath the shade of these blossoming trees, thought to himself in the spirit of poetry: "This place must have once been called 'Hanasaki' (the blooming of flowers)," and composed:*

　　*Yorozu yo wo / mitsu no yashiro no / haru aki wa /
　　hana saki minore / kotonoha no michi* — same author

　　*(For ten thousand generations, in the shrine of Mitu,
　　may spring flowers bloom and autumn fruits ripen on the path of poetry.)*

**The Shrine of Lord Shunzei**
The shrine of Lord Shunzei is located behind a row of houses to the south of Matsubara-dōri Karasuma. The deity enshrined here is the spirit of Lord Shunzei (Fujiwara no Shunzei), the Third Rank at Gojō (Go-jō no Sanmi). This site is said to have been the residential land of that lord.

　　When compiling the *Senzaishū* anthology, Shunzei, upon reading the poems of poets of former ages, composed the following poem, which is included in the *New Kokinshū*:

　　*Yukusue wa / ware wo mo shinobu / hito ya aran /
　　mukashi wo omou / kokoro narahi ni*

　　*(In ages to come, will there perhaps be someone who will remember me,
　　just as I, by habit of heart, think fondly of those who came before?)*
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　— Shunzei
