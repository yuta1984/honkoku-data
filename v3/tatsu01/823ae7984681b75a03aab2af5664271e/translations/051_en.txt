## Gion Otabisho (Gion Temporary Shrine)

The Gion Otabisho is located at the intersection of Shijō and Kyōgoku. Every year on the seventh day of the sixth month, three portable shrines (mikoshi) of the Gion Festival make a divine procession (shinkō) to this place, and on the fourteenth day of the same month, after the festival rites, they return in procession (kankō) to the main shrine. On both days, all the yamaboko (festival floats) pass before this sacred precinct. The northern shrine enshrines Susanoo no Mikoto and the Hachióji deities, while the southern shrine enshrines the Shōshōi deity. The first two shrines are called "Ōmandokoro," and in former times, the Otabisho was located to the south of Karasuma-dōri Gojō-Bōmon.
　　　　　　(Currently known as "Ōmandokoro-chō")
The single shrine of Shōshōi was located to the north of Karasuma Nijō.
　　　　　　(Currently known as "Shōshōi-chō")

The three-shrine complex enshrines Amaterasu Ōkami, Hachiman-gū, and Kasuga Myōjin.

## Kansha-den (Official's Hall)

The deity enshrined here is kept secret, but there is a popular belief that this is where the spirit of Tosanosuke Shōson is enshrined — this is a great error. Also, as the deity of the "Seigan-butsu," many people visit on the twentieth day of the tenth month for the Ebisu gathering (Ebisu-kō). This deity is said to be the one who stood between the Sun Goddess (Amaterasu) and Susanoo no Mikoto to bear witness to their sacred oath (seibun).

## Akuōji Shrine

This shrine is located on the north side of Otabi-chō. When the Gion Festival mikoshi makes its divine visit, offerings are prepared according to ancient custom from Akuōji-chō, located to the north of Karasuma-dōri Gojō.

## Shijō Dōjō-ji Temple (Kinren-ji)

Kinryōzan Kinren-ji is located to the north of Kyōgoku-dōri Shijō. It is commonly called "Shijō Dōjō." It belongs to the Ji-shū (Time sect) and its principal object of worship is Amida Buddha. The temple was founded by the priest Jōa. The Oyakoi Jizō (Parents-longing Jizō) is a work of the sculptor Unkei, and was originally located at the burial grounds of Toribeno.

**Kumano Shrine**: This is the tutelary shrine of the temple and the guardian deity of the Ji-shū sect.

**Hototogisu-matsu (Cuckoo Pine)**: Located to the east of the abbot's quarters. It is said that when the hototogisu (Japanese lesser cuckoo) comes to Rakuyō (Kyoto), it first arrives at this tree to begin its song.

---

## Jūshin'in (Somedonojizō)

Jūshin'in is located at the southern entrance of Shijō Dōjō. It belongs to the Shingon sect, and its principal object of worship, the Jizō Bodhisattva, is said to be a work of Kūkai (Kōbō Daishi). Empress Somedono, who had great and constant faith in this temple, founded the hall; therefore it is called "Somedono Jizō."
　　(The hanging tablet bears the inscription "Somedono," written by the hand of Sōjō Kenka.)
