**Rendaisan Amidaji Temple**

Rendaisan Amidaji Temple is located south of Kurama-guchi on Kyōgoku-dōri. It belongs to the Jōdo (Pure Land) sect and is affiliated with Hyakumanben (Chion-ji). The principal image of Amida Buddha was crafted by Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai). The temple was founded by the monk Seigyo. In the abbot's quarters are enshrined portraits of Oda Nobunaga and his son Nobutada. The graves of both lords are here, along with the graves of several retainers who were killed at Honnō-ji during Akechi Mitsuhide's rebellion.

　　(Marginal note) As the monk Seigyo enjoyed Nobunaga's favor and patronage, Nobunaga was buried here.

**Kakūzan Jūnenji Temple**

Kakūzan Jūnenji Temple is located south of Amidaji. It belongs to the same Jōdo sect, and the principal image of Amida Buddha was crafted by Kōbō Daishi. The temple was founded by the monk Shin'a.

　　(Marginal note) He passed away on the second day of the seventh month of Eikyō 12 (1440). Following his dying wishes, he was given a water burial in the Toba River. He was sixty-six years old and was a descendant of Emperor Gomurakami.

**Kōfuzan Honmanji Temple**

Kōfuzan Honmanji Temple is located south of Jūnenji. It belongs to the Hokke (Nichiren) sect and was founded by the monk Nichishū. The statue of the monk Nichiunin, enshrined in the Founder's Hall, was originally located in Kuroda Village in Tanba Province. Many people in that area fell ill with fever and died, and it was said that these deaths were caused by a curse from this statue. Thus, it was placed in a chest and abandoned in the mountains. Many years passed and no one knew of it any longer. One day, the sound of Buddhist chanting was heard from within the mountains. The villagers, finding this strange, entered the mountains to investigate and discovered the revered statue. It was then enshrined at Shōfuku-ji in the same village. Later, a man named Utsunomiya Shingaku seized the statue and brought it to the capital, attempting to sell it in the market. The monk Nichjū of this temple saw it, recognized it as the image of the great founding patriarch, and immediately purchased it and enshrined it at this temple.

　　(Marginal note) As prayers are said to yield remarkable miraculous effects, followers of the sect gather here regularly. According to one account, it is also said to be an image of Ganzan Daishi (Ryōgen). This is mentioned in the *Shin Chomonshū* (New Collection of Tales Heard).

**Jōkain Temple**

Jōkain Temple is located south of Imadegawa on Kyōgoku-dōri. It is one of the four main temples of the Jōdo sect. In the main hall is enshrined an image of the founding patriarch Hōnen. The principal image of the Amida Hall was crafted by Eshin (Genshin).

　　(Marginal note) This temple was formerly of the Tendai sect and was founded by Jikaku Daishi (Ennin). Its original location was on Tsuchimikado-dōri, to the west of Karasuma at what is now Kamichōja-machi. As it was close to the Imperial Palace, it was called the "Inner Place of Worship" (Naidōjō), and therefore has no mountain name (sangō).

　　(Marginal note) The restorer of the temple was the fifth generation successor to Hōnen, the monk Kōshi. His secular surname was Minamoto (Genji) and he was the son of Takeda Aki-no-kami Tokitsuna. Originally a monk at Onjō-ji (Mii-dera), he went by the name "Jōkaibō Shōken." In Kōan 10 (1287), he resolved to leave the mountain temple and hid at Kakaiin in the capital, subsequently founding this temple.

**Migawari Fudōson (Substitute Fudō Deity)**

This is enshrined at this temple. Long ago, when the monk Chikō of Mii-dera fell gravely ill, Abe no Seimei prayed to the various gods and said: "The time of death has come. Is there among the disciples a monk willing to stand in as a substitute?" Among thirty-six disciples, a monk named Shōkū stepped forward and said, "I will take my master's place and give up my life." At that moment, Fudō-son, whom Shōkū had always venerated, appeared to him in a dream and declared: "Moved by your resolve, I too shall stand as a substitute for Shōkū." Immediately, the master Chikō recovered from his illness and returned to full health. To this day, miraculous effects are celebrated.

**Rozan Tendai Kōji Temple (Rozan-ji)**

Rozan Tendai Kōji Temple is located south of Jōkain. It practices a combined curriculum of (marginal note: Tendai, Vinaya, Hossō, and Pure Land). It was founded by Jie Daishi (Ryōgen) and originally called Yogan Kongō-in. The temple was restored by the monk Jūshin. One day, a transformed being (a spiritual apparition) appeared and said, "I am the Chinese monk Eon (Huiyuan) of Lu Shan," and wrote the two characters for "Rozan" (Lu Shan) and gave them to the abbot Jūshin. Thus the temple was renamed Rozan-ji. The principal image is a self-carved statue of Ganzan Daishi (Ryōgen). On the altar to the south is enshrined a Yakushi (Medicine) Buddha.

　　(Marginal note) It is the work of Prince Shōtoku. It is known in the world as the "Koya no Yakushi" (Yakushi of the small hall).

　　(Marginal note) On this altar is also enshrined a Shō Kannon (Holy Avalokiteśvara). It is the work of Dengyō Daishi (Saichō). It is known as the "Funakōmyō Kannon" (Kannon of the boat's welcoming light).

　　(Marginal note) Among this temple's treasured objects is a handwritten copy of the *Senchakushū* by Hōnen. There is also a four-line verse written in the hand of Shinran Shōnin. This is said to be the four-line verse (gemon) bestowed upon him by the Kannon of Rokkaku-dō (Chōhō-ji).
