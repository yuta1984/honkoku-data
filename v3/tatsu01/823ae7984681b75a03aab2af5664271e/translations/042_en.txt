**Seigan-ji Temple**

Seigan-ji Temple is located at Kyōgoku Sanjō and is the head temple (ippondera) of the Fukakusa school of the Jōdo sect. The original vow of its founding is attributed to Emperor Tenji, and its founder-in-residence (kaiki) was the monk Eion Sōzu.

Originally located in Heijō (Nara) in Yamato Province, it was relocated to the west of Ogawa on Moto-Seigan-ji-dōri in the upper part of the capital (Kamikyō) following Emperor Kanmu's transfer of the capital. The monks Senjō Hōshi and Genshin Sōzu also resided here for a time, and more than ten generations later, the high monk Zōshun Sōjō converted to the teachings of the monk Hōnen, and the temple became a temple of the Jōdo sect.

The principal image, a seated Amida Buddha measuring eight shaku in height, is said to have been created through the spiritual influence of the Buddhist craftsman Kenmon and a master from Keshinokuni (India/a land of the Buddha). Spring Kasuga Daimyōjin is said to have come to the temple every night to protect the Buddha — hence the deity is also called the "Kasuga Deity-Buddha." On the face of the Buddha there is an inscription in red characters of the nenbutsu name, said to be in the imperial hand of Emperor Tenji. Within the statue's body are enshrined representations of the five organs and six viscera, making it a miraculous image whose divine efficacy is celebrated throughout the ages.

The temple plaque is in the calligraphy of Imperial Prince Kūshō of Daikaku-ji. It is said that "the great benefactress of the temple's revival, the Northern Lady of the Great Chancellor (Toyotomi Hideyoshi) — a daughter of the Sasaki-Kyōgoku clan — [donated this] for peace and happiness in this and the next life."

The plaque bearing the six-character nenbutsu (Namu Amida Butsu) is in the hand of the monk Ippen. On the altar inside the hall are enshrined portraits of Emperor Tenji and the monk Eion. The three-storied pagoda was founded in Genna 8 (1622), with Tani Yakushi as its principal image. The Founding Patriarch's Hall contains a statue of the monk Hōnen. The Shakyamuni Hall enshrines a jewel-crowned Shakyamuni Buddha. The tutelary deity is Kasuga Myōjin. The five-ringed stone stupa is the grave of Matsumaru-dono, a beloved concubine of Hideyoshi; her posthumous Buddhist name is inscribed as "Juhōin Gekkō Jōkyū Zenjōni." As she was the patron of the temple's revival, the stupa is called the "Danna-tō" (patron's tower).

**Benkei Stone**

The Benkei Stone is located on the ornamental garden hill (tsukiyama) in the abbot's garden. It is said that in former times it stood in Benkei-chō, to the west of Sanjō Kyōgoku.

The Kashiwa Stone is to the west of the small abbot's quarters, beside the hand-washing basin. The Habutae Well is in front of the abbot's quarters.

Among the temple's treasures is a relic of the Buddha said to ward off fire (smoke), passed down from Kōbō Daishi. The temple also preserves Hōnen's "Ichimai Kishōmon" (One-Page Vow), accompanied by a calligraphic inscription by the monk Ikkyū. The mounting's inner border is of purple-ground peony arabesque fabric from the old Takeyamachi weave. The lower border is of moegi (young green)-ground "treasure" pattern fabric, known either as the "Seigan-ji gire" (Seigan-ji cut) or the "Ankoku-an gire."

　　*I have heard that Hōnen is the living Tathāgata, seated at ease upon the highest lotus throne.*
　　*Nuns and lay monks alike are of the same foolish and ignorant kind. The One-Page Vow is most wondrous indeed.*
　　*Namu Amida Butsu*

(Ikkyū's inscription)

"Beyond this, Bodhidharma, Xutang — I have no need of any of them. I hold only the one great matter of Hōnen as worthy of reverence. From this day forth I have become a follower of the Jōdo sect. Respectfully, respectfully.
　New Abbot of Shūon-in
Second year of Ōnin, second month, fifth day — Ikkyū (seal)
　To the Buddha's Palace [addressed to Seigan-ji]"

**The Grounds**

Within the temple grounds are several red plum trees, and around the time of Kisaragi (the second lunar month), people from across the capital come here to admire the beautiful sight of the not-yet-fully-opened crimson blossoms (mikai-beni).

　　*The trees standing in a row also tremble — plum blossoms*

In the sub-temple Chikurin-in within the temple grounds, there is a sukiya (tea-ceremony room) designed by Kobori Enshū, known as "Enshū's Eight Windows." It is of incomparable excellence. Likewise, the garden of Chōsen-in nearby is a scene of outstanding beauty and is celebrated throughout the world.
