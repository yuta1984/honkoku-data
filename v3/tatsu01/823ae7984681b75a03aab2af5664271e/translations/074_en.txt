**Asahi-miya Shrine**

Asahi-miya Shrine is located to the north of Gojō on Hakusan-dōri (corresponding to present-day Fuyachō). The enshrined deity is Amaterasu Ōmikami (the Great Deity of the Sun). During the reign of Emperor Seiwa, in the Jōgan era (859–877 CE), the shrine was established in Anao Village, Kuwata District, Tanba Province, in accordance with a divine oracle from Yamato-hime-no-mikoto. Subsequently, in Genki 3 (1572 CE), during the reign of Retired Emperor Ōgimachi, it was relocated to its present site.

> The annual festival is held on the sixteenth day of the ninth month.
> A stone associated with the deity Sarutahiko-no-kami is located within the shrine grounds and is said to have many miraculous powers.
> The Tobiume Tenmangū is one of six subsidiary shrines, and houses an ancient piece of wood from the "flying plum tree" of Dazaifu, along with a votive tablet inscribed by the Sugawara family (i.e., associated with Sugawara no Michizane).

**Shinmei-gū Shrine**

Shinmei-gū Shrine is located to the north of Tominokoji Gojō. In ancient times, this area formed the boundary of the estate of Minister Minamoto no Tōru, and this site served as a place of remote worship (yōhaijo) for the Great Shrine of Ise (Ise Jingū). In later ages, a shrine was built here.

> The annual festival is held on the same day as Asahi-miya (the sixteenth day of the ninth month).
> Currently, a Shingon Buddhist monk serves as its caretaker.

---

**Suwa-sha Shrine**

Suwa-sha Shrine is located two chō to the south of Gojō, in Suwa-chō. The enshrined deity is the same deity as that of Suwa Shrine in Shinano Province.

> Those who eat meat (game) are said to receive the sacred chopsticks (shinbashi) of this shrine and eat with them, thus being cleansed of ritual impurity.

---

**Shin-Tamatsushima-sha Shrine**

Shin-Tamatsushima-sha Shrine is located on Matsubara-dōri in Tamatsushima-chō. The enshrined deity is Princess Sotōri-hime (Sotōori-hime), the same deity as that of Tamatsushima Shrine in Kishū (Kii Province). According to tradition, it was established through the devotion of Lord Fujiwara no Toshinari (Shunzei). The annual festival is held on the thirteenth day of the eleventh month.

> When Lord Fujiwara no Tameie was young, he is said to have dedicated one hundred poems to this shrine six times per month.

[From the Shinzoku Kokinshū (New Continued Kokin Wakashū):]

*"Tanomu kana / waga Fujiwara no / miyako yori / ato tare hajime shi / Tamatsushima-hime"*
— Former Grand Minister of State (Daijō Daijin)

("I place my trust in you— from the capital of we Fujiwaras, you were first enshrined, O Princess Tamatsushima.")

**Kan-Isshō-sha Shrine (Kan-Daijin Shrine)**

Kan-Daijin Shrine is located at Gojōbōmon Nishitōin. The enshrined deity is Tenmangū (Sugawara no Michizane), and this site was formerly the residence of Lord Sugawara no Koreyoshi. The annual festival is held on the sixteenth day of the eighth month. The plaque on the worship hall bears the inscription "Tenmangū."

> The plaque was written by the calligraphy of Ryōkei Hōshinnō, the monzeki (princely abbot) of Takenouchi.
> There is a stone marker inscribed in "hachi-bun" (clerical script/reisho) with the words "Tenmangū Kōtan no Chi" (The Birthplace of Tenmangū [Michizane]). It was written by the calligrapher Useki (Ūseki).

**Birth Well (Tanjō-sui)**

Located within the enclosure on the south side of the main shrine. The inscription on the monument was written by Useki.

> **Daishi-dō:** Enshrines a self-carved image attributed to Ganzan Daishi (Ryōgen).

> **Kakinomoto-sha:** In recent years, this was transferred here through the devotion of the Kami-Reizei family.

---

**Kita Kan-Daijin Shrine (North Kan-Daijin)**

Located in front of the north gate of Kan-Daijin Shrine. The enshrined deity is a child of the Kan deity (Sugawara no Michizane).

> **Jōki-in:** Adjacent to the west of Kita Kan-Daijin Shrine. Enshrines the Araki Tenmangū within the hall. The Kongō Rikishi (Niō guardian) hall is located in front of the gate.
