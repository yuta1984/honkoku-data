**Daihozan Enpuku-ji Temple**
Enpuku-ji Temple is located to the east of Kyōgoku-dōri Shijō-bōmon (now called Tako-Yakushi-dōri) and is the head temple of the Fukakusa school of the Jōdo sect.
In former times, it stood at Muromachi Sanjō-bōmon; that area is now called Enpuku-ji-chō.
The principal image, an Amida Buddha, is said to have been carved by the monk Hōnen.

---

**Tako-Yakushi (Octopus Yakushi)**
Tako-Yakushi is formally named Eifuku-ji and is located within the grounds of Enpuku-ji. Its former site was at Sanjō Muromachi, and it was also known as "Mizukami Yakushi."
The principal image, a stone statue of Yakushi Buddha, measures two shaku (approximately 60 cm) in height and is attributed to Dengyō Daishi.

In ancient times it was located in the northern valley of Mt. Hiei. As for why it came to be called "Tako-Yakushi" (Octopus Yakushi): at the former site there was a marsh (sawa/澤), and the people of the capital called it "Sawa-Yakushi" (Marsh Yakushi). In later generations, this was mistakenly altered to "Yaya-Yakushi" and eventually became popularly known as "Tako-Yakushi." The inscription on the beam of the original hall read: "Sanjō Muromachi Mizukami Yakushi-dō."
○ The Koi Jizō (Carp Jizō) stands in front of the hall (its history is omitted here as it is extensive).

**Saikō-ji Temple**
Saikō-ji Temple is adjacent to the north of Tako-Yakushi. The Tora-Yakushi (Tiger Yakushi) is attributed to Kōbō Daishi and is also called the Mikura-dō.

---

**Chōkin-ji Temple**
Chōkin-ji Temple stands directly across from Seishin-in to the west. It is also known as "Ichigon-dō" (One-Word Hall). The principal image, an Eleven-faced Kannon, is attributed to Kōbō Daishi. It is also one of the temples on the Rakuyō Kannon pilgrimage circuit.

---

**Seitai-ji Temple**
Seitai-ji Temple stands directly across from Saikō-ji to the west. The principal image, a Haraōbi Jizō (Sash-Wearing Jizō), is an earthen statue attributed to the monk Gyōki.
It is said to be highly efficacious for pregnant women praying for safe delivery.

---

**Seishin-in Temple**
Seishin-in Temple is adjacent to the north of Saikō-ji. It is commonly called the "Izumi Shikibu Temple." In former times, it stood to the north of Ogawa Ichijō. It was founded by the Regent Fujiwara no Michinaga (Lord Midō), and Izumi Shikibu herself entered this temple, became a nun, and resided here.

The principal image is an Amida Buddha, and on the side altar is enshrined a portrait of Regent Michinaga. Beside the tower of Izumi Shikibu stands a "noki-zui-bai" (a propitious plum tree at the eaves), and near the plum tree at the eaves is the grave of the haiku poet Shitanken Gonsui.

　　("At the end of the wind — the sound of the sea")　　— Yoshimizu

**On Izumi Shikibu**
Izumi Shikibu was the daughter of the Governor of Echizen, Masamichi, and became renowned throughout the world for her skill in waka poetry. She was also of great beauty, with magnificent black hair, and many people of her time were drawn to her. Around the age of forty, she became a nun and secluded herself in the small inner hall of Seishin-in, seeking provision for the life to come (the far future of countless rebirths).

When the monk Ippen was spreading his teaching of the nenbutsu (recitation of Amida's name) at Seigan-ji Temple, it is said that the spirit of Izumi Shikibu appeared and engaged him in dialogue — a theme that is fittingly rendered in the nō play (yōkyoku) as well.
