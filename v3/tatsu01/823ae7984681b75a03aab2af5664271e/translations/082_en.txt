**The Well of Sameushi (Sameushi no I)**

The Well of Sameushi is located to the south of Sameigai (Sameigai), Gojo. There is an inscription on the well curb:

"Sameushi Well — Rebuilt by Ishiraku in the second year of Genna (1616)."

It is said that when the Ashikaga shogun Yoshimasa was deeply devoted to the art of the tea ceremony, he was greatly fond of this water. Today, there is no one left to use it; grasses grow thick and moss runs deep, and the well curb itself is buried and hidden — yet the spring water flows up unchanged from ancient times. Li Bai's verses read:

*"The stone-paved well is cold with green moss,*
*The frigid spring is clear, reflecting the moon."*

These lines must surely be speaking of such pure springs as this one.

**Honganji Temple**

Honganji Temple is located in Nishi-Rokujō. Its doctrines are those propagated by the Holy Priest Shinran. The biography of the Holy Priest is recorded in the final volume, in the section on Kajōzan Uehatsukage-dō (the Hall of the Planted Hair).

The founding of this temple dates to the ninth year of Bun'ei (1272), during the reign of Emperor Kameyama, when the Holy Priest's daughter, the Reverend Nun Kakunshinni (wife of Hino Saemon-no-suke Hirotsunakyō), received an imperial order and first erected a memorial hall at Ōtani in the eastern part of Kyoto. This was eleven years after the death of the founder (Shinran).

Emperor Kameyama designated it an imperial prayer temple and bestowed upon it the name "Ryūkoku-zan Honganji."

**Second Generation: Nyoshin Shōnin**
He was the legitimate grandson of the founder (Shinran) — the son of Zennran Shōnin and nephew of Kakushinnni. At that time, he resided in Ōtsuna-gō in Ōshū (northern Japan). Therefore, the priest Kakue (son of Hirotsuna, whose mother was Kakushinni) became the father of the third-generation Kakunyo Shōnin, and served as the administrator in absentia at Ōtani.

**Third Generation: Kakunyo Shōnin**
Kakunyo Shōnin succeeded as the third generation, and in the first year of Shōan (1299), during the reign of retired Emperor Gofushimi, was granted an imperial decree confirming the temple as an imperial prayer temple.

During the time of the eighth-generation Rennyo Shōnin, the sect's teachings flourished greatly, to a degree that even surpassed the era when the founder was still alive. The monks of the mountain gate (Enryakuji) became jealous of this, and in the sixth year of Kansho (1465), they destroyed the temple. The monks of the temple gate (Onjōji/Miidera), however, sided with Rennyo Shōnin, donating the Chikamatsuji Temple and moving the Holy Priest's portrait image there.

From this point, Rennyo Shōnin traveled through the northern provinces, built a hall at Yoshizaki in Echizen, spread his teachings throughout the seven provinces of Hokuriku, and subsequently in the eleventh year of Bunmei (1479) built a portrait hall in Yamashina-gō, Yamashiro Province.

During the time of the ninth-generation Jitsunyo Shōnin, he was granted the red robe (kōe). During the tenth generation, Shōnyo Shōnin, the hall was moved to Ishiyama in Settsu Province (Osaka). During the eleventh-generation Kennyo Shōnin, an imperial document of the Second-Rank Prince was granted and the title of monzeki (gate of the imperial family) was imperially approved. The hall was then moved to Sagimori in Kii Province, and finally in the eighth month of the nineteenth year of Tenshō (1591) it was relocated to Rokujō-Horikawa.
(Further details are found in the *Nobunagaki Shūi*.)

In the main hall, a self-carved portrait image of the founder, Holy Priest Shinran, is enshrined.
