## Shiruyatani-zan Bukkōji Temple

Shiruyatani-zan Bukkōji Temple is located on Gojō Bōmon-dōri Street.
(It was originally named Kōshōji.)

The temple belongs to the Jōdo Shinshū tradition as propagated by Saint Shinran, and is called the Bukkōji-ha (Bukkōji sect). The main hall enshrines a portrait (御影, *omiei*) of Shinran, the founder, which he is said to have made himself.

The principal image (*honzon*) of Amida Hall is a standing statue of Amida Buddha.
(There is also a seated statue, measuring over two *shaku* in height.)
(The standing Amida Buddha statue measures three *shaku*, one *sun*, and eight *bu* in height, and is a work of Jikaku Daishi.)

This principal image has the following legend: During the reign of Emperor Daigo, thieves broke into the temple and stole the sacred image. However, it was too heavy to carry away, so they abandoned it at Nijō Riverbed. From that very night, the statue emitted an auspicious light (*zuikō*) that illuminated the Imperial Palace, causing all court officials to marvel at the phenomenon. The Emperor inquired into the origin of the light and it was determined to be the radiance of Amida Buddha. An imperial envoy, in astonishment, presented the sacred statue to the Emperor, and it was enshrined within the Palace. Thereafter, it was moved to Kōshōji Temple, the temple's name was changed to Bukkōji, and an imperial plaque was bestowed. Furthermore, the Emperor personally wrote the illustrated biography (*etoki-den*) of Saint Shinran in his own hand, and bestowed an imperial decree (*rinji*) designating the temple as the leading institution for the exclusive practice of *nembutsu* (recitation of Amida's name).

In the flanking altar of Amida Hall are enshrined a wooden statue made by Prince Shōtoku himself and a statue made by Saint Hōnen himself. The side chamber is called the "Zonkaku Room," as Zonkaku Shōnin—son of Kakunyo Shōnin, the third abbot of Honganji—resided here and compiled works including the *Rokuyōshō* and *Shibu Kujō*.

The temple was founded when Saint Shinran was forty years old, established in Higashino Village, Yamashina Township, Yamashiro Province, and named Kōshōji. It was entrusted to his leading disciple, Shinbutsu Shōnin. Later, the villa called Hanazono-tei at Gojō Nishi-no-Tōin, belonging to Lord Kujō Kanezane, was donated to the Saint, who named it Hanazono-in, which became the cloister name of Kōshōji.
(During the reign of the ninety-fourth Emperor, Emperor Hanazono, "en" [園, garden] was changed to "on" [恩, grace].)

In the first year of Gen'ō (1319), during the reign of Emperor Go-Daigo, the temple was relocated to Shiruyatani in Takenaka-no-shō, present-day Imahibie. Its boundaries extended from Amida-ga-mine in the east to Yanagihara (present-day Shichijō Riverbed) in the west, from Sugetani in the south to Shiruyatani Ōji in the north. Later, Ashikaga Takauji donated rice fields (*bupponden*) for Buddhist offerings as this was his temple of prayer (*kiganji*). From this time the sect flourished, with devotees—monks and laypersons alike—spreading throughout the country, and the sub-temples (*tatchū*) numbering as many as forty-eight. However, during the Bunmei era (1469–1487), the fourteenth abbot Kyōgō Shōnin became affiliated with Rennyo Shōnin of Yamashina Honganji, and forty-two sub-temple monks along with many followers from various provinces followed suit. Therefore, Kyōgo Shōnin's younger brother, Kyōyo Shōnin, succeeded as the fourteenth-generation abbot.

The six sub-temples of the area remain within the temple precinct today.

During the time of Lord Toyotomi Hideyoshi, the temple was moved to its current location due to the construction of the Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden).

## Shijō Tachiumri (Fourth Avenue Market)

Shijō Tachiumri refers to the area east of Higashi-no-Tōin on Shijō-dōri (Fourth Avenue). In former times, when the Great Imperial Palace (*Daidairi*) was here, this area served as a marketplace where various goods were traded.
(Even today, every morning to the north of Takakura on Shijō, there is a market for wild herbs—perhaps a remnant of the customs of old.)

---

## Shinmei-gū Shrine

Shinmei-gū Shrine is located to the west of Takakura on Ayanokōji Street. The deities enshrined here are the Inner and Outer Shrines of Ise (*Naikū* and *Gekū*).

---

## Ōhara-sha Shrine

Ōhara-sha Shrine is located to the east of Shinmachi on Ayanokōji Street. The deity enshrined is Izanami-no-Mikoto.
(It enshrines the same deity as Ōhara-sha Shrine in Kuwata District, Tanba Province.)

---

## Kōyaku Dōjō (Plaster Hall)

The place called Kōyaku Dōjō was formerly located between Shinmachi and Nishi-no-Tōin, south of Shijō Avenue. It is now known as Kōyaku-zushi (Kōyaku Lane).

---

## Tsutsumi Tenjin Shrine

Tsutsumi Tenjin Shrine is located to the north of Takatsūji, between Higashi-no-Tōin and Karasuma Streets. The area is called Takeno-zushi (Bamboo Lane), and the shrine is also known as Seikō-an.
