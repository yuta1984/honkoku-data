## Kamigoryō Shrine (*Kamigoryō no Yashiro*)

Kamigoryō Shrine is located to the south of Kuramaguchiدوری (Kuramaguchi Street) in Heiankyō (Kyoto). The deities enshrined here are the **Eight Goryō** (*hasshо goryō*):

> Prince Sawara, Prince Iyo, Lady Fujiwara, Fumitayū (Funya no Miyatamaru), Tachibana no Hayanari, Fujiwara no Hirotsugu, Minister Kibi, and the Fire-Thunder Deity (*Karai-jin*)

They were solemnly enshrined in the second year of Tengyō (939), during the reign of Emperor Suzaku. In ancient times, this site was the location of Kamiidumo-ji Temple; therefore, the shrine is also known as "the Goryō of Izumoji." The annual festival (*reisai*) is held on the eighteenth day of the eighth month.

> *(Marginal note): Naka-Goryō (Middle Goryō Shrine) is located to the south of Rosanji Temple on Kyōgoku Street, and serves as the otabisho (temporary resting place for the deity's procession) of this shrine.*

### Brief Biographies of the Enshrined Deities

**○ Prince Sawara** (*Sawara Shinnō*) was the second son of Emperor Kōnin. In the ninth month of Enryaku 4 (785), rumors spread that he had conspired to overthrow the court. He was subsequently exiled to Awaji Province, where he lost consciousness and died at a place called Takase in that province. Because his vengeful spirit (*onryō*) caused calamities, he was posthumously granted the title "Emperor Sudō" (*Sudō Tennō*) by imperial decree in the seventh month of Enryaku 19 (800).

> *(Marginal note): He is the same deity as that enshrined at Fujinomori Shrine in Kii District.*

**○ Prince Iyo** (*Iyo no Shinnō*) was a son of Emperor Kanmu. When his treasonous intentions were revealed during the reign of Emperor Heizei, he was confined to Kawaradera Temple, where he refused all food and drink until he died.

**○ Lady Fujiwara** (*Fujiwara no Bunin*), also known by the name Yoshiko, was the consort of Emperor Sudō (Prince Sawara) and the mother of Prince Iyo.

**○ Funya no Miyatamaru** was exiled to Izu Province in the twelfth month of Jōwa 10 (843) on account of a conspiracy he had planned, and died there.

**○ Tachibana no Hayanari** was the son of Tachibana no Iruko, Junior Fourth Rank Lower, Right Middle Counselor. He was a celebrated calligrapher during the reign of Emperor Saga, and is counted among the **Three Great Calligraphers of Japan** (*Honchō Sanpitsu*). In the seventh month of Jōwa 1 (834), during the reign of Emperor Ninmyō, he was implicated in a conspiracy and exiled to Izu Province, where he died in the ninth month of the same year.

**○ Fujiwara no Hirotsugu** was the eldest son of Fujiwara no Umakai. He raised a rebellion at Dazaifu. Ōno no Azumabito received an imperial order and rode out to engage him in battle. Hirotsugu was defeated and cut off his own head with a sword. His severed head instantly rose into the sky and transformed into a red mirror (*shakkyō*) in the air. Everyone who witnessed this died on the spot. Kagami-no-Miya in Bungo Province and the Itahitsu Myōjin in Hizen Province enshrine this spirit.

**○ Minister Kibi** (*Kibi no Daijin*) held the office of Minister of the Right (*Udaijin*), Senior Second Rank. He was an unparalleled genius of Japan, and served as an envoy to Tang China (*kentōshi*) during the reign of Empress Genshō. While in China, he was tasked with interpreting the "Poem of the Wild Horse Terrace" (*Yabadai no Fumi*), but found its meaning impenetrable. At that moment, he concentrated his mind in prayer to the Kannon of Hase (Hasedera Temple) in Japan, whereupon a spider descended, trailing a thread to guide him, and he was thus able to read it with ease. He returned to Japan in Tenpyō 5 (733) and passed away in Hōki 6 (775) during the reign of Emperor Kōnin, at the age of eighty-two.

**○ The Fire-Thunder Deity** (*Karai-jin*) is identified with Kitano Tenmangū's deity (*Kitano Tenman Tenjin*).

The principal image (*honzon*) of the Kannon Hall is a *Shō Kanzeon* (Holy Kannon) said to have been carved by Prince Shōtoku. This is said to be the original honzon of Izumoji Temple.

### On the Nakagawa (Middle River)

The Nakagawa refers to the stream that flows in front of Kamigoryō Shrine. The Kamo River is called the "East River" (*Higashikawa*), and the Katsura River is called the "West River" (*Nishikawa*); since this stream runs between them, it was named "Middle River" (*Nakagawa*). It is also known by the alternate name "Kyōgoku River" (*Kyōgoku-gawa*). Today, this river runs along the street lined with temples on Kyōgoku Avenue. According to the *Moshiogusa* (a poetic reference work), the "Nakagawa" referred to in poetry is this Kyōgoku River, which flowed between the Midō Palace (Fujiwara no Michinaga's residence) and Hōjōji Temple.

Furthermore, in the *Tale of Genji* (*Genji Monogatari*), there is a scene in which Genji secretly visits the lodging at Nakagawa and plays a game of go with Utsusemi.

**（From *Shoku Kokinshū*）**
> *Kono koro wa / nagaruru mizu o / seki irete / kokage suzushiki / Nakagawa no yado*
> These days, the flowing water has been dammed and diverted — how cool and pleasant is the shade of the trees at the lodge by Nakagawa.
> — *Mitsutoshi* (Fujiwara no Mitsutoshi)

**（From *Shin Shoku Kokinshū*）**
> *Misogi suru / waga Nakagawa no / ōnusa wa / tsui ni yoseru ya / ausē naran*
> The great purification wand (*ōnusa*) with which I perform ritual ablutions in my Nakagawa — will it at last drift and bring us together?
> — *Jakushin Hōshi*
