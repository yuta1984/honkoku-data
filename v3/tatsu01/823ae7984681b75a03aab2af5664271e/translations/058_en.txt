## From Hakusen-shū (Collection of Moored Boats)

The evening cool at Shijō riverbank: from around the time of the evening moon until past the hour of the dawn moon, beds (yuka, riverside platforms) are lined up in the middle of the river, and through the night people drink sake, eat food, and make merry. Women wear their obi sashes tied primly and properly; men wear their haori jackets long and elegantly. Buddhist monks and elderly people mingle together, and even apprentices and children of coopers and blacksmiths join in the lively singing and revelry — truly a scene befitting the capital.

　*Kawakaze ya / usumono kitaru / yū-suzumi*
　(The river breeze — wearing thin summer robes / the evening cool)　— Bashō

## On the Kabuki Theatre

The theatre is located to the east of Shijō on the Kamo River. During the Eiroku era (1558–1570), a rōnin from Ōmi Province named Nagoya Sanzaemon colluded with a refined woman called Izumo no Okuni, and devised a form of theatrical performance called "Kabuki," featuring both male and female performers. They staged performances in the Kitano forest, in the southern grove of Gion, and south of the Gojō riverbank and bridge. When Lord Hideyoshi traveled up to the capital from Fushimi Castle, the crowds of spectators caused an obstruction, and so the performances were relocated to the Shijō riverbank. After a period of suspension, in the second year of Jōō (1653), a man named Murayama Matabei revived the performances on the central island of the Shijō riverbank. The venue was then moved to north of Shijō on Nawate Street, and finally, during the Kanbun era (1661–1673), it was relocated to its present site and became a permanent theatre (*tsune-shibai*).

## Chūgen-ji Temple

Chūgen-ji is located at the southeastern corner of the Yamato Ōhashi bridge on Shijō. It belongs to the Jōdo sect and is affiliated with Chion-in. The principal image, a Jizō Bodhisattva, is a sacred statue that appeared from within the earth.
　(One account says it is the work of the sculptor Jōchō.)
People commonly call it the "Eyesore Jizō" (*Meyami Jizō*). Praying here for the healing of eye diseases is said to bring miraculous efficacy. In truth, it was originally called the "Rain-stopping Jizō" (*Ameyami Jizō*), derived from travelers taking shelter in this hall during sudden summer showers. On the side altar, an Amida Buddha made by the Venerable Eshin Sōzu (Genshin) is enshrined to the south; a Senju Kannon (Thousand-armed Avalokiteśvara) of Kasuga's making is to the north; and a Yakushi Buddha is placed in the abbot's quarters. All are said to have been made by Kōbō Daishi.

## On the Miyakawa River

The Miyakawa is another name for the Kamo River south of Shijō. In ancient times, there was a shrine to the legendary King Yu (*Uō*) in this area. (King Yu is the deity who subdued floods.) In later ages, as houses were built one after another, it became the name of the district.

## Kenninji Zen Temple, Higashiyama

Kenninji Zen Temple of Higashiyama is located south of Yamato Ōji and Shijō.
(The street south of Shijō in front of the gate is called Kenninji-machi. South of the inner gate, bamboo fencing is used in place of a wall — this is called *Kennin-gaki* [Kenninji fencing].)

It ranks third among the Five Mountain temples (*Gozan*). Its founder is the National Master Senkō, the high priest Yōjō, whose posthumous name (*imina*) was Eisai. He was born in Kibitsu, Bitchū Province, of the Kaya clan — said to be the great-grandson of the Governor of Satsuma Province, Sadamasa. He passed away on the fifth day of the seventh month of the third year of Kenpō (1215), at the age of seventy-five.

As the temple was founded at the prayer-wish (*chokugan*) of Emperor Tsuchimikado, the Shōgun Minamoto no Yoriie donated the grounds, and in the third year of Kennin (1203), the entire temple complex was constructed. Because it was a *chokugan* temple, the era name was used for the temple's name. The main image in the Buddha Hall is Śākyamuni Buddha, flanked by Mahākāśyapa and Ānanda. The Founder's Pagoda, named Kōzen-gokoku-in, stands on the eastern hill and serves as the mausoleum of National Master Eisai.
