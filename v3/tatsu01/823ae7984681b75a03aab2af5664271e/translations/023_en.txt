**Hanjū Zanmai-in Temple**

Hanjū Zanmai-in is located to the west of Itoyadachō on Imadegawa-dōri. The temple practices a combined tradition of Tendai Ritsu, Shingon, and Pure Land Buddhism, and is referred to as an inner sanctuary of the Imperial Palace. The temple was founded by the Venerable Eitoku, who took the Buddhist name Zenkū; his courtesy name was Kokosen, and his posthumous title was the Reverend Enci. The principal image is a seated Amida Buddha, said to be the work of Jikaku Daishi (Ennin). Mortuary tablets (shinpai) of successive emperors are enshrined here. The funerary pagoda of Emperor Go-Tsuchimikado-in stands to the west of the main hall. The burial mound of Princess Shikishi (Shokushi Naishinnō) is also on the temple grounds, and is known as "Teika-kazura no Tsuka" (the Mound of Teika Vine). In ancient times, this site was the country villa of Lord Fujiwara no Teika. The temple takes its name from the crossroads at its gate. Originally, this temple was located at Shizuki in Fushimi, but was moved to its present site in the third year of Bunroku (1594).

**Eishōzan Jōfuku-ji Temple**

Eishōzan Jōfuku-ji is located to the west of Ichijō-dōri. It belongs to the Pure Land (Jōdo) sect and is affiliated with Chion-in. The principal image of Amida Buddha is said to be the work of Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai). The temple was founded by the Venerable Jinyo of the Kōren-sha. The plaque bearing the name "Jōfuku-ji" on the main hall is in the personal calligraphy of Emperor Go-Nara.

---

**Annonzan Daichō-ji Temple**

Annonzan Daichō-ji is located to the west of Jōfuku-ji. It belongs to the same Pure Land sect. The principal image of Amida Buddha is said to be the work of the Venerable Eshin Sōzu (Genshin). When Eshin Sōzu made a seven-day pilgrimage to the Grand Shrine of Ise, the Amida Triad appeared to him in mid-air. He then carved the three figures modeled on those sacred forms he had witnessed. At that time, a mysterious visitor (kenjin) came and worked alongside him, which is why people say that the images are the work of the divine spirits.

---

**The Origin of the Name "Nishijin" (Western Camp)**

The name "Nishijin" (literally, "Western Camp") derives from the period of Meitoku, when the two regent clans of Yamana and Hosokawa fought numerous battles within the capital. Those who encamped to the west of the Horikawa River, north of Ichijō, were called the "Western Camp" (Nishijin), while those east of the Horikawa were called the "Eastern Camp" (Higashijin).
