**Right Page**

**Hanjū Zanmai-in** (般舟三昧院) is located to the west of Itoyadori on Imadegawa Avenue. The temple is eclectic in its doctrine, encompassing Tendai, Shingon, Ritsu, and Jōdo (Pure Land) teachings, and is designated as a practice hall within the Imperial Palace grounds. The temple's founder was the Venerable Keitoku, who took the religious name Zenkū, the courtesy name Keisen, and the posthumous title of Reverend Enji. The principal image is a seated statue of Amitābha Buddha, crafted by Jikaku Daishi (Ennin). The memorial tablets (shinpai) of successive emperors are enshrined here. The pagoda of Emperor Go-Tsuchimikado stands to the west of the main hall. The burial mound of Imperial Princess Shikishi (Shokushi) is also within the temple grounds and is known as the "Teika Kazura no Tsuka" (Mound of Teika's Kudzu). In former times, this location was the villa of Lord Fujiwara no Teika, and the alleyway in front of the gate is called "Teika no Zushi." The temple originally stood at Shigetsuin in Fushimi and was relocated to its present site in the third year of Bunroku (1594).

**Eshōzan Jōfuku-ji** (恵照山浄福寺) is situated to the west of Ichijō Avenue. It belongs to the Jōdo sect and is affiliated with Chion-in. The principal image of Amitābha Buddha was crafted by Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai). The temple was founded by the Venerable Jinyo of Kōrenshain. The plaque on the main hall bears the inscription "Jōfuku-ji" in the imperial brushwork (shinpitsu) of Emperor Go-Nara.

**Annonzan Daichō-ji** (安穏山大超寺) is located to the west of Jōfuku-ji and belongs to the same Jōdo sect. The principal image of Amitābha Buddha was created by the Venerable Eshin Sōzu (Genshin), who, during a seven-day retreat at the Grand Shrine of Ise, witnessed the vision of Amitābha and his two attendant bodhisattvas appearing in the sky. He then carved the Three Holy Figures in accordance with this sacred vision. As a divine incarnation (keshin) arrived and assisted in the carving, people came to regard the statue as the work of the deity Shinmei (the Ise deity).

The name **Nishijin** ("Western Camp") originates from the time of the Meitoku era, when the two shogunal deputies (shikkens) Yamana and Hosokawa engaged in multiple battles within the capital. The forces that gathered to the west of Horikawa River, north of Ichijō Avenue, were called the "Western Camp" (Nishijin), while those to the east of Horikawa were called the "Eastern Camp" (Higashijin). (For further details, refer to the *Ōnin-ki*.)
