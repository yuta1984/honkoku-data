**Daikōzan Honkokuji Temple**

Daikōzan Honkokuji Temple is located to the south of Horikawa Matsubara. It belongs to the Nichiren sect, specifically the Itchi-ha (Unified School). The temple was founded by the Venerable Nichiren, initially built at Matsugayatsu in Kamakura, Sagami Province, and named Hokkedō (Lotus Sutra Hall). This was the first monastery of the Nichiren sect.

Venerable Nichiren's family name was Mikuni, and he was a distant descendant of Emperor Shōmu. His father was Shigetada, the second son of Nuki-no Shigezane, the governor of Tōtōmi Province, and his mother was of the Kiyohara clan. He was born on the 16th day of the 2nd month of Jōō 1 (1222), at the Hour of the Horse (around noon), at Kominato-ura in Bōshū (Awa Province).

At age 12, he climbed to Kiyosumidera Temple in the same province to study Shingon Buddhism. At 18, he shaved his head and took the religious name "Zeshō," which he later changed to "Nichiren." From childhood he was exceptionally gifted, and he constantly prayed to Kokūzō Bosatsu (Ākāśagarbha Bodhisattva). One night, in a dream, an elderly monk appeared carrying a jewel as radiant as the morning star and presented it to him. From that point forward, he could understand ten things from hearing just one. He then traveled widely among various Buddhist schools, visiting Nanto (Nara) and Hokurei (Mount Hiei), and entering Onjōji Temple (Miidera), studying with the same fervor as one who gathers fireflies by the study window. Though he carefully judged the doctrines of the various schools, their arguments proved difficult to clear away like mist. He then searched the scriptures himself, arriving at the supreme golden teaching, the king among all sutras, and came to understand it as the fundamental source of sentient beings' attainment of Buddhahood.

On the 21st day of the 3rd month of Kenchō 5 (1253), at age 32, he faced the rising sun with palms pressed together and chanted for the first time the seven characters "Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō." At the southern face of Kiyosumidera, he gathered the temple's monks, as well as the provincial constable Tōjō Sakon'e Kagenaga and others, to preach the Lotus Sutra and present a written treatise on its doctrines. The monks of other sects followed like leaves in the wind. This was the beginning of the sect's propagation.

In the 5th month of Kōchō 1 (1261), Taira no Shigetoki, out of jealousy, had him exiled to the coast of Itō in Izu Province. At another time, at the shore of Tatsunokuchi in Sagami Province, he was about to be executed, seated upon a straw mat, when suddenly the sky darkened and the earth shook. The executioner's eyes were blinded, and the sword broke into pieces. The Governor of Sagami was greatly alarmed and spared his life. In Bun'ei 8 (1271), he was again exiled to Sado Province, but the spread of his teachings continued to grow. A pardon was sent to that island, and from then on the sect spread throughout the entire country without exception. Eventually, even the Governor of Sagami came to revere him.

In the 5th month of Bun'ei 11 (1274), the Venerable Nichiren left Kamakura and entered Mount Minobu in Kai Province, where he built a thatched hermitage. In spring mornings he would pick flowers to offer to the Buddha; on autumn evenings he would wait for the moonlight to illuminate his scriptures. And once, when night rain came knocking at his window, he composed this verse:

> *As for the heart— the rain falling aslant is a storm, and the wind is the guest of the night, shifting and uncertain.* — Venerable Nichiren

In the reign of Emperor Gouda, on the 13th day of the 10th month of Kōan 5 (1282), he passed away at the residence of Ikegami Saemon Munenaka in Ebara, Musashi Province. He was sixty-one years of age.

The Hokkedō at Matsugayatsu in Kamakura was bequeathed to Nichirō. Nichiin also resided here, and during the time of Nichijō, it became a prayer temple by imperial wish. In Jōwa 1 (1345), by imperial decree of Emperor Kōmyō, the temple was moved from Kamakura in Sagami Province to Rokujō Horikawa in the capital (Kyoto).

> This site was formerly the residence of Rokujō Hangan (Minamoto no) Tameyoshi. That Minamoto no Yoshitsune also resided here is recorded in The Tale of the Heike.

**Main Hall (Hondō):** The Lotus Sutra is enshrined as the principal object of worship.
> Written in the "one-character, three-prostrations" style by the Monk-Bishop Nicchō.

**Standing Image Hall (Ryūzōdō):** A standing image of Shakyamuni Buddha is enshrined.
> This is a sacred Buddha that miraculously appeared from the sea floor off Itō in Izu Province, caught in a fisherman's net.

**Patriarch's Hall (Soshidō):** Portrait images of Venerable Nichiren, as well as Nichirō, Nichiin, Nichijō, and Nicchō, are enshrined. This hall was originally the Yakushi Hall of Kaikōji Temple at Hachijō. The temple name "Kaikōji" is inscribed on the roof tiles.

**Setsu Hall (Satsudō):** Kishimojin (Hāritī) and the Ten Rākṣasī Daughters are enshrined.

**Abbot's Quarters (Hōjō):** Called Myōhōke-in. This pavilion was originally located in Azuchi in Ōmi Province. The paintings are by Kanō Eitoku, and the hanging placard was calligraphed by Mito Kōmon, Lord Mitsukuni.

**Hitomaro Shrine:** Located beside the Abbot's Quarters. Lord Takauji built a tower here, naming it the Kanryūtei (Pavilion for Viewing Willows).

**Sōtai Stone:** Also known as the Prayer Stone. Located to the east of the Abbot's Quarters.

**Oshidori Mandala (Mandala of Mandarin Ducks):** Painted by Venerable Nichiren. The mounting is on a floral-colored ground with a small mandarin duck pattern of about one sun (approx. 3 cm). This is called the "brocade robe of Yang Guifei," and it is designated the "Honkokuji-gire" (Honkokuji fabric/cut).
