## The Water of Ono no Komachi's Sōshi-arai (Paper-Washing)

The water where Ono no Komachi is said to have washed her poem sheets (sōshi) is located in the garden of a daimyo's residence to the northeast (ushitora) of Modori-bashi. It is also called "Seiwa-sui" (Seiwa Water). Beside it stands a memorial pagoda to Komachi.

---

## Seimei-sui (Seimei's Sacred Water)

This water source is located here. It is said that when Abe no Seimei was performing esoteric rites and prayed for sacred water (shinsui), water miraculously welled up at this spot in answer to his prayers.

---

## Sagari-matsu (The Drooping Pine)

This tree stands here. This is the famous "Sagari-matsu of Ichijo" known throughout the ages, celebrated from ancient times.

---

## Abe no Seimei Shrine

The shrine of Abe no Seimei is located to the west of Ichijo, in the districts of Yoshiya-machi and Seimei-chō. The deity enshrined there is the divine spirit of Seimei himself. It is said that this site was formerly Seimei's own residence.

---

## Suika Tenjin (Water-and-Fire Heavenly Deity Shrine)

Suika Tenjin is located to the north of Horikawa, in the alleyway (tsujiko) of Tenjin. The deity enshrined is said to be the divine spirit of water and fire. According to one theory, it is in fact Tenman Tenjin (the deified spirit of Sugawara no Michizane).

---

## Zuikō-in Temple

Zuikō-in is located to the north of Agui. It was formerly the private residence of Asano Danjō. The guardian shrine deity is called "Asano Inari." There is one memorial pagoda for Asano Takumi-no-kami Naganori, lord of Akō Castle in Banshū, and his retainers — with the names of Ōishi Kuranosuke Yoshio and all forty-six loyal retainers (gishi) inscribed upon it. Among the temple's treasured possessions are a portrait of Kuranosuke, his farewell death poems (jisei), and letters and other writings.

---

## Ekōzan Honryū-ji Temple

Ekōzan Honryū-ji is located to the north of Itsutsuji. It belongs to the Nichiren (Hokke) sect, and was founded by the monk Nisshin. The stone stupa bearing the daimoku (sacred title) is in the calligraphy of the monk Nichizō. Originally this temple stood to the west of Shijō; later it was relocated to Nishijin, and then moved to its present site at the time of the construction of Jurakudai (Jurakutei).

---

## Sakuraha-no-Miya Shrine

Sakuraha-no-Miya is located to the east of Senbonstreet on Demizu-dōri. The deity enshrined is Amaterasu Ōmikami. Because the goddess is said to have descended from heaven (amakudari) to the right guard's riding ground (Ukone no baba) in ancient times, the shrine is called "Hifuri no Shinmei" (the Shinmei deity who descended with light).
