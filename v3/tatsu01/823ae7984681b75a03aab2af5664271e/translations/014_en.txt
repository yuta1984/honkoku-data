## Shōkoku Shōten Zen-ji (Shōkokuji Temple)

Mannenzan Shōkoku Shōten Zen-ji is located to the north of Imadegawa. It is the second among the Five Mountains (Gozan) of Kyoto. Its founding patron was National Master Musō Soseki, and the second abbot was Myōha. It was constructed by Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, the third shogun, in the third year of Meitoku (1392), during the reign of Emperor Gokomatsu. In the Buddha Hall, a statue of Śākyamuni Buddha is enshrined, with Mahākāśyapa and Ānanda placed to the left and right, and images of Bodhidharma and Daigen are placed on the sutra dais. The Founder's Hall contains a statue of National Master Musō. The hall was rebuilt by Retired Emperor Gomizunoo, whose spirit tablet is also enshrined there. The three-storied pagoda has Mahāvairocana (Dainichi Nyorai) as its principal image and was likewise rebuilt by Retired Emperor Gomizunoo. The main gate is called the Entsūkaku (Gate of Perfect Penetration), the pond is named the Kudokuike (Pond of Merit), and the bridge is called the Tenkaikyō (Bridge of the Heavenly Realm). Beside the monastery kitchen, Bishamonten (Vaiśravaṇa) is enshrined. ⟨The deity is known for striking miracles, and there are always many worshippers visiting.⟩ In the bamboo grove of the sub-temple Fukōin, there is the tomb of Lord Teika, the Minister of the Right (Sadaijin). ⟨Before the tomb stands one stone lantern, inscribed: "Built in the twelfth month of Jōkyō 3 (1686) by Uchiushō Tametsuta-kyō."⟩ The spring known as Hōnen-sui (Hōnen's Water) is found at the sub-temple Shōōken. ⟨This site was originally the shrine-temple of the Kamo deity and the former location of Hyakumanben. It is so named because the monk Hōnen Shōnin once resided here.⟩

## Kyōgoku Hachiman Shrine

Kyōgoku Hachiman Shrine is located to the west of Kamigoryō Shrine. According to the *Shoshakongenki* (Records of the Origins of Various Shrines), "In ancient times it stood at Sanjō Kyōgoku, and after the Ōnin War it was relocated to its present site." The *Seisui-ki* states: "Kyōgoku-ji is a subsidiary shrine of Hiyoshi." Today, it is maintained by a monk of the Shingon sect.

## Izumojinokami Shrine (Sainokami)

The deity of Izumoji is located to the west of Kyōgoku and to the north of Imadegawa. The enshrined deity is Saruta-hiko-no-Mikoto, a god of the crossroads (*dōsojin*). It is now called "Sainokami." The original site was to the east of Kyōgoku.

## Agata Well (*Agata-ido*)

The Agata Well is a famous scenic spot (*meisho*) of the capital. In ancient times, it stood to the north of Ichijō and to the west of Higashinōin. ⟨It was located beside the Agata Shrine, from which it takes its name.⟩

## Myōkakuji Temple

Gusokuzan Myōkakuji Temple is situated at the northern end of Shinmachi street. It belongs to the Hokke (Nichiren) sect and was founded by the Venerable Nichijitsu. The *Niō* (Kongorikishi, Guardian Kings) at the two-storied gate are works attributed to Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai). In the Founder's Hall, images of three masters—Nichiren, Nichinō, and Nichizō—are enshrined. ⟨This hall was constructed by a craftsman from Hida Province, and its design and proportions are incomparable, serving as a model for the construction of all the other halls.⟩ The Kahō Pagoda contains the *Lotus Sutra* in Nichiren's own hand. The *Purple Seal Gold Mandala* and the *Kakuryū Mandala* are both painted in Nichiren's hand and are treasures of this temple. ⟨This temple originally stood to the south of Koromotana Nijō—the area is still called "Myōkakuji-chō" today. By order of Toyotomi Hideyoshi during the Tenshō era, it was relocated to its present site. The temple also contains the tombs of the painter Kanō Ko-hōgen Motonobu and several successive generations of the Kanō family.⟩

## Myōrenji Temple

Ubokuzan Myōrenji Temple is located to the west of Teranōchi street at Ogawa. It belongs to the same Hokke sect, and was founded by the Venerable Nichizō. In ancient times it stood at Nishidōin Gojō, where a man named Yanagiya Nakaoki, a devout follower of Nichizō, donated his residential land, and the temple was called "Yanagi-dera" (Willow Temple). It was subsequently moved to the south of Ōmiya street at Shijō, then to Motoganganji street at Ōmiya, and finally relocated to its present site in the twenty-fourth year of Tenshō (1596). Among the temple's treasures is a *Lotus Sutra Mandala* written in Nichiren Shōnin's own hand, venerated as the principal image for rain prayer (*kiū*). During the reign of Retired Emperor Gokōgon, there was a great drought throughout the land. This principal image was carried to the banks of the Katsura River, where a rain-summoning ritual (*shōu no hō*) was performed, and miraculously, heavy rain fell for several days. For this reason, Nichiren Shōnin was granted the honorific title "Great Bodhisattva" (*Daibosatsu*).
