**Mannenzan Shōkoku Shōten Zen-ji (Shōkoku-ji)**

Shōkoku-ji is located north of Imadegawa. It holds second rank among the Gozan (Five Mountains) temples of Kyoto. It was founded by the National Master Enkei Sogan, with Shunoku Myōha as the second abbot. The temple was built by the third Ashikaga shogun, Yoshimitsu, in the second year of Meitoku (1391), during the reign of Emperor Gokomatsu. In the main hall (butsuden), a statue of Shakyamuni Buddha is enshrined, with Mahakasyapa and Ananda placed to the left and right, and an image of Bodhidharma on a side altar. The Patriarch's Hall (Soshidō) houses an image of National Master Musō Soseki, rebuilt by Emperor Gomizunoo, whose memorial tablet is also enshrined there. The three-storied pagoda enshrines Dainichi Nyorai (Vairocana) as its principal deity and was also rebuilt by Emperor Gomizunoo. The main gate is called "Entsūkoku," the pond is named "Kudoku-ike" (Pond of Merit), and the bridge is named "Tenkai-bashi" (Bridge of the Heavenly Realm). A statue of Bishamonten (Vaiśravaṇa) is enshrined beside the kitchen quarters; this deity is renowned for miraculous powers, and the temple is always frequented by many worshippers.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　（Sub-temples）

In the bamboo grove of Fukōin (a sub-temple), there is the grave of Lord Teika (Fujiwara no Teika), the former Middle Counselor (Kōmon). In front of the grave stand two stone lanterns, inscribed: "Built in the twelfth month of Jōkyō 3 (1686) by Lord Tamemichi, former Middle Captain of the Right Guard."

The "Hōnen Water" spring is located at the sub-temple Shōkakuken. This site was originally the shrine temple of Kamo Shrine and the former location of Hyakumanben. Since the Venerable Hōnen resided here, it took its name from him.

**Kyōgoku Hachiman-gū**

Kyōgoku Hachiman-gū is located to the west of Kamigoryō (Shrine). According to the *Shosha Kongen-ki*, it was formerly located at Sanjō-Kyōgoku but was relocated to its current site after the Ōnin War. The *Seisui-ki* states: "Kyōgoku-ji is a subsidiary shrine of Hie Shrine." Currently, a Shingon Buddhist monk tends the shrine.

**Izumoji-jin (Saino-kami Shrine)**

The Izumoji Shrine is located to the west of Kyōgoku, near Imadegawa. The deity enshrined is Saruta-hiko no Mikoto, a god of crossroads (dōsojin). It is currently called "Saino-kami." Its former site was to the east of Kyōgoku.

**Agata no Ido (The Agata Well)**

The Agata Well is a famous site in Rakuyō (Kyoto). It is located north of the former Ichijō, to the west of Higashi-no-tōin. It takes its name from having been situated beside the Agata Shrine.

[Poem from the *Gosen Wakashū*:]
"Come, people of the capital, and linger here — where the frogs cry, the yamabuki (kerria) flowers bloom at the Agata Well." — Tachibana no Kōhei-jo (Lady Kōhei of the Tachibana clan)

[Poem from the *Zoku Gosenshū*:]
"As spring deepens at the Agata Well where the frogs cry, have the yamabuki flowers bloomed, or have they fallen?" — Emperor Gotoba

**Gusokuzan Myōon-ji**

Myōon-ji is located at the north end of Shinmachi. It belongs to the Hokke (Nichiren) sect and was founded by the Venerable Nichijitsu. The Niō (Guardian Kings) statues at the main gate are said to be the work of Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai). The Patriarch's Hall enshrines the images of three patriarchs: Nichiren, Nichirō, and Nichijitsu. This hall was built by a master craftsman from Hida Province and is of unparalleled artistry, serving as the model for the construction of temple halls in general.

The Hanabō Pagoda houses a copy of the Lotus Sutra in Nichiren's own handwriting. The Gold Mandala with Purple Seal and the Kakuryū Mandala are both in Nichiren's hand and are the temple's most prized treasures.

This temple was originally located south of Koromo-no-tana-Nijō; the area is still called "Myōkaku-ji-chō." During the Tenshō era, by order of Lord Hideyoshi, it was relocated to its present site. The temple also contains the graves of the painter Kanō Kogen Motonobu (Kanō Motonobu), as well as those of several subsequent generations of the Kanō family.

**Ubokizan Myōren-ji**

Myōren-ji is located west of Ogawa on Teranōchi-dōri. Like Myōon-ji, it belongs to the Nichiren sect and was founded by the Venerable Nichizō. It was formerly located at Nishi-no-tōin-Gojō, where a man named Yanagiya Nakako took faith in Nichizō and donated his residential land, leading the temple to be called "Yanagi-dera" (Willow Temple). Thereafter, it was relocated south of Ōmiya on Ōmiya-dōri at Shijō, then moved again to the former site of Ganzoji on Ōmiya-dōri, before being moved to its present location in the twenty-fourth year of Tenshō (Bunroku 5, 1596).

Among the temple's prized treasures is the "Rain-praying Principal Image" — a Lotus Sutra mandala in the hand of the Venerable Nichiren. During the reign of Emperor Gokōgon, there was a great drought throughout the realm. This principal image was taken to the banks of the Katsuragawa River, where rites for the pacification of the realm were performed. An immediate miraculous response occurred, and heavy rain fell for several days. As a result, the honorific title of "Great Bodhisattva" was bestowed upon the Venerable Nichiren.
