## Sekizō-ji Temple · Kangi-ji Temple · Ishi-jinja Shrine · The Former Site of Jurakudai

---

**Karyūzan Sekizō-ji Temple** is located to the north of Itsutsuji on Senbondōri Avenue. It belongs to the Jōdo (Pure Land) sect, and its principal image of Amida Buddha is said to have been carved by Sugawara no Michizane (Lord Kan). In the Jizō Hall stands a stone image of Jizō Bodhisattva in the form of a standing figure, carved by Kōbō Daishi. Those who believe and pray are met with divine response, and the temple's miraculous efficacy is remarkable. The name "Sekizō-ji" (Temple of the Stone Image) derives from this very image. Within the temple grounds there is also a memorial pagoda for the former Governor of Kazusa, Fujiwara no Ason Ietaka.

---

**Kitamuki-zan Kangi-ji Temple** is situated to the west of Kamitatéuri. It belongs to the Shingon sect, and its principal object of worship, the Kangiten (the Joyful Deva), is a work of Kōbō Daishi. This temple was established as a prayer temple (*chokuganjo*) by Emperor Saga, and its founding is attributed to Kōbō Daishi.

---

**Ishi-jinja Shrine** is located to the west of Kangi-ji. The object of worship is a great boulder approximately six shaku in length, enshrined within a storehouse (*dozō*). It is said to have originally stood within the precincts of the Great Imperial Palace (*Daidairi*).

---

**The Former Site of Jurakudai** stretches from south of Nijō to north of Nijō, bounded to the east by Ōmiya-dōri Avenue and to the west by Suzaku-dōri Avenue (present-day Senbondōri). In the thirteenth year of Tenshō (1585), the Grand Minister Toyotomi Hideyoshi constructed a great fortified palace here, declaring that only magnificent splendor could lend true authority and dignity. The palace halls were encrusted with the seven precious gems, and rare trees and curious stones were gathered from far and wide, making it a treasury of wonders rivaling the Afang Palace of the Qin dynasty and the Weiyang Palace of the Former Han dynasty. In the fourth month of Tenshō sixteen (1588), Emperor Go-Yōzei paid an imperial visit, during which gatherings for the composition of *waka* poetry and performances of *Nō* drama were held. Thereafter, the Regent Toyotomi Hidetsugu resided here, but upon his downfall in the fourth year of Bunroku (1595), the towers and halls were dismantled and distributed among various temples throughout the city. Today, only the name "Juraku" survives, having become the general designation for the surrounding district. The number of town blocks bearing the name "Jurakugumi" totals approximately one hundred and twenty.
