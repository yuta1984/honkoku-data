Shubin confined all the dragons by means of incantations, sealing them inside a jar. Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai) summoned the Zennyo Ryūō (the benevolent female dragon deity) from the Anavatapta Lake in India, relieving the realm of the suffering of drought and earning the deep admiration of the Emperor. Ono no Takamura also composed a waka poem and brought down rain. A heron, upon receiving an imperial decree, lowered its wings and crouched, whereupon officials were able to capture it with great ease. It was here that the Emperor, moved beyond measure, bestowed upon the heron the rank of the Fifth Court Rank. Furthermore, during a pleasure excursion by the Retired Emperor Shirakawa, cormorant fishing was performed for the imperial viewing, and the cormorant dove into the pond and emerged carrying a sword with a gold-rimmed fitting in its beak. From this event, the sword came to be named "Umaru" (Cormorant). It was passed down to Emperor Sutoku and then bestowed upon Rokujō Hangan Tameyoshi. The Gion Festival also originated here, and in the third year of Kōka (1846), Emperor Saga held a flower-viewing banquet within this garden — this was the origin of the flower-viewing banquet (hana no en). In the Honchō Monzui (Anthology of Japanese Literature), Minamoto no Shitagō wrote of the Shinsen-en: "The Shinsen-en is the flourishing heart of the imperial gardens. The crimson groves spread wide, swallowing the forests of Chu within them; the green pond's waters rise high, compressing the rivers of Wu beneath one's gaze." As the years accumulated, the garden fell into ruin around the Kenpo era. After the Jōkyū Rebellion, the Zen master of Bushū raised the earthen walls high and reinforced the gates in order to restore it. Thereafter it fell into neglect again and the old site became obscure, until during the Genna era a monk from Tsukushi named Kakuga petitioned the authorities and revived it, establishing it as a sacred site of the Shingon sect. Though what remains of the Right Near Guard's riding grounds at Kitano and this Shinsen-en may be meager, they are nonetheless remnants of the great imperial palace complex.
