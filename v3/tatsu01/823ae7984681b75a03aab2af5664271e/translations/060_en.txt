## Kenninji Temple — Description of the Temple Precinct

**[On the Bodhi Tree]**
The bodhi tree that (Zen Master Eisai) brought back upon his return from China is located within this temple. It has since flourished and grown into several trunks.

**[On the Kawara-no-in Bell]**
To the north of the Buddha Hall there are two bell towers; the large bell to the east is this one. It was originally at the Kawara-no-in, the palace that Minister Tōru (Minamoto no Tōru) built along the Rokujō riverbank, which was later converted into a Buddhist hall and named "Kawara-no-in." After the Kawara-no-in fell into ruin, the bell sank to the bottom of a deep pool to the south of Shichijō on the Kamo River. Zen Master Eisai learned of this, appealed to the authorities, obtained permission, and arranged to have it hung at this temple. When they tried to raise the bell from the pool, it would not budge at all. Therefore, through the master's wisdom, he instructed the laborers to call out in unison "Eisai!" as their chant, and also to call the name of the master's disciple "Chō Shuso (Head Monk Chō)" as they pulled. With this great cry among all the laborers, the bell was moved to this temple with surprising ease.

Even today, when moving heavy objects, calling out this name during transport derives from this very episode. Furthermore, some seven chō (approx. 760 meters) to the south of Shichijō on the Kamo River, there is a place called "Kama-ga-fuchi." This is the spot where the bell lay submerged, and in truth it should properly be called "Kane-ga-fuchi" (Bell Pool).

This bell is struck ninety times from the hour of the Rat (midnight) every night, and at dawn eighteen more strokes are added to make a total of one hundred and eight. In former times, the Dhāraṇī Sūtra was recited while the bell was struck; for this reason, the sound of this bell came to be called "the Dhāraṇī of Kenninji." The pond is named "Hōsui-ike" (Dharma Water Pond), and the middle gate is called "Yadate-mon" (Arrow-Stand Gate).

　　It is said that this was the gate of the residence of Lord Kadomaki Noritaka of the Taira clan.

**[On Zenkyoan and Marishiten]**
Marishiten is enshrined at Zenkyoan (sub-temple). This sacred image was brought from China in the year of the Horse, in Karyaku 2 (1327). Its miraculous efficacy is ever fresh, and worshippers constantly come to visit.

**[On the Myōtoku-seki Stone]**
The "Myōtoku Stone" refers to the stone bridge to the north of the Hōjō (Abbot's Quarters).

**[On the Rakushin-byō Shrine]**
The Rakushin-byō Shrine was established through the invocation of the Zen Master (Eisai) and serves as the guardian deity of this mountain. It enshrines the divine presence of the third deity ■ (one character illegible) of the Kibitsu Shrine in Bitchū Province.

**[Ankokuji Pagoda]**
Located in the garden of the Hōjō.

**[Oda Urakusai Pagoda]**
Located at Shōden-in. The sukiya (tea house) of the venerable Urakusai is also there.
