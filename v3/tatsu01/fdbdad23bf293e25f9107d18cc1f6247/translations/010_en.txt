**Sakai Village (境邑)**　Village Head: Taemon (of the Konno clan)

This village is a post station (umaya). It is called as such because it lies on the border between Kawabe District to the north and Senboku District to the south. According to the *Wakun no Shiori* (a classical Japanese dictionary), the word *sakai* ("boundary") derives from the meaning of *sakagai* ("meeting of slopes/borders"). In the *Nihon Shoki* (Chronicles of Japan), the characters for *Sakaibe* (境部, "border clan") are also written as *Sakaiabe* (坂合部). For this reason, it is also called *sakaime*. In the *Man'yōshū*, the characters 三中 are read as *sakai*, because Mt. Fuji (Fujisan, the "Exhaustless Mountain") spans the three provinces of Kai, Shinano, and Suruga. However, it is also argued that this should be read as *minaka*, meaning "the very center." The correct character for *sakai* (boundary) is 畍. The colloquial expression *nanjya sakai na* ("that's why" / "because of that") likely derives from this sense of division or demarcation. The boundary between Ōmi and Yamashiro provinces is at Oidawake, which is said to mark the border between the thirty-three provinces of the west and the thirty-three provinces of the east — so the text records.

According to the *Kyōhō Gun'yūki* (a regional gazetteer from the Kyōhō era), "Sakai Village has eighty-five households, of which one is a temple. The post-horse distance to the Yodogawa is eight *chō* and thirty-two *ken*; to Toyoshima Motomachi, three *ri*, twenty-five *chō*, and twenty-two *ken*. The boundary with Funaoka Village in Kawabe District is defined by Aikainо (合貝野), Kariyasawa (苅谷澤), and the bridge on the Mizuochi Highway."
