This verse praises the origins of the founding of this temple. How fitting it is, indeed! The recitation of the Mahāyāna sutras is the primary cause (shōin) for attaining the highest grade of rebirth (jōbon ōjō) in the Pure Land, and is one of the three meritorious acts (sanpuku) expounded in the Contemplation Sutra (Kanmuryōjukyō). For those who make even a single pilgrimage to this place, could the benefits received from the Wondrous Sutra ever be in vain? (Surely not.) At the moment of death, one goes straightaway to the Golden Gate of the World of Peace and Bliss (the Pure Land) — how reassuring and dependable that is! That this temple has been designated the foremost among all the pilgrimage sites — unless this were the authoritative decree of a manifestation of a Buddha or bodhisattva (gonke), who else could have reached such a judgment? One must not regard this place with a casual or careless eye.

○ Station Two: Ōōdana　Daihōzan Shinpukuji　*[Hall: five ken on each of four sides, facing south]*
　Principal Image: Shō Kannon　*[Standing image, height: one shaku five sun one bu]*　Carved by the Bodhisattva Gyōki

Inquiring into the origins of the principal image, Shō Kannon, of this temple: the founder of this temple, the Zen Master Daihō (Ōdana), was a person who possessed both wisdom and practice in equal measure. He concealed his brilliance and hid his virtue, embodying a crisp and pure wind, standing upright like towering peaks, cutting off (ordinary) food and avoiding grain, and never, even for a moment, parting from his dharma robes.
