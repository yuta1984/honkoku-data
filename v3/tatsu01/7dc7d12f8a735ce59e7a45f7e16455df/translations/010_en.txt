How fitting indeed — reciting the Mahayana sutras is the primary cause (shōin) for rebirth in the highest grade (jōbon ōjō), and it is one of the Three Meritorious Deeds explained in the Kanmuryōjukyō (Sutra of Contemplation on Amitāyus). Could the benefits of the Wondrous Sutra possibly be in vain for those who make even a single pilgrimage to this place? How reassuring it is that at the end of one's life, one shall immediately proceed to the golden gate of the World of Peace and Bliss (the Pure Land). That this temple has been designated as the foremost of all the pilgrimage sites — could anyone have achieved this without it being the determined decree of an incarnated bodhisattva (gonge)? One must not regard this place with a careless eye.

○ The Second Temple — Ōdana — Mount Ōdana, Shinpukuji Temple　[Side note: Main hall five bays, facing south on all four sides]
Principal image: Shō Kannon (Āryāvalokiteśvara)　[Side note: Standing image, height one shaku five sun one bu]　Carved by the Bodhisattva Gyōki

When we inquire into the origins of the principal image — Shō Kannon — of this temple, we find that the founder of this temple, the Zen Master Ōdana, was a person who combined wisdom and practice. He concealed his brilliance and hid his virtue, maintaining a clear and pure character (like a bracing wind), and upright integrity (like towering peaks). He abstained from ordinary food and avoided grains, and his monastic robes had never, even for a moment, left his body.
