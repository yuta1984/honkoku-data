...a man who lived there. By nature possessed of a twisted and perverse disposition, upon hearing the promptings of a false teacher (*jashi*), he became ever more consumed by greed and miserliness (*kentō*). He rejected the Buddhist Dharma, denied the principle of cause and effect (*inga*), heaped abuse and humiliation upon monks and Dharma teachers, and offered no relief to widowers, widows, orphans, or the destitute. As for pilgrims and wandering monks, he would not give them so much as half a coin, and word of this had spread so widely among traveling monks that none would even pause before his gate. Then one day, a pilgrim came and begged for food. Tange cried out, "What an infernal nuisance!" and drove him away with a staff. The pilgrim, not in the least startled, said: "If you will not give, then you should at least say so in words.

What boorish behavior this is!" and stood his ground, clearly agitated. Tange, growing all the more furious, declared: "What is that you say?! Our country is a divine land (*shinkoku*), and every single grain of rice is the sacred treasure of the gods. When I revere the gods, honor the Emperor, and exalt the lords of the realm, the Way of our household is fulfilled right here. Above all, you are a follower of the detestable Buddhist law.
