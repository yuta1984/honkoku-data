(Continued from previous page) ...no one dared to bring complaints to their lord, fearing his [the retainer's] exercise of power and authority. Thus, he grew ever more brazen in his wickedness. However, having sided with Soma no Masakado in his treasonous rebellion, in the third year of Tengyō he was utterly destroyed by the imperial forces.

The men and women of the village, harboring deep resentment over the outrages he had inflicted upon them day after day — loathing him so much they could have eaten his flesh — pursued him relentlessly, driving him into flight. He had intended to make his way toward Kōzuke province, but his path was cut off, and so, barely managing to slip away, he hid himself deep in the mountains and forests. With neither wife, children, nor retainers to accompany him — for there were none — he passed his days in circumstances utterly unlike those of his former life. In time he fell gravely ill, and at last breathed his last.

Around that time, a certain monk, who had been sheltering in the mountain forests of this very place with this sacred image in his keeping, having fled the turmoil of war, took pity on the dead man and buried his remains beneath a single pine tree. As time passed and the world grew calm once more, the common people who had scattered and wandered in all directions returned to their homes. When the dead man's wife and children came back and looked about, they found their dwelling had been destroyed, and they could not even determine the whereabouts of the husband. Searching and inquiring at length, they heard word that he had been buried in the mountains. They came in alarm and grief, and through the whole night came before the principal image to pray...
