...In order to forget the weariness of the long journey, pilgrims would each set the *eika* (詠歌, pilgrim's verse) to a melody and sing as they made their way to worship. I remember the *eika* of this temple very well," and with that, the elder recited the *eika* of this temple. The wandering monk was delighted and exclaimed: "How wonderful! How admirable that you have recalled this poem. This verse surpasses all other *eika* in the preciousness of its meaning. Do not forget the spirit of these words; in all your daily activities — walking, standing, sitting, and lying down — devote yourself solely to the single practice of reciting the nenbutsu (称名, the name of Amida Buddha). Surely, before long, the fighting in this land will be quelled, peace will be restored, and the Buddha's teaching will flourish greatly for a long time to come." With these words, he vanished as if melting into thin air.

The villagers who had been present were all left with a profound sense of wonder as they returned to their homes. And indeed, before long, an era of peace and prosperity descended upon the realm (四海昇平, *shikai shōhei*, "the four seas at peace"), and the Kannon pilgrimage sites throughout this district all at once burst into full bloom like flowers of prosperity, and the time came for each pilgrim to bear the fruit of enlightenment (*bodai*). The *eika* of this temple reads:

*Tada tanome / makoto no toki wa / Saizenji / kitari mukaen / Mida no sanzon*
(ただ頼め　まことのときは　西善寺　来り向かえん　弥陀の三尊)
"Only place your trust [in Amida]; when that true moment comes, at Saizenji, the Amida Triad shall come forth to welcome you."

As for the name "Saizenji" (西善寺) that appears in the poem, it is said to be the Buddhist name taken by the lay founder (*kaiki*, 開基) of this temple when he shaved his head and took the tonsure in his later years. That the wandering monk — a manifested being (*ke-nin*, 化人) — should have praised the elderly villager so fervently for remembering this temple's *eika* is something beyond the capacity of an ordinary mind to fathom fully...
