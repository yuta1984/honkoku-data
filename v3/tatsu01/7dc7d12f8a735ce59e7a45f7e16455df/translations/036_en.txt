...In order to forget the hardships of the long journey, each pilgrim would set the verse to a melodic chant (*fushihakase*) and sing it as they made their way to worship. "I remember well the verse of that time," the old villager said, and without delay, recited the *eika* (詠歌, devotional verse) of this temple. The traveling monk was greatly pleased and exclaimed: "How wondrous! How admirably you have recalled this poem! This verse surpasses all other *eika* in its profound grace. Never forget the spirit of this poem; in your walking, standing, sitting, and lying down—in all aspects of daily life—strive earnestly in the single practice of reciting the name of the Buddha (*shōmyō*, nembutsu). Assuredly, before long, the warfare in this land will be pacified, peace will be restored, and the Buddha's teachings will flourish greatly and enduringly." And with these words, he vanished as if wiped away.

All those who had been present were filled with a sense of the miraculous as they returned home. Before long, the world became one of peace throughout the four seas (*shikai shōhei*), and sure enough, the Kannon sacred sites throughout this district blossomed simultaneously with the flowers of flourishing prosperity, and the time arrived for each person to bear the fruit of enlightenment (*bodai*).

The *eika* of this temple reads:

　*Tada tanome / makoto no toki wa / Saizenji / kitari mukаen / Mida no sanzōn*
　(Only trust and rely; when the time of sincerity comes, at Saizenji, the Amida Triad shall come forth to receive you.)

The name "Saizenji" (西善寺) in the verse, it is said, was the Buddhist name taken by the lay devotee (*koji*) who founded this temple in ancient times, when he shaved his head and entered the clergy in his later years. That the wandering monk (*ke-nin*, a transformed/supernatural being) should have praised the villager for remembering this temple's *eika* is a matter that surpasses what ordinary human minds can fathom...
