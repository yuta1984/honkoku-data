…having fallen into reduced circumstances (from Settsu Province) and come here, he lectured on the Confucian classics and instructed the villagers, so that sandals piled up outside his gate (such was the number of his visitors). Yet, as is the custom of worldly Confucianists, he made it his chief principle to repudiate Buddhism, and made no effort to correct improper conduct or reform the dissolute customs of the people. The common people all followed his example, reviling monks as bandits and scoundrels, and refusing to believe in the principle of karmic cause and effect. This had become a most lamentable state of affairs. Then, one evening, at a moment when the Confucian scholar was gazing at the moon and reciting ancient Chinese verses, he heard the sound of stealthy footsteps, followed by a gentle rapping at his door. Wondering who it could be, he opened the door, and there stood a monk of imposing and dignified bearing.

[Left page]

"From what place have you come, Master?" he asked. The elderly monk replied, "I am a monk of Daiji-ji. I have come to forge a bond of friendship with you that transcends the boundary between monk and layman. Please, do not be alarmed." The Confucian scholar offered the monk a seat and bade him sit, and for a time they conversed, the hours passing by. At that point, the Confucian scholar posed a question, saying: "The teachings of the Buddha are all empty falsehoods and hollow words, suited only for fools, and are by no means the way of a true man of virtue. It is for this reason that the gentleman-scholar rejects them. In the gāthā of the Fumon-bon (*Universal Gateway* chapter), there is the teaching concerning the Land of Rākṣasa demons. Just where is this Land of Rākṣasa demons to be found? The Buddhist teachings are of absolutely no benefit whatsoever for one's circumstances in this present life."
