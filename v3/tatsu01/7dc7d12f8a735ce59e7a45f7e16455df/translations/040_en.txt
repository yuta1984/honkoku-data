"I have bestowed my presence upon that land in order to rid it of pestilence. Now that I have moved to this place, the old land has fallen entirely into the possession of the plague deity. You must return quickly to the old land. If you do not, there shall not be a single soul left alive among the people of that land." This divine proclamation was so urgent it could not be stayed even for a moment. Upon hearing this sermon, everyone clapped their hands in admiration and reverently returned to the former place of worship. Thereupon, the people's illnesses were immediately healed, those who had collapsed from starvation were revived and returned to their homes. It was as if rice crops withered in a great drought had suddenly sprung back to life upon receiving rain. Truly,

Now, regarding why this mountain is called Myōjō-zan (Star Mountain): Around the Tenshō era, in the village of Yokose, there lived a man called Hyōe. His mother had unexpectedly lost her sight. As Hyōe was still a young child, he had no means of sustaining the livelihood of their impoverished household, and so he would come to the forest of this temple to gather nuts and berries, scraping together the most meager of rations day by day. His mother, tormented by worry over what would become of Hyōe's future, and already afflicted with blindness, became distraught in mind and wandered about in a state of madness.
