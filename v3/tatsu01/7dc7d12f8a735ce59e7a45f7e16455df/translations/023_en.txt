In an instant, people from the entire district (*kekkyō*) gathered together, and though the miraculous efficacy of the principal image needed no further elaboration, all marveled at witnessing such a wondrous event, and gave voice to their admiration for the Bodhisattva's sublime wisdom and power (*myōchiriki*). From this time forward, the principal image of this temple has been popularly called "Kogaeshi Kannon" — the Kannon Who Returns Children. The votive poem reads:

　　*Chichi haha no / megumi mo fukaki / Koka no dō / daiji daihi no / chikai tanomoshi*
　　(Deep as the grace of father and mother is this hall of Koka — how reassuring is the vow of great compassion!)

**The Sixth Temple: Ogi-no-dō — Kōyōzan Bokunji** *(The hall is four *ken* on each of the four sides, facing south)*

**Principal Image: Shō Kannon** *(Standing figure, height: one *shaku* two *sun*)* **Carved by the Bodhisattva Gyōki**

This sacred image was enshrined in a modest grass hermitage in former times. The location was a place where mountains soared and valleys ran deep; though spring came, no visitor ever arrived; though autumn came, there was nothing but the cry of monkeys calling to the moon. Pine trees and chrysanthemums overgrew and buried the traces of the three garden paths (*sankei*), and it was truly an ideal setting for solitary meditation, with one's face turned to the wall (*kozen menpeki*).
