...'the possessions of the divine spirit (*mitama*).' If that is so, then I too am one among those who are possessions of the divine spirit. Why then do you say to my face that you are sparing the divine treasures of the gods? If you truly honor the Emperor (*Tenshi*), you ought first to give alms to me. The Emperor is the father and mother of the people. If I were to starve to death, it would become a cause of grief to the Emperor. Have you not heard: 'Here there are unharvested sheaves of grain, there there are dropped bundles of stalks, here there are fallen ears of grain — these are the benefit (*ri*) of the widow'? If the world shows me no charity now, I shall be driven to become a thief. If I become a thief, the country will be thrown into turmoil. Therefore, if you truly revere the lords of the realm (*kokkō*), you ought first to give alms to me. The gods teach the heart of *sumemutsubi* — devotion to the imperial family — while the Buddha preaches the equality of self and other (*jita byōdō*). Moreover, it is said, 'All living beings (*issai shujō*)

[Left page]

are without exception my own children (*goshi*).' Seeing now the crookedness of your heart and foreseeing that you shall wander in transmigration through the three realms (*sangai*), it is truly as though I were gazing upon a lost child of my own begetting. Why is it that you claim to revere the people of the divine land, yet say you will give nothing in charity to a beggar? Rid yourself quickly of your evil spirits (*jaki*)!" Having thus admonished him with great sincerity, Tange was suddenly stirred to the quick (*hokki*) and broke into tears. "The teachings of the Buddha are truly vast and wide. From this day forth I shall enter the Way and renew my resolve. I was born and have yet to pay reverence to the Buddha. I have heard that there is none who surpasses Kannon (*Kanzeon*) in forming bonds of transformation (*keen*) with all living beings.
