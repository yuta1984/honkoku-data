...are the children of the divine spirit (*mitama*).' If that is so, then I too am one among those belonging to the divine spirit. Why then do you say to my face that you begrudge me the treasure of the gods? If you truly honor the Emperor (*Tenshi*), you should first give alms to me. The Emperor is the father and mother of the people. If I were to die of starvation, that would surely be a source of grief to the Emperor. Have you not heard the passage: 'Here there are sheaves left ungathered, there there are bundles of grain left behind, here there are fallen ears of grain — these are for the benefit of widows'? If I receive no alms from this world, I shall become a thief. And if I become a thief, the country shall be thrown into turmoil. Therefore, if you revere the lords of the realm (*kokkō*), you ought first to give alms to me. The gods teach the spirit of *sume-mutsU* — love for the imperial family and kin — and the Buddha taught that self and others are equal (*jita byōdō*). Furthermore, it is also said, 'All sentient beings are every one my children.' That I must now witness your heart becoming warped and your soul transmigrating through the three realms of existence (*sangai*) is truly like watching a child I myself have borne go astray and become lost. Why do you say that you honor the people of the gods' land, and yet declare that you will give nothing in alms to a mendicant?" — and with these words the pilgrim admonished him with heartfelt sincerity. At this, Tange was moved to awakening (*hokki*) and shed tears, saying: "The teachings of the Buddha are truly vast and all-encompassing. From this day forward, I shall take the path of Buddhist practice (*nyūdō*) and reform my intentions. I was born and have yet to pay reverence to the Buddha. I have heard that there is none who surpasses Kannon (*Kanzeon*) in extending the karmic bond of salvation (*keen*) to all sentient beings.
