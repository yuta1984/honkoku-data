"If you wish to cure [your mother's illness], recite these words"—and with that, the elderly monk conferred and taught the two phrases: *Muku shōjō kō* ("the pure and undefiled light") and *Enichi ha sho'an* ("the sun of wisdom that breaks through all darkness"). Instantly, the monk's form vanished. Though Hyōe was young, he was a child of deep filial devotion and remarkably retentive memory, and he committed the phrases perfectly to heart. Returning home, he taught his mother every single word of the phrases without error. Mother and son then spent the entire night before the principal image in worship, reciting the phrases and praying with wholehearted sincerity. That very night, as dawn was already approaching, a single brilliantly blazing star flew forth from within the inner sanctum and illuminated the mother's forehead. Instantly, the blind woman's eyes opened. Mother and son were so astonished and overcome with joy that

[Left page]

they could not contain themselves—they danced and stamped their feet without knowing what they were doing. Their faces overflowed with joy, and when they hurried home, the villagers gathered from all around, and the sounds of their amazement and admiration filled the whole village with a great clamor. The local lord, revering and believing in Hyōe's filial devotion and the miraculous power of the principal image, named the mountain "Myōjō-san" (Bright Star Mountain) to demonstrate its miracles to all future generations, and he granted Hyōe exemption from taxes and corvée labor, and bestowed upon him rice fields and gardens, thus commemorating his filial virtue in perpetuity. Truly, is there any darkness that the light of Kannon's sun of wisdom cannot illuminate? It is said that thereafter, those who suffered from eye ailments, as well as those who had lost their minds and fallen into madness, prayed to this principal image and found its miraculous power to be manifest and remarkable.
