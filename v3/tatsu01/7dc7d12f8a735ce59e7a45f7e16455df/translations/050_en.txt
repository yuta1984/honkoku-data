…kept vigil through the night. As the night deepened and grew still, and his heart too became clear and tranquil, from the inner sanctuary (*naijin*) came a wondrous voice that chanted:

*Oi no mi ni / kurushiki mono wa / Nosakaji / ima omoishiru / nochi no yo no michi*

"For an aging body, what is most trying is Nosaka Temple — realize now the path of the world to come."

This was chanted three times in this manner. Afterward, all was hushed and silent, without a sound. The person pondered the meaning of this sacred verse over and over in his heart, and shed tears unbidden; and with renewed and even fiercer devotion, he made his way (to the temple) and paid reverent homage. It may be presumptuous for one with an ordinary, deluded mind (*bonshin*) to try to fathom the Buddha's intent — yet the meaning of this sacred verse is as follows: "When one has grown old, to walk along a steep and difficult road is indeed trying and wearisome. And let this very fact remind one of the

[Left page]

suffering of the world to come. On the journey to the realm of the dead (*meido*) and the Yellow Springs (*Yomi*), even one of the highest rank — the Son of Heaven — goes utterly alone, wandering through a dark and desolate wilderness (*kōya*), and the anguish thereof is beyond the reach of words. Through the realms of the six destinies and four modes of birth (*rokushu shishō*), one wanders here and there, now drifting, now standing paralyzed; one should contemplate the suffering of endless kalpas (*yōgō*). If one wonders how to escape that suffering, there is no means other than to rely upon the vow of the Buddha. Simply and wholeheartedly chant the Buddha's name. Know that all manner of grave sins, even the five grave offenses (*gogyaku*), are extinguished thereby — and so do not commit even minor sins. In a single recitation, a single moment of mindfulness (*issho ichinnen*), there is surely hope. How much more so for one who recites continuously without ceasing!" If one takes this teaching to heart and practices it without slackening, one will accord with the intent of this sacred verse (*goei*).
