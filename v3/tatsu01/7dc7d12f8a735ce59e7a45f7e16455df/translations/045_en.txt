…only the foundation stones remain (of the Chōja Yashiki). The pilgrim's verse (*eika*) reads:

　*Hitasura ni / tanomi wo kakeyo / Daijiji / muttsu no chimata no / ku ni kawaru beshi*
　(Place all your trust, without reservation, in the Daiji Temple; it shall take the place of your suffering on the six crossroads.)

There is no need to annotate this temple's pilgrim verse at length—it simply urges the devotee to place their trust wholeheartedly in Kannon, who will surely take upon herself the sufferings of the Six Paths. Mahākaruṇā Kannon (*Daihi Kannon*) destroys the three obstacles of the hell realm; Mahāmaitrī Kannon (*Daiji Kannon*) destroys (the three obstacles of) the hungry ghost realm; Lion of Fearlessness Kannon (*Shishi Muhi Kannon*) destroys the three obstacles of the animal realm; Mahābrahma Universal Illumination Kannon (*Daibomfushō Kannon*) destroys the three obstacles of the asura realm; Heavenly Being and Courageous Man Kannon (*Tennin Jōbu no Kannon*) destroys the three obstacles of the human realm; and Mahābrahma Profound and Distant Kannon (*Daibom Jin'on Kannon*) destroys the three obstacles of the heavenly realm—

—as these destroy them. Considering these textual meanings, it is most fitting that (this verse) should have such a basis.

**The Eleventh Temple: Sakagōri — Nansekisan Jōraku-ji**
*(Main hall: three-bay square building, facing south)*

Principal image: Eleven-Faced Kannon *(Jūichimen Kannon)*
*(Standing figure, height: three shaku, one sun, five bu)*
Carved by Gyōki Bosatsu

This temple was established when Gyōki Bosatsu, traveling through the provinces, designating districts, building roads, constructing bridges, and erecting provincial temples (*kokubunji*) in each province, arrived at this place. Enchanted by its tranquil and secluded scenery, he built a grass hermitage here and rested from the labors of his long journey through the land.
