[Right page]

...having prepared and taken the medicinal decoction (from this spring), the illness was suddenly cured. From that time on, whenever the villagers took medicine with this water, there was never a case in which the patient failed to recover. For this reason it bears the name "Long Life" (*Chōju*). The *eika* (devotional verse) says:

*Fudaraku wa Iwamoto-dera to ogamu beshi / mine no matsukaze hibiku takitsu-se*
(Potalaka — one should worship it as Iwamoto-dera; / the pines on the peaks rustle and the cascades resound.)

Considering the meaning of this devotional verse, it was likely composed when the image still resided in the old hall at Yakyōchi. The sound of the waterfall, bathed in morning and evening sunlight, is none other than the voice of the Buddha's "broad and long tongue" (*kōchōzetsu*). By contemplating that sound, one realizes that Mount Potalaka (the paradise of Kannon) is not far away.

[Left page]

Indeed, contemplating this very Iwamoto-dera (as Potalaka itself), and hearing the wind through the pines on the peaks — sounds that surpass those of the mundane world — one is taught not to listen to the roar of the falls in vain. This is a teaching of most excellent spirit and intent, and filled with joyful reverence (*zuiki*), I add my note on its great meaning.
