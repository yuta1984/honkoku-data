[Right page]

...prepared as a medicinal decoction and drank it, whereupon the illness was suddenly and completely cured. From that time onward, whenever the villagers took medicine using this water, there was never a case in which they failed to recover. For this reason, the spring came to be known by the name "Long Life" (*Chōju*). The verse composed in its praise reads:

*Fudaraku is Iwamoto-ji — let us reverently behold it:*
*the pine-wind echoes over the peaks, and the cascading rapids roar.*

Reflecting on the meaning of this verse, it was likely composed when the sacred image still resided in the old hall at Yakyōchi. The sound of the waterfall, shining in the morning and evening sun, is — in its entirety — the voice of the All-Encompassing Tongue (*kōchōzetsu*) of the Buddha. Contemplating these sounds, one realizes that Mount Fudaraku (Potalaka) is not far away.

[Left page]

Indeed, one should regard this very Iwamoto-ji as Fudaraku itself, and one should not idly let pass — without due attention — the sound of the pine wind over the peaks and the roar of the waterfall, which surpass all worldly sounds. As this poem teaches with supremely profound intent, I record its great meaning here with a heart full of joyful reverence (*zuiki*).
