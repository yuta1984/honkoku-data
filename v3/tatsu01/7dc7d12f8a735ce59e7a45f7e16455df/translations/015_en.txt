...a radiant light, blazing like a thousand whistling arrows loosed at once, came shining forth and illuminated this place. The villagers, greatly astonished, gathered together and rushed to see what had happened, but of the pilgrims there was no trace — they had vanished without a sign. And yet, the sacred image that had been enshrined in Yakyōchi Village now stood in solemn dignity at this very spot. The village elders and country men were greatly astonished; the prior (*inshu*) of Yakyōchi Village also came, and all together conferred with one another, deciding to relocate the hall from that former site and enshrine the image permanently at this present location. The place names Yakyōchi (矢行地) and Yaoi (矢追) are said to have originated at this very time — so the elders of this place have handed down. As for the exact date and year, it remains yet

unclear. Among the sacred stones (*reiseki*) within the temple precincts, the *Fusui-seki* ("Sleepless Stone") is said to be unfailingly efficacious when one prays for the healing of eye ailments. Furthermore, when one prays facing this stone, it is said to effectively dispel drowsiness and sleeplessness — hence the name "Fusui" ("no sleep"). As for the *Komochi-ishi* ("Child-bearing Stone"), those without an heir who offer prayers to it will receive a response, and will be blessed with a child of virtue, good fortune, and wisdom. All of these are manifestations that flow from the true and innermost compassionate heart of Kannon, the Great Being of Perfect Penetration (*Entsū Daishi*), and are beyond the comprehension of ordinary people. Furthermore, the spring called *Chōmei-sui* ("Water of Long Life") — when the resident priest of this temple was troubled by an illness that resisted treatment, following a divine instruction received from the principal image, he used the spring water in the garden to prepare medicinal remedies...
