…left to rot in frost and dew — this he had long intended to restore; but now, with the spiritual sign and auspicious omen of this recent miraculous event (*reizui*), his devotion deepened all the more. He resolved: "In the end, it pains me greatly to leave (this sacred image) buried away and decaying in the shadow of these mountains. I shall enshrine the one who saved my very life in Chichibu. That place is a sacred ground (*reichi*) with a karmic connection (*uyen*) to Kannon." And so, having made up his mind, he carried (the image) upon his back and journeyed from afar. As he came upon a mountain road where he might again encounter bandits, he recalled what had happened before and began chanting the Buddha's name — when, quite unexpectedly, the very bandit from long ago who had renounced the world (*tonsei*) appeared before him at that same spot. The two marveled at the mysterious nature of their reunion and, joining their efforts together, enshrined the principal image (*honzon*) at this place. Thereafter, through many

years and seasons (*seisō*), when this hermitage (*iori*) was caught in a "disaster of the fish of the pond" (*chikyo no wazawai*) — (i.e., a conflagration) — the deity Konpira appeared and, tucking the sacred form under his arm, flew to the present site of this mountain, placed it upon a rock, and departed. From then on, the present location was permanently established as the place of enshrinement (*dōjō*).

The poem of devotion (*eika*) at this mountain differs from those of the other (pilgrimage sites): it is truly a poem composed by Kannon herself. Do not regard it carelessly. A certain person had been making the journey to this mountain for many long years; but as his age had advanced somewhat, his steps could no longer keep pace with his wishes, and his devout faith had naturally begun to wane.
