One evening, in a mountain hermitage where all four directions lay in perfect stillness—a place where not even a soul came to call even in broad daylight—the sound of sutra-chanting in unison rose up and mingled with the wind blowing through the pines, so pure and sublime that there was nothing in the world to which it could be compared. Wondering at this strange occurrence, [Gyōki] pushed open the window and looked out, whereupon upon the rugged, towering rocks of a distant peak, the sacred form of the Eleven-Faced [Kannon] stood in majestic splendor, attended by a host of heavenly youths (*tendō*) gathered around before and behind, chanting the Mahāyāna scriptures. Overcome with wonder and admiration, Gyōki reverently beheld this sacred vision and swiftly carved an image replicating it. When he set up an altar and enshrined it there, the sacred form emitted a great radiance, illuminating the newly carved statue. The august body that had appeared in this divine manifestation (*yōkō*), together with the transformed heavenly youths (*keten dōji*),

[Left page]

rained down flowers and incense, and then in an instant entered into the radiance and disappeared without a trace. Gyōki worshipped reverently from afar in the direction of that sacred site, and, wishing to keep this carved image permanently on this mountain as a sacred trace (*reiseki*) of Kannon and to allow the sentient beings of the latter age (*masse*) to form a karmic bond with it, he thereupon built a small hall and enshrined the image within it. The very rock upon which this divine apparition appeared still exists today at the very summit of this mountain.

The head priest who held office four generations before the current incumbent of this temple—the Venerable Monkai—was a holy man of outstanding wisdom and virtue. He had conceived the wish to construct a Niōmon (temple gate) at this mountain, but before the construction work was half complete, he was stricken by illness, his body no longer responding to his will, and work on the construction fell into neglect.
