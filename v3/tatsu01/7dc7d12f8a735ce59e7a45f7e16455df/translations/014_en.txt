As for who founded the temple, there is no reliable account. That place is one of sheer cliffs and precipitous walls—a site of tranquil seclusion bestowed by heaven and of sacred eminence granted by the earth. The scenic views known as the Asahi Falls, the Yūhi Falls, the Yoko Falls, and the like are nothing less than a living painting. The place lies several leagues away from the mundane world, and once one has wandered through it, the bonds of worldly attachment are immediately loosened and thoughts transcending the dusty world naturally arise. 【The scenery of this place is described in detail in the travel record; it is omitted here.】

At the time when the principal image was enshrined in this place, nearby there was a settlement called Yaoi Village. 【This was at the time part of the precinct of Iwamoto-ji.】 Since ancient times, images of the Ten Kings, carved by Bodhisattva Gyōki, had been enshrined here.

【This is what is now the fuda-dō (tablet hall).】 Some two chō to the south, separated by that distance, stands another hall, called the Oku-in (Inner Sanctuary), where Atago-san Daigongen has been invited to reside. Beside it there is also a rocky grotto, within which a Jizō Bodhisattva is enshrined. There is a sacred spring, known as Chōmeisui (Long-Life Water). Moreover, there are strange rocks and curious stones bearing names such as Fusui-seki (the Sleepless Stone) and Komochi-ishi (the Child-Bearing Stone)—a sacred site of extraordinary character.

One day, a group of pilgrims—more than ten companions in all—whose bearing appeared to be no ordinary folk, came to this place and kept an all-night vigil at the Hall of the Ten Kings. The sound of their sutra chanting purified the hearts and ears of the villagers, who rejoiced with reverent joy (*zuiki*).
