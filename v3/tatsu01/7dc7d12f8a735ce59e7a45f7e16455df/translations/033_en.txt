It thus became the sacred hall (梵宮) of the Seventh Sacred Site of Kannon, and it was made clearly known that the cow had departed from the realm of beasts and had immediately been welcomed among the holy assembly (聖衆) of Anyō—the Pure Land of Amida. The reason this place has been called "Ushifushi" (Cow-Prostrating) for all generations is because of this very karmic connection. It is also said that the place names in this area, such as "Yokose" and "Karikome," derive from the karmic connection with the wanderings of that freely grazing cow.

A poem composed about this place reads:

*Rokudō wo kanete megurite ogamu beshi / mata nochi no yo wo kiku mo Ushifushi*
(One should go around, encompassing all six realms, and offer worship here; / even when one hears of the next world in ages hence—this is Ushifushi.)

The meaning of this poem is: even if one wears the sacred rope-sash (木綿たすき) of the seven shrines, do not turn back to the six realms. Just as expressed in this poem, it is a teaching to hold the afterlife in deep reverence and awe.

Not a single thing among all one possesses can be taken along (at death). It is the way of things that only one's karma follows and accompanies one. Even in this present life, if one pays careful attention and reflects deeply, the six realms are a truth that already exists (within us), and though this is something everyone knows, we shall here set out a general explanation for the sake of women and children (who may not be learned in the teaching).

In general, when a person's heart is foolish and cannot distinguish good from evil—or even when one understands a little of good and evil but is shrouded in wicked influences, preaches perverse doctrines, walks the wrong path, and commits wrongful acts—such a person darkens and obscures the innate luminous virtue (明徳) that is one's original nature. Because of this, a dark hell utterly without the light of sun or moon manifests within one's own breast. Whatever arises from this darkness—is there anything among it that is not the suffering of hell?—
