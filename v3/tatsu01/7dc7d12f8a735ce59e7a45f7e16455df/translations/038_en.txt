　　**Ninth Temple　　Myōjōzan　Myōchiji** (Akechi Temple)
　　　　*Main hall: six ken on each side, facing south*
　**Principal Image: Nyoirin Kannon** *(standing statue, height: eight sun three bu)* *Carved by the Bodhisattva Gyōki*

It is handed down by tradition that the Nyoirin Kannon of this temple was brought here by the Zen Master Myōchi in the second year of Kenkyū (1191), who built a hall at this place and enshrined the image therein. In recent times, during the Tenshō era (1573–1592), a warrior in the service of the Yokose clan by the name of Katō became a devout believer in this temple's principal image, and moved the hall to a place within his domain called Mochiyama. Thereupon, a great epidemic broke out in this village: the households of the common people were plunged into distress, hunger and thirst spread widely, and the number of those who collapsed in the roads and lanes was beyond counting. The village headman came to Mochiyama and said: "This must surely be a curse (*tatari*) brought about by the removal of the principal image to this place. I beg that it be returned to its original location with all haste." The resident monk agreed, saying: "My own heart feels the same. Tomorrow I shall inform the lord of this matter and return the image to its former site"—and was making preparations to do so, when a messenger from the lord arrived and said: "Last night, the principal image gave a divine oracle in a dream, saying: 'The old site lies in the direction of the *ushitora* (northeast, the *kimon* direction), and serves as the guardian gate against evil for this district. Evil spirits of pestilence (*ekishin*) gather at that location and are liable to afflict the people...'"
