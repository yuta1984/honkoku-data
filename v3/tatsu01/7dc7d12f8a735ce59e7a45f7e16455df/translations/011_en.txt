...Although this place was far removed from the red dust of the mundane world (the noise and bustle of secular life), those who came to study and practice Zen came in an unbroken stream, and the way of the ancestral masters brought from the west by Bodhidharma flourished greatly. The people of the village looked up to the master's lofty virtue, and using the Japanese reading (wakun) of the Daihō Zenji's name, they renamed the place "Ōtana" (Great Shelf). As the Zen master advanced in age, he came to cherish the realm of quiet solitude all the more, and retreating into a rocky cave nearby called "Onimaru," he buried his traces from the world, swept away all worldly attachments, and in the mornings did nothing more than swallow a wisp of mist, while in the evenings there was not even a wisp of smoke from damp firewood (living in complete and austere seclusion). And yet, at some point, a single old woman began coming day and night, paying reverence to the image of Kannon enshrined within the master's cave, offering flowers, presenting fruits as offerings, paying her respects to the master, and then

[Left page]

departing — and this happened repeatedly. One day, the Zen master turned to the old woman and asked: "From where exactly do you come to pay reverence to this sacred image?" The old woman replied, saying: "I am a demon who walks by night (a yakō oni). I have dwelt in this area for many years. Looking up to the master's virtue, and forming a karmic bond with the principal image, I wish to shed this body of a demon and beast. Please extend your compassionate mercy to me, and guide me to salvation." The Zen master took pity on her and transmitted to her the text for Breaking Open Hell (haji goku no mon), and also conferred upon her the Three Refuges precepts (sangikai). The demon woman leapt for joy and declared: "Long ago, I was the wife of a farming family in this village. My jealousy was exceedingly intense, and being both miserly and greedy (kendon)..."
