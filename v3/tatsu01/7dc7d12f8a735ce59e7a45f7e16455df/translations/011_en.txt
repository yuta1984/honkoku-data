(Continued from previous page) ...Although this place was far removed from the red dust of the mundane world (the hustle and bustle of secular life), students of the dharma came in an unbroken stream, and the Way of the Patriarch from the West (the path of Zen transmitted by Bodhidharma from the west) flourished greatly here. The people of the village, looking up to the master's lofty virtue, adopted the Japanese reading "Ōtana" and renamed the place "Ōtana" (Great Shelf). As the Zen Master's years advanced, he came to love ever more deeply the realm of quiet solitude, and he entered a cave called "Onimaru" nearby, burying his traces from the world, sweeping away all worldly attachments. In the morning he had nothing but a drift of mist to swallow (subsisting on mist alone), and in the evening not even a wisp of smoke from damp firewood rose (he lit no cooking fire whatsoever). Nevertheless, a single old woman came day and night, venerated the image of Kannon enshrined within the master's cave, offered flowers, presented fruits as offerings, and bowed in reverence before the master,

[Left page]

...departing thus, time and again. One day, the Zen Master turned to the old woman and asked, "From whence do you come to pay reverence to this honored image (the Kannon)?" The old woman replied, saying, "I am a demon who walks in the night (yagyō no oni). I have lived in this vicinity for many years. I look up to the master's virtue, and by forming a karmic bond (kechien) with the principal image, I wish to shed this body of a demon beast. I beseech you, Master — extend your compassionate mercy and deliver me (from suffering)." The Zen Master took pity on her and bestowed upon her the verse for breaking the gates of hell (Hajigoku no mon), and conferred upon her the Three Refuges precepts (sankikai — taking refuge in the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha). The demon woman, overcome with great joy and leaping with delight, said, "In ages past, I was the wife of a farming family in this village. My jealousy was extreme, and being miserly and greedy..."
