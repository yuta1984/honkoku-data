The mother and daughter, overcome with shock and grief, wept with their heads bowed to the ground. Sorrow pressed upon their hearts so deeply that they could not manage even a single word in reply, and they lay prostrate before the cow. The cow then raised its voice once more and said: "Your grief only adds to my suffering all the more. There is nothing you can do now to save me. Though I had heard the verse, 'When one is beset by immeasurable suffering, the wondrous power of Kannon's wisdom can save all the suffering of this world,' and though I knew the words, 'If one calls the name even once, all sins shall be entirely removed,' yet throughout my entire life I never believed it, and I laughed coldly at even a single recitation of the sacred name and never uttered it myself. That is why my fate has come to be as it is. Quickly, devote your hearts for my sake and pray to this Kannon for the repose of my soul. If this place should become a sacred site of Kannon for all eternity, you should know that this will be the sign that I have surely escaped the evil realms and attained rebirth in the Pure Land."

The moment the cow finished speaking, it gasped in agony and suddenly died. The mother and daughter, resolved not to go against the cow's words, wiped away their tears, buried the cow, and did not even return to their lodgings. They cut off their hair, and together, mother and daughter, settled in that place. They prayed for Kannon's compassionate salvation, becoming practitioners of nembutsu of single-minded and unwavering faith, and in the end they both fulfilled their deepest wish of attaining rebirth in the Pure Land at that very place.

Thereafter, this place flourished greatly and became a site where the Buddhist teachings spread far and wide...
