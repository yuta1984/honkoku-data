In a dream that night, a yellow-faced elderly monk came accompanied by a Kongō-shin (vajra deity), and said, "Monkai's illness — I shall cure it well," whereupon he commanded the Kongō-shin, and it appeared to seize Monkai by the shoulders and lift him up. When Monkai awoke from this dream, the malignant illness had vanished in an instant, his energy had redoubled compared to ordinary days, and without any further hindrance, he was able to perform the completion ceremony (*rakkei*) and extend his years of service. This event was a miracle witnessed directly before one's very eyes, and is something that all the people of this Sakagōri locality know well. The pilgrimage verse (*eika*) for this mountain reads:

*Tsumi toga mo / kieyoto inoru / Sakagōri / asahi wa sasite / yūhi kagayaku*
(Praying that sins and transgressions may melt away at Sakagōri — the morning sun shines upon it, and the evening sun shines brilliantly)

&emsp;&emsp;Principal image: Koyasu Kannon (standing image, five *shaku* tall) — carved by Prince Shōtoku

Upon tracing the origin (*hon'en*) of this mountain, we find that in former times, a merchant from Kai Province came every year to this province (Musashi) to trade, and had already been doing so for more than ten years. One year, as was his custom, he set out laden with various goods, traversed the long and perilous road known as the "Sixty-*ri* Pass" (*Rokujūri-goe*), and made his way onto the Chichibu road.
