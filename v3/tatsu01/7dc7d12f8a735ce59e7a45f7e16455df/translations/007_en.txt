(Continued from previous page) ...having founded numerous temple complexes throughout the provinces, his virtue became known even to distant foreign lands, and the number of eminent monks who came to submit and convert was beyond counting. The Brahmin Sōjō from South India (Nantenjiku), Buttetsu from Linyi (林邑), and Jianzhen (Ganjin) from Tang China each came to court, praised the Emperor's virtue, and diligently propagated the Buddhist teachings. At this time, using the two masters Gyōki and Rōben, prayers were offered for the peace and safety of the nation, with lectures on the Lotus Sutra, the Sutra of the Most Victorious King, and the Two Mandalas. Furthermore, as the Emperor ordered the construction of provincial temples (kokubunji) and provincial nunneries (kokubuni-ji) throughout the provinces, the Bodhisattva Gyōki traveled along the roads in all four directions, and wherever he perceived a place to be spiritually auspicious, he carved Buddha images, erected temple halls, and ensured that the Buddhist teachings would never decline in those lands.
