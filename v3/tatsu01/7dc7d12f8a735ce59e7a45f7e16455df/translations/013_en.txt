So it is said. (The above is a paraphrase of the meaning.) The proof that the evil karmic retribution of jealousy inevitably leads to rebirth among demons and beasts is contained in the Buddha's own words — what doubt could there be? One should take great care never to give rise to jealousy. The votive poem (*eika*) for this temple reads:

*Megurikite / tano wo kakeshi / Ōtana no / chikahi mo fukaki / tanigawa no mizu*
(Wandering about and at last placing one's trust here — the vow of Ōtana is as deep as the waters of the mountain stream.)

The votive poem of this temple expresses the idea that the demon woman who roamed the night — wandering here and there in search of a connection with the Buddha — came at last to this place, and through the profound power of the principal image's vow was instantly released from her body as a demon-beast. It likens the deep grace of the Bodhisattva

[Left page]

to the fathomless depths of a mountain stream's waters. This poem may also be read as conveying the meaning of the boundless depth and breadth of the Ocean of Wisdom and Vows (*chigan-kai*) of the Original Teacher Tathāgata, and as resonating with the heart of the Bodhisattva of the Next Succession (*fusho bosatsu*) — "whose great vow is as deep as the ocean." Thus I provide only a brief annotation on its meaning.
