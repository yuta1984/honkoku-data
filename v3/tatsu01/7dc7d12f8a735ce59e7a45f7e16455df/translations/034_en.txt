Is there anything that does not become a seed of hell? The evil karma (悪業) that one accumulates in body and utters with mouth is none other than the Mountain of Blades and the Forest of Swords (刀山剣樹, *tōzan kenju*). To be lost in sensual desire and to drown in drink until one reaches a state beyond saving—is this not the sinking and drowning (沈淪) of the Blood Pond (血の池) and the River of Three Crossings (三途の川, *Sanzu no kawa*)? To forget kindness received, to act against righteousness, to indulge freely in desire, and to feel no shame—this is the realm of animals (畜生道) in the present world. To treat others with contempt through a heart of arrogance and pride, and to stir up quarreling and strife—what is this if not the realm of the Asuras (修羅)?

If one does not free oneself from these Three Evil Realms (三悪趣: hell, hungry ghosts, and animals) before death, the karma of one's daily actions and conduct will follow one's soul into the next existence. The very names of the sufferings of the six realms in the world to come are too burdensome even to hear—reflecting on both the old tale of the cow that was reborn into an animal body and all of these various meanings, one should interpret this poem accordingly.

Now, the Zen Master Bankei (盤珪禅師) of Banshu (播州, Harima Province) composed a "Barley-Pounding Song" (麦搗き歌) to teach the common people, and in it he said: "If you commit sin, your heart itself becomes the demon—there is no hell anywhere outside of you." The compassionate heart (慈心) with which Master Bankei applied himself to enlightening the common people is truly worthy of reverence.

As for the teachings on hell and the six realms in general, they are set forth in detail in such texts as the *Qishi jing* (起世経, *Kisekkyō*), the *Zhengfa nian jing* (正法念経, *Shōbōnenkyō*), the *Vibhāṣā* (婆沙論, *Basharon*) fascicle 172, the *Zhujing yaoji* (諸経要集, *Shokyō yōjū*) fascicle 18, and the *Ōjō Yōshū* (往生要集), and I therefore omit them here.
