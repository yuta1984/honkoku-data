　　The Fifth Temple: Ogawasan Kogadera (Mt. Ogawa, Temple of Song-and-Poetry)
　　*[Side note: The hall measures five ken on each of its four sides, facing east.]*

Principal Image (*honzon*): Junrei Kannon (Cundi Kannon)
　　*[Side note: A seated image, nine sun one bu in height.]*
　　Carved by the Great Master Jikaku (Ennin)

The great patron (*daidanna*) who founded this temple was a man called Honma Mōhachi, the local headman (*osa*) of this place. Deeply believing in the exalted virtue of the Great Master, he wished to enshrine a sculpted sacred image at this very site, and so he gave generously of his wealth (*kisha*) to build the hall, worshipping with reverence day and night. Since Mōhachi was a man of such deep and sincere faith, perhaps through the protecting care (*gonen*) of the Three Treasures and the heavenly deities,

[Left page]

his household grew prosperous and flourishing, and near and far people were drawn to his virtue, his name becoming renowned. Though born in the rustic countryside — described in poetry as "the provinces where heaven descends" (*amasagaru hina*) — he devoted his heart to the Way of Shikishima (the art of waka poetry), and directed his thoughts toward the Waka shore. One day a traveler came and spent the night in vigil (*tsuya*) at this hall, and throughout the night he and Mōhachi discoursed on the innermost secrets (*ōgi*) of waka poetry. As the cock began to crow, the traveler lectured on the poems exchanged between Prince Shōtoku and the transformed person (*kejin*) of Kataoka Mountain, and further revealed the fundamental intent (*honji*) of the Patriarch's coming from the West. Just as the eastern peaks were beginning to grow pale in the light of dawn over the countryside, the figure of that person suddenly vanished. Mōhachi was filled with joy and reverence, and forthwith named the hall "Kogadera" (Temple of Song-and-Poetry). Surely this name derives from the poetry (*ka*)…
