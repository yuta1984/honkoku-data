　　The Fifth Temple　Ogawa-zan Goka-ji　(*The hall measures five ken on a side, faces east on all four sides*)

　Principal Image: Junrei Kannon (*Cundi Kannon*) (*Sculptured image, height nine sun one bu*) — Carved by the Great Master Jikaku

The chief patron (*daidan'na*) who founded this temple was the headman (*osa*) of this place, a man by the name of Honma Mōhachi [Mago-hachi]. Deeply believing in the eminent virtue of the Great Master [Jikaku Daishi], he donated his wealth (*kishu*) with a joyful heart in order to enshrine the carved sacred image here, built the temple hall, and paid reverent homage (*kyōrei*) to it day and night. Because Mago-hachi was a man of such profound faith, through the protective blessing (*gonen*) of the Three Treasures and the heavenly deities,

[Left page]

his household prospered and flourished, and people from near and far came to admire his virtue, so that his name became widely known. Although he was born in a rustic place "far from the capital," he devoted his heart to the Way of Shikishima (the art of Japanese poetry), and turned his thoughts toward the Bay of Waka (the realm of *waka* poetry). One day, a traveler came and spent the night (*tsuya*) in this hall, discussing the profound mysteries (*ōgi*) of *waka* poetry with Mago-hachi throughout the night. As the cock began to crow, he discoursed on the exchange of poems between Prince Shōtoku and the beggar of Kataokazan [the mysterious figure on Kataoka Hill], and then revealed the fundamental intent (*honshi*) of the Patriarch's coming from the West. When the eastern peaks began to grow faintly light, the man's figure suddenly vanished. Mago-hachi was overcome with joy and wonder, and thereupon named the hall "Goka-ji" (Temple of Recited Song).
