"...Due to the karmic retribution of having lived without taking refuge in the Three Treasures (the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha), I fell into the realm of demons (kidō), with no prospect whatsoever of ever escaping from it. But through the master's teachings, I have come to encounter the Buddha and have learned to revere the Dharma. Surely I shall now be able to leave behind this body of a demon and beast. Please extend your compassion to me yet further, and by going out to a place near where people travel back and forth (to the village), and allowing the principal image to form karmic bonds widely with all sentient beings — if you would but do this, then both I and all sentient beings alike shall together attain bodhi (enlightenment)." The master nodded and said: "What you desire is in accord with reason. I shall return once more to the land of Ōtana and enshrine the principal image there."

[Left page]

At this, the demon woman rejoiced, and presenting to the master the bamboo staff she had been leaning against, she said: "This is not a thing of the human world. Please accept this as today's offering (fuse)." When she had finished speaking, the old woman vanished as if dissolved into thin air — like the wind blowing through the pines, like clouds drifting across the sky — leaving not a single trace behind. The Zen master prayed all the more fervently to the principal image for her liberation (tokudatsu — release from the cycle of suffering), and in later years he built a hall of one structure, establishing it permanently as a sacred site of Kannon. The aforementioned bamboo staff is preserved to this day in the treasure repository (hōzō) of this temple as a precious treasure. Now, the *Shōbōnen-kyō* (Sūtra of Correct Mindfulness; fascicle 16, leaf 3) states: "Women are mostly born into the realm of demons (kidō). Why is this so?..."
