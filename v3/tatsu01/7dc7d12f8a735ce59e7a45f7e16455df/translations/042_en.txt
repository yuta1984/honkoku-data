The votive poem reads:

*Meguri kite / sono na wo kikeba / Akechidera / kokoro no tsuki wa / kumorazaru ramu*

"Pilgrimaging here and hearing its name—Akechi Temple—the moon of the heart shall not be clouded."

This poem, in the two characters *mei-chi* ("Akechi" / "bright wisdom"), contains the meaning that the radiant light (*chikō*) of Kannon's pure and immaculate light of the void (*kokū muku shōjō*), being of the same substance as the luminosity (*kōmyō*) of the Dharmadhātu-prakṛti-jñāna ("wisdom of the essential nature of the Dharma realm") of Mahāvairocana (Dainichi Nyorai), when once this light of wisdom illuminates all things, it shatters the darkness of the immeasurable afflictions (*bonnō*) and delusions (*mōsō*), and the moon of original, ever-abiding nature (*hon'u jōjū*) shines forth in brilliance—such, it seems, is the intended meaning. Furthermore, it may also be understood as an allusion to the meaning of that blind woman's eyes being opened and her returning to her right mind—and in this sense too, the poem carries through perfectly.

**The Tenth Temple — Banshōzan Daijiji** *(Hall: six *ken* on all four sides, facing east)*

**Principal Image: Shō Kannon** *(Wooden image, height one shaku three sun)* **— carved by the Venerable Eshin Sōzu**

This sacred image came about because there was a wealthy man, the headman of this area, a certain Nanigashi, who, during the lifetime of the Venerable Eshin Sōzu, beseeched [him] for one of the sacred images that the Sōzu had carved, brought it here, and built a hall to enshrine it. Thereafter, several hundred years passed and [the hall] fell into ruin, but in the second year of Meiō (1493), the year *mizunoto no ushi* (Cycle Year Cancels Ox), the Zen Master Tōyū undertook its restoration, and he is now regarded as the founding patriarch of this temple.
