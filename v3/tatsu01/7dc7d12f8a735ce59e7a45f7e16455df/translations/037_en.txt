...Though it may be presumptuous to do so, if we briefly attempt to interpret the meaning of the verse: in the five characters "Tada tanome" (只頼め, "Only trust and rely"), there lies the meaning of *ikkō sennen*—single-minded, exclusive devotion. The phrase "the time of sincerity" (*makoto no toki*) refers to the moment of one's death. This corresponds in meaning to the ancient poem that reads, "It is only in death that sincerity is made manifest," and so it should mean the hour of one's dying. Casting aside all other thoughts—anything thought or spoken beyond the single plea "Amida Buddha, save me!" being nothing but delusion—if one recites the nenbutsu (*shōmyō nenbutsu*, the oral recitation of the Buddha's name), then the Buddha's coming to receive one at death is a certainty. This is the Buddha's vow (*seigan*). Who could harbor any doubt? The wondrous mystery of the Primal Vow (*hongan*) is not something that ordinary, unenlightened beings (*bonbu*) can fully comprehend. One should simply practice the recitation of the name without ever abandoning it (*nennen fusha no shōmyō*), and thereby prepare the karmic conditions (*gōin*) for assured rebirth in the Pure Land (*ketsujō ōjō*).

Furthermore, the text states: even though one is urged in this manner, if one does not truly believe—if one is merely a practitioner of empty, insincere nenbutsu (*koke no nenbutsu-sha*)—then one cannot expect to receive the divine grace (*myōga*) of the Buddhas and heavenly beings (*butten*). One must engage in practice so that one's own heart becomes genuine and sincere. "Sincerity" (*makoto*) means having not a single trace of self-serving intent (*watakushi*) within one's heart, and conducting oneself in a way that would cause no shame before the gods. Whether one's heart is sincere or false, one must ask within one's own heart. Knowing well that the crow is black and the heron is white, even if someone were to claim that "the crow is white and the heron is black," could one's heart accept it? This is because the truth of black and white is firmly settled, and so one is not led astray. In the same way, one should simply believe in the Primal Vow with true sincerity, settle one's faith with firm conviction (*ketsujō*), and await the evening of the coming of the Amida Triad (*sanzōn raigō*)...
