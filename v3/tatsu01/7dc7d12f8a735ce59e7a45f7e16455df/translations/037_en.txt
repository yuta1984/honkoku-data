...Though one may feel some trepidation in venturing this interpretation, let us briefly explain the meaning. In the five characters "*tada tanome*" (ただ頼め, "only trust and rely"), there lies the meaning of *ikkō sennen* (一向専念)—single-minded, exclusive devotion. "The time of sincerity (*makoto no toki*)" refers to the moment of one's final hour, the time of death. This is the same meaning as the ancient poem that reads: "*shisuru bakari zo, makoto narikeru*" ("Only death is true sincerity")—it must refer to the moment of death. Casting aside all other thoughts, if one, with singleminded devotion, abandons everything one thinks or says as delusion, holding only to the prayer "Amida Buddha, save me!" and recites the nembutsu, then the Buddha shall surely come forth to receive one. This is the Buddha's vow (*seigan*). Who could harbor any doubt? The mystery of the Original Vow (*hongan*) is not something that ordinary, unenlightened beings (*bonpu*) can grasp through their own understanding. One should simply practice the recitation of the Buddha's name without ever abandoning it (*nen-nen fusha no shōmyō*), and thereby prepare the karmic cause (*gōin*) for assured rebirth in the Pure Land (*ketsujō ōjō*).

Furthermore, it is said: even though we encourage this practice, if one does not truly believe, and is merely a practitioner of hollow, insincere nembutsu (*koke no nenbutsu-sha*), then one cannot expect to receive the divine grace (*myōga*) of the Buddhas and heavenly beings. One must practice and cultivate one's mind so that it becomes truly sincere. "Sincerity (*makoto*)" means having not a single trace of self-serving thought (*watakushi*) in one's heart, such that one need feel no shame even before the gods. This is what should be called a sincere heart. Whether one is sincere or deceitful, one must ask within one's own heart. Knowing full well that crows are black and herons are white, even if someone were to say "crows are white and herons are black," could anyone's heart truly accept such a claim? This is because the truth of black and white is firmly settled and unmovable—therefore one is not led astray. In the same way, one should truly believe and be firmly settled (*ketsujō*) in the Original Vow, and on the evening when the Amida Triad comes to receive one (*sanzōn raigō*)...
