Saigyō wrote: "Though I have not the depth of heart to comprehend the profound dharma, I pray that the Buddha may guide me to hold always in mind the impermanence of all things — and, setting aside attachment to my own self, to keep this thought as the spiritual practice of my life to come."

Furthermore, the Reverend Shōkō of Chinzei declared that "the essence of the Pure Land teaching lies in *nenshishi nenbutsu* — mindfulness of death and mindfulness of the Buddha." This too is equally a teaching that urges us to contemplate impermanence.

Whether noble or lowly, each and every one of us is a being that lodges but briefly in the reed leaves bound together by the blowing wind. Deeply revere this truth — do not forget it, even for a moment.
