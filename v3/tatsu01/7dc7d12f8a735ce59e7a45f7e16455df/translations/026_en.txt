Saigyō said: "Though I do not possess a heart deep enough to truly know the profound Dharma, I pray that the Buddha may grant me at least the resolve never to forget impermanence — to set my own self aside, fix my thoughts upon it, and make it my provision for the world to come."

Likewise, the teaching of the Holy Master Shōkō of Chinzei — that "the essence of the Pure Land school lies in *nenshishi nenbutsu* (念死念仏), the recollection of death and nembutsu" — equally instructs us to contemplate impermanence. Whether noble or humble, we are all but beings lodging briefly in the reed leaves bent together by the passing wind. How sacred this truth — never forget it, not even for a moment.

*Chichibu Sanjūshisho Kannon Entsūden*, Volume One — End. Composed by Baikan.
