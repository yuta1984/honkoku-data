The traveler who had discoursed on the Way was none other than a manifestation (*ōke*, an incarnation) of Kuze Daishi (Kanzeon Bosatsu, the Bodhisattva of Compassion), appearing in order to reveal this truth. There is also the account that it was because of a great awakening (*daigo*) achieved through the teaching of direct transmission (*tantendirect pointing, tanden jikishi*) that the hall came to be called the "Hall of Awakened Song" (*Goka no dō*). Both are ancient traditions, and while it is difficult to judge which is correct, the two characters "awakening" (*go*, 悟) and "discourse" (*go*, 語) each carry their own meaning, and neither can be said to be superior or inferior to the other. The matter is left to the discretion of the reader. After this, many cycles of the sixty-year calendar (*kasshi*) passed, and around the time the hall had gradually fallen into ruin, in a remote corner of the mountains of Shinano Province, there lived an old woman. She made a living

[Left page]

by growing cotton (*wata*). She had one daughter. Though she endured the hardships of life's difficult path, the years and months slipped by as her thoughts were absorbed in watching her daughter grow up. Then one day, her daughter disappeared without a trace, to no one knew where. The old woman was greatly alarmed, thinking: "Could this be the mischief of foxes and tanuki? Or perhaps it was a thief or a slave trader who seized her?" Her heart wandered in a daze as she went searching here and there, but not even a rumor resembling any news of her daughter came to her ears. The mother's strength faded completely away, and she spent her days in lamentation upon the empty floor beneath a chinquapin tree (*shii*) that she had relied upon as her shade and shelter.
