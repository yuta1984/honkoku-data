The merchant managed to flee wearing only a single thin garment barely concealing his skin, when one of the bandits came chasing after him, saying, "If we let you go free, who knows what trouble you might stir up for us later. We had best cut you down and kill you now, to remove the threat of future retribution." The bandit seized him and pulled him close, about to do him harm. The merchant, with one-pointed devotion (*isshin*), chanted "Namu Kanzeon" (Homage to Kannon). Wondrously, from within the amulet pouch (*mamori-bukuro*) that hung against the merchant's skin, a brilliant radiance shone forth, dazzling and resplendent, shooting into the eyes of the bandit, such that he was unable to open his eyes and look. The Buddhist teaching that says "even to look with wicked eyes is impossible" — how true it is! How much more impossible, then, to inflict harm! The bandit dropped his sword and

[Left page]

fell to the ground, tumbling and prostrating himself. Into the ears of the remaining bandits, the voice of the merchant chanting the sacred name (*shōmyō*) with single-minded devotion sounded as if a hundred or even a thousand people were chanting in unison; they were greatly alarmed and, abandoning all the valuables and clothing they had stolen, fled away. The bandit who had taken up his sword and advanced upon the merchant was suddenly seized by a spiritual awakening (*hokki*), returned all the stolen goods to the merchant, cut off his own hair, and became a renunciant (*tonseisha*). The merchant, miraculously saved from death, returned to his home province and told the people of his hometown what had happened; there was not a single person who heard the tale and did not weep with joy and reverence (*zuiki*). And so it was that in the shadow of the mountains of this merchant's home province, there resided — housed in a small hall for long years — a standing image of Shō-Kannon, five *shaku* tall, personally carved by (Prince Shōtoku) himself; and exposed to frost and dew…
