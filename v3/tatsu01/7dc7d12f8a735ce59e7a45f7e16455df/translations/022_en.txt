— suddenly remembered, and placed her trust in Kanzeon, crying out: "O great vow of boundless compassion (*daiji daihi seigan*), do not fail me — grant that I may be reunited with my lost child!" Weeping as she went, she set out from her hermitage, lamenting all the while over the thin and fleeting bond suggested by the name Usui Pass (*Usuiōtōge* — "thin/shallow fate"), and wandered all the long way to this village. As night fell and there was no lodging to be had, she collapsed and lay down beneath a thicket of summer grasses, and while she dozed there for a time, she heard a voice calling out repeatedly from behind her. Thinking, "Could that be my dear child's voice I've been searching for?", she started up with a jolt — and behold, a radiant and blazing light shone forth, and beneath the compassionate robes of patient forbearance (*ninniku jihi* — the robes of a Bodhisattva's enduring mercy),

the very lost daughter was sheltered and concealed, and the august form of Kannon manifested suddenly before her eyes. [Kannon spoke:] "Your beloved child had fallen victim to the calamity of great demons and *takatsudori* (high marsh birds of ill omen), and suffered for a time. Because you prayed to me so earnestly, I commanded the Twenty-Eight Attendants (*nijūhachibushū*) to retrieve her and return her to you. Never slacken in your faith." Thus the daughter was bestowed upon the old woman, and then the divine form vanished in an instant and disappeared. Mother and child wept tears of joy, and it seemed as though even the sleeves of their coarse hempen robes from Kiso would rot away from the drenching of those tears.
