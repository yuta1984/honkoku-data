she suddenly recalled [Kanzeon] and placed her trust in the Bodhisattva of Compassion, crying out: "O great vow of boundless mercy and compassion, do not fail me! Grant that I may be reunited with my lost child!" With tears streaming down her face, she left her hermitage, lamenting that even her bond with the world seemed as thin as the mists of Usui Pass, and wandered all the way to this village from afar. As night fell and she could find no lodging to borrow, she collapsed and lay down beneath a thicket of summer grasses. While dozing there for a time, she heard a voice from behind calling her again and again. Thinking, "Could that be the voice of my very child whom I seek?!" she started up — and there was a blazing radiance, and beneath the robes of compassion and forbearance

[Left page]

the lost daughter was sheltered and hidden, and the august form [of Kanzeon] appeared suddenly before her, saying: "Your beloved child had fallen into the calamity of great demons and high-perching birds (*kōtsudori*) and had been suffering for a time. Because you prayed so earnestly to me, I commanded the Twenty-Eight Attendants to retrieve her, and now I return her to you. See to it that you do not neglect your devotion." With that, the Bodhisattva gave the daughter back to the old woman, and the august form instantly vanished and disappeared. Mother and child wept tears of joy so copiously that the sleeves of their rough hemp robes from Kiso seemed as though they might rot through with moisture. And so the night gave way to dawn.
