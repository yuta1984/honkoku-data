## Tsuzure no Nishiki (Brocade of Rags) — A Guide for Sons Who Visit Courtesans

**[Editor's Note]**
According to the editor, Kazan playfully drafted this piece and intended to publish it with his own illustrations. It is thought to be of the same spirit as his work *Isso Hyakutai* ("One Sweep of a Hundred Attitudes/Figures"), but before the manuscript could be completed, he met with an unexpected disaster [the Bansha no Goku, the Barbarian Books Incident of 1839]. The preface is said to have been written by Takano Chōei.

---

**[Preface]**

There is an insect called the *chōmei* [蟭螟], said to build its nest in the eyelash of a mosquito; and there is the great roc [鵬, *peng*] that spreads its wings over the southern ocean. No matter how vast heaven and earth may be, even greatness has its limits, and even smallness has its bounds. This is by no means mere idle chatter from a white-haired old man — it is found originally in the *Liezi* and written in the *Zhuangzi*. To you companions who pile idle talk upon idle talk: if such talk is indeed right and proper, then consider — rather than the number of five hundred disciples, there is the uncountable number of clients; and on the lining of one's robe, there are occasional crooked-mouthed parables and convenient fictions. In a world where one muddles through however one can, can one really condemn the floating women [courtesans] alone as being in the wrong?

Here there is one mad Confucian student. Just as the worm that feeds on the bitter smartweed (*tade*) forgets its bitterness after a single cup of drink, this fellow, with a rush mat spread before the gate for a pillow for his elbows, is a carefree creature who takes the world lightly. Yet for some reason he came to visit, and striking his knee said: "Now listen — I know it is none of my business, but within the human world there exists yet another world — the world of courtesans. Unlike the 'five declines of a heavenly being,' this is a matter of a somewhat different direction..."
