[Frame 19]
[Right folio]

From the southeast (tatsumi) to [the northwest], the distance is over ninety-six ri. The land is divided into four districts (gun), of which Aizu district occupies more than two-thirds [lit. "two-strong"]. Of the remaining three districts, Yama district occupies one half. Of those two districts, Ōnuma occupies two-thirds and Kawanuma occupies one-third. Aizu district lies to the south and east, Ōnuma district lies in the center, Kawanuma district lies to the north, and Yama district lies yet further north. The terrain is rocky and precipitous, the vegetation dense and lush; cold comes early and heat comes late.

[Left folio]

Snow accumulates to several feet in depth, and icicles hanging from the eaves grow as thick as rafters. When wind and snowstorm arise together, travelers frequently find their breath cut off, lose their way, and collapse. From mid-winter [the eleventh month of the lunar calendar] through mid-spring [the second month], accumulated snow freezes solid and all travel by ox, horse, palanquin, and litter is suspended. Straw mats packed with snow are used as protection for doors and walls, and snow-sleds [割書: "yukiguruma," also written "yukifune," both read as "sori"] are drawn for use in transport of passengers and goods. In the fourth year of Kanbun [1664], there were two castles in the region, nine hundred and ten villages, and the paddy fields [割書: fifteen thousand, three hundred and forty]…
