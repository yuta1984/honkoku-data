【Right folio】

To know the strategic points of lands and states in all four directions, one must rely on the existence of maps and records. This was the responsibility of the Grand Minister of Education (Dàsītú) in the Rites of Zhou, with the Zhifang official overseeing the details, subordinated to the Minister of War (Sīmǎ). These records were kept secret and stored away precisely to guard against calamity. When Han destroyed Qin, Xiao He first secured the maps and records, and it was through his efforts that Emperor Gaozu (Liu Bang) came to know in detail the strategic passes of the realm and the size of its population. were overseen by the Minister of Works (Sīkōng), but gradually came to be leaked to the outside. At that time, when the various kings of Huainan plotted rebellion, they all consulted the maps to deploy their troops and determine their routes of entry. Wang Feng's statement that "books concerning terrain and strategic passes should not be held by the vassal kings" was precisely in accord with the far-sighted prudence of the men of Zhou. The Tang dynasty established four subordinate offices under the Ministry of War (Bīngbù), with the Zhifang being one of them—this served as a proper warning drawn from the mistakes of predecessors.
