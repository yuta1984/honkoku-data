...under the Ministry of War, and the completion of the Da Ming Yitong Zhi (Comprehensive Gazetteer of the Great Ming) was an achievement unprecedented since the Han and Tang dynasties. Nevertheless, the lands and populations of the various states were not yet fully recorded therein. Thus Qiu Jun deliberated on the matter and petitioned that, following the model of the Rites of Zhou, a separate register be compiled. This may truly be called an understanding of the foremost duty in assisting the king and bringing peace to the states. In our country, the Fudoki were overseen by the Daijō-kan (Grand Council of State), but as the imperial house declined, the official posts fell into disuse; some records were scattered and never collected,

【Left folio】

while others were lost and never replenished. What has today drifted into circulation among the people is, in many cases, not the original text. This is truly lamentable. Minamoto no Masayuki, Middle Counselor of Aizu, sought the origins of Ōyashima (Japan), lamented the loss of the Fudoki, and personally set down a record of Aizu's climate and customs. He commanded Karun to embellish the prose and compose a preface for it—all in anticipation of the day when the state would undertake the work of compiling and completing [a new Fudoki].
