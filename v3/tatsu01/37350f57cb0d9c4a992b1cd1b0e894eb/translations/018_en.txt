[Furthermore, when waka poetry or other records refer to the mountains and rivers of Yama, Ōnuma, and Kawanuma as "Aizu," this is simply a broad, general usage of the name. The Man'yōshū contains the poem: "Aizu-ne no / kuni wo sa-dōmi / awanawaba / shinobi ni semu to / himo musuba sane" (The land of Aizu Ridge being so far away — if we cannot meet, let us at least tie our cords together as a token of longing). The Gosen'shū contains the poem: "Kimi wo nomi / Shinobu no sato e / yuku mono wo / Aizu no yama no / harukeki ya nazo" (I travel only toward the village of longing for you — why must Aizu's mountains lie so far away?). The poem of Hōkkyō Kenshō reads: "Hokushi kakeshi ga ni / Aizu no yama nareba / iru ni kai aru / sato ni arikeri" (Just as a star sets behind it — being Aizu's mountain, it is a village worth entering). Another poem reads: "Aizu-yama / fumoto no yuki no / muragieni / sori no tsunade wo / hiki so wabinuru" (As the snow at the foot of Aizu Mountain melts away in patches, how forlorn I am, pulling the towrope of the sled).]

[Left folio]

[The Rokujo contains the poem: "Kokoro ni mo / arade watarishi / Aizugawa / ukina wo mizu ni / utsushitsuru kana" (Having crossed the Aizu River with no intention — oh, how I have cast my floating reputation upon the waters). The verse by Shinkei reads: "Tsuyu au shira nite / some no Aizu-yama" (Unknowingly met by dew — Aizu Mountain dyed in color). And again: "Ōfune kogu / koyoi ya hoshi no / Aizugawa" (Rowing a great boat — tonight the stars reflect in the Aizu River).]
