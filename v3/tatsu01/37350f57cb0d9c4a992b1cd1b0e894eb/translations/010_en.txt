【Right folio】

Since the reign of Emperor [Jin/Nin]mu, Japan came into contact with the Western Lands (China and the continent), and thereafter the name "Nihon" (Japan) was proclaimed by ourselves and also adopted by other countries. That other nations call it "Fusō Koku" (Land of the Fusang Tree) is because they observed the sun rising from the Fusang tree [in the east]. That it is also called "Kunshi Koku" (Land of Gentlemen/Noble People) is because they heard of our divine winds (kamikaze). Emperor Keikō established the names of the various provinces, Emperor Seimu demarcated the boundaries of provinces and districts, and Emperor Genmei organized the names of provinces, districts, villages, and hamlets.

【Left folio】

The Ōyashima (Great Eight Islands) of high antiquity were gradually divided into thirty-three provinces, and further subdivided into sixty-six provinces. The system of the Home Provinces (Kinai) and the Seven Circuits (Shichidō) was established in the middle ages [of Japanese history], and has not been revised since. The reason "circuit" (dō) is not used for the Home Provinces is analogous to how the "Yu Gong" (Tribute of Yu) does not specify boundaries for Jizhou. The names of the sixty-six provinces and the Fudoki (Records of Local Culture) originated with Empress Genmei and were completed in the reign of Emperor Daigo.
