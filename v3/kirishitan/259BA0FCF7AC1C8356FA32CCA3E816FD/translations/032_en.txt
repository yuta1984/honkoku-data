As the warriors entered the interior of the outer bailey, Kumagai and Sano descended through that palisade, with Itō following close behind. Eventually overtaking the two men, he charged toward the outer bailey, flew into the castle, and advanced side by side with Nabeshima's forces. Seeing that the allied forces were flooding into the front of the second bailey, he rushed diagonally to the left. Along the way, Hoshino Sōbei called out, saying: "We were among the first to charge in, but because the main body of troops did not follow, we are only now joining the advance force." He said this as a matter of record, by way of testimony. Also, Nakai Jirōsuke, who had long been an expert in archery, dashed ahead of the vanguard and shot arrows into a cluster of dozens of enemies, nocking and drawing to full capacity — and not a single arrow missed. All the

allied warriors responded with admiration. At this moment, three enemies came charging toward Jirōsuke. Sakichi turned to face the three men and attempted to thrust at the chest of an enemy advancing with arrows, when a musket shot fired from among the clustered enemies struck the right side of the neck of the enemy to Itō's left, felling him face-down. Sakichi, not knowing that the enemy had been struck by a musket ball, understood it as though the enemy had been brought down by a spear thrust, and braced himself, thinking: "He will surely recover and come after me. If so, I shall rise and cut him down." He gripped the hilt of his sword and waited, but when a large number of allies came rushing in, those two enemies withdrew.
