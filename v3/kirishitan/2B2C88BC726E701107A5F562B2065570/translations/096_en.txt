It is deeply regrettable. Naizen and Jūzō were not originally supposed to lead the charge, but since unexpectedly few troops advanced, both men had no choice but to go forward themselves. Naizen's entire retinue accompanied him. There were many wounded and killed. Those who came from your side also fought without any exceptions. The details regarding the wounded and killed will be reported by Tōuemon. Being caught up in the confusion, I write in haste. Respectfully submitted.

　　3rd day of the 1st month　　　Itakura Mondo Shigenori
　　　　　　　To: Suō-sama

Furthermore, I did not know the exact location where Naizen was killed, and I was unable to witness the circumstances of his death. It is utterly regrettable and mortifying. However, since both Naizen and Jūzō fought without holding anything back, I believe it is what they had hoped for. Dying in loyal service to one's lord, surpassing even one's parents in devotion — there is not the slightest regret in this. Please convey the above to Koide Tsushima's quarters as well. The men stationed here could report the numbers, but that would take too long, so please send word ahead from your location regarding the circumstances of Naizen-no-shō's death. In haste, that is all.

According to the supplementary record of the Shimabara Subjugation: On the 3rd day of the 1st month, Inoue Chikugo-no-kami was dispatched as an official envoy (jōshi) to Hishū Shimabara. The circumstances of his audience before the lord were as follows: he received direct words, was granted a ceremonial sword (with a silk outer robe on the mounting), ten pieces of gold, and an official vermilion-seal document (shuinjō) for men and horses. He withdrew at the hour of the Monkey (around 4 p.m.) and immediately departed.
