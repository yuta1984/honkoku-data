…harming others, concealing transgressions, associating with laypersons, while being monastics preferring noise and clamor, and not revering the Three Treasures. [Etc.] In this manner, such a monk not only fails to practice the dharma of a monk but further receives the name of one who defiles the conduct of a monk. The sūtra further states: "One who says that what is not the pure practice (*brahmacaryā*) is the pure practice, and that breaking precepts is keeping precepts, and who pays homage and reverence to those who do receive and uphold the precepts without recognizing any wrong in this—such a monk receives eight kinds of light [retributive] consequences: namely, being foolish; being dim-witted and mute; suffering skin afflictions (scabies) on the body; being afflicted with white leprosy and being ugly; becoming a poor, emaciated servant; being short-lived; being disrespected by people; obtaining an evil reputation; and not encountering the Buddha. This person has nowhere even to spit in the three-thousand-fold world, let alone going, coming, bowing and stretching. Why? Because in the past, a great king offered this very earth to precept-keeping monks. A person of virtue walked the path upon it. How much more so with other things—the four requisites of a monastic hall. Even if one hair were divided into a hundred parts, this person could not receive even one part, because they cannot requite the kindness of the donor." [End of citation.] The fuller text should be consulted.

**○** The *Kōketsu* (vol. 4), citing the *Daichidoron* (*Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra*), fascicle 19, states: "The monks asked the Buddha: 'A forest-dwelling monk (*āraṇyaka*) has died. Where is he now reborn?' The answer was: 'He is reborn in the Avīci Hell.'" [End of citation.]

**○** The *Nirvāṇa Sūtra*, Chapter on the Vajra Body (*Kōngōshin-bon*) (Commentary [*Esho*], vol. 3, folio 56), states: "One who fully receives and upholds the five precepts cannot be called a Mahāyāna person. One who does not receive the five precepts, for the sake of protecting the True Dharma—that person is called Mahāyāna. One who protects the True Dharma should take up swords, weapons, and implements, and attend upon and guard the Dharma teacher." [End of citation.]

**○** The same text (folio 53, verso) states: "One who protects and upholds the True Dharma does not receive the five precepts and does not practice the rules of deportment," etc. [End of citation.]
