...harming others, concealing one's offenses, associating closely with laypeople, delight in noise and tumult (despite being monastics), and failure to revere the Three Treasures. [Etc.] Such behavior does not merely constitute failure to practice the dharma of a monk—it is further called defiling the conduct of a monk. The sūtra also states: One who, while not practicing the pure life (brahmacarya), claims to do so; who, while breaking precepts, claims to keep them; who receives the reverential prostrations of those who do keep precepts; and who does not recognize this as evil—such a monk incurs eight lighter karmic retributions, namely: being foolish, being mute and dull, suffering skin afflictions (scabies), becoming ugly with white leprosy, being reborn as a poor and emaciated servant, dying young and being short-lived, not being respected by others, acquiring an evil reputation, and being unable to see the Buddha. Such a person cannot find a place to spit even on the great earth—let alone freely come, go, bow, and stretch. Why? Because kings in past ages gave this very earth as an offering to precept-keeping monks, and persons of virtue traveled the path upon it. How much more so the other goods, the four necessities of a monastic residence! Even if a single hair were divided into a hundred parts, such a person cannot receive even one of those parts, because that person cannot repay the kindness of the donor. [End of citation.] The full text should be consulted.

**○** The Shingon (True Word) school establishes its school on the basis of three sūtras and one treatise.

*Mahāvairocanābhisaṃbodhivikurvitādhiṣṭhānavaipulyasūtra* (abbreviated as the *Dainichi-kyō* / *Mahāvairocana Sūtra*): 6 fascicles, 31 chapters. **△ The sites where the three sūtras were preached:**

The *Dainichi-kyō* (Mahāvairocana Sūtra), fascicle 1, Chapter on Residing in the Mind at the Gate of the True Word (*Nyū Shingon Mon Jūshin-bon*), states: "Thus have I heard. At one time, the Bhagavat was dwelling in the Palace of the Vast Vajra Dharma Realm of the Tathāgata's Empowerment," etc. [End of citation.] Śubhākarasiṃha's *Gishaku* (Commentary), vol. 1, states: "In order to show the dwelling place of empowerment, it is called 'the Palace of the Vast Vajra Dharma Realm.' This palace is the place where the ancient Buddhas attained bodhi—it is the Heaven of Maheśvara." [End of citation.] This heaven is none other than the Heaven of Ultimate Form (*Shikukyōten* / *Akaniṣṭha*).

**△** The *Kongōchō-kyō* (Vajraśekhara Sūtra), fascicle 1, title of the Vast Ritual Section (*Kōdai Giki-bon*): "Thus have I heard. [End of citation.] The Akaniṣṭha Heaven is also the Heaven of Ultimate Form (*Shikukyōten*).
