*Question*: Equal-flow has four [kinds]. How can it be said that the two equal-flows of ripening-result and long-nourishment are absent? How then is it possible to say "the equal-flow category is absent"?

*Response*: Outside of and apart from the two categories of ripening-result and long-nourishment, there is no separate equal-flow. Hence [the text] does not say [they] are present. Now, in reference to original-nature [equal-flow], [the text] says equal-flow is absent. As for transformational equal-flow (*biàn yì děng liú* 変異等流), whether it exists or not is indeterminate. If [the faculties] are struck and contacted [by external stimuli], it is present; if not struck and contacted, it is absent. Hence [the text] does not say [it] is present. Theoretically it could also be present, as the *Jiā Chāo* (伽抄) explains.

From the text "[material forms of] the dharma-sphere (*dharmāyatana*, 法処)" to "without the initial long-nourishment":

"There is later long-nourishment but no place-becomes-wide-and-pervading (*chù kuān biàn* 処寛遍), etc.": The [*Yogācārabhūmi*,] fascicle fifty-four [folio seventeen verso] says: "Forms subsumed under the dharma[-sphere] have no ripening-result-born stream (*yì shú shēng liú* 異熟生流). For the rest, know [them] as [you would know] mind and mental factors (*citta-caitta*, 心心所)." (End of annotation passage.)

Explaining: Among the forms subsumed under the dharma-sphere, there are provisional (*jiǎ* 假) [forms] and real (*shí* 実) [forms]. Forms produced by meditation (*dìng suǒ shēng* 定所生), which are real [forms], have two kinds of equal-flow and only one kind of long-nourishment. Or [they] may also extend to "place-becomes-wide-and-pervading [long-nourishment]." The provisional forms of the dharma-sphere have only one long-nourishment — excluding "place-becomes-wide-and-pervading" — and extend to two kinds of equal-flow. This is precisely the interpretation in the chapter-master's *Jiā Chāo*. In the present chapter, however, [the text] says there are three kinds of equal-flow — how then [do the two accounts] not contradict each other?

*Response*: The intent of the *Jiā Chāo* is that outside of the "phase-increase-flourishing long-nourishment stream" (*xiāng zēng shèng cháng yǎng liú* 相増盛長養流), there is no separate equal-flow. Therefore [the *Jiā Chāo*] does not list the long-nourishment equal-flow, and states only that there are the latter two kinds of equal-flow. In the present chapter, all three kinds of equal-flow are listed together — hence [the two accounts] do not contradict [each other].

Master Jǐng says: "Among the dharma-sphere forms, the *extremely-abbreviated* (*jí lüè* 極略) [form] and the *extremely-reverted* (*jí huí* 極廻) [form] are both provisional [forms]. When [a form is] analyzed down to the finest subtle atom (*paramāṇu*, 極微), there is no real-form substance. It is simply when [the mind] provisionally analyzes [something] by taking form as object that it obtains, within the mental image (*xiāng fēn* 相分), an equal-flow stream (*děng liú liú* 等流流). As for non-indicative form (*avijñapti-rūpa*, 無表色), relying on the seeds (*bīja*, 種子) of indicative form (*vijñapti-rūpa*, 表色), one can provisionally posit the existence of an original-nature equal-flow stream. Where there is the significance of increase-and-flourishing, as in [the case of] wholesome and unwholesome [moral] discipline (*śīla*, 律儀), there is also the second long-nourishment stream. Form produced by meditation (*dìng suǒ shēng sè* 定所生色) and shadow-image form (*chǐng xiàng sè* 影像色) arising from [mental] constructive imagination (*parikalpita*, 遍計) also have original-nature equal-flow and the second phase-increase-flourishing stream — as [the *Jiā Chāo*] explains."

"Mind-and-mental-factors, etc.": The [*Yogācārabhūmi*,] fascicle fifty-four says: "Mind and mental factors (*citta-caitta*) have an equal-flow stream, a ripening-result-born stream (*yì shú shēng liú* 異熟生流), and a second phase-increase-flourishing long-nourishment stream (*dì èr xiāng zēng shèng cháng yǎng liú* 第二相増盛長養流)." (End of passage from the treatise.)

Explaining: Mind and mental factors that repay (*chóu* 酬) wholesome and unwholesome karma are ripening-result equal-flow (*yì shú děng liú* 異熟等流). Wholesome and unwholesome mental dharmas (*xīn fǎ* 心法), as well as deportment (*airy*, 威儀), craft (*kōng qiǎo* 工巧), and transformation (*biàn huà* 変化), etc., are original-nature equal-flow (*běn xìng děng liú* 本性等流). Now, the aforementioned wholesome and unwholesome dharmas increase in their substantial phase (*tǐ xiāng* 体相) through conditions such as food — and this increase is called the long-nourishment stream. Because [these] are not material dharmas (*rūpa-dharma*, 色法), there is no "place-becomes-wide-and-pervading long-nourishment." Hence [the text] says "without the initial long-nourishment."

From the text "the various aggregates (*skandha*, 蘊) of the form realm (*rūpadhātu*, 色界)" to "[long-nourishment of] the three realms (*dhātu*, 界) all extends to meditative-stabilization (*samādhi*, 等持)":

This is [an explanation that] discriminates, based on the realm [distinction], the greater and lesser number of conditions that long-nourishment is equipped with. The [*Yogācārabhūmi*,] fascicle fifty-four [folio seventeen recto] says: "The various forms of the desire realm (*kāmadhātu*, 欲界) attain long-nourishment by means of the four kinds of food (*āhāra*, 四食) and all other conditions (*nidāna*, 因縁) for long-nourishment. The various forms of the form realm do not attain long-nourishment by means of solid food (*kabaliṅkārāhāra*, 段食), sleep, and celibate practice (*brahmacarya*, 梵行)."

Explaining: Solid food (*duàn shí* 段食) has scent (*gandha*) and taste (*rasa*) as its substance. The form realm lacks scent and taste — hence [form-realm beings do] not [attain long-nourishment] by means of solid food. Sleep in general arises from three conditions: (1) dependence on food and drink, (2) dependence on the season (*jié* 節), (3) arising from the mind. The first two conditions extend to even arhats (*arhat*, 阿羅漢). The last condition belongs only to ordinary persons (*pṛthagjana*, 凡夫). In the upper two realms (form realm and formless realm) these conditions do not exist — hence [beings there do] not [attain long-nourishment] by means of sleep. This meaning is explained at length in the *Yíjiào lùn* (遺教論, *Treatise on the Buddha's Final Teaching*). Sustaining the body through celibate practice (*brahmacarya*) exists only in the desire realm; the upper realms do not have this matter — hence [they do] not [attain long-nourishment] by means of celibate practice.

*Response*: In the upper realms, although [beings] cultivate mental engagement (*manaskāra*, 作意), this is not the cause that sustains (*zhù* 住) the body. [They] cultivate mental engagement solely in order to attain meditative stabilization (*samādhi*, 定). Therefore, the aggregates (*skandha*, 蘊) of the upper realms are not long-nourished through celibate practice, but are long-nourished exclusively through meditative-stabilization (*samādhi*, 等持). As for "[long-nourishment of] the three realms all extends to meditative-stabilization": if one explains in accordance with [a] difficult [question] — when the body is in the desire realm and one cultivates and attains a higher [realm's] meditative stabilization (*dhyāna*, 定), it is through the power of that meditative stabilization that the desire-realm form and mind (*sè xīn* 色心) are nourished. Hence the long-nourishment of the desire [realm] also extends to meditative-stabilization.

From the text "among internal and external aggregates (*jù* 聚)" to "not because [this passage] discriminates [their] substance":

"Among internal and external aggregates, according to what is applicable, there are either three, two, or one category of great-element-produced form (*mahābhūta-nirmita*, 大種造色)": If [it is the] supporting-root sense-objects (*fú gēn chén* 扶根塵) etc., [they] possess all three categories. If [it is the] five faculty-forms etc., [they] have only two categories, without original-nature equal-flow. If [it is the] form of wholesome and unwholesome provisional bodily and verbal karma (*kāya-vāk-karman*, 身語業) and the outer receptacle world (*bhājana-loka*, 外器) etc., [they] have only the equal-flow stream and lack the remaining [streams of] ripening-result, etc. If the outer receptacle world etc. has definite long-nourishment [through meditation], it may also extend to long-nourishment.

In this way, according to what is applicable, all internal and external forms are equipped with three, two, or one category. These three categories each have what produces (*néng zào* 能造) and what is produced (*suǒ zào* 所造). Each one, following its own category, is called its own "great-element-produced form" (*dà zhǒng zào sè* 大種造色). That is: what is produced by ripening-result great elements (*yì shú dà zhǒng suǒ zào* 異熟大種所造), and so forth, up to what is produced by equal-flow great elements. These three categories of great-element-produced forms, mutually facing one another, can each produce [forms of] the other [categories]. To explain this meaning, there are briefly two *guān* (関, junctures/sections): (1) clarifying that the four great elements of the three categories each separately produce form; (2) clarifying that the four great elements of the three categories produce form in an interlocking-alternating manner (*qǐ hù* 綺互).

In the first [section], "separate production" (*bié zào* 別造): What is produced [by each] is in turn subsumed under its own separate category among the three. That is: the ripening-result great elements produce the ten ripening-result forms (*yì shú shí sè* 異熟十色) within the inner body. The long-nourishment great elements are able to produce the long-nourishment faculty-forms (*cháng yǎng sè gēn* 長養色根) and the forms of the eleven sense-spheres (*āyatana*, 処) inside and outside. The equal-flow great elements are able to produce the equal-flow forms of the outer six sense-spheres — forms, sounds, etc.

In the second [section], "mutual production" (*hù zào* 互造):

【Lower Section】

First: The ripening-result four great elements are able to produce the form and sound of meditation long-nourishment (*dìng cháng yǎng sè shēng* 定長養色聲). Hence the [*Yogācārabhūmi*,] fascicle sixty-six says: "If the concentrated mind (*dìng xīn* 定心) of that [person] contemplates the material dharmas of the desire realm, [and] shadow-images arise, it should be said [they are] produced by the desire-realm great elements." (End of passage from the treatise.) Because these desire-realm great elements already extend to ripening-result, etc., it is thus known that these meditation long-nourishment forms are produced from the ripening-result great elements.

Second: The ripening-result four great elements are able to produce equal-flow forms, sounds, etc. That is: when ripening-result forms and sounds can manifest (*biǎo* 表) the inner wholesome and unwholesome volitions (*cetanā*, 思), they are then called equal-flow forms and sounds. These equal-flow forms and sounds can be produced from the ripening-result great elements.

As for the long-nourishment four great elements being able to produce ripening-result faculties and sense-objects: food and drink etc. long-nourish the four great elements and the faculties and sense-objects. When these have been long-nourished [in this way], they also cause the ripening-result faculties and sense-objects to become superior (*zēng shèng* 増勝) and expansive (*kuān guǎng* 寬廣). It is precisely through the power of that long-nourishing and enlarging that the ripening-result faculties become superior. This is then producing through mutual dependence (*xiāng yóu jí* 相由籍), and not direct production (*qīn zào* 親造).

Regarding the statement "the long-nourishment four great elements are able to produce equal-flow forms, sounds, etc.": that is, when long-nourishment forms and sounds are able to manifest (*biǎo* 表) the wholesome and unwholesome volitional karma (*cetanā-karman*, 思業), they are then called equal-flow forms and sounds. Therefore these equal-flow forms and sounds are produced from the long-nourishment great elements. The remainder can be understood.

As for the equal-flow four great elements being able to produce ripening-result and long-nourishment forms, etc.: Since the substance of food and drink is equal-flow [great elements] capable of producing what is produced — relying on this food and drink, [the equal-flow four great elements] are not merely able to produce long-nourishment forms, sounds, etc. alone, but further also cause the ripening-result faculties and sense-objects to become superior (*zēng shèng* 増勝). Therefore, the equal-flow four great elements within this food function as a remote support (*yuǎn yī* 遠依), long-nourishing the ripening-result [forms] etc. so that they either arise or become superior. Using this mutual production as an example, the remaining cases of mutual arising can be understood by analogy.
