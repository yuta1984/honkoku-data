…[the character is established] upon the seed of *cetanā* (思種, volitional intention); it is called "form" (*rūpa*, 色) from what is being prevented. The seed of the *cetanā* of the *asaṃvara* (不律儀, non-restraint) does not prevent these evil bodily and verbal actions; therefore it is able to manifest in activity (*xiànxíng*, 現行). Because the seed of *cetanā* gives rise to this manifesting form, a "form-nature" (*rūpa-svabhāva*, 色性) is provisionally established upon the seed of *cetanā*. Although that manifesting form (*xiàn sè*, 現色) does have its *biǎo* (表, explicit/indicative [action]), here [the analysis] does not rely on the manifesting form. [The functioning here] is only the capacity (*gōng néng*, 功能) of the seed of *cetanā* and is not the explicit (*biǎo*, 表) [action of] evil bodily and verbal [karma]; therefore the seed of *cetanā* is called *wúbiǎo* (無表, non-indicative [karma]). In terms of actuality, one may also say that the *wúbiǎo* of *asaṃvara* (不律儀) is spoken of in reference to the wholesome form that is being obstructed. As extensively explained in the *Duìfǎ shū* (対法疏) of that master [i.e., Master Fàn] — one should consult that text and examine it.

From the text "its *jí lüè*, *jí* (迥)" to "what arises [from them] is the same": "Its *jí lüè* (*jí luè*), etc." — Among the five forms of the *dharmāyatana* (法処, mind-only dharma-base), three forms are briefly indicated in order to clarify the manner of *production* (*zào*, 造). "Extremely-condensed form" (*jí lüè sè*, 極略色): "*jí*" (極) means "to arrive at the ultimate" or "to exhaust." "*lüè*" (略) has two meanings: (1) the meaning of *gathering together* (*zǒng*, 惣) — analyzing (*zhé*, 折) all material forms (*rūpa*) gathered together down to their ultimate atoms (*paramāṇu*, 極微), called "extremely-condensed form"; (2) the meaning of *minimum* (*shǎo*, 少) — analyzing faculties and sense-objects down to the most minimal point, called "extremely-condensed form." The *Duìfǎ lùn* (*Abhidharmasamuccaya*, 対法論) [fascicle 1, folio 8 verso] explains "extremely-condensed form": "It takes subtle atoms (*paramāṇu*) as its substance (*tǐ*, 体); that is, it takes as its nature the subtle atoms (*paramāṇu*) of the five faculties (*pañcendriya*, 五根), five sense-objects (*pañcaviṣaya*, 五塵), four great elements (*catvāri mahābhūtāni*, 四大種), and the real forms of the *dharmāyatana* (法処)."

"Extremely-diaphanous form" (*jí jiǒng sè*, 極迥色): This is the *jiǒng*-form (*jiǒng sè*, 迥色) of the *rūpāyatana* (色処, form-domain) — it is that which becomes manifest only when freed from obstruction (*āvaraṇa*, 礙), and "diaphanous" (*jiǒng*, 迥) is the name established [for it] on account of [being freed from obstruction]. The *Duìfǎ lùn* says: "Namely, the form that is free from all other obstructing contacts (*sparśa*, 触); it takes the subtle atoms (*paramāṇu*) of the space-element form (*ākāśa-rūpa*, 空界色) as its substance."

"Conceptually-constructed arising form" (*biànjì suǒ qǐ sè*, 遍計所起色): Because consciousness (*vijñāna*, 識) of the three natures (善・悪・無記, wholesome, unwholesome, indeterminate) extensively analyzes and discriminates (*biànjì dù*, 遍計度) objects, and because [form] arises therefrom, it is called "conceptually-constructed arising form." The *Duìfǎ lùn* [fascicle 4, folio 17 recto] says: "This refers to image-forms (*pratibimba-rūpa*, 影像色) — the faculties and sense-objects that are transformations (*pariṇāma*, 所変) of solitary, non-meditative mental [consciousness], without the function of [genuine] faculties, etc. — like the reflection of the moon in water or an image in a mirror. These are not one [kind but many]."

"Following the great elements of the original substrate (*běn zhì*, 本質), etc." — as for the original substrate of the *dharmāyatana*-forms, the various texts are not in agreement. According to the *Pramāṇasamuccaya* (*Jí liàng lùn*, 集量論), the indirect (*śithila*, 疎) object-condition (*ālambanapratyaya*, 所縁縁) is

decisively (*juédìng*, 決定) present whenever any consciousness arises. As explained in the *Buddhabhūmi-sūtra* (*Fódì lùn*, 仏地論) etc.: "Even when non-discriminating wisdom (*nirvikalpajñāna*, 無分別智) takes *tathatā* (真如, suchness) as its object, it also produces a mental image (*pratibimba*, 影像)." Therefore, whenever any consciousness arises, there is definitely an original substrate (*běn zhì*, 本質). Relying on this principle, among the five [*dharmāyatana*-forms], four forms necessarily have an original substrate.

The first, "extremely-condensed form," takes as its original substrate the real forms of the ten form-bases (*rūpāyatana*, 有色処) of the desire-realm (*kāmadhātu*, 欲界) and form-realm (*rūpadhātu*, 色界) along with the fruits of meditative concentration in the form and formless realms (*rūpāvacaradhyāna*, 色無色定果).

The next, "extremely-diaphanous form," takes the form-base (*rūpāyatana*, 色処) of the desire-realm and form-realm as its substrate (*zhì*, 質). In the formless realm (*ārūpyadhātu*, 無色界) there is no separate domain (*suǒ chù*, 所処). Therefore, what is explained here is all generated by the analysis (*zhé*, 析) of those coarse forms — these are what [the text] takes as [the] substrate. It is not that having those [kinds of] forms is called the "original substrate."

"Conceptually-constructed arising [form]" takes as its original substrate the ten form-bases of the desire realm and the forms that arise through meditative concentration in the upper two realms (form-realm and formless-realm). All of these can lodge upon (*tuō*, 託) [their respective substrates] and transform into mental images (*pratibimba*, 影像); hence there are forms that have a substrate (*zhì*, 質). If there is no original object (*běn jìng*, 本境), one uses verbal teachings (*míng jiào*, 名教) as the original substrate — since it is admitted that the cognitive aspect (*ākāra*, 行相) of consciousness extends to both the direct cognizing (*dṛṣṭi*, 見) [aspect] and the image (*pratibimba*, 影) [aspect], one knows that what is cognized (*ālambana*, 所縁) definitely has both the image and the substrate.

The *Chéngwéishí lùn* (*Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi-śāstra*, 成唯識論) [fascicle 7, folio 31] states: "The indirect object-condition (*alambana-pratyaya*, 疎所縁縁) may or may not exist for the cognizing consciousness — since [consciousness] can arise even without depending upon an external (*bāhya*, 外) substrate of reflection (*suǒlǜtuō*, 所慮託)."

If one follows this interpretation, [then]: for the forms of "extremely-condensed," "extremely-diaphanous," and "conceptually-constructed-arising" [categories] — those that analyze dependent forms and rely on verbal teachings (*míng jiào*, 名教) have their original substrate as stated above. Those that, based on one's own discursive thinking (*xúnsī*, 尋思), discriminate such [things as] self (*ātman*, 我), dharmas (*dharma*, 法), sky-flowers (*ākāśa-puṣpa*, 空花), rabbit-horns (*śaśaviṣāṇa*, 兎角), past, future, the end of *kalpas* (*kalpa*, 劫), and the eternally subtle — without depending on others' teachings — have no original substrate whatsoever. As extensively explained in the Chapter-Master [Kuìjī's] *Duìfǎ chāo* (対法抄) [fascicle 2, folio 51 verso].

Now [when the text] says "following the great elements of the original substrate, etc." — the coarse forms that are analyzed (*suǒ xī*, 所析) already extend to both *immediate* (*jí zhì*, 即質) and *apart-from-substrate* (*lí zhì*, 離質) [production]. Based on this, the "extremely-condensed" and "extremely-diaphanous" [forms] that are established from it also extend to both *immediate* and *apart-from-substrate*. The great elements (*mahābhūta*, 大種) that produce (*néng zào*, 能造) the coarse forms of the original substrate already extend to both *immediate* and *apart-from-substrate*. *Question*: Can the forms of the three periods of time (*trikāla*, 三世) accomplish *suspended production* (*xuán zào*, 懸造 — production across temporal distance)?

*Response*: Master Fàn says: "In the Mahāyāna school, the past and future have no substance (*tǐ*, 体); only the present has substance — therefore there is no *suspended production*. However, if [things] are distantly interconnected, then [such production] is also possible. If, through present great elements, future produced-forms are drawn out and caused to arise from afar, one may provisionally establish this as *suspended production* — however, this is not genuine (*zhēn*, 真) production. As for presently-produced forms, one may also provisionally say that they are produced by the great elements of the past — there is no obstacle to explaining it in this way (*yì shuō*, 義説). (End of the master's text.)" The meaning of the present chapter-author is likewise the same.

"As [discussed] in the *wúbiǎo* chapter": The seventh gate of the *Biǎo Wúbiǎo Yílín* (表無表義林, "Meaning Grove on Indicative and Non-Indicative [Karma]") [folio 49 recto] says: "Even if [one considers] the Buddha's body's undefiled (*anāsrava*, 無漏) non-indicative [karma] and the formless-realm's concentration-concomitant [precepts] — if these are [also] brought about by the four great elements of the past desire-realm — [then] since they provisionally rely on something distant (*shū yuǎn*, 疎遠) for the name 'form,' and likewise rely on something distant for the name 'production,' for this reason they are not [simply] named 'form' from what is [immediately] manifested (*suǒ fā*, 所発), nor named 'production' [from what is immediately manifested] — because [the relation] is indeterminate, and it is not necessarily the case that [corresponding] bodily and verbal [actions] are manifested (*suǒ fā shēn yǔ*, 所発身語). (End of the chapter text.)" Here [the author] points to that passage, hence saying "as [discussed] in the *wúbiǎo* chapter."

Master Fàn says: "In the mutual production among the three realms, consider someone who, while dwelling in the desire-realm, has attained the divine eye (*divyacakṣus*, 天眼): the consciousness of the desire-realm, relying on the eye of the upper realm, sees the forms of the upper realm. The shadow-form (*pratibimba*, 影色) that is the transformation of this eye-consciousness follows the cognizing consciousness (*néng yuán shí*, 能縁識) and is solely included within the desire-realm. Yet [this consciousness] uses the four great elements of the upper realm as its remote producing [elements] (*néng zào*, 能造) — because the shadow-form (*pratibimba*, 影色) of the desire-realm has no four great elements of a different realm. Taking this as an example, the rest is likewise. Furthermore, the autonomous forms (*zìzài sè*, 自在色) of form-realm and formless-realm meditative concentrations
