**Q:** Within the (syllogism where) conditioned dharmas (有為) serve as the subject-term (有法), is there a "difference-of-subject-qualification contradiction" (有法差別相違) fault?

**A:** There is. Constructing a "subject-qualification contradiction" (有法差別相違) syllogism: "Eye-consciousness (眼等) and other conditioned dharmas ought not to be conditioned dharmas that are non-existent in the ultimate (真無の有為). [Thesis] Because they arise from conditions. [Reason] Like form (色) and other (dharmas) that are conventionally existent (俗有). [Example]"

If one accepts that "the conventionally existent (俗有) is precisely what is non-existent in the ultimate (真無)," then the "conventional existence of dependent-arising" (俗有の縁生義) in the positive example (同喩) — the illusory phenomena (似事幻) — of the original syllogism cannot function as the establishing reason (能立因). Because (the illusory phenomena are also) accepted as being non-existent in the ultimate (真無). From this it is clearly known: the meaning of "non-existent in the ultimate" (真無) and the meaning of "existent in the conventional" (俗有) are each separately distinct.

**Q:** By what means can one know that this contradiction (fault) exists?

**A:** In Tripiṭaka (Xuanzang's) syllogism it is said: "Universally established form (極成色) is the self-nature of the subject-term (有法自相). That is, form-as-consciousness (識の色) and form-apart-from-consciousness (識を離れた色) are the qualification of the subject-term (有法差別)." In accordance with this it is clearly known: "Eye-consciousness and other conditioned dharmas" is the self-nature of the subject-term (有法自相), and "conditioned dharmas that are non-existent in the ultimate — not being conditioned dharmas non-existent in the ultimate" is the qualification of the subject-term (有法差別). Therefore this fault exists. Since the reason in the original syllogism of "conditioned dharmas are empty" does not already say "self-accepted" (自許), one cannot construct an "indeterminate" (不定) (fault). This (limitation) prevents (the construction of) this fault.

**Q:** Does this syllogism contain a "dissimilar-case example" (異法喩)?

**A:** In accordance with what the *Zhǎngzhēn lun* says, there is no dissimilar-case example (異法喩). Because that treatise's meaning of negation (遮義) is already established, there is no dissimilar case (異品) etc. (And so on.)

**Q:** If there is no dissimilar case (異品) in this syllogism, why does the treatise on *hetuvidyā* say: "Only these three parts are called the 'capable of establishing' (能立)"?

**A:** When the treatise says "only" (唯), it simply excludes the "four non-capable ones" (四等非), meaning that the capable of establishing necessarily possesses these three parts.

**Q:** This reason of "arising from conditions" (縁生の因) ought not to be able to demonstrate (the thesis). [Thesis] Because it lacks the three characteristics (三相, *trairūpya*) (of a valid reason). [Reason] Like a reason possessing only one characteristic. [Example] How is this objection to be resolved?

**A:** There is a "conclusively contradictory" (決定相違) fault (with that objection). That is, the reason "because of dependent arising" (縁生故) can definitively demonstrate the thesis. Because it possesses the first two characteristics (初二相) and does not operate in the dissimilar case (異品において行じない) — just like the reason "being produced" (所作因).

**Q:** If among the three capable-of-establishing (三能立 — thesis, reason, example), there is no "establishing the thesis through the dissimilar case," then is there also no "establishing the thesis through the similar case"?

Having thus generally asked against whom (the syllogism of) emptiness is established —

**Answer:** In the third scroll of (the *Cheng weishi lun*), after refuting Bhāviveka's thesis, it concludes: "From this, one can believe in the existence of the eighth consciousness, etc." (And so on.) Having already refuted those who claim "conditioned dharmas are empty based on true nature" and then concluded "there is an eighth consciousness" — from this it is clearly known: Dharmapāla also says, based on "true nature" (真性), that the eighth consciousness exists. Now, against this thesis (of Bhāviveka), (Dharmapāla) establishes the syllogism of emptiness (空比量) — hence there is no problem.

**Q:** In accordance with the third scroll of the treatise, it would seem that the meaning is as such. But Dharmapāla does not say "the consciousness exists in true nature (真性識有)." How then is there a contradiction?

Therefore there is no contradiction. If one superficially searches through the treatise, the meaning may appear to be so. But if one deeply seeks the treatise's intent, the meaning is not so.

**Q:** If both schools (清弁・護法) equally say "true nature" (真性), yet do not mutually accept the meaning of "empty" and "not-empty" — so it is called "non-establishment" (不成) — then both schools equally say "sound" (声) yet do not mutually accept "permanent" and "impermanent." The meaning of "permanent and impermanent" is a secondary meaning (別義) of sound, not its proper meaning (正義). Therefore they cannot be treated as analogous.

**Q:** The proper meaning (正義) of "true nature" (真性) is the meaning of "neither empty nor existent" (非空非有). Yet there are the "two meanings of empty and existent." From the perspective of the secondary meaning (別義) over which (the two schools) dispute, how does this stand? The syllogism "sound is impermanent" has no fault of "non-establishment of one part of the subject-term" (有法一分不成). (You say:) "Like true nature, therefore." 

**A:** For Bhāviveka, the meaning of "empty" (空の義) is the proper meaning (正義) of "true nature" (真性). (Therefore) the subject-term is not universally established (極成). Hence there is a "one-part fault" (一分過). The syllogism of sound is not like this, so they cannot be treated as analogous.

**Q:** How does one know that "the meaning of resistance (質礙) is the proper meaning of sound"?

**A:** In the category of the five aggregates (五蘊の門), the "meaning of resistance" is the same (for sound and form). Sound is therefore encompassed within the form-aggregate (色蘊). By this reasoning, it is clearly known that the proper meaning of sound is the meaning of resistance.

## Section Two: Resolving the Faults of the Tripiṭaka Master Xuanzang's Consciousness-Only Syllogism (玄奘三蔵唯識比量淨過決第二)

**The Tripiṭaka Master (Xuanzang) traveled throughout the Western Regions (西域) and, responding to the invitation of King Harṣavardhana (戒日王), established the Consciousness-Only syllogism (唯識比量) as follows:**

"Form that is universally established in truth (真故極成の色) is not separate from eye-consciousness (眼識). [Thesis] Because of what is self-admitted (自許) as belonging to the first three (categories), (which is) encompassed by the eye (眼所摂) but not encompassed (by the eye-consciousness itself, though it serves as its object condition) (初三摂眼所不摂). [Reason] Like eye-consciousness itself (眼識). [Example]"
