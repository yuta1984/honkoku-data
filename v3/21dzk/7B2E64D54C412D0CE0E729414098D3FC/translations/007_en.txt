This is the correct interpretation. (etc.)

In what way does this violate (correct) principle? (etc.) (End of this section.)

When correct wisdom cognizes true thusness — although this is newly attained (wisdom), because (true thusness) is (the wisdom's own) self-nature (*zìxìng* 自性), it does not transform reflected images. Subsequently attained wisdom (*hòu dé zhì* 後得智) cognizes conventional truth (*sú dì* 俗諦) (Note: the *Ki* quotation lacks "zhī 之"), and although its true-thusness object is (the wisdom's own) self-nature, it is not an object belonging to its own division (*zì fēn jìng* 自分境). Hence it transforms reflected images (Note: "xiàng 像" is probably an error for "yuán 緣").

*Question:* If "because the principle of true thusness is the nature of wisdom (*zhì xìng* 智性), it does not transform an image" — then, since the self-witness-part is also of the nature of wisdom, could it not transform an image and cognize the self-nature-part (*zì xìng fēn* 自性分)? (Note: the three characters "bù biàn yǐng 不変影" are absent in the original and are supplied from the *Shiki* private notes.)

*Answer:* Although the self-witness-part (Note: the nine characters from "yé zì xìng etc." are absent in the original and are supplied from the *Shiki*) is of the nature of wisdom, it has aspects (*yǒu xiāng* 有相); hence it cognizes (it) by transforming reflected images (Note: the four characters "biàn yǐng děng 変影等" are supplied from the *Shiki*). Correct wisdom cognizing true thusness — (Note: "bù 不" is probably superfluous) by principle (true thusness) is without aspects (*wú xiāng* 無相); hence it cognizes (it) without transforming (an image) (Note: the four characters "bù biàn děng 不変等" in the original text read "bǐ zhì yóu yuán 彼智猶緣" and are revised based on the *Shiki*).

*Question:* The reflected image that appears when the perceiving-part at the buddha-fruit stage cognizes (its own) etc. — to what part does it belong?

*Answer:* According to Kuiji's commentary (*shū* 疏), scroll eight: the reflected image that appears when the perceiving-part cognizes (its own) etc. is also separately transformed. However, it is subsumed under the aspect-part, because it takes (objects) externally in the same way as the corresponding dharmas (mental factors). (etc.)

*Question:* Based on what sūtra or treatise did Bodhisattva Dharmapāla establish the four parts?

*Answer:* The establishment of the two parts is based on evidence from Dignāga's *Pramāṇasamuccaya* (*Jí Liàng Lùn* 集量論). Hence the *Pramāṇasamuccaya* says: "What resembles the appearance of the object is the measured (*prameya* 所量). The appearance of the grasper (*grāhaka* 能取相) is the self-witness (*svāsaṃvitti* 自証). This is precisely the measurer (*pramāṇa* 能量) and its fruit (*phala* 果). These three are not different in substance." Furthermore, the establishment of the four parts is based on evidence from the *Ghanavyūhasūtra* (*Hòuyán Jīng* 厚嚴経, also known as *Mitsugon-kyō* 密厳経). Hence that sūtra says: "The mind of sentient beings has a twofold nature (*èr xìng* 二性); within all divisions, internal and external, the enwrapments of grasped-object (*grāhya* 所取) and grasping-subject (*grāhaka* 能取) (appear as) various distinctions within the perceiving (part)." (etc.) Hence one knows that there is also a fourth part.

*Question:* If so, since it is by means of two enwrapments (*chán* 纏) that (there come to be) four parts — (yet) the non-outflow (consciousness) has no enwrapment — there should be no four parts (in the non-outflow consciousness). If (one) says "it is not because of enwrapment that (there is the) four(th) (Note: probably "fēn 分" has dropped out after "sì 四") — (then) the sūtra's statement is useless.
