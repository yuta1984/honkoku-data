## Right Page: The Inconceivable (continued)

### Upper Section

Even if one cannot perform a hundred practices all at once, for one who — even in the smallest measure — attains a clear mind through the Buddha-dharma, this arises from faith. Among the eleven wholesome mental factors, if one follows and gives rise to even a single one, the remaining ones are also all present together. Moreover, when the mind is calm, distraction does not arise. If universal defilement (bi染, *vyāpti-kleśa*) is already absent, the remaining afflictions too will not be produced.

　　　Concluded.

*Transcription completed on the twenty-ninth day of the first month of Genkō 3 [1323].*

　\[Bodhicitta \[Intent of the copyist\]\] The *Xinlun* (Heart Treatise) states: "Within the inner mind, contemplate the disc of the sun and moon. Through performing this contemplation, one perceives the original mind as serene and pure, like a full moon." (And so on.) It further states: "If deluded thoughts arise, be aware of them and do not follow them. When delusion ceases, the source of the mind is empty and still; all virtues are complete therein, and its wondrous function is without limit." (And so on.) It further states: "All sentient beings entirely contain the mind of Samantabhadra (Universal Virtue). When I observe my own mind, its form is like the lunar disc. Why is the lunar disc used as a metaphor? \[Note: one instance of 為 may be a scribal repetition\] Because the perfect brightness of the full moon is in its essence similar to the mind of enlightenment (bodhicitta)." (And so on.)

　The *Hyōdō* (*Biǎodǎo*) states: "The second is ▢▢ without defilement. Within this there are four: first, the characteristic of self-nature (svabhāva-lakṣaṇa); second, the characteristic of dwelling in flavour (āsvādana-lakṣaṇa); third, the characteristic of following along with faults (anuvartin-doṣa-lakṣaṇa); fourth, the characteristic of following toward liberation (anuvartin-niḥsaraṇa-lakṣaṇa)." (And so on.)
