**Question:** The sūtra text states: "At that time, the Buddha addressed the Spirit of the Bodhi Tree (*bodhivṛkṣa-devatā*): O Good Heavenly Woman (*zen-nyoten*, 善女天) (etc.)." If so, is this Spirit of the Bodhi Tree the same as the Earth-Goddess Pṛthivī (*Kenrō Chijin*, 堅牢地神), or is it something else?

**Answer:** There should be two possible positions: identity (*dō*) and difference (*i*). Regarding this, neither (position) is (self-evidently) clear. If (they are) different (entities), (one may) consult the commentary master's (*shoshu*, 疏主) interpretation: "The Spirit of the Bodhi Tree is also called Kenrō (*Pṛthivī*), and is also called the Earth-Spirit (*chijin*) (etc.)." This interpretation appears to clarify that (they are of) the same substance (*dōtai*). On this basis, in the new-translation sūtra (*shin-yaku-kyō*), (the text reads) "At that time the Buddha addressed the Spirit of the Bodhi Tree"; the old-translation sūtra (*ko-yaku-kyō*) states "At that time the Buddha addressed the Earth-Spirit Pṛthivī (*Kenrō*)." If one takes (both) sūtra accounts together as a standard, should their substance be definitively the same? (However,) if (one accepts) that they are the same (and then reflects further): the tree-spirit is named "Spirit of the Bodhi Tree," while the earth-spirit is named "Pṛthivī (*Kenrō*)." How can their substance be the same? Moreover, in both the new and old translations of the sūtra,

the Spirit of the Bodhi Tree and the Earth-Goddess Pṛthivī (*Kenrō Chijin*) are each spoken of separately. If that is so, neither position (is self-evidently) clear. How (should this be understood)? [Marginal note: Zenshin of the Four Seasons (*Shiki no Zenshin*) raises this (as a problem).]

**Personal view:** The commentary master cites "there are said to be two positions (*ni-gi*)" without deciding which is correct. Therefore, even though this matter is difficult to settle definitively, (one should) for the time being establish the meaning of "same substance, different names (*dōtai imyō*)." The new translation says "At that time the Buddha addressed the Spirit of the Bodhi Tree," while the old translation states "At that time the Buddha addressed the Earth-Spirit Pṛthivī." When the accounts of the two sūtras are brought together and compared, (they appear to be) the same substance. Furthermore, the Great Master of Zīzhōu (Huizhao, 慧沼), in his interpretation of this sūtra text, states: "The Spirit of the Bodhi Tree is called the Earth-Spirit Pṛthivī (*Kenrō Chijin*)." Therefore, the commentary master's fundamental intent appears to (hold that they are) the same substance. However, concerning the objection that "the tree-spirit and the earth-spirit should be distinct (entities)": (the commentary text) *Zuànjué* (*Sanketu*, 纂決) states: "The tree-spirit and the earth-spirit are one. Since (the tree-spirit) does not depart from the earth, (it) is called earth-spirit" — this is the interpretation given. If that is so, although (the spirit) is called the Spirit of the Bodhi Tree because it protects and guards the Bodhi Tree, it also has the quality of not being separate from the earth, and therefore is also called the Earth-Spirit.

It is also said: A *kami*-spirit (*shin*, 神) is subsumed under the realm of hungry ghosts and spirits (*ki-shu*, 鬼趣). While it is not possible to apply the name "heavenly (being)" (*ten*, 天), since (this spirit) is a heavenly woman (*ten-nyo*) of eminent and venerable status among spirits (*kijin*), one praises her virtue by calling her *ten* (Heavenly Woman).

**It is advanced:** The third interpretation states: "The illusion-master is the Buddha; the illusion-disciple is the Bodhisattva (etc.)." Regarding this: based on the commentary master's interpretation, and considering the beginning and end (*kijin*, start-to-finish) of the sūtra text, (the text) clarifies the purport that the dependent-nature is like an illusion and presents three kinds of similes: the simile of the illusory realm produced by conditions (*ensei no genkyō*), the simile of the foolish person grasping at it as real (*gūnin-shū-jitsu*), and the simile of the wise one who comprehends (it) through wisdom (*chie-ryōtatsu*). **Personal view:** The commentary (*sho*) offers three interpretations of the illusion-master and illusion-disciple. Among these, (the commentary master) states of the third interpretation: "In accordance with the intent of the lower (later) text (*junka-gōi*, 准下合意), this interpretation is the best," judging it to be the correct interpretation (*shōshaku*). Therefore this interpretive intent should be established. In general, just as the illusion-master and illusion-disciple manifest various forms such as illusory phenomena, the Buddha and bodhisattvas, in accordance with conventional (truth) (*sezoku*), teach the names of all dharmas and manifest (things such as) gold, silver, fish, rice, and so forth, causing sentient beings to give rise to the thought of (their) real existence (*jitsū no omoi*). Therefore, the Buddha and bodhisattvas are designated the illusion-master and the illusion-disciple. Furthermore, the lower text of the sūtra brings forth "the profoundly deep correct Dharma (*jinjin shōhō*)" and resolves (the question of) the substance of dharmas, (stating that there are) "many illusory beings (*gen-nin*, 幻人) with (their) mind and mental factors (*shin-shinjū-hō*, 心心数法) who are able to comprehend the profoundly deep correct Dharma (etc.)." Since the text already calls those who comprehend the profoundly deep correct Dharma — the substance of the dharmas — "illusory beings (*gen-nin*)," it is thereby known that the Buddha and bodhisattvas are designated the illusion-master and the illusion-disciple. That the commentary states "in accordance with the intent of the lower text (*junka-gōi*)" is, I would suggest, precisely this intent. However, regarding (the objection concerning) "the simile of the wise one who comprehends — (interpreted as) the Buddha and bodhisattvas": even if those (of the latter simile) are also Buddha and bodhisattvas, what fault is there if the illusion-master and illusion-disciple are also the Buddha and bodhisattvas? Since the illusion-master and illusion-disciple who teach and reveal all dharmas — manifesting fish, rice, and so forth — are the Buddha and bodhisattvas, the Buddha and bodhisattvas are designated the illusion-master and the illusion-disciple. Next, as for the "simile of the wise one who comprehends": just as the illusion-master and illusion-disciple know the origin of the illusion, since the Buddha and bodhisattvas comprehend the dependent-nature (*paratantra*) as being like an illusion, the Buddha and bodhisattvas are also spoken of as the "simile of the wise one who comprehends." **A further objection states:** "It is not tenable to establish the (*junka-gōi*) 'accordance with the intent of the lower text.' **Question:** In the opening chapter of the present scroll, (the text) "praises the virtues of the Buddha's material body (*shikishin*, 色身)" with (the verse): "The supremely pure Sage Honored One (*Muni-son*, 牟尼尊) without superior — the radiance of his body illuminates and shines like the color of gold (etc.)." If so, what characteristic (*sō*, *lakṣaṇa*) is being praised and extolled?

**It is advanced:** The commentary offers two interpretations. One states (the verse) "praises the characteristic of the skin of the (Buddha's) body being the color of gold (*shinshin konshiki no sō*, 身皮金色相)," and the other states (the verse) "praises the characteristic of the constant radiance (*jōkō no sō*, 常光相)." Regarding this, (it should be noted that) neither of the two interpretations is (self-evidently) clear. With regard to the first interpretation, looking at the sūtra text — does (the verse) not appear at all to be praising the Buddha's body? Furthermore, the lower text (contains the line) "The most superior body of the World-Honored One is the color of gold (etc.)." Would this not itself be the characteristic of the skin of the (Buddha's) body being the color of gold? With regard to the second interpretation, looking at the lower text, (one finds that it) "praises the characteristic of the constant radiance: 'The circular radiance (*en-kō*, 円光) of one *hiro* (*jin*, 尋) illuminates without limit (etc.)'" If the present passage (were already) praising the characteristic of the constant radiance, would (the lower passage) not then be a redundant statement (*jūgen*, 重言)?
