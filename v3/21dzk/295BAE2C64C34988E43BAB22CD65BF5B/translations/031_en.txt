*(Continued from previous page)* ...is described as the four unobstructed discernments (*shi-muge-ben*, 四無礙辨), and named "the four dhāraṇīs." The meaning of the interpretation is clear — (the four unobstructed discernments) are seen as the retained (fruit) of the spell-dhāraṇī and the patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇī. If, relying on this, the matter stands thus: in the *Shōshaku* (*Sheshì*, 攝釋) of the Great Master Bōkyō (*Puyang*, 撲陽), it describes "the matter of subsuming and attributing the four unobstructed understandings to the four dhāraṇīs," stating: "The unobstructed understanding of speech (*shi-muge-ge*, 詞無礙解) is subsumed within the dhāraṇī of Dharma; the unobstructed understanding of expression (*ben-muge-ge*, 辨無礙解) belongs to the two kinds (of Dharma and meaning) (etc.)." If these are indeed the retained fruits of the spell-dhāraṇī and the patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇī, then why are they not, in order, subsumed and attributed to these? It is clearly known that the two retained kinds of expression and speech (*ben-shi*, 辨詞) are not the fruit of the spell and the patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇīs. What is to be said?

**Personal view:** Regarding the four kinds of Dharma (*hō*, 法), speech (*shi*, 詞), expression (*ben*, 辨), and meaning (*gi*, 義) — to comprehend them without obstruction, and to expound them without obstruction: these are called the "four unobstructed discernments." Therefore, what should rightly be considered the retained result (*shoji-no-ka*, 所持果) ought to be discussed in relation to the two dhāraṇīs of Dharma and meaning. The "Dharma dhāraṇī" (*hō-darani*, 法陀羅尼) is the expressing-teaching (*nōsen-kyō*, 能詮敎); the "meaning dhāraṇī" (*gi-darani*, 義陀羅尼) is the expressed-meaning (*shosen-gi*, 所詮義). Holding these is what is called the "two dhāraṇīs of Dharma and meaning." The "spell dhāraṇī" (*ju-darani*, 呪陀羅尼) refers to words, meanings, passages, and phrases, through which there is efficacious power (*igen*, 威驗). The "patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇī" (*mushōnin-sōji*, 無生忍總持) is such that the substance of patience is itself wisdom (*e*, 慧). Therefore, one directly looks to only these two kinds (the spell and patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇīs). The unobstructed discernments cannot be said to be the "retained fruit." Accordingly, the *Shōshaku* interprets: "The unobstructed understanding of speech is subsumed within the Dharma dhāraṇī; the unobstructed understanding of expression belongs to the two dhāraṇīs of Dharma and meaning" — and does not discuss (the unobstructed discernments) in relation to the spell and patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇīs. If one discusses this from a more distant perspective, (the four unobstructed discernments) can also be considered retained fruits by virtue of the spell and patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇīs. The interpretation of the present commentary should be understood in this sense. However, regarding the interpretation of the *Xuanzan* (*Gensan*, 玄贊) — if one relies on the two interpretations of the *Shōshaku*, the matter does not constitute the difficulty raised in the objection.

**Further inquiry:** Consulting the *Xuanzan* properly from beginning to end: "The dhāraṇīs are of four kinds. In order to attain the four unobstructed understandings of Dharma, meaning, expression, and speech,

### [Lower Section]

the four (dhāraṇīs) are explained in sequence (etc.)." The intent (of this passage) is that the four kinds of dhāraṇī are explained in sequence as four kinds of dhāraṇī in order to attain the four unobstructed understandings of Dharma, meaning, expression, and speech. Furthermore, (the text) clearly indicates that "the four kinds of dhāraṇī take the four unobstructed understandings as their retained fruit (as observed)." The two interpretations of the *Shōshaku* appear to contradict the actual text (*genbun*, 現文) of the *Xuanzan*. What is to be said?

**Reconciling response:** Bōkyō (*Puyang*) is the foremost disciple (*jōsoku*, 上足) of Zizhou (*Sishū*, 淄洲), and on the matter of knowing the true intent of the *Xuanzan*, one should rely on the *Shōshaku*. First, regarding the intent of the initial interpretation: (the *Xuanzan*) heads (the passage) with "the dhāraṇīs are of four kinds," and it appears that (the text) should set forth the *hōtai* (*fǎtǐ*, 法體) — the substance of the dharma — of the retaining dhāraṇī. Yet (instead) it raises the unobstructed (understandings of) expression and speech. From this one knows that the text of the *Xuanzan* contains an error. Correctly, it should enumerate "Dharma, meaning, spell, and patience-of-non-arising." Moreover, looking at the passage immediately below, (the *Xuanzan*) sets forth the four dhāraṇīs of Dharma, meaning, spell, and patience-of-non-arising in answer to (the question). It is known that what is raised in the question should be the four dhāraṇīs (Dharma, meaning, spell, patience-of-non-arising). This is a case where (the text) raises the fruit, yet the beginning and end do not accord. Therefore, could the fact that the *Xuanzan* places the two characters "for attaining" (*i-toku*, 爲得) also be an error? As for the intent of the next interpretation: even though the *Xuanzan* does not directly call (the four unobstructed understandings) "the fruit," because there is the aspect of their being (treated) as fruit, it comprehensively designates the four dhāraṇīs as the "retaining" (*nōji*, 能持) and the four unobstructed understandings as the "retained fruit" (*shoji-ka*, 所持果). The *Shōshaku*, probing the intent (of the *Xuanzan*), interprets it so that the correct direct fruits are: "the unobstructed understanding of speech is the fruit of the Dharma dhāraṇī; expression (together with) Dharma and meaning are the fruit of the two dhāraṇīs." Truly, the interpretive intent of Bōkyō may be considered most profound.

**The *Xuanzan*, fascicle ten, states:** "Question: The dhāraṇīs are of four kinds. In order to attain the four unobstructed understandings of Dharma, meaning, expression, and speech, the four are explained in sequence. What does this explanation refer to? Answer: In the long prose passages (*jōgyō*, 長行) above, (there are) the dhāraṇīs of Dharma and meaning. In this present (passage), there is the spell dhāraṇī, which is made clear in order to protect the preceding two (Dharma and meaning dhāraṇīs). There is (no mention of) attaining the patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇī (etc.)."

**The *Shōshaku*, fascicle four, states:** "'The dhāraṇīs are of four kinds, namely Dharma, meaning, etc.' — Now, the four dhāraṇīs are: (1) Dharma (*hō*, 法), (2) meaning (*gi*, 義), (3) spell (*ju*, 呪), (4) (that which) enables the bodhisattva to attain the patience of non-arising (*mushōnin*, 無生忍). Regarding what the commentary (*疏*) establishes as 'expression and speech' (*bensai-shi*, 辨才詞), there are two positions. First: (it is) an error in the commentary, because expression (*bensai*, 辨才) and speech (*shi*, 詞) are not dhāraṇīs. Second: (it is) not an error, because the unobstructed understanding of speech (*shi-muge-ge*) is subsumed under the Dharma dhāraṇī, and the unobstructed understanding of expression (*ben-muge-ge*) is comprehensively collected within the Dharma and meaning dhāraṇīs — hence it raises them (etc.)."
