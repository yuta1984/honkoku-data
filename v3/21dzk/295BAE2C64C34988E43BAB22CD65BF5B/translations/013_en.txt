### [Upper Section]

(Continued from previous page) If there were no basis (*shoye*) for the ground of clouds (*un-chi*), by what (criterion) would one distinguish (the heavens)? Hence it is known that even in the fourth dhyāna (*dai-shi-jōryo*), there must necessarily be a ground of clouds as a basis. However, regarding the name "Cloudless" (*muun*): the present commentary's interpreter (seems to be saying that) although the ground of clouds exists, since the karmic fruition (*kahō*) of those heavenly beings is superior, it is not associated with the existence of clouds [Colophon note: (Editor's note): Below this there is probably a missing character or characters], and hence it is interpreted as "named 'Cloudless.'" The statement "abiding apart from clouds" (*riunkyō*) carries this meaning. If there were no ground of clouds whatsoever, one should simply say "no ground of clouds." The expression "apart from clouds" (*riun*) conversely appears to imply the existence of a ground of clouds.

Raising a further objection: Even if there were no ground of clouds, how could there be no distinction (*sabetsu*) in the dwelling places of the heavens? In the formless realm (*mushiki-kai*), there is no ground of clouds, yet terms such as "the realm of infinite space" (*kūmuhen-sho*) are used, and dwelling places are cited. Moreover, distinct locations can be established on this basis. How (should this be understood)?

Resolving the objection: The formless realm, having no material form (*gyōshiki*), can differentiate (its levels) through the superiority or inferiority of their births (*shō*), even without a ground of clouds. The form realm (*shiki-kai*) is different from this. If the form realm has no ground of clouds, by what (means) could one distinguish it?

One person (this represents the synthesizing interpretation) says: Although there may be no coarse (*ara*) ground of clouds, there can be a subtle (*misai*) ground of clouds. The name "apart from clouds" (*riun*) may be established according to this understanding. According to the interpretation in the *Fayuan zhulin* (*Hōon Jurin*, "Forest of Gems in the Dharma Garden"), the formless realm is named as it is because it lacks coarse material form, yet it is interpreted as having subtle material form. This may be taken as an analogous precedent. Furthermore, "the formless realm has no fixed location, yet by virtue of birth there are four kinds" etc. (etc.) — this may be found in the *Kyōron Tsūyō-shō* where it is cited and collated.

**Question:** In the passage of the sūtra that clarifies how the *dharmakāya* (*hosshin*) mutually contains (*sōshō*) all *dharmas* (*ichisai shohō*): when it says "all *dharmas*," can the *dharmakāya* be included?

**(Advancing a position):** When the sūtra says "all *dharmas*," it does not include the *dharmakāya* (etc.).

Regarding this: Since it already says "all *dharmas*," why does it not include the *dharmakāya*? Furthermore, when it says "*dharmas* of the *dharmakāya*" (*shohosshin*), it includes all *dharmas* — so when it says "all *dharmas*," why does it not include the *dharmakāya*?

### [Lower Section]

([Colophon note:] Seion raised this question at the four-season lectures.)

**Personal view:** Characteristics (*sō*) and nature (*shō*) are neither one nor different (*fuchi-fui*). If one proceeds from the perspective of the "not-different" (*fui*) aspect, the *dharmakāya* and all *dharmas* can mutually contain each other. However, regarding this particular sūtra passage, the commentary interprets it as: "Because the true-suchness (*shinnyō*) principle-body (*rike*) pervades and is constant, it contains the *dharmas*. The remainder is the reverse of this, and therefore does not contain suchness (*nyorai*/*nyoryo*)" (etc.). The meaning is: when the *dharmakāya* proceeds from the perspective of the "not-different" aspect, it pervades the *dharmas*. Already saying "it pervades" — what *dharma* would it not contain? Next, when it says "all conditioned *dharmas*" (*ui ichisai hō*), proceeding from the perspective of the "not-one" (*fuitsu*) aspect: conditioned (dharmas) have no quality of universal pervasion and permanence (*henjo*). How could they contain the *dharmakāya*? Restating the intent: the *dharmakāya*, by the principle that its essence depends upon and gathers (the *dharmas*) — pervades the *dharmas* and reveals itself as the "not-different" aspect, and therefore contains the *dharmas*. When conditioned (dharmas) are called "all *dharmas*," although the "not-one" aspect is revealed, the quality of pervasion (*hen no gi*) is not made clear; hence the two are not the same.

**Question:** The sūtra text says: "If one wishes and desires to seek the three (higher) knowledges (*san'myō*), the six supernormal powers (*rokku*), the śrāvaka (vehicle), and the pratyekabuddha (vehicle)" (etc.). In that case, among the *vibhāgaja-pratyekabuddhas* (*bugyō-dokugaku*, pratyekabuddhas who live within a group), are there those who have attained liberation through wisdom alone (*echidatsu-sha*)?

**Both sides (presented):** If (such persons) exist: examining the present sūtra text, above it says "those who wish to seek the fruit of stream-entry (*yoru-ka*)" etc. — among the śrāvakas, those who have attained liberation through wisdom alone (*echa-datsu-sha*) do not eliminate the hindrances to meditation (*jōshō*), and therefore do not attain the foundational meditations (*konpon-jō*). The supernormal powers arising from meditation are therefore absent. Next, "those who wish and desire to seek the three knowledges, six supernormal powers, the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha (vehicles)" etc. — this presents śrāvakas who have attained the six supernormal powers. These are precisely those who have attained liberation through both (wisdom and meditation) (*guchidatsu-sha*). The pratyekabuddha (case) is likewise. In what the sūtra text clarifies regarding the śrāvakas, although those who have attained liberation through wisdom alone are mentioned, regarding the pratyekabuddhas, only those who have attained liberation through both are cited. Hence it is known that among the *vibhāgaja-pratyekabuddhas*, there are no persons who have attained liberation through wisdom alone. Based on this, in the present commentary, it is judged that "although among the solitary pratyekabuddhas (*bugyō-dokugaku*) there are two types of distinction, all of them together

**Question (another side):** Among the *vibhāgaja-pratyekabuddhas*, can there be those who do not attain supernormal powers (*tsū*)?

One person says: The treatise clarifies (that) "among those who attain the cessation meditation (*metsujō*), there are non-learners (*mugaku*) and also learned sages (*ugaku no shōja*)" (etc.). Already having placed the expression "there are" (*u* 有), it is clearly known: it is not the case that all non-learners (attain it); there are those who attain it and those who do not. Those non-learners extend to both śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas. Since among pratyekabuddhas there are already those who do not attain the cessation meditation, these are persons who do not attain the nine successive meditations (*kyū-jishadai-jō*). This type accordingly does not attain supernormal powers. Supernormal powers reside in the foundational (meditation). How could those who do not attain the nine successive meditations give rise to them? Based on the character *u* 有 ("there are") in the phrase "it is said that there are non-learners" (*ii u mugaku*), this meaning is established. Therefore the Master's interpretation (says): "Using the meaning of that text, and taking this present text as the standard, therefore among pratyekabuddhas there are those who do not attain the cessation meditation — that is, among the *vibhāgaja* (pratyekabuddhas), there are also those who do not attain supernormal powers" (etc.). Considering this meaning now: among the *vibhāgaja* (pratyekabuddhas) there are those who attain liberation through wisdom alone (*echidatsu*) and those who attain liberation through both (*guchidatsu*). Those who attain liberation through wisdom alone rely on the *anāgamya* stage (*michi-chi*, the stage just short of the first dhyāna) to sever delusion and realize principle directly, being persons who do not attain foundational meditation at all. The *Sanrin-shō* also has this understanding. Therefore the Master's interpretations in various places maintain the view that among the *vibhāgaja* (pratyekabuddhas) there are persons who have attained liberation through wisdom alone. However, regarding the sūtra text and Zizhou's (*Sōshū*) interpretation, (Zizhou) spoke of this in terms of the majority (*tōbun*); in reality, this type (those who attain liberation through wisdom alone) ought to exist.

**Question:** In the context of the sūtra text's clarification of the repentance of the three karmas (*sango-sange*): can the karma of mind (*igō*) contain a thought that arouses and initiates (the other karmas) (*dōhatsu shōshi*)?

**Both sides (presented):** If not: when one externally gives rise to bodily and verbal (karma), one inwardly moves the mind. In the karma of mind, how could there be no arousing-and-initiating superior thought (*dōhatsu shōshi*)? Based on this, Zizhou the Great Master appears to have interpreted this meaning in various places. ([Colophon note:] Commentary on the *Metsugoshōhon* ("Chapter on Extinguishing Karmic Hindrances"); Commentary on the *Jippō Bosatsu Sandanhon* ("Chapter on the Praises of Bodhisattvas of the Ten Directions"); various passages in the *Ryōgitō* ("Torch of Definitive Meaning").) If one follows this and such is the case (that the karma of mind does contain this thought): in the *Vijñaptimātratā-śāstra* (*Yuishiki-ron*), the two thoughts of deliberation and determination (*shinketsu no ni-shi*) are taken as the starting point (*kyōdō*, "conducting way") of the mental karma, while the arousing-and-initiating superior thought (*dōhatsu shōshi*) is related to the two karmas of body and speech. Furthermore, in the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* (*Yuga-ron*), "the thought (*shi*) that does not give rise to body and speech is named mental karma" (etc.). The understanding in the *Yoga* and *Vijñaptimātratā* (texts) appears to show that the karma of mind does not contain an arousing-and-initiating superior thought. Neither side is clear. How (should this be understood)?

**Personal view:** As the objection states, when one externally gives rise to bodily and verbal (karma), one inwardly moves the mind — the karma of mind should properly contain an arousing-and-initiating superior thought. Hence in the *Ryōgitō* ("Torch of Definitive Meaning") (at the first passage of homage), (it is written): "Now, based on principle, the mind can also have an arousing-and-initiating superior thought; because it is called 'moving the mind,' and because there is non-greed (*mu-ton*) and so forth" (etc.). Is not this interpretation clear? Likewise, the present commentary's clarification that "the arousing-and-initiating thought (*dōhatsu-shi*) properly gives rise to the three karmas" (etc.) is of the same meaning. Furthermore, the commentary on the *Jippō Bosatsu Sandanhon* (chapter), (interpreting) "that which gives rise to body and speech is precisely this mental karma" — does it not judge that "the arousing-and-initiating superior thought (*dōhatsu shōshi*) that gives rise to body and speech is precisely the karma of mind"? However, in the treatise-texts (*rombun*), since (the subject) is explained as extending across the three vehicles (*sanjō*), the expression "arousing-and-initiating superior thought of mental karma" is not used. Next, in the *Yoga* and *Vijñaptimātratā* teachings: since the arousing-and-initiating superior thought that gives rise to body and speech simultaneously moves the mind and is therefore named "mental karma," there would arise confusion in the matter. Because this is concealed: "the thought at the time of not giving rise to body and speech, being free from confusion and unambiguously called 'mental karma,'" — only that is singled out for clarity. It does not mean that the arousing-and-initiating superior thought that gives rise to body and speech is blocked from being understood as mental karma.
