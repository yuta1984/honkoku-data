**Question:** In the sūtra's clarification of the ten perfections (*jūhāramitsu*, *daśa-pāramitā*), is the sixth, *prajñā-pāramitā*, limited exclusively to the fundamental wisdom (*konpon-chi*, *mūla-jñāna*, i.e., non-discriminating wisdom), or does it extend to something beyond this?

**It is advanced:** If it is limited to fundamental wisdom, then looking at the explicit text of the sūtra, where *prajñā-pāramitā* is clarified and it states "(one who) yearns for the profound Dharma of all Buddhas and Tathāgatas and elucidates the method of penetrating the five sciences (*go-myō*, *pañca-vidyā*)," it becomes clear that it extends to subsequently-attained wisdom (*gotoku-chi*, *pṛṣṭhalabdha-jñāna*) as well. Based on this, the Great Master of Zizhou (Zhizhou, 知周) interprets it as extending to subsequently-attained wisdom. However, if that is so, then in the system of ten perfections, the sixth should be limited exclusively to fundamental wisdom. If it extends to subsequently-attained wisdom, in what way does it differ from the tenth, *jñāna-pāramitā* (the perfection of wisdom/knowledge)? Furthermore, the *Vijñaptimātratā-śāstra* (*Yuishiki-ron*) states: "The sixth encompasses only non-discriminating wisdom (*mufunbetsu-chi*)" (etc.). How should this be understood? **Question:** The sūtra text states "(one who) gives rise to (*hoki*) the ignorance (*mumyō*, *avidyā*) of various (*shuju*) karmic acts (*gyō*, *karman*)" (etc.). In that case, is this the ignorance within the obstruction to knowables (*shochi-shō*, *jñeyāvaraṇa*)?

**It is advanced:** The commentary (*疏*) states: "Among the two (kinds of) folly that are cut off at the second ground, this is the folly of various karmic destinies (*shuju-gyōshu-gu*)" (etc.). Therefore this is the ignorance of the obstruction to knowables (*shochi-shō*). Regarding this: the obstruction to knowables is not a "binding dharma (*baku-hō*, *bandha-dharma*)." How can it give rise to karmic acts (*gyō*)? Based on this, the *Vijñaptimātratā-śāstra* (Fascicle Eight) states: "It has no function of generating karma (*hatsu-gō*) or moistening birth (*junjō*)" (etc.). The obstruction to knowables definitely has no function of generating karma or moistening birth. Therefore the commentary's interpretation is unclear. How should this be understood?

**Personal view:** This matter is a question-and-answer by the commentary master (*shushu*, the author of the *疏*). In answer, (the commentary) states: "With respect to not being able to generate karma that brings about (*maneku*) birth in the various realms (*kai-shu*, *dhātu-gati*) (through) differentially-ripening karmic fruit (*ijuku-ka*, *vipākaphala*), it is said 'does not generate karma.' As for not being able to bring about the three karmic acts (*sanyō*) in general, this obstruction to knowables is not (one that) cannot generate (such karma)..."

"...the fruits are the two fruits of outflow (*tōryu-ka*, *niṣyanda-phala*) and dominance (*zōjō-ka*, *adhipati-phala*)" (etc.). The meaning is: that the obstruction to knowables does not generate karma means it does not generate the karma that brings about (*maneku*) the differentially-ripening fruit (*ijuku-ka*) of birth in the three realms and five destinies (*sangai-goshu*, *traidhātuka-pañcagati*). **Question:** The sūtra text (mentions) "At that time, the Bodhisattva Lion-Aspect-Unobstructed-Radiance-Flame (*Shishi-sō-muge-kōen-bosatsu*, 師子相無礙光焰菩薩)" (etc.). On account of what superior meritorious qualities did he receive this name?

**It is advanced:** The commentary states: "Because he has attained the four unobstructed (wisdoms) (*shi-muge*, *catur-pratisaṃvid*) of the bodhisattva, he is named 'Lion-Aspect (*Shishi-sō*).' Because he is separated from obstructions, he is named 'Unobstructed (*Muge*).' Because he has attained sovereign wisdom (*chi-no-jizai*) and can illuminate both ultimate and conventional truths, he is named 'Radiance-Flame (*Kōen*)'" (etc.). Regarding this: all bodhisattvas of deep stages (*jinmi-i*) are all endowed with these meritorious qualities. Why does only this (bodhisattva) receive this name? Furthermore, which ground (*chi-i*, *bhūmi*) does this bodhisattva's name represent? Attaining the four unobstructed (wisdoms) is the superior capacity of the ninth ground. The term "Unobstructed (*Muge*)" represents the ninth ground. The epithet "Radiance-Flame (*Kōen*)," (i.e.,) the term for unified contemplation of ultimate and conventional truths, represents the superior capacity of the fifth ground. If so, then in interpreting the present name (of this bodhisattva), the bodhisattva's distinct name is not made clear, and his stage (*chi-i*) is also confused. How could this possibly resolve the original intent of the sūtra text? How should this be understood?

**Personal view:** Establishing a distinct name (*betsu-myō*) within general (qualities) is the regular practice (*jōshū*) of Buddhas and bodhisattvas. Even if any bodhisattva possesses these capacities, what fault is there in establishing a distinct name? However, as for the indication of the ground and stage (*chi-i*), according to the commentary master's intent, it represents the bodhisattva of the ninth ground. The reason for this is: "expressing the attainment of the four unobstructed (wisdoms) and sovereign wisdom" manifests the attainment of the thusness (*shinnyо*, *tathatā*) that serves as the basis for sovereign wisdom (i.e., the thusness of "freedom from bondage and liberation," *mubaku-gedatsu-shinnyo*, realized at the ninth ground). Next, regarding the matter of illuminating ultimate and conventional truths becoming confused with the (fifth or) ninth

**Question:** [Ruby annotation: (Composed by) Shiki no Kunkyō.] (In the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*,) in the clarification of the ten kinds of arousing the aspiration (for awakening, *jisshu-no-hosshin*, *daśa-cittotpāda*): should "the arousing of aspiration before the attainment of the fruit (*mishōka-hosshin*)" be taken to include the stage of resolute understanding (*shōge-gyōchi*, *adhimukticaryā-bhūmi*)?

**It is advanced:** (Looking at the commentary,) the commentary does not appear to include it. Regarding this: the expression "arousing of aspiration before the attainment of the fruit" contains nothing to exclude (certain stages). Why would it not include that stage? Moreover, reading the text of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*, it says: "(from) the stage of resolute understanding (*shōge-gyōchi*) ... through the tenth ground" (etc.). Therefore the commentary's interpretation is unclear. How should this be understood?

**Personal view:** "The arousing of aspiration before the attainment of the fruit" should broadly extend to encompass the stage of resolute understanding, because the text of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* is explicit about this. However, as for the commentary's interpretation, it says: "'The arousing of aspiration before the attainment of the result' means those in the stage of cause (*in-i*, prior to Buddhahood)." The expression "stage of cause (*in-i*)" is broad — why would it exclude that stage? It should encompass all five stages (*gochi*, the five stages of the bodhisattva path).

The *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*, Fascicle Seventy-Two, states: "Furthermore, there are ten kinds of arousing the aspiration (for awakening) ... 'The arousing of aspiration before the attainment of the fruit' means all the aspirations aroused from the stage of resolute understanding through the tenth ground. 'The arousing of aspiration having already attained the fruit' means all the aspirations aroused at the Tathāgata's stage" (etc.).
