**Question:** In the sūtra text (recorded by Shiki-Ryūhan), the "already-unstained dhāraṇī" (*kidenchaku-darani*, 既染着陀羅尼) is clarified as having four kinds of phrasal meanings (*kugī*, 句義). Since Śāriputra raises only the single phrase "not-region/location and not-not-region/location" (*hi-hōsho hi-hi-hōsho*, 非方處非々方處), how does the commentary master interpret this?

**(Preliminary) statement:** Śāriputra awakened to (the matter) partially, while the Buddha awakened to it fully; therefore (Śāriputra) raises and explains only a partial (phrase).

**Objection thereto:** The Venerable Śāriputra, having awakened to the nature and substance (*taishō*, 體性) of the dhāraṇī, raises the phrase "not-being and not-empty" (*hi'u hi-kū*, 非有非空). Regarding the remaining phrasal meanings such as "not-Dharma, not-not-Dharma" (*hi-hō hi-hi-hō*, 非法非々法) and so on — why did he not know and perceive these? Reading the sūtra text accordingly, it is headed above by "just as you have said" (*nyojo-sō setsu*, 如汝所說), and seven kinds of phrasal meanings are enumerated. From this one knows that Śāriputra had also awakened to all seven kinds together — is this not clear from the sūtra text? What is to be said?

**Personal view:** The intent of the initial interpretation is that by raising the first (phrase), (Śāriputra) reveals the remaining six kinds of phrasal meanings — this follows the sūtra text and should be considered the correct meaning. However, the deeper intent of this interpretation is that, while in reality Śāriputra had awakened to all seven phrasal meanings, in order to reveal the difference (between Śāriputra and the Buddha) regarding the full measure (of awakening, *manbun*, 滿分), he provisionally raises just one phrase.

**Question:** In the commentary on the present sūtra, regarding the matter of "citing a passage from the sūtras (*keishō*, 契經) to clarify the turning of the mind (*esin*, 廻心) of those who have attained the four fruits" — is it appropriate to subsume "the number of kalpas required for turning of the mind, such as twenty-thousand kalpas" under "the stage of resolute understanding" (*shōge-gyō-chi*, 勝解行地)? What is to be said?

If (they are so) subsumed: those who turn the mind (toward the bodhisattva path) after eighty-thousand, sixty-thousand, forty-thousand, or twenty-thousand kalpas have not yet entered the incalculable-practice (*sōgi-gyō*, 僧祇行). Since their practice is already not excellent (*shūshō*, 殊勝), how can one say it is subsumed under "the stage of resolute understanding"? Accordingly, in another place (the *Tō* commentary, fascicle 7), it says: "Not (yet at) the stage of resolute understanding, because it has not yet become superior (etc.)."

If, relying on this, they are not (so) subsumed: the present commentary states "subsumed under the resolute practice (stage) (*shōge-gyō*, 勝解行) (etc.)." In that case, both (sides of the argument) are unclear. What is to be said? (Recorded by Shiki-Ryōin.)

**Personal view:** Within "resolute understanding," there can be superior and inferior degrees. Because (these practitioners) have not yet aroused a deep and firm great mind (*jinkoko-daishin*, 深固大心), they have not yet entered the incalculable-practice. If one follows this meaning, one should say (these practitioners are) "not at the stage of resolute understanding." This is what the *Tō* commentary of the ninth fascicle

**Question:** The sūtra text (recorded by Shiki-Seiu) says "not-region/location and not-not-region/location (etc.)." If so, among the three natures — the imagined (*henge*, 遍計), the dependent-arising (*eta*, 依他), and the perfected (*enjō*, 円成) — should each have the meaning of "not-region/location and not-not-region/location"? (Both sides of the question are posed.)

If the three natures each have this meaning: "not-region/location" (*hi-hōsho*) is the meaning of "not-being" (*hi'u*, 非有), and "not-not-region/location" (*hi-hi-hōsho*) is the meaning of "not-empty" (*hi-kū*, 非空). Yet the nature and substance of the imagined (*henge-taishō*, 遍計體性) is entirely non-existent (*tsuyu nashi*, 都無) as a dharma, so it is completely impossible for it to have the meaning of "not-empty." And since the dependent-arising and the perfected have dharmas that do exist (*ari*, 體), how can they have the meaning of "not-being"? Consulting accordingly the text of the *Madhyānta-vibhāga* (*Chūhen-ron*, 中辺論): "Imaginary discrimination (*kōmō-funbetsu*, 虛妄分別) exists; in these two (— being and nothingness —) it is entirely absent," and so forth — stating this, and by contrasting the three natures, it clarifies "the purport of not-empty and not-being." Likewise, the *Vijñaptimātratā-śāstra* judges "self and dharma are not-being; the empty consciousness is not non-existent," and does not argue the middle way (*chūdō*, 中道) only on the level of a single dharma (*ichi-hō*, 一法). If, relying on this, (these meanings) are absent (from the individual natures), then upon the two natures of the dependent-arising and the perfected, both meanings of emptiness (*kū*, 空) and being (*u*, 有) are jointly present, warding off the two attachments of increase (*zōjō*, 增) and decrease (*genjō*, 減), thereby conforming to the purport of the middle way. But if one retains even a single side of "being" (*u no ippin*, 有一邊), how could the attachment-mind not increase? Thus, in the present commentary, it appears that all three natures are seen as jointly possessing the meaning of "not-region/location and not-not-region/location."

**Personal view:** Regarding "the middle way in a single dharma" (*ichi-hō-chūdō*, 一法中道), there is a doctrinal dispute between one's own school and other schools. The intent of one's own school permits (the middle way in a single dharma). The intent of the other school — that is, the Dharma-characteristics school (*Hossō-shū*, 法相宗) — does not permit "the middle way in a single dharma." Within one's own school's "middle way in a single dharma," even though there are divergent opinions among scholars of Dharma-characteristics, provisionally maintaining one position: in general, regarding the "middle way," there are two levels.

Namely, "the conventionally-expressed middle way" (*eison-chūdō*, 依詮中道) and "the middle way beyond language" (*rigen-chūdō*, 離言中道). "The conventionally-expressed middle way" means that nature (*shō*, 性) and characteristics (*sō*, 相) are neither identical nor different — like the skin and the flesh (of fruit) — because: just as the imagined-nature (*henge-shoshū*, 遍計所執), similarly (those) who, upon the dependent-arising nature, wrongly cling to it as really-existent, take the three natures as a single dharma, and thereby establish the middle way comprehensively (*sōji*, 總じて) upon the three natures. This shows that even though the three natures are individually distinct, one is not saying that the middle way is established (separately) upon each individual separate dharma. Why then do (commentators) such as Daocheng (*Dōshō*, 道詮) say that "(the Dharma-characteristics school) does not permit the middle way in a single dharma"? If that is the case, the accounts in the two treatises, the *Madhyānta-vibhāga* and the *Vijñaptimātratā-śāstra*, are the most important evidence for the middle way in a single dharma.

**Further inquiry:** When the three natures that are established together form a single dharma, the middle way is established. But if one speaks of them separately as "the three natures," does one not establish it? Now, the present commentary has each of the three natures individually, and establishes "not-region/location etc." upon the single dharma of the imagined-nature (*henge*, 遍計) — does this not differ from the position maintained in the lecture's answer?

**Reconciling response:** In the intent of one's own school, the three natures are neither one nor different. It is utterly impossible to separate (any one of) the three natures from (the others). How can one even speak of "when the three natures are separated"? It is said that one establishes (the middle way) comprehensively upon these (mutually interpenetrating) three natures. Therefore, the intent of the present commentary — even when saying that "not-region/location etc." is established upon the imagined-nature (*henge*) — is so because (the imagined-nature) does not depart from the dependent-arising and perfected natures; and likewise, even when saying that (not-region/location etc.) is present in the perfected-nature (*enjō*, 円成), it is because (the perfected-nature) does not depart from the imagined-nature. The intent of the commentary is in accord with the position (maintained).

Alternatively, the intent of the present sūtra is to establish "the middle way beyond language" (*rigen-chūdō*, 離言中道). "The middle way beyond language" means expounding that "all dharmas cannot be expressed in language" (*shoho-fukasetsu*, 諸法不可言), because the meaning of "cannot be expressed in language" (*fukaigen no gi*, 不可言義) is present in each of the three natures respectively. The commentary's interpretation thus reveals the "beyond language" (aspect).

**Question:** The sūtra text expounds the "unstained dhāraṇī" (*musen'jaku-darani*, 無染著陀羅尼). Now, within this, at what stage (*chi*, 地) does the "patience-of-non-substantiality dhāraṇī" (*mushō-nin-sōji*, 無性忍總持) confer its benefit?

**(Preliminary) statement:** The commentary cites the text of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* (*Yuga-ron*, 瑜伽論) and judges: "Attained before entering the stages (*chizen-shotoku*, 地前所得) (etc.)."

**Objection thereto:**

Consulting the sūtra text according to the interpretation of Zizhou (*Sishū*, 淄州): "These bodhisattvas — the practices they cultivate — (are) what the bodhisattvas of the past upheld and received — this is the mother of bodhisattvas (etc.)." Since it already says "practices cultivated by bodhisattvas," must these not necessarily be the practices cultivated by bodhisattvas who have entered the stages (*chijō*, 地上)? Accordingly, in the *Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra* (*Jinshin-kyō*, 深密經) it says: "At the third stage one attains the dhāraṇī of retaining what is heard (*monji-darani*, 聞持陀羅尼); at the ninth stage one attains the four kinds of dhāraṇī (*shishu-sōji*, 四種總持) (etc.)." Accordingly, is the intent of the present sūtra the same as the *Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra* — that the four kinds of dhāraṇī are all to be attained at the stage level (*chijō*, 地上)? On what basis is the interpretation "attained before the stages" (*chizen-shotoku*, 地前所得) made? (Recorded by Shiki-Haruu.)

**Personal view:** In general, following what is commonly taught, the "patience-of-non-arising dhāraṇī" (*mushōnin-sōji*, 無生忍總持) should be attained at the stage level. However, regarding the present commentary's interpretation: the intent of fascicle forty-five of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* (瑜伽論) describes the "patience dhāraṇī" (*nin-darani*, 忍陀羅尼) as follows: "These bodhisattvas — not long (from now), they shall attain the pure and superior intention (*jōshō-iryaku*, 淨勝意樂); (they have) already attained (this) by relying on the superior patience (*shōnin*, 勝忍) of the superior stage of resolute understanding (*shōge-gyō-chi*, 勝解行地) of the higher grade (etc.)." This passage appears to clarify "attained before the stages." Following the text, the commentary master judges the *Yogācārabhūmi* passage as "(referring to) attainment before the stages," and speaking in terms of the actual reality, (also) reconciles (the matter) by saying "if one measures by degrees of excellence, completeness, and deficiency, the stage of attainment is not necessarily fixed." This is said in the manner it is said because of that text. It is not saying, generally speaking, that (the attainment) is limited to before the stages.
