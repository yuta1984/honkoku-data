**Question:** In the sūtra, when the four Buddhas came to the chamber of Myōdō (Bodhisattva Supratiṣṭhita) and expounded the principle of Śākyamuni's great longevity, they transformed the chamber into a Pure Land (etc.). In that case, was the transformation of the chamber into a Pure Land accomplished through the supernatural power (*jinriki*) of Śākyamuni, or through the supernatural transformation (*shinpen*) of the four Buddhas?

**Response (Answer):** The commentary offers two interpretations, holding that (the reading that) the four Buddhas transformed it to be superior (etc.).

Regarding this: when a guest Buddha (*kyakubutsu*) comes to visit, it is all the more fitting that the host Buddha (*shubutsu*) should bring about the transformation. "Through the awesome power of the Buddha, that chamber instantly became vast, magnificent, and purified" (etc.) — this is unambiguous in the sūtra text, is it not? (May it not be seen as) the supernatural power of Śākyamuni?

Regarding this: Vaiśravaṇa (Tamon Ten, the Heavenly King of Abundant Hearing) is a king of the desire-realm heavens, and represents a superior fruition of the good destiny (*zenshu*). **Question:** (The) Lifespan Chapter (*Juryō-hon*). "(The sūtra states:) 'I constantly abide on Vulture Peak, proclaiming this precious sūtra'" (etc.). Among the three bodies (*sanshin*), which body is being referred to?

**Response (Answer):** The commentary states: "the two bodies of Dharma and Reward (*hō-hō nishin*)" (etc.).

Regarding this: although the three bodies are neither one nor different, the things that their reality (*ji*) and principle (*ri*), essence (*tai*) and form (*sō*), take shape as are different. The Dharma Body (*hosshin*) is the formless true principle (*shinri*), with no form or color. The Reward Body (*hōshin*) is a physical body (*shikishin*) that pervades everywhere, but does not preach the sūtra teachings. Therefore one understands that it is not the two bodies of Dharma and Reward. Based on this, a Korean (Silla) master said "it is not the two bodies of Dharma and Reward" (etc.). How is this to be understood?

**Personal note:** The present text fully describes the characteristics of the three bodies. "I constantly abide on Vulture Peak, proclaiming this precious sūtra" — this expounds the two bodies of Dharma and Reward; "(In order to) bring sentient beings to completion, the manifestation of final nirvāṇa (*parinirvāṇa*)" — this speaks of the Transformation Body (*keshinshin*). If this text does not expound the two bodies, there would be the fault of the three bodies not being complete. Having already said "I constantly abide on Vulture Peak," this clearly states the two bodies of Dharma and Reward (as can be seen). Just as the Lotus Sūtra says "I constantly abide in this Sahā world" and states "constantly dwelling on Vulture Peak" — this is what is called the Reward Body. However, as for the objection: the commentary states "(the text) uses the Reward Body to manifest the Dharma Body." The meaning is that since the Dharma Body is the fundamental root (*konpon*) of the Reward Body, the preaching of the Dharma by the Reward Body for the benefit of others (*tajuyū*) fundamentally arises from this (Dharma Body).
