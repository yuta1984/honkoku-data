## Saishō Mondō Shō, Volume One

[Note] The original manuscript has no internal title for each volume. I have privately supplied this below, following the same format.

### Volume One

**Question:** Regarding the causes for the arising of this sūtra, and specifically the particular causes (*betsu-kiin*): how does Zichou Dashi (the great master of Zizhou, i.e., Jingmai) explain this?

**Response (Answer):** The commentary says, "By 'particular' (*betsu*) is meant that which is specific to this teaching" (etc.). Regarding this, when one looks at the six particular causes listed below — namely "to remove and liberate from the causes and effects of unwholesomeness, and to bring about the blissful fruit of humans and gods" — this meaning is found universally in all Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna teachings. How can it be limited to this sūtra alone?

**Question:** Regarding the particular causes for the arising of this sūtra: how does Zichou Dashi explain this?

**Response:** The particular causes are limited solely to this sūtra, namely: the Chapter on the Lifespan of the Tathāgata (*Nyorai jūryō hon*), the merits of the Three Bodies, and the Chapter on the Elimination of Karmic Hindrances (*Metsugōshō hon*). Regarding this: the teaching of confession through phenomena and principle (*jiri sange*) to eliminate the hindrances of sin comes from the Contemplation of Samantabhadra Sūtra (*Fugenkan gyō*). The Tathāgata's lifespan and the merits of the Three Bodies come from the Lifespan Chapter of the Lotus Sūtra. Why, then, is such a judgment made?

**Question:** Regarding the division of the general preface (*tsū-jo*), the specific preface (*betsu-jo*), and the separate preface (*betsu-jo*) by means of the Seven Perfections: how is the second perfection, the Samādhi Perfection (*zanmai enman*), to be judged?

**Response:** The first (Preface Perfection) and the third (Assembly Perfection) are called the "general preface," also called the "preface of attestation" (*shōshin-jo*). The rest are the specific preface (etc.). Regarding this: considering the two types of preface — general and specific — what is common to other sūtras is called the "general preface," and what is limited to this sūtra is called the "specific preface." However, in other sūtras such as the Lotus and the Renwang (Benevolent Kings), there are also events like entering samādhi. These should most properly be called "general preface." How then are they called "specific preface"? Moreover, the third Congregation Perfection is already a general preface. Why is the second (Samādhi Perfection) not so? Based on this, do not the Chinese masters judge that from "Thus have I heard" down to "supremely wonderful Dharma" is the general preface, and from "At that time, the Bhagavat" onward is the specific preface?

In this sūtra's understanding, within the phrase "Thus have I heard... supremely wonderful Dharma," the Samādhi Perfection is included. It is for this reason that such an objection is raised.

**Private note:** Consulting an existing commentary, it says: "The entering-into-samādhi (*nyūjō*) in sūtras such as the Lotus and the Renwang is only partial, not extensive. Therefore it is called the specific preface" (etc.).

Furthermore: The commentary clarifies the particular causes and presents six reasons. First: it clarifies the undefiled fruit (*muro no ka*) of bodhi and nirvāṇa. Second: it clarifies the removal of unwholesome causes. Third: it liberates from the retribution of unwholesome deeds. Fourth: it leads to cultivation of the undefiled causes of bodhi and nirvāṇa. Fifth: it leads to cultivation of defiled (*uro*) wholesome causes. Sixth: it leads to the attainment of the blissful fruit of humans and gods (etc.). However, other sūtras do not fully clarify all six of these causes. Therefore it is specifically with regard to this sūtra that one speaks of "particular causes of arising."

**Further doubt:** The commentary, in explaining the sūtra text, takes from "Thus have I heard" to "supremely wonderful Dharma" as the "general preface," and judges what follows "At that time, the Bhagavat" as the "inciting preface" (*hossoki-jo*). This commentary has already explained the Samādhi Perfection as part of the general preface, has it not?

**Private note:** This commentary is indeed difficult to reconcile. However, since the Samādhi Perfection is located in the middle, between the Preface Perfection and the Congregation Perfection, following the context before and after, it is provisionally judged in this way.

**Further inquiry:** Why is the Samādhi Perfection placed before the Congregation Perfection? Since both the Preface Perfection and the Congregation Perfection are general prefaces, they should alike come first.

**Private reconciliation:** This matter is addressed in the commentary's own question-and-answer section. The commentary answers it as follows: because both the dwelling of body and the dwelling of mind are equally called "dwelling."

**Question:** In the commentary, in clarifying the benefits obtained from this sūtra, the benefit of the Purification of the Dharma Eye (*hōganjō*) is presented. Among Mahāyāna and Hīnayāna benefits, to which does this belong?

**Response:** The commentary presents two interpretations, of which the latter is superior. It judges the interpretation that calls it a Hīnayāna benefit to be superior (etc.). Regarding this: since the present sūtra is a definitive teaching of the Middle Way (*chūdō ryōgi no kyō*), the benefits listed should certainly be Mahāyāna benefits. What basis is there for saying it is a Hīnayāna benefit? Moreover, in the Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra (*Shōgon ron*), it is described as a Mahāyāna benefit (etc.). Following this, the benefit obtained from the present sūtra should be a Mahāyāna benefit. How then (can it be said otherwise)?

**Private note:** Consulting the sūtra text and examining the benefits obtained, the Mahāyāna benefits are stated as: "arousing the aspiration for unsurpassed bodhi (*mujō bodaishin*), and receiving the prophecy (*ki*) of *anuttarā-samyak-saṃbodhi*." Furthermore, following what is stated in the Kūman願品(Void-fulfillment Chapter), where outside of the benefit of the insight into non-arising (*mushō hōnin*), the benefit of the purification of the Dharma Eye is cited — it becomes clear that this is not a Mahāyāna benefit. Also, consulting the explanation of the Wonderful Sūtra (*Myōkyō*, i.e., the Lotus Sūtra) on Mahāyāna benefits: "attaining unsurpassed bodhi, attaining non-arising patience (*mushōnin*), attaining the *dhāraṇī* of giving" — these are each distinct from one another (as observed). For this reason, what is stated in the Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra (*Yuga ron*) as the benefit of the first fruit — this is where Zichou's esteemed view may have originated. The present sūtra is a teaching that pervades all three vehicles. Does it mean that what is expounded is limited to Mahāyāna benefits? **Question:** The sūtra text says, "Among all dharmas, one attains the Purification of the Dharma Eye" (etc.). Therefore, is this benefit of the Purification of the Dharma Eye obtained in the Pure Land? [Two positions.] If it is obtained in the Pure Land, then the Xuanzan (Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi commentary) describes it as a Hīnayāna benefit. Hīnayāna practitioners do not obtain benefits in the Pure Land. How can one say so? If, on the other hand, one says it is obtained in the defiled land (*edo*), then according to one's own school, in the chapters from Bhaiṣajyarāja onward, the lands in which beings dwell are all Pure Lands. We can therefore know that it is obtained in the Pure Land. Moreover, if this is so, does it not go against the spirit of the other school (*tasshū*)?

**Question:** The commentary on this sūtra presents the benefit of the Purification of the Dharma Eye. Is this benefit limited to the benefit of the first fruit (*shoha*, i.e., *srotāpatti*) of the Hīnayāna? [Two positions.] If it is limited to the first fruit: realizing the true principle of the Dharma-nature (*hōshō shinnri*) and thereby attaining the undefiled wisdom-eye (*muro egan*) is called "Purification of the Dharma Eye." Since later stages (*gogo*) already have this meaning too, how can one say it is limited to the first fruit? Based on this, the Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra discusses this as applying to all four fruits (*shika*), showing that it extends to the later stages as well. Other treatises likewise say the same. If so, then how is it that the present commentary and the Xuanzan both interpret it as limited to the first fruit?

**A certain person says:** Regarding the benefit of the Purification of the Dharma Eye: first cutting off the kleśas of discrimination (*funbetsu-waku*) is called "attaining" (the Dharma Eye).
