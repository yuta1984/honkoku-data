**Personal view:** Seen in broad outline, the objection seems plausible. But when one reads the commentary text carefully, there is no contradiction between earlier and later parts of the interpretation. Regarding the seven meanings such as "region/location" (*hōsho*, 方處) — when the two truths, ultimate and conventional, are placed in mutual contrast, the principle of the true suchness (*shinnyo no ri*, 眞如理) applies to both gates of negation (*sha*, 遮) and affirmation (*hyō*, 表). That is, "negating 'region/location' and calling it 'not region/location'" still results in (the subject) being cast (*被せられる*) as "being non-Dharma-location (*hi-hōsho*)" — this is the case of the gate of "non-identity" (*fuitsu-mon*, 不一門) when ultimate and conventional are placed in opposition. If, on the other hand, one proceeds according to the "gate of non-difference" (*fui-mon*, 不異門), then ultimate and conventional truth have a side (*hen*, 辺) that is one; therefore, "not being a region/location" is negated, and "not-not being a region/location" (*hi-hi-hōsho*, 非々方處) is in turn affirmed — because (the ultimate truth) is not different from the conventional "region/location," and therefore "the meaning of region/location" applies. The sūtra text expounds the side of negation without revealing the side of affirmation, but Zizhou (*Sishū*, 淄州 — i.e., the commentator Yixing 一行) reveals this, producing an interpretation (*shakusei*, 釋成) within the two gates of non-identity and non-difference.

Therefore, the commentary's lower passage stating "based on ultimate truth (*shōgi*, 勝義), (this) negates 'region/location' and affirms 'not being a region/location'" interprets the two gates of negation and affirmation within the gate of non-identity (*fuitsu-mon*). In the commentary's upper passage, "non-not-region/location (*hi-hi-hōsho*) is not empty (*kū*, 空)" means: in the gate of non-difference, phenomenal characteristics (*jisō*, 事相) have "region/location" and so are not empty; and since the true principle does not depart from them, there is "region/location" and (things are) not empty — this negates "not being a region/location" and also reveals "the affirmation of the side of non-not-region/location." Thus, the commentary's statement "if one takes (the matter) according to ultimate-of-ultimates (*shōgi-shōgi*, 勝義々々), the seven phrases all negate" interprets the negating side of the two gates of non-identity and non-difference, while the subsequent "comprehensively taking both negation and affirmation" interprets the two meanings of negation and affirmation within the two gates.

**Personal view:** The *Vijñaptimātratā-śāstra*'s statement — "depending on the newly arisen — the seeds of this consciousness, drawn by karma, with differentiated functional capacity, with a determined duration of abiding — the life-faculty is provisionally established (etc.)" — means: *nāma*-seeds (*myōgenshu*, 名言種子) drawn by karma, giving rise to the eighth consciousness (*daihachi-shiki*, 第八識), in causing its duration of abiding (*jūji-bun*, 住時分) to be determined — this (process) is what is called "establishing the life-faculty." Now, "prolonging the lifespan" means extending (what has been) determined as the duration of abiding of the eighth consciousness. This is wholly a matter of *nāma*-seeds drawn by prior karma. *Nāma*-seeds newly熏-implanted in the present are not drawn by prior karma, and do not have the power to determine the duration of abiding and thereby extend it. How then can they serve as the basis for establishing the life-faculty?

As for the interpretation (of the passage) "or depending solely on new (seeds)" — (the text) says that the life-faculty is established by (depending on) newly熏-implanted (*shinkun*, 新薰) seeds, and does not say "new seeds熏-implanted in the present." These (in question) are newly熏-implanted seeds (*shinkunshuji*, 新薰種子) that were熏-implanted in prior existences. Moreover, that interpretation is not of a single uniform judgment, and it should not be criticized by confusing (the two cases).

**Further inquiry:** Even granting that one does not extend (the duration in which) prior-karma-drawn seeds determine the duration of abiding, if newly熏-implanted seeds熏-implanted in this world, drawn by the supremely good karma of hearing the Dharma, arise in the eighth consciousness and determine the duration of abiding — why, by relying on this, would one not establish the life-faculty?

**Reconciling response:** Whether *nāma*-seeds熏-implanted in the eighth consciousness in this world give rise to the eighth consciousness in the world (existence) in which they will be熏-implanted (i.e., the next existence) or not — there are two possible positions. If one adopts the position that they "do not give rise (to the eighth consciousness)," then this objection is moot. If one adopts the position that they "give rise (to it)," there is no contradiction: namely, the *nāma*-seeds drawn by prior karma, having superior power, determine the duration of abiding of the eighth consciousness. The power of *nāma*-seeds熏-implanted in this world, though they may barely have the power of "arising" (*shō no chikara*, 生の力), does not have the power to determine the duration of abiding.

**Question:** In the sūtra text of the present fascicle (recorded by Shiki-Ryūhan), (it is stated:) "The Bodhisattva, Lord of Vajra Secrets, expounds the divine spell (etc.)." Now then, is this bodhisattva to be (regarded as) a different form (*igyō*, 異形) of Avalokiteśvara (*Kannon*, 觀音), or what?

If (he is) a different form of Avalokiteśvara: reading the sūtra text from beginning to end and inquiring into the original ground (*honji*, 本地) of the Lord of Vajra (*Shūkongōjin*, 執金剛神), (one finds that) Avalokiteśvara and Mìjī (*Misshaku*, 蜜迹 — the Vajra-bearer) each individually expound the divine spell — the matter of (their being) a single substance in different forms (*ittai-igyō*, 一體異形) is not clear in the sūtra text. Furthermore, consulting the explanations of other fascicles, (it is stated that the Lord of Vajra is) "one among the thousand Buddhas of the Bhadra-kalpa (*kengō*, 賢劫) (etc.)."

If this is indeed the case, then (conversely) in the *Fahua xuanzan* (*Hokke-gensan*, 法華玄賛), it is judged that "the Vajra-holding deity is a different form of Avalokiteśvara," and the accounts in the commentaries of other fascicles are the same. What is to be said?

**Personal view:** In what the present sūtra expounds, the Bodhisattva Lord of Vajra Secrets should not be (regarded as) a different form of Avalokiteśvara. Since Avalokiteśvara has already expounded the divine spell, if this bodhisattva then expounds it again and (they are) one substance, would it not mean that the provisional (*gon*, 權) and the real (*jitsu*, 實) are both expounding it repeatedly? Among these considerations, (the most important is that) this Bodhisattva Lord of Vajra Secrets (*Shūkongō-himitsu-shu bosatsu*, 執金剛秘密主菩薩) should not (be identified with) the Vajra-holding deity (*Shūkongōjin*, 執金剛神), because he is designated with the name "Bodhisattva" (*bosatsu*, 菩薩). **Question:** In the present sūtra, regarding the meaning of clarifying the substance of total-retention (*sōji-tai*, 總持體): how does the commentary master interpret the substance of the spell (*ju*, 呪) that is retained (*shoji*, 所持) in the total-retention of the patience of non-arising (*mushōnin-sōji*, 無生忍總持), as well as the "retaining" agent (*nōji*, 能持)?

**(Preliminary) statement:** Both the retained (*shoji*, 所持) and the retaining (*nōji*, 能持) take mindfulness-wisdom (*nenni*, 念慧) as their substance (etc.). Regarding this: since the retaining agent is already mindfulness-wisdom, why would the retained also have the same substance? If both are to take mindfulness-wisdom as their substance, how can a distinction between the retaining and the retained be made? Accordingly, in the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* (*Yuga-ron*, 瑜伽論), the "Basis Section" (*honji-bun*, 本地分), fascicle forty-five, the substance of the retained spell is put forward: "Through what the Buddha has expounded, one attains the bodhisattva-patience (*bosatsu-nin*, 菩薩忍) and the phrases and syllables of the various spells (*shojuˈ shōku*, 諸呪章句) (etc.)." It is clear that "the phrases and syllables of the various spells" puts forward the retained Dharma (*shoji-hō*, 所持法). What is to be said?

(Recorded by Shiki-Ryūhan.)

**Personal view:** If one discusses the reality of the matter, the substance of the retaining agent (*nōji-tai*, 能持體) should be mindfulness-wisdom. However, as for (also) saying that the retained is mindfulness-wisdom (*nenni*, 念慧): in the passage just above the commentary, (it says:) "The retained is the cause of patience (*nin*, 忍). (The commentary) is not directly saying that the substance of the retained is mindfulness-wisdom, (and so) even when the *Yoga*-text (*Yuga no bun*, 瑜伽文) is brought (to bear), it should not constitute a contradiction, should it?
