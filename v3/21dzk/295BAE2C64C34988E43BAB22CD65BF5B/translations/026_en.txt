### [Upper Section]

**Saishō mondō shō (最勝問答抄), Fascicle Seven**

**Fascicle Seven** [Interlinear note: "Chapter on the Dhāraṇī of Non-Attachment" (*Muzenjaku darani-hon*, 無染著陀羅尼品)]

**Question:** Among the four kinds of total-retention (*sōji*, 總持), which one is the "dhāraṇī of non-attachment" (*muzenjaku darani*, 無染著陀羅尼) expounded in the present chapter?

**Answer:** The commentary (*sho*, 疏) states: "It is the total-retention of the patience of non-arising (*mushōnin sōji*, 無生忍總持) (etc.)." Regarding this: (the dhāraṇī of non-attachment) protects through the dhāraṇī of non-attachment, causes one to be freed from calamities and troubles, and prevents retreat from practice. Therefore should it not simply be (classified as) the dhāraṇī (*ju*) total-retention? Is not the dhāraṇī (*darani*) expounded in the "Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Chapter" (*Nyoi-hōju-hon*) called the dhāraṇī total-retention? Moreover, in the present chapter there is no further exposition of the functional application (*hōyō*, 法用) and the (practitioner's) stage (*jii*, 次位) of the patience of non-arising of phenomena (*mushōhōnin*, 無生法忍), so one might conclude there is no total-retention of the patience of non-arising. For example, in the "Dhāraṇī Chapter" (*Darani-hon*) of the *Lotus Sūtra*, it is stated that sixty-eight thousand persons attained the patience of non-arising of phenomena (*mushōhōnin*); yet since the *Shōseki* (攝釋, summarizing commentary) therein does not expound the functional application and stage of the patience of non-arising of phenomena, it (is held that the *Lotus* dhāraṇī chapter) does not expound the patience of non-arising of phenomena. If so, then the interpretation in the present commentary is not clear. What is to be said?

**Personal view:** Among the many interpretations in the commentary, the intent of the interpretation that speaks of the total-retention of the patience of non-arising (*mushōnin sōji*) is as follows. In general, the substance (*tai*, 體) of the patience of non-arising is that which penetrates and comprehends that the intrinsic nature (*jishō*, 自性) of all phenomena (*shohō*, 諸法) as expressed (*shōzen saru*, 詮ぜられる) by all verbal expression (*gengon*, 言說) is in every case not truly established (*jitsujō sezu*, 實成せず), and that there is only the class of wisdom (*chihon*, 智品) that cognizes the intrinsic nature of all phenomena beyond verbal expression (*rigon-jishō*, 離言自性). This class of wisdom is called "patience" (*nin*, 忍). Since this patience penetrates the emptiness of all phenomena and is free from attachment, it is called the "dhāraṇī of non-attachment." What is made clear in the present chapter is most fittingly (classified as) the total-retention of the patience of non-arising. Even without expounding the functional application and stage, the title of the chapter reveals that meaning; hence one can say it expounds the total-retention of the patience of non-arising. Therefore, the "Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Chapter" and the *Lotus Sūtra*'s

"Dhāraṇī Chapter" cannot be taken as analogous cases. There is no error (in this interpretation).

**Question:** Do the two total-retentions of Dharma (*hōji*, 法持) and meaning (*giji*, 義持) extend through all teachings (*shokyō*, 諸敎) in general, or are they limited to dhāraṇī? (The question goes) both ways.

If they extend through all teachings, then within dhāraṇī (alone) (they) are divided into four kinds — Dharma, meaning, dhāraṇī-spell (*ju*, 呪), and patience of non-arising (*mushōnin*, 無生忍). But while the spell and (patience of non-) arising are limited to dhāraṇī, why should Dharma (and meaning) extend through all teachings, both esoteric and exoteric (*kenmi*, 顯密)? If, following this, (one argues that) they do not extend through all teachings, then the commentary master has judged: "Dharma and meaning are general (*sō*, 總); spell and non-arising are particular (*betsu*, 別)." From this it is known that Dharma and meaning are not limited to dhāraṇī. Furthermore, in the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*, it is said: "Dharma-retention (*hōji*) (consists of) the names, phrases, and phonemic groups (*myō-ku-monjishin*, 名句文身) of limitless sūtras; meaning-retention (*giji*) (consists of) the import of these (etc.)." That this extends broadly through all teachings is evident from the explicit text (*genmon*, 現文). What is to be said?

**Personal view:** Following the interpretations of Zizhou (窺基, Kuiji/Jizō, i.e., Kuiji of Zizhou) and the explicit text of the *Yogācārabhūmi*, the two kinds — Dharma and meaning — should extend through all teachings. The reason is this: "dhāraṇī" (*darani*, 陀羅尼) is a Sanskrit word, translated here as "total-retention" (*sōji*, 總持). With regard to the expressing texts (*nōsen no mon*, 能詮之文) and expressed meanings (*shosen no gi*, 所詮之義) of all teachings, insofar as one does not forget them — in (that aspect of) total-retention — one speaks of "dhāraṇī." This is the point at which Dharma and meaning are called "dhāraṇī." This import is seen in the *Xuanzàn* (*Gensan*, 玄賛). However, the dhāraṇī-spell total-retention (*ju-sōji*, 呪總持) has efficacious power (*iken*, 威驗) in divine spells (*shinju*, 神呪) and causes calamities and troubles to be eliminated; hence, though likewise (called) dhāraṇī, it is limited to dhāraṇī among all teachings and does not extend through all teachings (in general). Next, the total-retention of the patience of non-arising (*mushōnin sōji*, 無生忍總持) penetrates and comprehends, within this dhāraṇī-spell total-retention, that the intrinsic nature of all phenomena as expressed by all verbal expression is in every case not truly established, and that there is only the class of wisdom that cognizes the intrinsic nature of all phenomena beyond verbal expression. (This) absolutely cannot extend through all teachings. Therefore there is no error.

**Further inquiry:** Why is this so?

**Answer:** Because the four kinds of total-retention have differences in breadth and conciseness, shallowness and depth, and in what they establish and what they set aside (*hairistu*, 廢立).

**Question:** In the commentary of the present fascicle (Shiki and Ryūgyō compose this), (the commentary) expounds the virtue of dhāraṇī. If so, is the substance (*tai*, 體) of this total-retention to be exclusively the mental factors (*shinjō*, 心所) of mindfulness (*nen*, 念) and wisdom (*e*, 慧), or should the mental factor of meditative concentration (*jō-shinjō*, 定心所) be taken as its substance? (The question goes) both ways.

If the mental factor of meditative concentration is not taken (as substance), (then consider this): it is by the power of freedom in meditation (*jō-jizai no chikara*, 定自在の力) that divine spells are endowed with efficacious power — why then would the substance of total-retention not include the mental factor of concentration? Based on this, in the present sūtra, in expounding the merit of dhāraṇī, it says: "Through wondrous wisdom and supreme meditation, (one is caused to) praise (and extol)." In the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* (fascicle [thirty-five]), in clarifying the dhāraṇī-spell total-retention, it says: "The bodhisattvas attain such freedom in meditative absorption (*tōji-jizai*, 等持自在) (etc.)." Following what the Patriarch's (*shūke*, 宗家, i.e., Kuiji's) work states in certain passages ([*Yugashō*, Fascicle Twelve]), the dhāraṇī-spell total-retention is interpreted as taking concentration (*jō*, 定) as its substance. If this is so, then the Great Master (Kuiji) in various passages ([*Gensan*, Fascicle Ten]) has judged: "This (dhāraṇī) is called total-retention; mindfulness and wisdom are the substance." What is to be said?

**Personal view:** The answer to this disputation (*rongi*, 論義) is as given in the disputation answer immediately below.

**Question:** Should total-retention take the wisdom mental factor (*e-shinjō*, 慧心所) as its intrinsic substance (*jitai*, 自體)?

**Answer:** Yes. Regarding this, in the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* it is interpreted and stated: "Wondrous wisdom (*myōe*, 妙慧) is the cause of total-retention." From this it is known that (wisdom) is not the intrinsic-nature substance (*jishō-tai*, 自性體).

**Personal view:** The matter of identifying the substance of total-retention (*sōji ni tsuite tai wo idasu koto*) is not uniform (*ichijun*, 一準). As for the substance determined through the conditioning cause (*ein*, 依因) of the dhāraṇī-spell total-retention: this is concentration (*jō*, 定). (This is) because divine powers (*jintsū*, 神通) arise in dependence upon concentration. As for the substance determined through the discerning-and-selecting (*nōkantaku*, 能簡擇) faculty of the patience-of-non-arising total-retention (*ninsōji*, 忍總持): this is wisdom (*e*, 慧). That is its intrinsic-nature substance (*jishō-tai*, 自性體). For the remaining three total-retentions, when following this principle, all should take wisdom as their substance. When determining substance through the aspect of memorization (*kioku*, 記憶), all four kinds take mindfulness (*nen*, 念) as their substance. [Interlinear note: The above three modes of identifying substance — the commentary presents these.] However, as for the statement in the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*: total-retention (*sōji*) means the comprehensive retention of the dharmas held, together with the mindfulness and wisdom that do the holding. If one looks toward the retained (*shoji*, 所持) teaching-Dharma (*kyōhō*, 敎法),

wisdom is the proximate cause (*kinnen*, 近因). This is what the *Yogācārabhūmi* states. If one follows the meaning of the discerning-and-holding (*nōkanjō*, 能簡持) function, wisdom is most fittingly the intrinsic-nature substance (*jishō-tai*). Therefore there is no error.

**Question:** (Regarding) the sūtra text "What is the meaning of this phrase (*ku*, 句) (etc.)?": how does the commentary master interpret this "phrase" (*ku*)?

**(Preliminary) statement:** "The phrase (*ku*) is a name that expresses (*nōsen*, 能詮), not the 'name' (*myō*, 名) within the category of names-and-phrases (*myōku*, 名句) (etc.)." Regarding this: a secret total-retention (*himitsu sōji*, 秘密總持) consists of syllables and phrases (*shōku*, 章句) containing the profoundly deep, inconceivable, wondrous dharma, (composed of) name-characters (*myōji*, 名字) that are without (fixed) meaning (*mugi*, 無義). Therefore the "phrase" in this "meaning of the phrase" (*kugi*, 句義) is asking: "What is the meaning of such wondrous and profound syllables and phrases?" Why then is it interpreted as an expressing name (*nōsen no myō*, 能詮の名) that is not the "name" within the category of names-and-phrases (*myōku no naka no myō*)? What is to be said?

**Personal view:** Dhāraṇī (*darani*) is described as being of condensed, abbreviated, and esoteric (*shōryaku mitsu*, 少略密) (character), with name-characters without (explicit) meaning (*mugi no monji*, 無義文字). Though the verbal expressions are abbreviated, the meaning expressed (*shosen no gi*, 所詮の義) is immeasurable. Therefore "the dhāraṇī of non-attachment" (*muzenjaku darani*, 無染著陀羅尼) is a name that expresses (*nōsen no myō*, 能詮の名) the immeasurable meaning conveyed (*shosen no muryō no gi*, 所詮の無量の義) in the present (chapter). This name is not the "name" (*myō*) within the category of names-and-phrases. The reason is that the "name" within the category of names-and-phrases reaches directly to the substance (*tai*, 體) of phenomena (*shohō*, 諸法), whereas the present name — "dhāraṇī of non-attachment" — is not a name (that reaches) the substance of phenomena. (That is,) this is the name of the dhāraṇī, not a name of the substance of phenomena. This is a fine point, but the import of the interpretation is subtle and the meaning is not easy to grasp. Therefore, for now, perhaps this is how one should explain it.

**Question:** Was the present sūtra (the *Golden Light Sūtra*, 最勝王経) expounded before the *Lotus Sūtra*, or what? (The question goes) both ways.

If it was expounded before (the *Lotus*), then when one considers the cause for the present sūtra's exposition, (it was this): Bodhisattva Myōdō (*Miào Chuáng*, 妙幢菩薩) heard the declaration that "three months from now I (*Śākyamuni*) shall enter *parinirvāṇa*," and because of doubting the brevity of the World-Honored One's lifespan, (the sūtra was preached). From this it is known that (the *Golden Light Sūtra* was preached) after the *Lotus Sūtra*. Based on this, in the "Chapter on the Dhāraṇī of Non-Attachment" of the present sūtra, the World-Honored One praises Śāriputra (*Jushi*, 鷲子, "Vulture's Son") saying: "You have already set forth well and have faith in and understanding of the great [Mahāyāna]…"
