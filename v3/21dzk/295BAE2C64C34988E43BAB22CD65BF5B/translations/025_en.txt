(Continued from the previous page) "Cannot be measured" — (the commentary) reveals the meaning of pervading the entire dharma-realm (*shūhen hōkai*, 周遍法界) and says "like space," and expressing the import of the cessation of the path of language (*gengodōdan*, 言語道断), says "cannot be measured." It is indeed abundantly clear that this speaks of the true principle (i.e., the dharma-body). The phrases "radiating brilliance" and "like a flame or illusion" precisely describe the arising of the functions of the recompense-body (*hōshin*, 報身) and transformation-body (*keshin*, 化身). Based on this, the ancient eight-fascicle sūtra (the old Chinese translation of the *Suvarṇaprabhāsa-sūtra*) states: "The buddha-body and dharma-body are like empty space (etc.)." If so, it is the second interpretation (*goshaku*, 後釈) that is the correct interpretation.

**Question:** In the commentary on the present sūtra, in clarifying the proper rules of the True Dharma (*shōbō no kisoku*, 正法軌則), (the commentary) cites the explanation in the "Foundation Section" (*Honchi-bun*, 本地分) of the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra* (*Yuga shiji-ron*, 瑜伽師地論) [Interlinear note: fascicle forty-eight] (to the effect of): "Revering the True Dharma (*shōbō wo kyōkei suru*, 恭敬正法) (etc.)." If so, does this "reverence" (*kyōkei*, 恭敬) (here) mean the meaning of "not holding in contempt" (*fukyō*, 不軽), or what is it? (The question goes) both ways.

If "reverence" (*kyōkei*) is the same meaning as "not holding in contempt" (*fukyō*), then looking directly at the text of the "Foundation Section," it clarifies the separation from the defilement of contempt (*kyōman no zen*, 軽慢染) through four characteristics (*sō*, 相), and presents four kinds of characteristics: "revering the True Dharma," "revering those who teach it," "not holding the True Dharma in contempt," and "not holding those who teach it in contempt." Since (these four) are each cited separately, it is known that they are not of a single meaning. However, if one follows this (reasoning) accordingly, "reverence" (*kyōkei*) means the meaning of treating with care and solemnity (*inchō*, 慇重); how could it not be the meaning of "not holding in contempt"? Based on this, in the *Xuanzàn* (*Gensan*, 玄賛) the epithet "the Great Being Who Does Not Hold in Contempt" (*Fukyō Daishi*, 不軽大士) is interpreted (as follows): "'Not holding in contempt' (*fukyō*) is the meaning of 'reverence' (*kyōkei*)" — such is the judgment given. [Interlinear note: Shiki and Eison compose this.]

**Personal view:** In (the context of) "reverence" (*kyōkei*), there is necessarily the meaning of "not holding in contempt" (*fukyō*). In (the context of) "not holding in contempt" (*fukyō*), there is not necessarily the meaning of "reverence" (*kyōkei*). That is to say: "reverence" (*kyōkei*) means arousing pure faith (*jōshin*, 浄信) and thereby venerating (the Dharma); therefore at such a time there is necessarily the meaning of "not holding in contempt." "Not holding in contempt" (*fukyō*) means that even without arousing deep faith (*jinshin*, 深信), one does not go so far as to hold (it) in contempt. When one arouses deep faith and (thereby) does not hold (it) in contempt, that is precisely the meaning of "reverence." The "Great Being Who Does Not Hold in Contempt" (*Fukyō Daishi*, 不軽大士, i.e., Bodhisattva Sadāparibhūta) saw that the fourfold assembly (*shibu-shū*, 四部衆) possessed buddha-nature (*busshō*, 佛性) and revered them; therefore, "not holding in contempt" is the same as "reverence." If so, the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*'s separate listing of (the two) is based on this intent.
