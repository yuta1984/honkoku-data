**Further inquiry:** In the next passage below in the commentary, when it interprets the matter that "the position of Kauṇḍinya the Brahmin is *jizen* (pre-ground, i.e., before the first bodhisattva ground)," [text damaged] — because it is the Transformation Body (*keshin*) that preaches, (the commentary) has judged that if the one who transforms (*nōke*, the transformer/teacher) is a Transformation Body, then the one who is transformed (*shoke*, the one being taught/converted) should be at the *jizen* stage. Yet why does (the text) now say that it is the Dharma and Reward Bodies and not the Transformation Body?

**Resolution:** The present verse "I constantly abide on Vulture Peak" etc. concurrently encompasses and expounds all three bodies. From the aspect of "constantly abiding," it speaks only of the two bodies of Dharma and Reward. "Abiding on Vulture Peak" speaks of the Transformation Body. Hence, saying above that "(the text) expounds the two bodies of Dharma and Reward" is an interpretation of the aspect of "constantly abiding." Saying "(the text) expounds the Transformation Body" is an interpretation of the aspect of "abiding on Vulture Peak." Therefore, the upper and lower (interpretations) are not in contradiction. Alternatively, if they are in contradiction, one should say there are two (separate) interpretations.

**Question:** (This question has already been formulated and discussed by Seiben [Bhāviveka/Qingbian].) The sūtra text reads: "Kauṇḍinya of the Brahmin lineage" (etc.). In that case, the fact that this Brahmin is (at the stage of) the Tenth Dedication (*dai-jū eko*) among the Ten Dedications (*jū eko*) — how does the Zichou Master interpret this?

**Response:** "Because he has received the prediction (*juki*, *vyākaraṇa*) from the Dharma Teacher" (etc.).

Regarding this: the "Dharma Teacher" (*hōshi*) refers to the Tathāgata as transformer/teacher. The "prediction" (*juki*) is the record/designation (*kibetsu*) that is received (by the one being taught). However, since the receiving of a prediction is not fixed to any particular stage, how can one say it is the Tenth Dedication? The three-capacity Śrāvakas (*sankon shōmon*), at the levels of those in training and those beyond training (*gaku mugaku*), receive predictions of Buddhahood. Vimalagarbha the Elder (*Daiba Chōja*) is of a profound stage and has already received the prediction of Buddhahood. How then can receiving a prediction be used to interpret (his stage as) the Dharmadhātu Immeasurable Dedication (*hōkai muryō eko*, the Tenth Dedication)?

**Question:** The sūtra text reads: "Kauṇḍinya of the Brahmin lineage" (etc.). What then is the original ground (*honji*, fundamental position/stage) of this (figure)?

Examining the sūtra text and investigating the prediction from the Dharma Teacher, (the Brahmin) seeks the Buddha's relics (*busshari*) and wishes to be born in the Trāyastriṃśa Heaven (*Tōri-ten*). How then can one call it the Tenth Dedication? Much less (can one say it is) a bodhisattva who has already entered the grounds.

**Personal note:** Because there is no definitive (meaning) in the sūtra text, the commentary offers two interpretations. The intent of the first interpretation is as follows. The sūtra already states: "I constantly abide on Vulture Peak, proclaiming this precious sūtra" (etc.). The Buddha who transforms (others) is (seen as) the Transformation Body (*keshin*) residing on the Sacred Mountain (*Ryōzen*, Vulture Peak). Since the transforming Buddha is already a Tathāgata (operating at the) *jizen* level, the one being transformed must certainly also be at the *jizen* stage. Furthermore, as the objection (in the question) suggests, the behavioral pattern (*gyōsō*) of wishing to be born in Trāyastriṃśa Heaven is (seen as) that of a shallow position within the *jizen* stage. As for seeking the Buddha's relics in order to open the gate of skillful means (*hōben*) and to benefit sentient beings — even a bodhisattva at the *kegyō* (preparatory practice) level, why would such a one lack this practice? However, as for the claim that (the Brahmin) is said to be at the Tenth Dedication because he received a prediction — in the *Śūraṃgama Sūtra* (*Shuryōgon-kyō*), predictions are explained as being of four types: prediction before awakening the aspiration (*mihosshin juki*); prediction at the moment of awakening aspiration (*tekihosshin juki*); secret prediction (*mitsu juki*); and prediction manifestly present upon obtaining the wisdom of non-arising (*mushō-nin*). The forty stages of mind (*yonjusshin*) before the grounds constitute "prediction at the moment of awakening aspiration." Now the Tenth Dedication represents the fullness of the utmost stage (*kyokui manshin*) after arousing the firm and resolute aspiration for enlightenment (*kengomyōshin*, bodhicitta). It is for this reason that one says "Tenth Dedication." 〔Note: One should consult the commentary on the Prediction Chapter (*Juki-bon-shō*).〕

**Resolution:** Regarding the claim "from the moment of awakening aspiration onwards" — the commentary cites the sūtra text translated by the Tripiṭaka Master Paramārtha (*Shintai Sanzō*): "The meaning taught by the Buddha — I have already heard and understood" (etc.). Having already said "I have already heard and understood," it is clearly known that this is an (stage of) awakening and understanding (*kaigo*) and not the position of "before awakening aspiration." Although this is the meaning of the latter interpretation (in the commentary), the point of negating (*shago*) "prediction before awakening aspiration" is shared (by both interpretations). Moreover, in the present sūtra text, the fact that the Brahmin explains the characteristics of the three bodies is profoundly deep. It is (seen as) not being the behavioral pattern of the "before awakening aspiration" stage.

**Resolution:** The commentary cites the Paramārtha recension (*Shintai-bon*), taking the statement "I have already heard and understood" as evidence, (showing that this is) the stage of awakening after having transcended worldly existence through the attainment of the noble nature (*shōshō risen*, i.e., the path of seeing, *kenjōdō*).
