**Question:** Regarding the sūtra passage "being able to well abide (*nō zen anjū*, 能善安住) and correctly receive and retain (*nō shō juju*, 能正受持) (etc.)," how does the commentary master interpret this?

**(Preliminary) statement:** "(The commentary master) interprets (the passage as follows:) 'Able to well abide' means taking this dhāraṇī-spell as the basis of support (*shoye*, 所依). 'Correctly receive' means the wisdom of hearing (*monge*, 聞慧). 'Retain' means the two wisdoms of reflection and cultivation (*shishu nige*, 思修二慧)."

Regarding this: based on the interpretation and reading the sūtra text, the two phrases of this sūtra text should be said to expound the three wisdoms of hearing, reflection, and cultivation (*mon-shi-shu no sange*, 聞思修三慧). That is: "able to well abide" is the wisdom of hearing; "able to correctly receive" is the wisdom of reflection; "retain" is the wisdom of cultivation. For example, just as the *Buddhabhūmi-śāstra* (*Butsujironbu*, 仏地論) says "able to bear" (*nō kan*, 能堪), "able to reflect" (*nō shi*, 能思), and "able to retain" (*nō ji*, 能持) — which correspond in order to the three wisdoms. If so, what is to be said (about the commentary master's interpretation)?

**Personal view:** (The meaning is that) one relies upon this divine spell as the basis of support and arouses the three wisdoms; hence (the text) says "able to abide" (*nō anjū*, 能安住). "Abide" (*anjū*) means "to rely upon and rest in" (*esho no gi*, 依止之義). Why would one abruptly call the wisdom of hearing "abiding"? If the first phrase is not

Also, someone raises a doubt and says: In the *Lotus Sūtra*, when expounding the practice of the five kinds of Dharma-masters (*goshu hōshi*, 五種法師), "receiving and retaining" (*juju*, 受持) is counted as one (kind of) practice. (Does this mean that) here, too, (one should say that "receiving" and "retaining" are) a single practice?

Resolution: "Receive" (*ju*) is defined as "accepting and taking in" (*ryōno*, 領納), and "retain" (*ji*) is defined as "upholding and maintaining" (*ninjō*, 任持). Since they follow distinct principles of meaning (*betsu gimo*, 別義門), how can they be treated as uniform?

**Question:** When "dhāraṇī" is named after its analogy (*yu*, 喩) as the "Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Chapter" (*Nyoi-hōju-hon*), should (the chapter title) be subsumed under the six kinds of interpretive analysis (*rokushu shaku*, 六種釈)?

**(Preliminary) statement:** The commentary says it is "not subsumed under the six kinds of interpretive analysis (etc.)." Regarding this: when the Dharma-name is made to follow the analogy (*yu*) as the chapter title, why would it not fall under the six kinds of analysis? Should there not be (at least) two interpretations — the "substance-predicate" (*jigyō shaku*, 持業釈) and the "possessive" (*ūzai shaku*, 有財釈) interpretations?

**Personal view:** When the full (compound) "Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Chapter" (*Nyoi-hōju-hon*) is taken together, the two interpretations of substance-predicate and possessive should apply. But when only "Wish-Fulfilling Jewel" (*nyoi-hōju*) is cited alone, it (does) not (fall under) the six kinds of interpretive analysis. Alternatively: "Wish-Fulfilling Jewel" (*nyoi-hōju*) is the analogy (*yu*), and "chapter" (*hon*, 品) is the Dharma. Since (the title) simply juxtaposes the analogy (against the Dharma), how can one discuss the six analyses?

**Question:** Should the divine spell of the wish-fulfilling jewel (*nyoi-shinju*, 如意神呪) in the "Protection of the Kingdom Chapter" be expounded by all four Heavenly Kings together?

**Answer:** The commentary says: "The four kings expound it together (etc.)." Regarding this: when one looks at the explicit text of the sūtra, only the Heavenly King Vaiśravaṇa alone expounds it. Why (then) does (the commentary) say it is "expounded by the four kings"? Moreover, the earlier passage of the commentary has judged "expounded by one alone." The earlier and later passages are contradictory. What is to be said?

**Personal view:** The earlier passage of the commentary [interlinear note: at the location of the Protection of the Kingdom Chapter] says: "In order to make it easy to accomplish single-minded prayer (*sensin kijo*, 専心祈請), the spell is expounded by only one (king) (etc.)." The meaning is: if the four kings were to expound it together, the minds of the sentient beings being converted (*shoke no ujō*, 所化有情) would be scattered and distracted, making the accomplishment of prayer difficult. Therefore, when one heavenly king expounds it, single-minded prayer is easy to accomplish. That is why it is said that one heavenly king expounds it. In reality, the four kings expound it together.

**Further inquiry:** If that is so, from beginning to end one heavenly king should be expounding it. Why then is it said that there is (a case of) the four kings expounding it?

**Resolution:** The spiritual faculties (*kikon*, 機根) of the beings to be converted are of a thousand varieties. By hearing what all four kings expound together, are there perhaps those who accomplish their prayer?

**Question:** The sūtra text says: "augmenting their wisdom, and perfecting the adornment of eloquence in speech (*gonsetsu no ben*, 言說之辯) (etc.)." Now then, does this passage (in its entirety) expound the four kinds of eloquence (*shibenai*, 四辯才)?

**(Preliminary) statement:** The commentary says "(This expounds only) the eloquence of words (*shiben*, 詞辯才) (etc.)." Regarding this: this text as a whole should be said to expound the four kinds of eloquence. That is: "augmenting their wisdom" (*masui chie*, 益其智慧) refers to the two kinds of eloquence of Dharma and meaning (*hō-gi no niben*, 法義二辯); "perfectly adorned" (*gusoku shōgon*, 具足莊嚴) refers to the eloquence of skillful exposition (*kōsetsu-benjai*, 巧說辯才); and "the eloquence of speech" (*gonsetsu no ben*, 言說之辯) refers to the eloquence of words (*shiben*, 詞辯才). Following this, other masters (*yoshū*, 余師) interpret it in this way. If so, the commentary master's interpretation is not clear. What is to be said?

**Personal view:** "Augmenting their wisdom" (*masui chie*) is (done) in order to perfectly adorn the eloquence of words (*shiben*). "Perfectly adorned" (*gusoku shōgon*) is also (part of) the eloquence of words. Therefore, what is properly expounded is called "the eloquence of words (*shiben*)." Hence there is no error.

**Question:** The sūtra text (states): "swift and keen eloquence (*shōri-benjai*, 捷利辯才), inexhaustible great wisdom (*mujin daichi*, 無盡大慧), skillfully understanding the various treatises (*zenkai shūron*, 善解衆論) and the various arts (*shogijutsu*, 諸伎術) (etc.)." Now then, should this passage be said to expound the four kinds of eloquence together with the five sciences (*gomyō*, 五明) such as logic (*inmyō*, 因明)?

**(Preliminary) statement:** The commentary appears to expound, in its main thrust, the four eloquences (*shiben*, 四辯) and seven eloquences (*shichiben*, 七辯), without expounding the five sciences. Regarding this: looking at the sūtra text, broadly one should clarify the four eloquences and five sciences. That is: "swift and keen eloquence" refers to skillful exposition and eloquence of words (*kōsetsu-shiben*, 巧說詞辯); "inexhaustible great wisdom" refers to the two eloquences of Dharma and meaning (*hō-gi niben*); the "seven eloquences" are a subdivision of the eloquence of skillful exposition (*kōsetsu-benjai*); "the various arts (*shogijutsu*)" refer to the science of craftsmanship (*kōgyōmyō*, 工巧明); and "skillfully understanding the various treatises" refers to the remaining four sciences (*yoyo no shimyō*, 余四明). Following this, the Venerable Kō (*Kō Hōshi*) interprets it thus. If so, the commentary's interpretation is not clear. What is to be said?

**Personal view:** "Swift and keen eloquence and inexhaustible great wisdom" refers to the four eloquences and seven eloquences. This is because among the seven eloquences there is "the eloquence without cessation or exhaustion" (*mudanjin-ben*, 無斷盡辯). "Skillfully understanding the various treatises" refers to the two eloquences of Dharma and meaning. Therefore, from "skillfully understanding the various treatises" upward, (the text) is said to expound the four eloquences and seven eloquences. As for "and the various arts" (*oyobi shogijutsu*): one cannot completely exclude the clarification of the science of language (*shōmyō*, 聲明), the science of craftsmanship (*kōgyōmyō*, 工巧明), and the science of medicine (*iHōmyō*, 醫方明). The reason (the text) speaks of "arts" (*gijutsu*) is precisely because of these (sciences). Why then does the commentary's interpretation limit (the text) to the four and seven eloquences? However, other masters' interpretation of "skillfully understanding the various treatises" as the remaining four sciences probably does not follow the text. "And the various arts" refers to (only) three sciences (i.e., sound science, craftsmanship science, and medical science).
