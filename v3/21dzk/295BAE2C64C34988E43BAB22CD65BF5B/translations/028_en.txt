**Question:** What is the reason for calling *nenni* (念慧, "mindfulness-wisdom") by the name *hōsho* (方處, "region/location")?

**(Preliminary) statement:** The commentary calls it "region/location" because it depends on the sound-base (*shōsho*, 聲處) (etc.). Regarding this: among the five sense-objects (*gojin*, 五塵), within the first object — the form-object (*shikijin*, 色塵) — there exist differentiations such as manifest form (*ken*, 顯) and shape (*gyō*, 形). Therefore, with regard to shape and form (*gyōshiki*, 形色), "region/location" and so forth should apply. If that is so, sound (*shō*, 聲) and smell (*kō*, 香) and so on have no such differentiations as manifest form and shape. Why, then, is it said that "region/location" applies (to them)?

The commentary says: "If one takes (the matter) according to the mutual configuration of the two truths (*nittai sōgyō*, 二諦相形), it comprehensively applies to both negation (*sha*, 遮) and affirmation (*hyō*, 表). As in the first case, 'not being a region/location' (*hi-hōsho*): based on ultimate truth (*shōgichū*, 勝義), (this) negates (*sha*) 'region/location' and affirms (*hyō*) 'not being a region/location.' As for 'not-not being a region/location' (*hi-hi-hōsho*): taking (the matter) within conventional truth (*zokutai*, 俗諦), since the body of the Dharmas retained (*shoji no hōtai*, 所持法體), which constitutes the substance of total-retention (*sōji-tai*, 總持體), depends on sound (*shō*, 聲), and since the intrinsic nature (*jishō*, 自性) of total-retention is mindfulness-wisdom (*nenni*, 念慧), mindfulness-wisdom is not regional/locational, yet because it depends on a region/location, (it is 'not-not being a region/location'). The remainder may be understood by analogy (etc.)."

**Personal view:** This matter (as questioned) is mistaken. The intent of the commentary is: when, setting ultimate truth and conventional truth in opposition, one discusses "region/location" and "non-region/location" with respect to total-retention, there is (the duality of) negation and affirmation. When negating, one negates "region/location" and affirms "non-region/location." If one discusses this from the standpoint of conventional truth: among the substance of total-retention, the Dharma that is retained depends upon the sense-object of sound (*shōjin*, 聲塵); since the sense-object of sound is not "regional/locational," its substance is not "regional/locational." The intrinsic body of the self-nature of total-retention is mindfulness-wisdom, and its body is not "regional/locational." Also, because (total-retention) takes as its object form-objects (*shikijin*) such as manifest (form) and shape, it may be said to be "regional/locational" from the side of its object. It is not saying that mindfulness-wisdom is "regional/locational" because it depends on sound. (The question) does not (accord with) the intent of the disputation (*rongi*), does it?

**Question:** The goddess Sarasvatī (*Benzaiten*, 辨才天女) manifesting (herself as) the "cow-herding Joyful One" (*Bokugū-Kangi*, 牧牛歡喜) — should this be considered an "outer virtue" (*gaitoku*, 外德)?

**(Preliminary) statement:** The commentary's intent appears to see this as an "inner virtue" (*naitoku*, 內德). Regarding this: "inner virtues" are meritorious qualities such as the four eloquences (*shiben*, 四辯). Manifesting various forms and likenesses is most properly an "outer virtue," is it not? Taking analogies and examining the matter: looking at the passage just above, (the text speaks of) "manifesting (the form of) the elder sister of Yama (*Emma*, 閻羅)" and "manifesting (the form of) the younger sister of the great deity Vāsudeva (*Baso-daiten*, 婆蘇大天)" — these are all outer virtues. Why should "manifesting the Joyful Woman (*Kangi-nyо*, 歡喜女)" be called an "inner virtue"?

**Personal view:** Indeed, manifesting various forms and likenesses is most properly an "outer virtue" — just as in (the cases of) manifesting the elder sister of Yama and so forth. However, the present commentary's interpretation does not call (the manifestation of) the Joyful Woman an "inner virtue." In general, reading the sūtra text: "Contemplating among all sentient beings, (Sarasvatī,) the tranquil and supreme (*jakushō muka*, 寂勝無過) among heavenly women, manifests by expedient means (*gongen*, 權現) (the form of) the cow-herding Joyful Woman, and when fighting against the devas (*ten*, 天), she constantly obtains victory (etc.)." "The supreme and unexcelled among heavenly women" — (the text) praises her great wisdom, calling her "supreme and unexcelled." It is with this wisdom that (the text) speaks of "inner virtue." Therefore, in the commentary, (the passage about) "in the place of battle-formation, she always wins when fighting, and nurtures and subdues (beings) with a compassionate and patient mind, manifesting as the elder sister of Yama" is

**Question:** In the "Chapter on the Dhāraṇī of Non-Attachment" (*Muzenjaku-darani-hon*, 無染著陀羅尼品), in establishing (*jutsujō*, 述成) the doubt (*gi*, 疑) of Śāriputra (*Sharihotsu*, 身子) concerning the substance of total-retention, (the text) puts forward seven kinds of distinctions (*shichishu no fudō*, 七種不同) such as "not being a region/location" (*hi-hōsho*, 非方處) and "not-not being a region/location" (*hi-hi-hōsho*, 非々方處). Now then, regarding the theoretical substance (*ributai*, 理體) of "discarding designation to discuss the import" (*haisen-danshi*, 廢詮談旨), should the two gates of negation (*sha*, 遮) and affirmation (*hyō*, 表) be expounded?

**(Preliminary) statement:** In the commentary there is an interpretation with the two gates of negation and (affirmation — written as) *e* (*衣*, i.e., *hyō*). Regarding this: the particular characteristics (*jisō*, 自相) of conditioned (phenomena) (*ue*, 有為) are shallow and proximate, and yet they are beyond the reach of words and concepts (*myōgen*, 名言). The true principle of the unconditioned (*mue*, 無為) is deep and remote. How then can there be designational expression (*sentai*, 詮表) of "region/location" and so forth? How much more (noteworthy is that) the founders of our school (*shūke*, 宗家) have a passage (in *Tō* (燈), fascicle 4) which discusses the establishment of the three times (*sanze*, 三世) upon the perfectly accomplished (nature) (*enjōjitsu-shō*, 圓成實性), or interprets (this) as "relying on designation (*sen*, 詮) in order to expound the attainment and expression of the three times," or judges that "if one takes (the matter) according to the provisional establishment (*keryō-seshō*, 仮施設) following (dependent) conditions." It is thereby known that when one "discards designation to discuss the import," one should not establish the distinctions of the three times (*sanze no fudō*, 三世不同). Moreover, looking at the secondary passages (*bōmon*, 傍文) of the present commentary: "If one takes (the matter) according to the conceptually constructed (*henge*, 遍計) and ultimate-of-ultimates (*shōgi no shōgi*, 勝義々々), the seven phrases all negate (without affirming) (etc.)." The import of the interpretation is clear: when (one is in) the gate of the true ultimate (*shin-shōgi-mon*, 真勝義門), (the text) merely negates and does not affirm (as seen). If that is so, the present commentary's interpretation, which in general takes both negation and affirmation (together), appears to be self-contradictory. What is to be said?
