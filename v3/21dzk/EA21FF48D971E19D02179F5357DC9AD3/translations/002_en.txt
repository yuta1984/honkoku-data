A Brief Commentary on the Diagrams of the Six Types of Mandala Deities, Symbols, and Mudrās in the First Chapter of the *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha* (Scripture of the Great Teaching-King that Fully Illuminates All Tathāgatas' Truth)
《Interlinear note: Together with the Collected Essentials of Dhāraṇī and Preface》

Now, when the bodhisattvas of the three ages sit at the site of enlightenment and are about to succeed to the Buddha's position, if the assembly of the Yoga [practitioners] does not go forth to bear witness, one cannot attain the ultimate fruit. Since the Great Teaching flowed eastward, teachings were transmitted from master to disciple, and according to each one's wisdom, commentaries and phrases arose. In every generation there were such people—some ordinary beings, some sages. The sages each adhered to their respective vehicles of the Three Vehicles, while ordinary beings roughly articulated the truth based on the written texts. The Teaching-King [scripture] had not yet been transmitted to China (諸夏), when at the beginning of the Kaiyuan era [713 CE], the Tripiṭaka master Śubhākarasiṃha (善無畏), having formed a karmic connection with this land, came through perilous roads. Deeply versed in Yoga, he carried with him scriptural fascicles of more than one hundred thousand verses, leaving nothing behind. He had an audience with the Emperor, poured forth his heart to save sentient beings, penetrated their natures, and made the painted images, guiding illustrations, prefaces, and dhāraṇīs available for clear viewing. Those who received [these teachings] were invariably protected, and the whole world looked up to them. For nearly eighty years, through the power of the sages, [these teachings] did not fall into ruin.

While Śubhākarasiṃha was propagating [the teachings], the Tripiṭaka master Vajrabodhi (金剛智) came across the sea. Profoundly versed in the true school of Yoga and having exhaustively penetrated the ultimate principles of the Vajra [teachings], the scriptures and teachings he brought were roughly the same as those that had come from the West. Before even reaching this shore [China], a violent wind arose and wrathful waves surged. The ship's captain managed the ship's mast, but the precious objects, scriptures, and texts became mixed together with the cargo, and when they encountered the waves, they were all equally cast away and abandoned. Then the two great masters met each other, cleaving together as if bound with glue; when they discussed the essentials of the Dharma, it was as if two halves of a tally-stick fit perfectly together.
