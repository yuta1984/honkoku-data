## [Right Page, Upper Section]

*(Continued from the previous page.)* "Principle establishing teaching" (*lǐ chéng jiào* 理成教) refers to: using [the analysis of] domain (*jìng* 境), practice (*xíng* 行), and fruition (*guǒ* 果) to establish and set forth the root teaching (*běn jiào* 本教). "Domain" refers to the eight consciousnesses (*bā shí* 八識), the two truths (*èr dì* 二諦), the three natures (*sān xìng* 三性), and so forth. "Practice" refers to the contemplative wisdom of Vijñaptimātratā (*wéishí guān zhì* 唯識觀智) and the five pāramitās (*wǔ dù* 五度) and so forth. "Fruition" refers to wisdom (*zhì* 智), elimination [of defilements] (*duàn* 断), enlightenment (*pútí* 菩提), and nirvāṇa (*nièpán* 涅槃).

**"I, Jī (*Kuiji*), am ashamed in scholarship before [Dao]róng and [Hui]kǎi, yet I have undeservedly attended the translation [project] and taken up the writing tablet (*gū* 觚). In [scholarly] endeavor I fall short of Yán [Huí] and [Zi]yóu, and have wrongly insinuated myself as an assistant (*zī* 資) in the assembly [around the master's] case and staff (*hánzhàng* 函杖)."**

This is the seventh section, in which Dharma Master Jī (*Kuiji*) humbly reflects upon and describes himself. Here he cites two eminent figures from the [Buddhist] path and two eminent figures from the secular [Confucian] world to express humility about himself. *Tiǎn* 忝 is pronounced *tiāodiǎn* (天點切) or alternatively *hùdān* (怙簞切), and means "to be disgraced; to dishonor [through undeserved favor]." *Péi* 陪 is pronounced *púpēi* (蒲坏切) and means "to attend upon; to serve at the side of." "Róng" 融 refers to Dharma Master Dàoróng (道融), a disciple of [Kumāra]jīva. "Kǎi" 愷 refers to Dharma Master Huìkǎi (慧愷), a disciple of Paramārtha. *Cāo* 操 is pronounced *jídāo* (疾刀切) and means "to take hold of; to grasp." *Gū* 觚 refers to a rectangular piece of wood used by the ancients for writing, analogous to the writing strips (*jiǎn* 簡) of later times. "Yán" 顏 refers to Yán Huí (顔回). "Yóu" 游 refers to Zǐyóu (子游). Both of these men were disciples of Confucius. *Cè* 厠 means "to mingle with; to join among." *Zī* 資 means "disciple." "函杖" (*hánzhàng*, "case and staff"): *hán* 函 means "to contain; to hold." In the context of lectures and questions, [the disciples] stand facing [the master], who is sufficiently [authoritative] to point and draw [diagrams] with his staff.

**"Entrusted by the various accomplished ones (*yǎchuī* 雅吹), I have in truth engaged in the affair of [playing along without ability, like] the pretending wind-instrument player (*làn yú* 濫竽). Looking [at myself] and [knowing I am] unlike the skilled artisan (*liánggōng* 良工), [I] greedily (*tāo* 叨) bask in the radiance of [the text] and harbor its jade [within my poor vessel]."** (Annotation: In one edition, *làn* 濫 is written as *jiān* 監.)

"諸" (*zhū*) here means "at; in." Regarding "誠事濫竽" ("truly engaging in the affair of the pretending flutist"): The *Hánzǐ* (韓子, i.e., *Hán Fēizǐ*) says: "King Xuān of Qi (*Qí Xuān Wáng*) had people play the *yú* 竽 (wind instrument) [for him], always requiring three hundred players [at once]. The gentleman (*chǔshì* 処士) of Nánguō (南郭) presented himself as a *yú* player and joined [the ensemble]; the King was pleased with him. When King Xuān died, King Mǐn (閔王) also loved the *yú*, but wished to have each person play individually. The Nánguō gentleman, upon hearing this, fled." (走也, "he ran away.") The *Guǎng Yǎ* (広雅) says: "The *yú* 竽 resembles the *shēng* 笙 (a mouth organ) and has thirty-six reeds (*huáng* 簧, i.e., tongues); the pipes are arranged around the center." "事" (*shì*) means "endeavor; occupation." Regarding "顧異良工" ("looking [at myself] and [knowing I am] unlike the skilled artisan"): In the state of Chǔ (楚), a person capable of recognizing jade was called a "*liánggōng*" (skilled artisan) or "*yùrén*" (jade artisan). Biàn Hé (卞和) is precisely such a person.

## [Right Page, Lower Section]

Regarding "叨暉蘊玉" ("greedily basking in the radiance and harboring jade within"): *yùn* 蘊 means "to accumulate; to store." *Tāo* 叨 is pronounced *tālāo* (他勞切) and means "to covet; to greedily appropriate." The *Wén Fù* (文賦, "Rhapsody on Literature") says: "Stone harbors jade and the mountain shines; water holds pearls and the river is beautiful." The meaning of the two phrases "顧異良工" and "叨暉蘊玉" is this: the text (*wén* 文) of this preface is clumsy, yet its meaning (*yì* 義) is supremely wondrous. The text is like a stone that harbors jade, while the meaning is like the jade harbored within. A skilled artisan who can recognize jade does not discard the ore-stone. Would a wise person who understands the meaning ever criticize the [literary] style? *Yùn* 韞 is pronounced *yūkūn* (於昆切) and means "to wrap; to enclose; to contain."

**"All of this compilation and preface (*zuǎn xù* 纂敍) has been carried out in full accordance with [the master's] direction and guidance (*zhǐ huī* 指麾). I can only hope that those of profound discernment (*xuán jiàn* 玄鑒) who come [after], with shining [clarity] (*lái yīng* 来英), will carefully examine (*jìng* 鏡) the subtle purport (*yōu zhì* 幽致) — that is all (*yún ěr* 云爾)."** (Annotation: In one edition, the character *yún* 云 is absent.)

"纂" (*zuǎn*) is pronounced *zǐ'uǎn* (子緩切) and means "to continue; to compile; to weave together." "指麾" (*zhǐ huī*, "direction and guidance"): to raise one's hand is called *huī* 麾. It also means "to indicate; to point." "爾" (*ěr*) is a grammatical particle meaning "thus; so; that is all."

**End of the *Commentary on the Preface to the Notes on the Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi-śāstra* (成唯識論述記序釈　竟)**
