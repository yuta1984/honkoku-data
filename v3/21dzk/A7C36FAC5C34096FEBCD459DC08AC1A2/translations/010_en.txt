#### The Six Sūtras and Eleven Treatises Cited as Evidence in the *Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi-śāstra*

The commentary states: "Furthermore, this treatise here cites six sūtras, namely: *Avataṃsaka*, *Saṃdhinirmocana*, *Tathāgatotpatti-saṃbhava-guṇālaṃkāra*, *Abhidharma*, *Laṅkāvatāra*, and *Ghanavyūha*; and eleven treatises: *Yogācārabhūmi*, *Prakaraṇāryavācā*, *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra*, *Pramāṇasamuccaya*, *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, *Daśabhūmika*, *Madhyāntavibhāga*[-bhāṣya], *Ālambana-parīkṣā*, *Viṃśikā*, *Madhyāntavibhāga*, and *Abhidharmasamuccaya* — as textual evidence."

---

**I. The Great Vaipulya Buddha Avataṃsaka Sūtra (大方廣佛華嚴經)**

1. In the fourteenth year of Yixi of the Jin dynasty (418 CE), the Tripiṭaka master Buddhabhadra from Northern India (translated as "Juéxián" / 覚賢, "Awakened Worthy") translated the Sanskrit text of 36,000 verses at the Xie Sikong Monastery in Yangzhou (i.e., Daochang Monastery, named after its patron donor), producing the Jin recension of 50 fascicles (or 60 fascicles). The śramaṇa Faye served as scribe, and Huiyan and Huiguan polished the literary style.

2. In the first year of Yonglong of the Great Tang (680 CE), the Tripiṭaka master Divākara from Central India (translated as "Rìzhào" / 日照, "Sunlight") translated two passages missing from the "Entering the Dharmadhātu" chapter (*Gaṇḍavyūha*) at Taiyuan Monastery in the western capital.

3. In the first year of Zhengsheng (695 CE), the Tripiṭaka master Śikṣānanda from Khotan (translated as "Xǐxué" / 喜學, "Rejoicing in Learning") re-translated the old text at Foushoji Monastery in the eastern capital, while also supplementing various lacunae, adding 9,000 verses. Combined with the old text, the total came to 45,000 verses.

**Lower Section:**

This produced the Tang recension of 80 fascicles. Tripiṭaka master Yijing, Vinaya master Hongjing, Dharma master Wŏnch'ŭk, Dharma master Shenying, Dharma master Fabao, and Dharma master Xianshou (Fazang) all participated in the translation, while Dharma master Fuli edited the literary composition.

4. Although several passages were added to the third recension above (Śikṣānanda's), the text of Tripiṭaka master Divākara's supplemented passage — describing "Mañjuśrī pressing the crown of Sudhana's head" — had been omitted. Dharma master Xianshou (Fazang) therefore collated the new and old recensions against the Sanskrit original, inserted Divākara's supplementary passage into the lacuna in Śikṣānanda's translation, and thus achieved a text in which the wording and meaning flow continuously.

5. The Tripiṭaka master Prajñā from Kaśmīr in the Tang dynasty (translated the *Samantabhadracarya-praṇidhāna*, producing 40 fascicles).

**II. *Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra*, 5 fascicles**
*(The Jiějié jīng [解節経], 1 fascicle, translated by Tripiṭaka master Paramārtha of the Chen dynasty, is an alternate translation of the first five chapters of the Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra. The Xiāngsù jiětuō jīng [相續解脱経], 1 fascicle, translated by Guṇabhadra, is an alternate translation of the last two chapters.)*

The Tang Tripiṭaka master Xuanzang began translation at Hongfu Monastery on the eighteenth day of the fifth month of the eleventh year of Zhenguan (637 CE), completing it on the thirteenth day of the seventh month. The śramaṇa Dasheng Guang served as scribe.

**V. *Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra***
*(This sūtra has four translations in total, of which three recensions are currently extant.)*

1. In the twentieth year of Yuanjia of the Liu-Song dynasty (443 CE), Guṇabhadra from India translated it at Daochang Monastery, producing 4 fascicles. Huiguan and others served as scribes. It is titled *Lengqie Abaduo Luo Bao Jing* (楞伽阿跋多羅寶経).

2. In the second year of Yanchang of the Northern Wei (513 CE), Bodhiruci from India translated it, producing 10 fascicles. Senglang and Daozhan served as scribes. It is titled *Ru Lengqie Jing* (入楞伽経).

**I. *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*, 100 fascicles**　　　*(Expounded by Maitreya Bodhisattva; composed by Asaṅga Bodhisattva)*

Tripiṭaka master Xuanzang began translation under imperial decree at the Translation Academy of Hongfu Monastery on the fifteenth day of the fifth month of the twentieth year of Zhenguan (646 CE), completing it on the fifteenth day of the fifth month of the twenty-first year (647 CE). The śramaṇas Linghui, Mingjun, and others served as scribes.

**II. *Prakaraṇāryavācā-śāstra* (顯揚聖教論)**　*(Root verses: 1 fascicle; Commentary: 20 fascicles)*　Composed by Asaṅga Bodhisattva.

Tripiṭaka master Xuanzang translated it at the Translation Academy of Hongfu Monastery, beginning on the first day of the tenth month of the nineteenth year of Zhenguan (645 CE) and completing it on the fifteenth day of the first month of the twentieth year (646 CE). The śramaṇa Zhizheng and others served as scribes.

**III. *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra-śāstra* (大乘莊嚴経論)**
*(13 fascicles, or 15 fascicles. Root verses expounded by Maitreya Bodhisattva, composed by Asaṅga Bodhisattva; commentary composed by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva.)*

In the summer of the fourth year of Zhenguan (630 CE) at Shengyuan Monastery, the śramaṇa Prabhākaramitra (rendered in Tang Chinese as "Zuòmíng zhīshi" / 作明知識, "Lamp-maker of Knowledge") translated it simultaneously with the *Prajñādīpa-śāstra*, completing it in the spring of the seventh year (633 CE).

**IV. *Pramāṇasamuccaya* (集量論)**　*(4 fascicles; the Kaiyuan Catalogue records it as [□] recension; two translations exist.)*　Composed by Dignāga Bodhisattva.

1. In the second year of Daqing of the Liang dynasty (548 CE), Tripiṭaka master Paramārtha translated it.

**Lower Section:**

2. In the second year of Jingyun of the Tang dynasty (711 CE), the śramaṇa Yijing translated it.

**V. *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* (攝大乘論)**　*(Three translations exist.)*　Composed by Asaṅga Bodhisattva.

1. Tripiṭaka master Paramārtha from India translated it during the Chen dynasty, producing 3 fascicles.
2. Tripiṭaka master Buddhafanta from India translated it during the Northern Wei, producing 2 fascicles.
3. Tang Tripiṭaka master Xuanzang began translation on the twenty-sixth day of the intercalary twelfth month of the twenty-second year of Zhenguan (648 CE) at the Western Hongfa Academy of Ziwei Hall north of the palace, completing it on the seventeenth day of the sixth month of the twenty-third year (649 CE) at Ci'en Monastery. Dasheng Wei served as scribe.

***Mahāyānasaṃgraha-bhāṣya* (攝大乘論釈)**　*(Composed by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva; composed by Asvabhāva Bodhisattva)*

1. Commentary by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva. Translated by Tripiṭaka master Paramārtha from India during the Chen dynasty, producing 10 fascicles (or 15 fascicles). Since Dharma master Paramārtha was active during both the Chen and Liang dynasties, various texts label it the "Liang treatise."

2. Commentary by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva. Translated by Tripiṭaka master Dharmagupta from India during the Sui dynasty, producing 10 fascicles.

3. Commentary by Vasubandhu Bodhisattva. Tang Tripiṭaka master Xuanzang began translation on the eighth day of the second month of the twenty-second year of Zhenguan (648 CE) at the Hongfa Academy north of the palace, completing it on the seventeenth day of the sixth month of the twenty-third year (649 CE) at Ci'en Monastery. Dasheng Wei served as scribe, producing 10 fascicles.
