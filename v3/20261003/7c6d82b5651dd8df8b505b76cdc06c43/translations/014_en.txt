**Mount Takaoka**

O wind blowing in the morning sun of Takaoka—
it must be calling with the voice of birds yet unseen.

**Hayama Barrier**

At the gate of Hayama Barrier, buried deep in bamboo,
still the sound of snow-bent branches breaking echoes outside.

**The Narrow Bamboo Path**

The dew plays, trailing threads like a spider's web,
along the narrow bamboo path threading through the lower leaves.

**The Village That Asks of the Moon**

How familiar it has become—how many autumns the pines have lamented,
the evenings and dawns of the village that asks after the moon.

**Moon-Awaiting Shore**

There were those who played clear, pure music, coming rarely,
amidst the auspicious evening air of the waves at the shore awaiting the moon.

**Utsugi Barrier**

The hushed tones neither grant nor withhold their treasure,
at the barrier of the deutzia flower—
opening the gate and ever waiting.

---

Written beneath the moon at Kōnantei, on the night of the full moon (望, *mochizuki*) of mid-autumn (the eighth lunar month), in the twelfth year of Bunka [1815].
Kibun, formerly [of the household]

Is there anyone in the world who does not enjoy the moon and flowers? (Surely there is not.)
On a certain snowy night, the retired *jōrō-shōshō* [a court lady of high rank], sunk in melancholy…
