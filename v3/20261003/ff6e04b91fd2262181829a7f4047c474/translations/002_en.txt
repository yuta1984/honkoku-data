**Yokuon-en Garden — A Map as It Appeared During His Lordship's Residence**

Change is the way of the world, yet what is especially to be lamented is the fate of Yokuon-en Garden. This place was, in the days when His Lordship served in his great office [as Senior Rōjū], a residence granted by imperial favor, into which he poured his heart and soul over many years, arranging scenic views for each of the four seasons. As the years passed, the flowers and trees grew to cast their shade, and it was indeed a place he had designated as the home where he would live out his remaining days.

However, in the spring of the Year of the Ox in the Bunsei era [Bunsei 12, 1829], the garden was struck by a great fire, and not only were the main residence and all the garden pavilions reduced entirely to ashes, but even the trees and plants that had been lovingly tended over so many long years were lost.

After His Lordship's departure [from public life], when the residence was rebuilt, it was made considerably larger and more expansive than before, so that the area around the Senshūkan [Thousand Autumns Hall] was utterly transformed. Later still, the area around the Kagetsu-tei [Flower-and-Moon Pavilion] was converted into a riding ground, and there too not a trace of the garden's former appearance remained. Hearing how it falls further into ruin with each passing year, overwhelmed with longing for the days of its glory, I have endeavored to render the appearance of the garden in this illustration accompanying the map of the residence — the residence from which His Lordship, in his retirement, composed the verse: *"In this reign where even clouds and wind are at peace, I gaze at the moon, settled into my grass hermitage."*

I am, of course, a person of no particular learning or skill — how could I possibly capture the full splendor of such a renowned garden? My inadequacy goes without saying. Nevertheless, I have imagined the garden's appearance as it was during His Lordship's residence — from the pavilions and trees down to the very rocks and stones — placing each element in its proper location without error. Setting aside my previous drafts, I have redrawn everything from the beginning. I humbly hope that even a hundred years hence, when read alongside the *Kantoku-roku* [Record of Gratitude for Virtue], this illustration may serve as some small aid in appreciating the grandeur of His Lordship's spirit in delighting in the natural scenery of heaven and earth, and in perceiving the depth of his cultivation of elegant arts. With this hope, I set it down alongside the *Kantoku-roku* to be handed down to future generations.

*Recorded with reverence on the 13th day of late autumn, the Year of the Tiger [Jinjin], Tenpō 13 [1842]*

**Minamoto Jijō**
