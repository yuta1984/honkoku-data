Item: The distance from the front column at the veranda edge (*engigawa*) between the eave (*noki*) and the tree is one *jō* five *shaku* (approx. 4.5 m); the distance between tree and tree is two *jō* (approx. 6 m).
　If the front garden is narrow, the spacing between tree and tree may be reduced to as little as one *jō* eight *shaku* (approx. 5.4 m). In that case, the distance between the eave and the tree may also be used as one *jō* three or four *shaku* (approx. 3.9–4.2 m). One should not plant trees with a spacing between them of more than one *jō* eight *shaku*. The original ■ (illegible), perhaps if the space is narrow.

Item: When planting trees, they should be planted leaning slightly outward. If planted perfectly upright, they will crowd together (lean in toward each other) in later years.

Item: The planting of the *kaiboku* ("all-trees" / the complete set of trees) is carried out by the Imperial Court (*dairi*), the Retired Emperor's palace (*sentō*), the Shogun (*kubō*), or the Regent (*kanpaku*), or by people of the kemari households. Item: If plum trees are planted in the *ushitora* (northeast, *Gen*), *tatsumi* (southeast, *Son*), and *hitsujisaru* (southwest, *Kon*) directions, and a pine is planted in the *inui* (northwest, *Ken*) direction, this does not conform to the *kaiboku* order. If plum trees are planted in the *ushitora*, *tatsumi*, and *inui* directions, and a pine in the *hitsujisaru* direction, this conforms to the *kaiboku* standard. Although the pine is normally planted in the *inui* (northwest) position, when planted in the *kon* (southwest) position, it may be considered a provisional arrangement (*kari no kokoro*) that still conforms to the *kaiboku* standard. Whether it is a *motōe* (root/main planting) or a *sanbonue* (three-tree planting), this principle should be observed. The four trees are each to be assigned accordingly.

Item: When taking one's seat, the right foot is placed underneath and the left foot is placed on top, with both feet aligned at the level of the neck (*kubi no tōri*), and the legs are crossed and set in place. In the Naniwa school (*Naniwa-ryū*), the left foot is placed underneath and the right foot on top when crossing. The Naniwa and Asuka-i schools are schools of brothers (*kyōdai no nagare*). Although Asuka-i is the younger brother's school, in this matter of how to cross the legs it surpasses the four virtues (*shizen*). This method is adopted in our school (*tōryū*).

Item: From the moment a *kakari-nin* (a player who is "on," i.e., about to kick) steps out, they should hold the fan in the left hand. The fan should be held even when seated. When kicking, the fan and the *tatamigami* (folded paper) should be placed — when on a round mat (*enza*) — under the right knee and the round mat. If on a *tatami* floor or a long wooden floor (*nagayuka*), they should be placed to the rear and to the right. Furthermore, for those of the warrior class (*buke*) who carry a sword, the sword, together with the fan and folded paper, should all be placed together at the rear. The tip (*saki*) of the sword must not be directed toward the eave (*noki*) side. The hilt (*tsuka*) should face toward the eave when placed. This means one must not point the blade tip toward persons of high rank.

Item: After the ball-kicking is finished, one returns to one's seat and promptly takes the fan in the left hand. The reason for holding it in this manner is so that, should the ball fall and roll toward one's position, one may use the fan to hold it back ■ (illegible). One must not push the ball with the tip of the fan.
