He composed this poem:

　　*Omoi tatsu / Kiso no nunogoromo / asaku nomi / somete yamubeki / sode no iro ka wa*

(Having resolved to set forth, shall the sleeves of my hemp robe of Kiso be dyed only shallowly and then stop? — Surely not; my commitment to this mountain life runs deeper still.)

He then built a small hermitage and lived there for a time. But the provincial governor (国の守), having gone out hunting, brought a great number of people with him, and they came chasing game all the way to the vicinity of his remote mountain hermitage. The commotion was so dreadful and unbearable that he composed:

　　*Koko mo mata / ukiyo nari keri yo / tokoro nagara / omoishi mama no / yamazato mo kana*

(Even this place, too, is the sorrowful world — and yet it is a mountain village just as I had imagined it to be.)
