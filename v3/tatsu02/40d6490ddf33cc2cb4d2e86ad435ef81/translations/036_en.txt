　　Postface

Kenkō, who was posthumously granted the title of Provisional Superintendent of Buddhist Monks (*Gon-sōzu*), has now been gone for more than three hundred years. Even to this day, his writings are increasingly admired and his name shines ever more brightly. It is regrettable that a complete record of his life and deeds cannot be found. Nevertheless, we are fortunate that the *Endairyaku* exists, by which such matters may be verified. Relying also upon such works as the *Kujiiki*, various family records, the *Kenkō Kashū* (Kenkō's poetry collection), the *Yoshino Shūi*, and the *Sekirekishū*, I have gathered materials from here and there, compiled and arranged them, and assembled them into a small volume.

【Left Page】

This I have titled *Kenkō Ryakuden* (A Brief Biography of Kenkō). It is out of admiration for his supreme virtue that I forgot the presumptuousness of my undertaking. Such is indeed the case.

　The fifteenth day of the eleventh month, the *Kinojū no Inu* year of Genroku \[1694\]
　　　　　　Shinoda Atsuyoshi, a recluse of Heian, written by his own hand
