...serving at court and, without pause, transmitting the sacred divine arts to Omimaro. From this time forward, generation after generation came to be appointed to the Jingikan (the Office of Divinities). Omimaro was eventually appointed to the positions of Jingihaku (Head of the Office of Divinities), Shōshiinojō (Senior Fourth Rank, Upper Grade), Sadaiben (Left Major Controller), and Chūnagon (Middle Counselor). His son was Ōnakatomi no Kiyomaro, who held the ranks of Shōnii (Senior Second Rank), Udaijin (Minister of the Right), concurrently serving as Tōgū no Fu (Tutor to the Crown Prince), and Jingihaku. Next was Morouo (諸魚), who served as Jingihaku, Sangi (Counselor), Shōshiinojō, and Sadaiben. Among the collateral branches was Harumaro (治麻呂), who held the rank of Jushiinojō (Junior Fourth Rank, Upper Grade) and served as Governor of Ōmi, Governor of Harima, and Uchūben (Middle Controller of the Right). 【Left Page】

Hiramaro; Toyomune; Yoshizane [whose official posts and court ranks over two generations are unclear]. Kanenori (兼延), Jingisuke, Jōjō (Senior Attendant), Jugoinoge. It is recorded with the utmost reverence that Emperor Ichijō personally wrote out and bestowed the character "兼" (kane). This was because the character is the right-hand element (tsukuri) of the character "鎌" (kama, as in Kamatari). Perhaps for this reason, the character "兼" came thereafter to be invariably used in the personal names of this lineage. The children of Kanenori were: Kanetada (兼忠), Jingikan Jōjō, Jugoinoge; Kanechika (兼親), Jingifuku (Deputy of the Office of Divinities), Jugoinojō (Junior Fifth Rank, Upper Grade); Kanemasa (兼政), Jingikan Jōjō, Jugoinoge; Kanetoshi (兼俊), Jōhokumen (Upper Guard of the North), Jingigon-Daifuku (Provisional Senior Deputy of the Office of Divinities), Jijū (Chamberlain); Jingigon-Daifuku...
