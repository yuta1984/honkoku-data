(His travels by sea, too, amid rough winds and waves —) when he reflected upon the state of this world, it seemed to him that indeed the great journey of human life must be much the same.

　　*Yo no naka wo / watari kurabete / ima zo shiru / Awa no Naruto wa / namikaze mo nashi*

(Having compared my crossings of the rough seas of this world, now at last I understand — even the fearsome straits of the Naruto in Awa are nothing compared to the storms of life.)

And so, near Ninnaji, at a place called Narabinooka (the Hill of Companions), he had a resting place prepared for his death, and beside it he arranged for cherry trees to be planted, composing:

　　*Chigiri oku / hana to Narabi no / oka no he ni*

【Left Page】

　　*Aware iku yo no / haru wo sugosan*

(O cherry blossoms — I have made a promise with you here on the slopes of Narabinooka; ah, how many springs shall we spend together?)

It is said that it was in this place that he wrote the booklet known as *Tsurezuregusa*. On the other hand, the reason he is known in the world as "Yoshida no Kenkō" is said to be because, after taking holy orders, he lived in Yoshida and wrote the work there. Which account is true, one cannot say.
