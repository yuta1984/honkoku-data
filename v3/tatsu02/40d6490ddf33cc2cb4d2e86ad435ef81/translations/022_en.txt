【Right Page】

　　*Tsune ni sumu / miyama no tsuki ni / tatofunaru / ōgi no kaze ni / kumo ya harawan*

(Just as the moon that ever shines clear in the deep mountains is likened [to a fan], may the breeze from this fan sweep away the clouds.)

This was apparently written in recollection of the words of the Great Teacher Tiantai [Zhiyi], who, [gazing at the moon hidden behind a range of mountains], raised his fan and used it as a simile. Furthermore, upon looking at a pillar of the hall, he found inscribed upon it something to the effect that in the fifth year of Einin [1297], the "Nijō of Kose" — during the memorial offering of the five divisions of the Mahāyāna sūtras — had climbed up and played the koto there. Next to this inscription was written:

　　*Hiku koto wo / aware to shiraba / naki yo made*

【Left Page】

　　*(katami ni shitae / matsu no aki kaze)*

(If you would know the pathos of this playing, then even from beyond the grave — take as a keepsake [the sound of] the autumn wind through the pines.)

The inscription, half-obscured by the mist, was faintly visible — a moving sight — and beside it he wrote:

　　*Matsukaze wo / taenu katami to / kiku kara ni / mukashi no koto no / ne koso nagarure*

(When I hear the wind through the pines as an undying keepsake, the notes of that koto from long ago seem to flow anew.)

At Mudōji Temple on that same mountain [Mt. Hiei], having gazed at the moon until a summer's night turned to dawn:

　　*Tori no ne no / kikoenu yama no / kai mo nashi / satemo akeyuku / mishika yo no tsuki*

(There is no reward in a mountain where no bird's song is heard — and yet, how beautiful is the moon of this night, now fading as dawn breaks.)
