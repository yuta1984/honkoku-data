The one known as the Grand Minister — Lord Mototomo — had been greatly favored by fortune and enjoyed splendid imperial favor, but he passed away and was interred upon a mountain called Iwakura. The following spring, Kenkō wandered in that vicinity, gathered some bracken shoots, and on a rainy day sent the following poem in remembrance:

　　*Sa-warabi no / moyuru yamabe wo / kite mireba / kieshi keburi no / ato zo kanashiki*

（Coming to the mountain's edge where young bracken fronds are just beginning to sprout, how sorrowful is the sight — the very place where the smoke of cremation once rose and then vanished away.）
