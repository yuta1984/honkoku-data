【Right Page】

　　*Somukite wa / ikanaru kata ni / nagamemashi / aki no yūbe mo / ukiyo ni zo uki*

(Having turned away from the world, in what direction shall I gaze? Even the autumn evening is dreary in this dreary world.)

(Upon seeing people harvesting rice in various places:)

　　*Yo no naka wo / akita karu made / narinureba / tsuyu mo waga mi mo / oki dokoro nashi*

(As the world has come to the point of harvesting the autumn fields, there is nowhere left for either the dew or my own self to rest.)

At a place called Yokawa, he at last fulfilled his long-held wish and entered the religious life (at the age of forty-three). He retained his original name as it was, calling himself "Kenkō." This is presumably an example of what is called *jūhon ryūmyō* — "establishing one's religious name on the basis of one's original name."

【Left Page】

From around that time, a poem:

　　*Mi wo kakusu / ukiyo no hoka wa / nakeredo mo / nogareshi mono wa / kokoro narikeri*

(Though there is no world outside this dreary world in which to hide oneself, what I have managed to escape is the heart that clings to it.)

Written at the end of a document recording the *shōshinku* (offering ceremony to a living image of the Buddha) at Reizanji Temple:

　　*Ukabu beki / tayori to wa nare / mizuguki no / ato tou hito mo / naki yo naritomo*

(Be thou a means by which I may be lifted up — even if this is a world where there is no one to seek out the traces left by my brush.)
