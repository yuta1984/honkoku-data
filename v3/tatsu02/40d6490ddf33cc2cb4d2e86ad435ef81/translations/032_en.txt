An imperial decree was issued, and the court physician Wake no Kiyomoto was dispatched to Iga. However, (Kenkō replied,) "Though the imperial decree is a most gracious and humbling act of kindness, the swiftness of impermanence — the coming of death — is something that one who has renounced the world ought to have prepared for in advance," and he shook his head and declined the offer. Kiyomoto, having no recourse, returned to the capital and reported the matter to (the Emperor).

His Excellency the Lord of Nijō (Lord Yoshimoto), upon hearing this, with great concern,

【Left Page】

and having it announced that (Kenkō) was confined to his quarters in retreat, quietly made his way down to that province (Iga), where they exchanged parting words and reminiscences, after which (Lord Yoshimoto) returned (to the capital). Word of this naturally spread, and people came to hear of it. "Perhaps it was because he had for so many years kept (Kenkō) close, summoning him as a companion in waka poetry — truly it was a most rare and deeply felt act of kindness," (they said,) and everyone shed tears.
