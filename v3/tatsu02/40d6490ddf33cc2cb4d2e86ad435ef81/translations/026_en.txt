【Right Page】

When the Vinaya Master Jōben was departing for Tsukushi (Kyushu), Kenkō sent him a flint (as a parting gift) with this verse:

　　*Uchi sutete / wakaruru michi no / harukeki ni / shitau omoi wo / taguhete zo yaru*

(Though you cast aside and depart down a road stretching far away, I send this along with it, bundled together with my longing thoughts that would follow you.)

Deep in a mountain path, on a day past the tenth of the fourth month (卯月), upon seeing cherry blossoms in full bloom:

　　*Ima mo saku / hana no tokoro wa / arinagara / sugi ni shi haru wo / tou kara zo naki*

(Though there are still places where blossoms bloom even now, there are no visitors coming to inquire after the spring that has already passed.)

【Left Page】

On having gradually spent years in a mountain dwelling:

　　*Sabishisa mo / narai ni keri na / yamazato ni / toi kuru hito no / itoraruru made*

(Even loneliness has become a habit — in this mountain village, to the point where those who come to visit become wearisome to me.)

For whatever reason, Kenkō went east again and lingered for a time around the mountain pass of Kiso.
