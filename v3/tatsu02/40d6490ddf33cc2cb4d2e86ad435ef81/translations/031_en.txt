(Harinari Tada having invited Kenkō,) he presently set out and made his way down to that province. Within that land, at the foot of Mount Kunimi, in a place called Tai no Shō, he had a hermitage prepared and took up residence there. Around this time, Tonna, Jōben, Keiun, and Kenkō were known to the world as "the Four Heavenly Kings of Waka poetry." The poems he had composed since his youth were included in the *Fūgashū*, the *Shinsenzan*'i*, the *Shinshuii*, the *Shingoshūi*, and the *Shinzoku Kokin* — altogether entering five successive imperial anthologies. There, in Iga, he accumulated many years, and at a certain time, when he was feeling unwell,

he continued to reflect upon the changing state of the world unfolding before his very eyes, and composed:

　　*Aruka naki yo no / narawashi mo wasurarete / okururu mi ni wa / yume ka to zo omou*

(The fleeting customs of this uncertain world are forgotten; for one who has been left behind by so many, it all seems as though it must be a dream.)

　　*Naki hito no / kono goro ōki / yo ya sara ni / tsune to shirarête / odorokareruru*

(In this world where those who have passed away have become so numerous of late, and yet — knowing that death is a thing most ordinary — one is struck with wonder anew.)
