Word was sent (to His Excellency Lord Yoshimoto) from Nariyoshi's dwelling: on the fifteenth day, the Monk Kenkō departed this world. He must have been sixty-nine years of age. What remained in his hermitage were: a copy of the Lotus Sutra in an old hand, a copy of the Laozi in his own hand, two scrolls on Suma and Akashi, the "Chapter of Visions" written by Ton'a, two volumes of the Age of the Gods, two bundles of waste paper and discarded writings, two black hemp robes, and beyond these, only a night coverlet and vessels for his meals — so it was reported. The servant he had employed in those days,

【Left Page】

a young attendant called Inochi Matsumaru, came before His Excellency some days later and reported (the following). On the twenty-eighth day of the first month, the poem that Kenkō had composed (was):

　　*Arito dani / hito ni mo shiraren / mi no hodo ya /*
　　*misoka ni chikaki / akebono no tsuki*

(That he existed at all was scarce known to others — such was his station in the world; like the moon near the last day of the month, faintly glowing at dawn.)

This poem was conveyed and came to be heard at the Imperial Palace, the Retired Emperor's court, and even as far as the consort's chambers, and all who heard it were moved with deep sorrow, it is said.
