【Right Page】

People laughed, saying it was a charming and graceful exchange. The same priest (Kenkō), during a spring when he was in mourning seclusion for his mother, on a day when snow was falling, sent this to Ton'a:

　　*Hakanakute / furu ni tsukete mo / awayuki no / kie ni shi ato wo / sazoso shinoburamu*

(Fleeting as it is, each time the snow falls, how deeply you must be recalling the traces of her who has vanished, like the light snow that melts away.)

Ton'a's reply:

　　*Nageki wabi / tomo ni kie nate / itazura ni / furu zo hakanaki / haru no awayuki*

(Weary with grief, yet not melting away together — falling in vain, how fleeting is the light snow of spring.)

【Left Page】

As years passed, the state of the realm settled once more, and the Emperor made his return to the capital. Kenkō sent this to the residence of Middle Counselor (marginal note: Reizei Tamefuji) —

　　*Yoyo wo hete / osamuru ie no / kaze nareba / shibashi zo sawaku / waka no uranami*

(As this household has governed the way of poetry across many generations, the waves upon the shore of *waka* will settle after a brief turbulence.)

Upon viewing the blossoms:

　　*Hana no iro wa / kokoro no mama ni / nari ni keri / kotoshigeki yo wo / itou shirushi ni*

(The color of the blossoms has become just as my heart wishes — a sign that I have turned away from this burdensome world.)
