**[Right folio]**

Of their own accord, they give rise to a mind of retreat from the path; some wait, separated by a mist of 5,670,000,000 years, for the distant spring morning of the Dragon Flower Assembly [of Maitreya]; others, piling layer upon layer the clouds of birth-and-death through countless rebirths across vast kalpas, gaze afar at the autumn sky of the moon of enlightenment. Though forming a karmic connection [with the teaching] is truly precious, swift attainment of realization already seems as though it were in vain. This is precisely because the principle is deep while understanding is slight.

Next, the Pure Land Gate [Jōdo-mon] is this: in the dissemination [of the teaching] through the ten thousand years of the Latter Age of the Dharma, compassion is bestowed at the time when all other sutras have perished; and for those beings whose human lifespan has fallen to ten years, the original vow rouses [the teaching] for the sake of those with no capacity to escape the cycle of birth and death. Even those of the lowest capacity are made to receive it, and they are given the profound teaching of the highest order. Without relying upon the movements or stillness of the mind, the recitation of the Name through the karma of speech [verbal action] is encouraged. This is called the Vow that Surpasses the World, and this is called the Dharma that is Difficult to Believe. If the superiority or inferiority of one's capacity is not spoken of, then the three grades and nine levels [of rebirth] all go equally to the Pure Land; and if one does not debate the quantity of practice, then [even] a single lifetime of念仏 or ten recollections equally lead to birth [in the Pure Land].

**[Left folio]**

And yet, even among those who naturally take refuge in the Original Vow and recite the Name, most remain preoccupied with the three karmic actions [of body, speech, and mind], and others are troubled by the four deportments [walking, standing, sitting, lying down], remaining ignorant of the essence of the teaching and not grounded in the settled mind [anjin]. During such a state, those who attain birth in the Pure Land are rare indeed. This is entirely because they go against the written teachings of the founding masters and violate the testamentary admonitions of the earlier virtuous ones.

It is here that I have recently had occasion to hear of the activities of the Holy Man known as Ippen Shōnin and his nenbutsu proselytization, and I find it profoundly moving and worthy of gratitude. This person was the son of Kōno Shichirō Michihiro, of Iyo Province. In the Kenchō era [1249–1256], he became a Buddhist monk and was engaged in scholarly study; at that time, there arose a matter of deep resentment within his kinship group, and someone attempted to kill him. Though he suffered a wound, he managed to wrest the enemy's sword away and thus escaped with his life. It is said that this event marked the very beginning of his awakening to the Buddhist path.
