【Right Folio】

As is the custom of our land, in the way of waka poetry — beginning with the verse of Susanoo-no-Mikoto, "Yakumo tatsu, Izumo yaegaki" ("Eight clouds rise, the eightfold fence of Izumo"), which first established the thirty-one syllables of Yamato language — it goes without saying that Kakinomoto no Hitomaro and Yamabe no Akahito were masters of the art; and among others who engaged in this path and drew from the flowing waters of the Tomi River (i.e., those versed in poetry) were Ariwara no Narihira, Ōshikōchi no Mitsune, Ki no Tsurayuki, and Kisen of Uji Mountain. Yet in the power of causing flowers to bloom on withered trees and branches, and of softening the hearts of even the merciless demons — in these virtues (of waka), he was not inferior even to the great poets and predecessors of old. The elegance of poetry in the flower-filled spring, the beauty of the moon amid autumn foliage, the archery practiced beneath the targets on the shooting grounds, the imperial bow in the Southern Garden — and in all the seasonal

…ceremonies and entertainments as well, he was invariably summoned and called upon. On days when he attended at the courtyard of the Imperial Palace (the "Phoenix Gate"), he passed his days gazing up at the clouds above the Seiryōden (the Emperor's residential hall), and on those nights when he was not granted leave from night duty, he kept vigil on the floor of the Shishinden (the Ceremonial Hall), waiting out the night. The imperial favor he received was unlike that bestowed upon any other. His household was prosperous, reflecting the wealth of Sudatta and Tanmyari; his servants, retainers, and all the seven treasures and ten thousand riches filled his home to overflowing. Despite all this, the sovereign, still not fully satisfied, repeatedly expressed his wish to promptly appoint him to the post of Tei-i (an officer of the Imperial Guards). Yet [Norikiyo], reflecting on Zhuangzi's vision of a hundred years of glory as nothing more than a single night's dream of a butterfly, wondered how much real joy such prosperity could hold.
