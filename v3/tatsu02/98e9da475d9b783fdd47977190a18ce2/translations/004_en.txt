【Right Folio】

…lamenting the fleeting and transient nature of the world. He recalled the story of Sakanoue no Masasuke, who, having dreamed that he would fall into hell, resolved never to become a kebiishi (imperial police official) and so returned his Fifth Rank cap — and with this memory in mind, he kept ever in his heart the following scriptural verse: "Wives, children, precious treasures, and the royal throne — none shall accompany you at the moment of death. Only the precepts, charity, and freedom from heedlessness shall be your companions in this life and the next." Reflecting quietly on this: to be born in human form is said to be as rare as threading a needle lying at the bottom of the great ocean with a thread dropped down from the heavens above. And to encounter the Buddhist Dharma is likened to a blind turtle finding its way through a hole in a floating log. Yet, despite this, to fix one's heart upon the illusory glories of the world and to be bound by transient attachments to wife and children, and thereby to take on the causes of suffering in the afterlife — how pitiable this is! The joys of the twenty-five years now past seem, upon reflection, more fleeting than a dream during a light afternoon nap. Even if twenty or thirty years remain hereafter, how many memories of true worth would they yield?

【Left Folio】

Though we have received our bodily form in the Eastern Lands (Japan), we have been fortunate enough to hear, from afar, the teachings of the Western Heavens (India — the birthplace of the Buddha). Who, at such a precious moment as this, would enter a mountain of treasure only to return empty-handed, without having fulfilled their spiritual duties? For this reason, the bodhisattva Nāgārjuna (Ryūju Bosatsu) said: "Though one be wealthy, if the aspiring mind is not satisfied, this is called poverty.
