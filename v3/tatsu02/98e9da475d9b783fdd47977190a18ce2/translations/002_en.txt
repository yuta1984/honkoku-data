【Front Matter (Annotation by Kawase Kazuma)】
Since copies dating up to the fifth year of Genroku (1692) must be regarded as later printings, I had been hoping to find the original blocks of the first printing, and I was fortunate to discover it at an antiquarian book fair in the Eastern Capital (Edo). It is a rare book. I have never seen another copy.
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Kawase Kazuma

【Left Folio – Main Text】
During the reign of Emperor Toba, there was a man who served in the Northern Guard (Hokumen no Bushi). His name was Fujiwara no Norikiyo, Junior Lieutenant of the Left Garrison (Saheie no Jō). After taking Buddhist vows, he was known as the monk Saigyō. His ancestor was the deity Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto; he was the sixteenth-generation descendant thereof, and a ninth-generation descendant of Fujiwara no Hidesato, the Governor-General of the Pacification Headquarters (Chinjufu Shōgun). He was the grandson of Emon no Tayū Hidekiyo, and the first son (eldest son) of Yasukiyo. Born into a house of bow and arrow, he cultivated renown in the martial arts. He trained in the art of Yǎng Yóu Jī's hundred arrows, and mastered the text of the Three Stratagems, attributed to Zhang Liang. In literary pursuits, he studied the ancient compositions of the Sugawara and Ki families, taking as his model those who gathered fireflies and collected snow to illuminate their studies (i.e., devoted themselves to learning through hardship).
