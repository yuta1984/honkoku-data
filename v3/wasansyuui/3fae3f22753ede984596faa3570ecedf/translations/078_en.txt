The "Wuzazu" (Five Assorted Offerings) states that "the reason heaven is deficient in the northwest is because the sun and moon travel north of the lunar mansion Dou (斗)," but this explanation also fails to connect with the theory that "the earth is deficient in the southeast." Although the Dou lunar mansion is indeed a northern mansion, it is positioned in the northeast, and the rationale for this has been roughly explained above. Furthermore, the sun and moon traveling north of a lunar mansion is not limited to the Dou mansion alone — this is the opinion of someone not well-versed in the principles of the calendar. As for the positions of the two trigrams Qian (乾) and Kun (坤) in the Yi (易/I Ching), those aspects that do not conform to this principle in either the Earlier Heaven (先天) or Later Heaven (後天) arrangements have their own reasons, as this constitutes the most fundamental principle of the Way of Yi, and cannot be settled in a single discussion. It is difficult to comprehend without being well-versed in Yi studies. Therefore, I will simply omit this for now.

The questioner (Suishi) asks: "There are those who say that compared to China (Kando), Japan (Wahou) excels in archery and mathematics — what do you think of this?"

The answer: "Regarding the superiority or inferiority of Japanese and Chinese mathematics, I have not heard any definitive conclusion. Though I am deeply ashamed, as one who merely occupies the lowest rank among the warrior class, regarding archery, it is something I have never studied and know absolutely nothing about. In my own estimation, works such as the 'Lixuan Quanshu' (Compendium of Calendar Calculations), the 'Suanxue Qimeng' (Introduction to Mathematics), and the 'Suanfa Tongzong' (Systematic Treatise on Arithmetic) are books of the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties respectively, and when their methods of calculation are compared to present-day Japanese techniques, they are found to be crude in many respects and cannot be said to be superior. In Japan, since the Enpō era in recent times — beginning with Seki (Takakazu) and Takebe (Katahiro) — the succession of mathematical books published, such as 'Shita Gaku' (Elementary Learning), 'Chuu Gaku' (Intermediate Learning), 'Kantou Tangen' (Profound Exploration at the Pole), and others, have been too numerous to count, and the recent works are full of wondrous and extraordinary techniques.
