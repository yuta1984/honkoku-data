(Continued from previous page) In former times, it is written that there were bows fitted with silver tsuku (end caps/nocks). This was because when a heavy arrowhead was nocked, the bow would tend to tilt (korobi) from the bow hand (oshite) side, and so the tsuku was attached. Now, however, there is a tradition of preventing this tilt without the use of tsuku. It is also said that there are traditions regarding kutsumaki (cord wrappings on the bow grip) as well.

So then, has one become more skilled than in former times? I do not know the true essence of the matter.

Both Suishi and Sanshi said: "You always say that if one studies mathematics (sū) and explores its profound mysteries, one will come to know the Way. Please explain this view."

I replied: "This is an important matter. But the night has already grown very late. Please sleep for a while, and I too shall rest."

The guests said: "The ancients said there is true reason to hold a candle and enjoy the night. Forget your fatigue and speak to us of this."

I (the author) said: "The midnight star has gradually shifted toward the fifth watch (around 3–5 a.m.), and my enthusiasm has run dry. Whether you hear it or not is a matter of fate. If this is to be heard, it need not be limited to me alone—others too will speak of it. Things that are full are quick to wane. Let us admit defeat and await the time of our next meeting." And with that, we all laid our heads upon our pillows together.
