In the thirteenth year of Kan'ei (1636), the Kan'ei Tsūhō coins were first cast. These are the coins currently in circulation. In the tenth year of Genroku (1697), during the reign of the 114th Emperor Higashiyama, new Kan'ei coins were again cast. Although coins were cast on many occasions after this, the Kan'ei Tsūhō was never replaced. This is well known to the people.

Regarding the use of large coins: in the fifth month of the fifth year of Hōei (1708), large Hōei Tsūhō coins were cast and put into circulation. One large coin was equivalent to ten small coins. They were abolished in the first month of the following year.

Regarding native copper (wadō): In the age of the deity Izanagi no Mikoto, there already existed a white bronze (masumi) mirror. Also, during the age of Amaterasu Ōmikami, the deity Ishikoridome no Mikoto cast a mirror using copper from Mount Amanokagu — yet matters of the Age of the Gods cannot be known with certainty. Since the casting and circulation of coins had a long history predating the Wadō era (708–715), copper brought over from foreign lands was likely used to cast and circulate coins. Such, in broad terms, is the history of coinage in Japan and China, though there is much that this author does not know. For further study, one should consult works such as the *Sancai Tuhui* (Illustrated Compendium of Three Powers), the *Wenxian Tongkao* (Comprehensive Study of Literary Remains), and the *Honchō Rikkokushi* (Six National Histories of Japan).
