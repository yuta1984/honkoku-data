## Udaishō (Right Great General) Yoritomo

Yoritomo was the third son of Yoshitomo. Rising from his place of exile in Izu, he raised an army of righteousness at Ishibashiyama, and thereafter defeated Yoshinaka at Awazu, destroyed the Heike clan in the western seas, and fulfilled his long-cherished ambitions, becoming the Nihon Sōtsuibushi (Grand Constable of Japan) and Sei-i Taishōgun (Generalissimo). He was the supreme leader of all warriors.

Yoritomo had a large head, a square face, and medium-sized eyes. His mouth was large and his tongue a deep crimson. His ears were especially long and large; his chin beard was sparse; he was not particularly tall, of medium build, with a generous and ample belly. He was truly a man of fortunate destiny and noble bearing. However, he habitually began to speak and then stopped himself, and even when conversing with his retainers, he would cast his eyes downward and use a variety of glances. This is said to indicate a deeply suspicious nature, prone to deception.

## Taira no Masako

Masako was the daughter of Hōjō Tokimasa and the official wife of Yoritomo. While the shogun Sanetomo was still young, she listened to affairs of state from behind a lowered bamboo blind, and was thus known to the world as the "Nun Shogun." Her conduct overall resembled that of Empress Lü, the consort of Emperor Gaozu of Han. Her physiognomy shows some semblance of beauty, but the corners of her eyes drooped, there was a knot at the root of her nose, deep lines ran beside her nostrils, her jawbone was firm and set, her chest was narrow, and the flesh around her hips was thin as if pared away. This is the physiognomy of one deeply jealous and of wicked character. Nevertheless, Masako was possessed of an intelligence uncommon for a woman, and while she acted according to her own heart — with both wrongdoings and good deeds alike — in her later years she traveled to the capital, was granted the Junior Second Court Rank, and also made a pilgrimage to Kumano.

## Princess Matsu of the Matsudono

The princess was the daughter of Regent Motomichi. The brilliance and loveliness of her appearance was beyond all comparison. Hearing of this, Yoshinaka forced himself to become her bridegroom and declared his intention to take her as his wife. The princess feared Yoshinaka as one would fear a demon, and was so grief-stricken that she wished to take her own life. However, she thought: "If I do so, what terrible suffering will my father be made to endure? If only I myself can bear this, my father will remain safe." And so she consented to marry this unexpected man — she was indeed a filial child. Yet does a person's fate not follow their physiognomy? Beautiful as she was, her hairline zigzagged unevenly on both sides, never settling into a clear line, and the color of her ears was pale bluish-white — these are said to be signs of one destined to be wed to an unexpected husband. That even such a filial child should bear such signs is truly a cause for grief.

## Kiso Samanosuke Yoshinaka

Yoshinaka was the son of Tateaki Senshō Yoshikata. Receiving the personal decree of the Takakura Prince, he slew Jō no Sukenaga, drove one hundred thousand mounted warriors of the Heike clan into the valley of Kurikara, and riding his momentum, advanced upon the capital. The Heike, unable to withstand him, retreated to Ichinotani. There, Yoshinaka grew proud of his martial valor and exercised cruel authority. Consequently, when Noriyori and Yoshitsune attacked him, he was destroyed in a single battle.

Yoshinaka's eyebrows were thick and long, with a cowlick at the inner end of each brow. His eyes shone brilliantly, his outer eye corners were upturned and fierce — these are signs of tremendous self-will and a short temper. Furthermore, his temples were always tinged with a dusky pallor — this is said to be the physiognomy of one who would perish midway through, without achieving his ambitions.

*Written by Kakutei Hideyo*
