Though he was an old man, weakened by illness, because he had struck a vital point, he died without suffering — an end in no way inferior to that of a vigorous warrior in the prime of life. Beside him was found a written testament, which read:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;That one should die having avenged one's lord is no rare or remarkable thing. Yet, in my dotage, from the foolishness of an addled old mind, I boasted openly and with unwarranted pride that my worthless son Shōzaemon was among the company — and now it has come to this: the rumor that he is lower even than a beast,

**[Left Page]**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;not only is a cause of deepest shame before our late lord, but leaves me without face before each and every one of you. I therefore hasten, at least, to seek death without a moment's delay. I ask only that you look with charitable understanding upon the bitter disappointment and wretched fate of a sick old man, and that you be so kind as to see to whatever affairs must be attended to after my death. This I humbly entreat of you.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Twentieth Day of the Twelfth Month — Oyamada
