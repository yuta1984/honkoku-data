## [Right Page]

**Kihei (the sundries merchant)**〽 "...So you've already bought one."

**Jūbei**〽 "Oh, Kohei, you've come. I just called the seller in and bought one, but it seems rather confusing to me, I must say."

**Kohei**〽 "Indeed, I imagine it would be. Whatever one may say, they're selling today an account of something that happened two — nay, three days ago, so naturally there must be all manner of inaccuracies in it. I haven't looked at that one myself, but my son Kosaburō copied something down from the mansion he frequents — a written record, no less — and from not just one but two lords' households, where it had been formally set down. He brought a copy of it here."

Upon hearing this, Jūbei, his heart still weighed down by the absence of his son's name from the list, reached out his anxious, faltering eyes and trembling hand toward the written record.

**Jūbei**〽 "Well, that is most welcome indeed. Here now — let us compare them and see."

## [Left Page]

He laid all three documents side by side and counted the names, reading through the briefly noted accounts of each man's deeds — yet neither "Oyamada" nor "Shōzaemon" appeared anywhere. Perhaps his aged eyes had missed it? He wiped the fog from his spectacles, but the sleeve that wiped them was damp with tears, and the characters grew hazier still — the words themselves seemed to blur into obscurity.

Kohei drew his knees close and leaned in.

**Kohei**〽 "Ah, Jūbei-san. Not knowing your background, I had not properly heard your son's full name — but the day before yesterday, when news of the vendetta first reached us, I learned that your son had likewise pledged himself to the cause of loyalty. What a great and fulfilling achievement of one's true purpose this is. I have already told the neighbors, and quietly, on your behalf—"
