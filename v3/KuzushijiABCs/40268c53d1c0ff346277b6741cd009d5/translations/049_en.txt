**[Right folio]**

...this matter being something I cannot remain silent about, I find myself resolved to act this very evening — such is my state of mind. Your great kindness toward me extends across many lifetimes, in this world and the next.

> *Yama wo saku / chikara mo orete / matsu no yuki*
> *(Even the strength that could split mountains now breaks and bends — snow upon the pine)*

Furthermore, Shunpan and Chikuhei are of the same resolve and tread the same path as I. As for Kensen, things are as you know them to be. The futon I had borrowed from you — I received it and have left it behind just as it was. I humbly request that you offer a parting verse in guidance.

**[Left folio]**

I humbly entreat you in this.

　　　The fifteenth day of the twelfth month　　　　　Shiyō

　　　To Master Sentoku, my revered teacher

The above appears to have been composed on the very eve of the vendetta. Truly, one must call him a man of great character — elegant, refined, and possessed of the spirit of a true hero. Now, the next letter to be presented is one sent to a fellow conspirator, expressing his firm resolve that the moment for vengeance had at last come...
