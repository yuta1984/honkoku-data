**[Right folio]**

...some time having passed:

*Hatsune kiku / mimi wa betsu naru / bushi kana*
*(The ears that hear the warbler's first song — these are set apart — a warrior, am I)*

And then, after another fourteen or fifteen days had passed:

*Mononofu no / uguisu kiite / tachi ni keri*
*(The warrior heard the warbler sing — and straightway arose)*

By the third attempt, he had arrived naturally at this excellent verse, and Ōboshi clapped his hands in delight, exclaiming: "Ah, how admirable! This celebrated composition is truly the work of one who has mastered both the literary and martial ways — and surely there is no other than this man who could be said to have done so." He praised him greatly.

And indeed, in time Bungo became an accomplished master among haiku practitioners, standing shoulder to shoulder with the celebrated poet Takarai Shinshi of that era, and took the elegant pen name **Kogetsudō Shiyō** (湖月堂子葉). After the dissolution of the Enya household, he devoted himself entirely to the pursuits of refined elegance, and his graceful compositions were not few. Here we shall cite one or two of them, to show younger readers the manner of refined correspondence.
