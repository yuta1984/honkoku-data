&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To the neighbors and those dwelling in the vicinity —

Such was the letter he left behind. Kohei and the people of the neighborhood, beginning with him, felt deep pity for the old man's true-hearted devotion, and while passing on with bitter condemnation the tale of Shōzaemon's disloyalty and lack of filial piety, they reported the matter to the authorities. Following the directions they received accordingly, the neighbors saw to a proper and careful memorial service for the deceased.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;But what could be the reason that Shōzaemon — born the son of so loyal and devoted a man — had so changed in his heart?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Chapter Two**

Now then — Oyamada Shōzaemon, following the orders of Ōboshi, had made his way to Engakuji, the temple of the late lord's memorial observances. There, he received three hundred *ryō* to be distributed among the like-minded retainers, to cover purchases and debts, and to provide for the support of their wives and children. Carrying this sum in his breast pocket, he had proceeded as far as a place called Ōsaraki Daimachi — when the bitter cold of a snow-threatening night pierced him to the bone, chilling him through and through. To stave off the cold he had drunk, and was somewhat warm with sake — and yet, resolved though he was to cast everything aside, to consider himself already as good as dead and face death on the morrow, even eyes steeled with such resolve are no match for love, that most treacherous of temptations. Was it now his season to fall into ruin? On he went —
