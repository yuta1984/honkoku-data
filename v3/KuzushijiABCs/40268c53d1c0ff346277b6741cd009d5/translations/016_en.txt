— using his spear as a walking staff. So I asked him, "Where might Oyamada be?" The master, ever kind to those of lower station, looked at me and said, "Oh! It's Gosuke, the servant of Lord Jūbei, isn't it? Is your master's illness any better? He is an old man of over eighty years — he must be having such a hard time. And yet, being such a loyal and dutiful person, he must feel it deeply that he could not take part in this raid." And with that he walked on past. So I hurried after him again and asked, "What of Shōzaemon?" He replied, "On the twelfth day, he received two things that were entrusted to him: three hundred ryō to be distributed among the group, and the late lord's personal effects to be delivered to Engakuji Temple. Having received both of these, he had a change of heart and absconded with them — after all, life is dear to a man. If Lord Jūbei were to hear of this, he would no doubt feel deeply resentful.

**[Left Page]**

But without fail, this is Shōzaemon's cowardice — if he were to hear it from someone else, his humiliation would only be the greater. Please do what you can to comfort the old man's heart" — and with those parting words, he set off chasing after Lord Ōboshi. — Has the young master sent no word here?" Upon hearing this, Jūbei Tadahisa was struck with astonishment. His face flushed red and then went pale; he clenched his teeth and balled his fists, and for a while could say nothing at all. Kohei, too, found himself unable to offer any words of comfort, and the awkwardness that hung over the room was painful. He too remained silent, but at last —

Kohe: "No, no — at a time like this, all manner of explanations arise. Surely there will yet be favorable news."
