"—to pray that he may once again be summoned forth to office — is that not the very essence of true loyalty? If, without any regard for the peace of the realm, you make a great disturbance, you will not only betray our late lord's deepest wish, but should censure fall even upon the main family and its kinsmen, you would be acting in disloyalty toward your very ancestors as well. Above all, to heedlessly form a conspiracy and faction is something whose punishment from the authorities is bound by all reason to extend even to your descendants. Think well upon this, I urge you." Thus admonished and persuaded, Koguchi — whose heart was already in a state of disorder — listened to this smooth-tongued, cunning self-serving argument as though it were entirely reasonable, and continued piling cup upon cup of sake. Just at that moment, the evening bell began to toll, its deep and solemn reverberations echoing all around.

Into this scene came the woman O-Yasu, adorned with a beauty and elegance surpassing all else, and she seated herself beside Shō-

zaemon and attended to him with the most devoted care. At last, even a heart as firm as iron and stone crumbled away; he lost his true resolve to the sake, and grew ever more giddily carried away. Ah, how sorrowful is the frailty of human feeling in this world! Enraptured in a moment's pleasure, one allows one's soul to be stolen away, only to have a sullied name passed down and reviled for a hundred years to come. From the very beginning, this calamity arose from nothing other than the alluring charms of O-Yasu. And so: in the prime of one's youth, the vital spirit is not yet settled — and the means to guard against this lies in restraint from sensual desire. How precious is this golden maxim of the ancients!
