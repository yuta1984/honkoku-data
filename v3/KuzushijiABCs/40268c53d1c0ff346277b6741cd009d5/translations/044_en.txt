(Okano) gave (the twenty ryō to Oshizu), and before the night raid, he quietly arranged for her to take her leave from Kōno's household, placing her in the care of her uncle Heibei. Before long, word of the night raid spread widely, and it came to be known that Miharu-ya Zenbei, a merchant at the gate of Kōno's residence, was also one of the loyal retainers among the Enya rōnin, and that even his shop apprentices without exception were fellow conspirators in this faithful cause. Upon hearing these detailed rumors about Okano's deeds, Oshizu's heart was shaken with astonishment. Her uncle Heibei had long since surmised the truth of the matter, yet without revealing what he knew, he quietly grieved for the feelings hidden in his niece's heart. Though he tried his best to shield her from the ever-growing gossip, Oshizu heard it nonetheless. Her breast

**[Left folio]**

was filled with anguish and sorrow. The man with whom she had shared a deep bond — the man now praised and celebrated by all the world — she longed for him so desperately she felt she could take flight, yet not only could she not meet him: nine chances out of ten, he would face ritual suicide, and even in the best of circumstances, she would hear that he had been made a keeper of some distant island, separated from her by the sea — and even that would be counted the greatest of good fortune. The pain of hearing such things was almost unbearable. Hiding her tears, she kept herself apart, while all the neighborhood gossip and wayside fortune-tellings were solely about the fate of the loyal retainers — and she could do nothing but worry about their outcome, day after day. Though this world is said to be a world of suffering, who could have foreseen that the vendetta of Enya — to which she had no connection whatsoever — would come to be the very source of sorrow in the heart of a girl who had barely passed sixteen or seventeen years of age, still so innocent and girlish?
