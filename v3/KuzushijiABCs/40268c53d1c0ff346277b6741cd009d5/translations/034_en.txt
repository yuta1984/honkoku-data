(Continued from previous page) When he thought of his fellow oath-bound loyalists, and being in the presence of his ailing father, he felt ashamed to turn his back on their cause, and had been on the verge of reforming his intentions — yet O-Yasu would not leave his side regardless, and Bun'emon and his wife together plied him with drink and sensual temptation. With that sum of three hundred ryō at hand, it became all the harder to discipline himself, and so he fell in among the company of the disloyal.

In contrast to one who dallied in such pleasures, there was a man who kept loyalty in his heart, was rich in compassion, true and steadfast as the flower of the pine — and the pitiable tale heard of him was that of Okano, a fellow rōnin. At the mere age of sixteen, he had become a wandering rōnin when the Enya household was dissolved, and while still remaining in the castle town, his father, Kin'emon——

——had died of illness. Sanjūrō, faithful to his father's dying instructions, was moved by Ōboshi's compassion for his men and his benevolence toward the solitary and forsaken, and his devotion to both loyalty and filial piety grew ever stronger. And so, preceding the others to Kamakura, and descending earlier than the rest, in order to gain thorough knowledge of the layout of the enemy Kōno's mansion, he established a residence directly in front of it. Stocking it with a wide range of goods — textiles, sundries, dried foodstuffs, and the like — he opened a general store, useful for all manner of things. The proprietor went by the name Miharuya Zenbei, but the true name of this merchant in disguise was Sugitani Han'nojō. His shop clerks were Zenba Sarobei, Okano Sanjūrō, and Yatō Emonshichi — all distinguished men of loyalty who had changed their names and altered their appearances, and as townspeople rather than samurai——
