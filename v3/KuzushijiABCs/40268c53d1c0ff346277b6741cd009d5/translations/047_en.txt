...being a man of similarly simple and guileless nature, upon hearing this he asked, "What sort of pursuit, if I were to study it, might bring my mind and spirit to calm?" In reply, he was advised: "You would do well to study haiku composition." And so, from that very day, Bungo set out to find a teacher and begin his learning — yet, as might be expected of a true beginner, he was too embarrassed to ask anyone, and so he shut himself away at home and pondered deeply on his own. Just then, from the garden, the first song of the season's warbler reached his ears with charming beauty, and he thought: "Here, surely, must be the spark of what they call *fūryū* — refined elegance." He tilted his head in contemplation, and at last managed to string together the following verse:

**[Left folio]**

*Uguisu no / hatsune wo kiku mimi wa / betsu ni shite oku / bushi kana*
　　*(The ears that hear the warbler's first song of spring — these I keep apart — a warrior, am I)*

He quietly wrote this down and showed it in secret to Ōboshi. Yuranosuke read it and was greatly pleased, saying: "The syllable count is not yet even correct, yet here is a man who has only just begun to turn his thoughts to such things, and already he has grasped something of this elegant spirit — there is no reason he should not attain some command of refined expression. It is a pity only that his *teniha* — his grammatical connectives — are still lacking. Apply your heart just a little more." Heartened by these words, Ōwashi Bungo returned home in evident delight — but word of this spread, and there was not a soul who did not mock and ridicule him; he became the subject of laughter, regarded as a truly foolish man.
