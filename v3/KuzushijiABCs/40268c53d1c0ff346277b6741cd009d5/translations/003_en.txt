## Preface to the Iroha Bunko (continued)

If one allows one's spirit to wander freely through the text and then drifts peacefully off to sleep, then that may indeed be counted as the "merit of nursing alongside." However, in the case of the storybooks I myself have written, fine plotlines are rare, and the reader, worn out with tedium, ends up falling asleep all the same. This is perhaps akin to an infant who, having sucked at a breast that yields no milk, falls asleep in sulky frustration.

Now, the *Iroha Bunko* I present here need not wait for any judgment of its literary merit, skilled or clumsy, for it is a collection of biographies of loyal retainers already well known to the world. Even children and young ones who have only just learned their *iroha* alphabet, upon beholding these tales of devotion and loyalty, will surely feel the sudden urge to shake off their drowsiness and read on.

　　Written on a day in the second month (*kisaragi*) of the Year of the Monkey in Tenpō [1836], when the honorary name shines bright for the third time.
