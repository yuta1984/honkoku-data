## Ōboshi Yuranosuke (大星由良之助)　Personal name: Yoshio (良雄)

**[Right folio, upper section]**

In loyalty, even Fan Li and Yuan Ang could not compare;
In courage, he stood alongside Wu Zixu and Guan Yu (Lord Yunchang);
In wisdom, he bore no shame before Zhang Liang and Zhuge Liang.

Of those who resolved to give their lives for their fallen lord, there were over three hundred men. Of these, fifty broke their oath and remained behind. Those who died of illness and those who died in righteousness numbered three men and over forty.

Following Yoshio's direction and command, on the very anniversary of their lord's death, they took the head of the enemy, Moronao, offered it at the memorial shrine, and fulfilled their sworn vows.

---

**[Left folio, upper section]**

Prior to this, Yoshio had deliberately thrown himself into dissipation — feigning indulgence in women and debauchery — thereby divorcing his wife and severing his obligations to his kin, in order to remove any future danger to them. What far-sighted and profound deliberation this was. Neither in Japan nor in China has there ever before been heard of such a hero of true loyalty and righteous valor. His glorious name has shone brilliantly across the generations, and he has been enshrined as the very mirror and model of loyal retainers.

---

**[Waka poem, attributed to Ōboshi Yoshio]**

*Mizu ni utsuru*
*hana ya mo kuzu ni*
*uki kaete*
*chirishi wo uramu*
*kishi no ume ga e*

(The flower reflected on the water — rising up tangled in waterweed, then scattering — is mourned by the branch of the plum tree on the bank.)

**[Left folio, lower section — Chinese verse, rendered in English]**

I have heard that in this present age there are loyal retainers,
Who repay their country and avenge their lord, with no thought of sparing themselves.
Submitting to death, they knew only this: to uphold righteousness and cast aside life.
How fitting the joy of together achieving benevolence!
What need is there for three thousand hangers-on like howling dogs?
They far surpass the five hundred who sang the *Xiege* lament.
Laying themselves upon their swords, in a single morning they descended beneath the Yellow Springs —
What hero could keep his tears from soaking his sleeve?
