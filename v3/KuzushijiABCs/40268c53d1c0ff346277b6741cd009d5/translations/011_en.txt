— and people rushed forward, each trying to be first to obtain a copy. Yet even among loyal and righteous samurai, there is fortune and misfortune alike. It would seem that no matter how steely and resolute a man's heart — forged with the determination that he would yield to no one — even such iron resolve can be melted away when circumstance defeats him.

Now, the man known as Jūbei, father of Oyamada Shōzaemon, had already reached the advanced age of eighty-one, enfeebled by the weight of years, and had moreover been excluded from the signed pledge of the league on account of illness. Yet his spirit of loyalty was unchanged. Whenever he thought of how his son Shōzaemon had so gallantly and admirably joined the ranks of the brave, upholding his sense of righteous duty without wavering as a member of the sworn league, his own heart swelled with pride, and he forgot even his own illness.

He purchased a broadsheet — the one just now being sold, which set out the full account of the night raid and bore the names of the loyal men — and laying it upon his knees, he read it through from beginning to end, again and again. Yet nowhere could he find his son's name.

"How very strange," he muttered to himself.

🎵 *(Jūbei, muttering):* "Hmph. What a sloppy, irresponsible piece of work, hawked about town with such extravagant fanfare. Shōzaemon — the very man Lord Ōboshi [Ōishi Kuranosuke] was counting on as a key lieutenant on one front — how could anyone be so careless as to leave out his name? How dare they call this a 'full and detailed account'? A thing that drops my own son's very name from the record is no better than wastepaper — utterly worthless."

He was still muttering thus when the gate was pushed open and in walked the neighbour, the proprietor of the sundry-goods shop next door. He too had passed his sixtieth year and was now a retired householder — and being on such familiar terms with Jūbei, he came right in without any ceremony and addressed him while still standing:

🎵 *(The sundry-goods shopkeeper):* "Ah, Jūbei-san! They've already had the whole business you were talking about set in print, and they're selling it out there right now. My goodness — even laid up as you are, you must have gone ahead and bought one right away—"
