【Right page】
　At this time, the construction of the warehouse was largely completed. Plasterers and carpenters were bustling about in noisy confusion. This merchant (Mitsubaran) was renowned throughout the land as a man of great wealth; the size of his establishment rivaled the mansion of a Japanese samurai receiving five or six thousand koku. As for the grandeur and splendor of the architecture, it was unlike almost anything in Japan. In the connected rooms, many island natives had been purchased as slaves and housed there. Every day, gold and silver goods were transported from one warehouse to another. Despite possessing such immense wealth, he invited the guests to his side, shook their hands, and treated them as close companions—laying out food and drink to share over cups—without the slightest pretension or guardedness.

○Also, more than ten years ago, a ship called "Kadocho," belonging to the Hayakawa estate in Echigo province, drifted to the rocky shore on the right side of the port. Chinese men (Cantonese) tried to help by pulling it up onto the rocks on the left side, but the ship struck the rocks and was smashed to pieces.

【Left page】
　The crew members, Denkichi, Chōta, and others, were sent to "Sitka" through the arrangements of this merchant (Mitsubaran), and eventually managed to return to Japan. The merchant asked the guests that upon returning home, they relay the news of the sailors' safe arrival. Later, when the guests were in Sitka and heard from the local people, the previous castaways (namely Denkichi and the others) were stubborn and difficult to understand—their intentions were hard to grasp. When given fine clothing and good hats, they would put them all away in a chest and instead wear Japanese clothes, put on Japanese wooden clogs (geta), and stroll neatly dressed through the main streets. The whole town burst into laughter at the sight. "You, by contrast, are the true foreigners (唐人 / outsiders)!" they were told.
　《Annotation: A certain student says that one of the Hayakawa castaways is still living today, and possesses two or three garments of fine foreign silk, as well as a stone inkstone that emits a golden light when ink is ground on it.》
