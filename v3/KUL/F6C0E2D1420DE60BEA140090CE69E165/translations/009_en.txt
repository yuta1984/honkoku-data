**[Right folio]**

The merchant's temporary residence was more than ten *ken* in length and over five *ken* in width, with a horizontally sliding front entrance. It too was a thatched building. The islanders handed the castaways over to the merchant. The merchant comforted them and related the full details of their drifting, whereupon the islanders wept bitterly and could not bring themselves to leave.

○ The merchant then brought out seats and gave them to the three men, bidding them to sit. Though the merchant's dwelling was low and humble, all the windows were fitted with glass panes, and the vessels and utensils at hand were largely of glass, lending an air of splendor and elegance. The floor had no planking; instead, grass and straw were spread thickly to a depth of over one *shaku*, upon which *anbera* (rush-woven) mats were laid. Before long, a lavish banquet was prepared in their honor. The merchant said: "In my homeland we use chopsticks, but here I follow local custom and use these," pointing with a fork (*nikusashi*—a fork) toward the others. Books were also piled up throughout the interior of the house.

**[Left folio]**

○ That evening, the three men lodged together. They were given one large mattress stuffed with bird feathers and a long pillow. A small box of about three *sun* square was also tossed to them. Opening it, they found it contained a gauze mosquito net. The merchant's own mosquito net was made of hemp cloth and hung constantly from the ceiling over the bed. As the night deepened, the Cantonese merchant came outside the mosquito net accompanied by an island woman and said: "Are you gentlemen not interested? It is at the captain's suggestion." But the guests, overwhelmed by their sufferings and anxieties, had no desire whatsoever for female company, and firmly declined. The merchant's concubine was also a native of this place.

○ The following morning, when they rose, a basin of hot water for washing the face was brought in a black lacquered tray with soap added alongside. Thereupon another banquet was laid out. The guests asked: "Why do you reside in such a remote place?" The merchant replied: "I only arrived here not long ago. I plan gradually to build up my quarters and settle here properly, but it is not yet accomplished."
