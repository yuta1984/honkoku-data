While they were in this situation, an east wind arose again, blowing them along through the muddy shallows, and for forty to fifty days they drifted without sighting a single island. During this time, however, the weather was remarkably warm, and the entire crew spent their days and nights with their upper garments removed.

As time passed, one day a south wind arose, and from then on they were blown northward, north upon north, for some fifty to sixty days. As no rain had fallen for a long time, their drinking water was completely exhausted, and for about twenty days they were unable to drink a single drop of water. The hardship was truly unbearable. With no other means of relief, they took small amounts of white sugar that had been stored in the ship's hold and placed it in their mouths little by little, moistening their throats with it and barely clinging to life. Yet two men from Toba ultimately perished from thirst. After that, grief and suffering pressed upon them one after another, and the crew prayed fervently to the gods and buddhas, begging for rain, but there was no sign of any swift divine response. The suffering they endured—merely licking the drops of dew that collected on copper vessels morning and evening, barely staving off their thirst—was something beyond the imagination of any ordinary person.

Perhaps because they had been blown northward, the cold became exceptionally severe, adding yet another layer of hardship to their ordeal. At this point, when they looked upon the rising moon, they found that its appearance was the same as when viewed from Japan, and at this, the crew rejoiced greatly and took heart, believing that they must surely be drawing near to Japan. They had now been adrift for approximately one hundred and fifty days, and among the strange things they had witnessed at sea was this: when they were being carried northward, on a certain day an enormous shark, some three ken in length, appeared along with countless others of its kind—too many to number—surrounding the ship, and it seemed as though at any moment the vessel might be capsized by them and they would all be consigned to a watery grave. Also, after that, in the southern seas, they came across an area where something resembling lotus leaves grew thickly, stretching for tens of ri in width of seven or eight shaku. On one night, they witnessed the sight of lights flickering and flying about from somewhere across the sea. And there were also places where something resembling pine trees could be seen flourishing abundantly beneath the surface of the sea.
