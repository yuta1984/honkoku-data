They stayed here for about twenty days waiting for favorable weather, when one day a man came by boat and spoke kindly in Japanese, saying things such as "From here to Nagasaki is one hundred and eighty ri by sea," and brought them sewing thread, a razor, sewing needles, scissors, about two to of rice, and some pickled bamboo shoots as gifts. Since this person came to Nagasaki from time to time and was well acquainted with our country's affairs, everyone gratefully rejoiced together, thinking that his generosity must have stemmed from a desire to repay the benevolence of our wise ruler.

On another day, a Chinese person who had come to see these castaways indicated by gesture that there were some goods prepared and that they should go to that mountain. When they went there, they were given a pot, eleven pieces of cotton clothing, two to three sho of rice, and a little salted fish. Since there were many temples in the area, the group visited two or three of them. Unlike Japanese temples with wooden and gilt Buddha statues of Shakyamuni or Dainichi, the inner parts of these temples had many objects resembling what Japanese tatami-layers carry, which were thought to be items for the resident monks to sleep on. two or three temples, a person who appeared to be the head official came and said, "Why have you come here? It will not be good for you to wander about freely without staying in the designated place," whereupon they immediately returned to the ship. The whole area had many temples, interspersed with what appeared to be farmhouses, and the appearance of the peasants was such that the entire head was shaved except for a patch of hair about one sun square left at the center, which was left to hang long down the back. It was said that in the past the custom was not to shave the head, but in recent years, having been conquered by the Manchu people, this had now become the universal practice. While they had been on Batan Island, they had no way of looking at a calendar and did not know whether the months were long or short, so everyone had estimated by turns — one month a long month, the next a short month — but after meeting the Chinese people, they learned that there had been an intercalary month the previous year, and thus discovered that what they had been thinking was the first month was actually the twelfth month, and what they had thought was the second month was actually the first month — they had been off by a full month.
