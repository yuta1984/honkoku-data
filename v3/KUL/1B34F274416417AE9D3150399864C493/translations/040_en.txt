The Fourth Tale. A person from Shima drifts to the island of Taiwan and returns home by way of a Qing Chinese vessel.

It was in the ninth month of the seventh year of Hōreki, the Year of the Ox [135 years before now]. A crew of six, led by a ship captain named Koheiji from Fusemura village in Shima Province, had loaded various cargo in Osaka and were making their way toward a place called Ōosaki in Shima Province, when on the fifteenth day of that month, at the Hour of the Tiger [2 AM in modern terms], a great westerly wind began to blow. They were blown off course without knowing where they were headed, traveling approximately one hundred ri each day, and as they drifted further and further east, on many a day they could see five or six large and small islands to the north. On the fifty-fifth day, they were blown onto a tidal flat. Before long the wind ceased, but the ship's hull had sunk deep into the mud, leaving them with no means of moving forward or backward. With no other option, they had no choice but to spend the night there. As dawn broke and they gazed in the direction of the rising sun, the entire sky was colored as though brushed with vermilion, a truly awe-inspiring sight.
