The eight villains who had earlier smashed the ship's tender were brought out here. Once again they were made to rub their noses on the ground and weep, and then they were beaten one by one with thick, vermilion-lacquered cudgels, until three of them breathed their last. Though they had once tormented us, to stand by and watch them subjected to this cruel punishment right before our eyes—how could we bear to look on? Hoping at least to save the remaining five, we tried to convey this to the officials, but as words did not reach them, we pointed to the five condemned men and performed a bow of supplication. At this, the three officials gave an order and the beating ceased. The five prisoners were then made to crawl one by one between the legs of our four men, and then between the legs of the three officials as well. Again they were made to rub their noses on the ground, stripped naked, and made to remove the bodies of the three dead prisoners. When this was finished, the curtains were drawn and the three officials withdrew to the inner quarters.

After that, they descended from the castle and lodged in a townhouse, where they were placed in a room with hides and felt spread on a raised platform, curtains of gold brocade hung on all four sides, and were treated to feasts every day. The meal arrangements—one soup with five dishes, or two soups with seven dishes—were the same as in Japan, and the flavors were particularly suited to their palates. Sake with a flavor similar to Japan's finest morohaku rice wine was also provided three times daily, and in addition, one koku and six to of rice, along with coins, were given each day by the officials. Among the coins, Japan's own Kan'ei Tsūhō could be seen. Hot baths were provided three times a day in large tubs, and with no particular inconvenience, they stayed for about forty days. However, every day people who appeared to be young nobles and ladies of rank came one after another without pause, wanting to see these exotic visitors, and all of them burst into great laughter. Women and children who came to watch would draw up close and toss them sweets and the like. The attitude of treating the four of them entirely as entertainment—like pet monkeys or parrots kept for amusement—was deeply galling, yet having drifted to a foreign land and being in the position of beggars for mercy, there was nothing to be done, and they could only endure the humiliation as the days passed.
