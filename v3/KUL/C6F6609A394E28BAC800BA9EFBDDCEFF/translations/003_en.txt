(Right folio)
Setting sail from Usa-ura, at a place called "Dondodo" in the offing of Yotsu, some fourteen or fifteen ri to leeward, we attempted to catch sea bass with longlines, but caught nothing. We then dropped anchor at a place further leeward called Yasoji no Misaki (Cape Eighty), and spent the night there. The following day, the sixth, we fished in the offing of Saga, but again caught nothing. We put in at a place called Ida no Shirahama and spent the night. At dawn on the seventh, we set out some fourteen or fifteen ri from the cape of Ashizuri-yama and began fishing, when a wind arose from the direction of the southwest (the hours of the Monkey and Rooster). We attempted to steer some five ri toward the shore, when at that very moment we spotted a school of small sea bream and mackerel. We set out all six tubs of longline, and just as the Hour of the Horse (noon) had passed—

(Left folio)
—the sky suddenly turned hazy as if filled with smoke, and a wind called "anase" began to blow. We managed to haul in three of our lines, but were forced to cut and abandon the remaining three. We attempted to make for the cape, but wind arose from the leeward direction as well, and we could do nothing. Each of us took up an oar and rowed with all our might, but made little headway. The other boats were already obscured by sea haze and could no longer be seen. The wind grew ever more fierce and the waves grew rough; finally night fell.
