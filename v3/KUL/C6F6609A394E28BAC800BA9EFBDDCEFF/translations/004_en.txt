(Right folio)
We drifted in the direction of the east-southeast (the hours of the Dragon and Snake), the freezing wind piercing our bodies to the core, and all of us huddled together numb with cold, doing nothing but praying to the gods and buddhas. Denzō alone remained at the helm. When morning came, the boat had suffered no damage, but all of the oars had been swept away, leaving not a single one. The wind raged on even more fiercely. We lashed the large and small masts horizontally to the sleeping platform as a precaution against capsizing, and let ourselves drift at the mercy of the wind. Though we could see the mountains of the shore, drawing near to them was impossible, and eventually we lost sight of even those mountains. On the ninth day, the provisions of rice we had prepared were completely exhausted, and we subsisted on the fish we had accumulated from our catch to stave off starvation. On the tenth day, rain began to fall and the cold wind blew strong.

(Left folio)
We burned straw matting to warm ourselves a little and shared out the meager fish that remained. Suffering thus from both hunger and cold, the days passed and the nights broke, until on the evening of the thirteenth day, we spotted mountains in what appeared to be the direction of the southeast. We could also faintly make out birds, whereupon Denzō said: "Seeing these birds called 'tōkuro,' there must certainly be an island. Please, let us somehow be carried there." We prayed to the gods and buddhas, and that very evening, we finally drifted ashore at a single island. However, the wind and waves were still fierce, our hunger and exhaustion extreme; we first dropped anchor and remained there adrift.
