We caught the fish, ate them, and passed the night. The following day, the fourteenth, we all roused ourselves with renewed determination, cut the anchor rope, and using the ship's planks and the like, rowed ourselves toward shore—whereupon the boat struck the rocks of the reef and was wrecked. Gozaemon was the first to leap up onto the rocky shore, and without thinking, grabbed hold of the grass and hauled himself up. The rest of us followed and made it onto the island, though it appeared to be no place where people could live. Only rocks with miscanthus grass and dwarf bamboo growing thickly upon them met our eyes, and not knowing what else to do, we looked around here and there, when we discovered a cave on the shore of what appeared to be the eastern side of the island. We decided to make this our place of rest and shelter, and as for the birds called "Tōkurō," of which there were many—

(Left folio)
—we killed them by throwing stones and ate them. As for water, what trickled down between the rocks we collected using a single bucket that had been washed ashore when the boat broke up, and we also caught rainwater to drink. There were times when we thought of throwing ourselves into the sea and dying, but then we would think better of it, saying to ourselves, "Let us live as long as we can live," and so we passed our days. Before long, as it became the season of summer, the Tōkurō birds gradually dwindled in number, climbing up from the rocky shore and flying away. One day, Gozaemon and Manjirō went together and climbed the steep slopes for about three or four chō, whereupon they found a wide, flat area of land, and there was an old well built of stacked stones.
