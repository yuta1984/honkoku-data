We arrived directly at Nagasaki on the sixteenth day of the seventh month of this year, and were summoned to the magistrate's office that same day, where we were ordered to perform the fumie (treading on sacred images). After a full inquiry into the circumstances of our drifting, we were sent to the detention house, and as we were subsequently questioned in detail, the full particulars of what we reported are as follows:

Item: Aboard the vessel of Yahachirō from Isohara Village, in the domain of the Lord of Mito — a ship with hime-iro (pale purple) sails, ninety-three tan of sail, crewed by six men — we departed for Onahama in Ōshū Province on the fifteenth day of the tenth month of Meiwa 2 (1765, the year of the Rooster). We received 620 bales of rice on behalf of Lord Makino Etchū-no-kami, and set sail in the evening after about twenty-five days. On the twenty-eighth day of the same month, we arrived at the shore of Chōshi in Shimōsa Province.
On the fifteenth day of the eleventh month, the wind was favorable at the hour of the Dragon, so we set out on our return voyage. However,

when we had traveled some six or seven ri to the north, a fierce storm suddenly arose from the southwest, blowing us out to sea. We lowered the sails and cast out two anchors, but the wind grew ever more violent and we could not hold the ship, so we cut down the mast. Nevertheless, the waves breaking into the ship were high, and though we bailed out the bilgewater and worked as hard as we could, we could not hold out against it. The crew, resigning themselves to their fate, each cut off their hair and prayed to the buddhas and gods, vowing their devotion. We lowered a large rope and [pulled?]... drifting in a north-easterly direction until the evening of the sixth day, not knowing in the least where we were, though we supposed we might be somewhere off the coast of Sōma in Ōshū Province.
