In the year 1813 CE, the eleventh year of Bunka in our country (Japan), the year of Kinoe-Inu (the 51st year of the sexagenary cycle), on the first day of December in that land (Europe), the nations held a council and swore to destroy Napoleon. Accordingly, the allied nations sent a letter to Napoleon from Frankfurt (place name). Napoleon read the letter but showed no intention of reforming his ways. Nevertheless, the armies of the allied nations had already crossed the Rhine River and were closing in, while the British military commander Wellington (personal name) crossed the Pyrenees Mountains and encamped in the plains of the Garonne (place name).
