**Shōjō (猩猩)**

According to the *Yùn Huì* (韻会, *Rhyme Compendium*): The shōjō is a creature that can speak. It resembles a monkey but has a human face. Its voice is like that of a small child, and it is fond of sake and footwear (clogs).

---

**Tengu (天狗)**

According to the *Ainōshō* (壒嚢抄): As for what is called a "tengu" — the character "ten" (天, heaven) carries the meaning of radiant light and freedom, and thus represents the attainment of Buddhahood. The character "ku/gu" (狗, dog) carries the meaning of ignorance and darkness and the lack of freedom, and thus represents the realm of sentient beings. Therefore, this name signifies the non-duality (funi) of sentient beings and Buddhas. In Japan, this is written and read as *tengu* (天狗).

**Zendōki (前童鬼) and Myōdōki (妙童鬼)**

Zendōki, also called "Zenki" (前鬼), is male. Myōdōki, also called "Goki" (後鬼), is female. They are demon-deities (kishin) who dwell on Mount Ikoma in Yamato Province. En no Gyōja (the ascetic En) captured them and placed them at Ōmine. He built a temple at Ikoma, which came to be called *Kitoriji* (鬼取寺, "Demon-Catching Temple").
