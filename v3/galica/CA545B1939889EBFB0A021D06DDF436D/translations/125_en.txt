The Sixteen Benevolent Guardian Deities of the Great Prajñāpāramitā (Mahāprajñāpāramitā)

【Upper right】
【Upper section】
Xuanzang Sanzō (Tripitaka Master Xuanzang)
【Lower section】
During the Zhenguan era of the Tang dynasty, at the age of twenty-seven, he traveled to India (Tianzhu). Over a period of seventeen years, he practiced throughout one hundred and thirty-seven countries, then returned to the Eastern Land (China), where he translated the Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra, comprising in total six hundred and fifty-seven fascicles of scripture.

Jinsha Shin (Deep Sand Deity / 深沙神)
【Lower section】
He is the great serpent of the Liusha River (流沙河/龍沙河). Over the course of Sanzō's seven lifetimes, he seized (devoured) him up to six times. Later, through the teachings of Buddhism, he became a guardian of the Mahāprajñāpāramitā.

【Upper section】
Tamon (Vaiśravaṇa / 多聞)
【Lower section】
The Dhāraṇī Collection (Tuoluoni ji / 陀羅尼集) states: his father's name is Rābhada (羅婆陀), also called Bānanda (婆難陀). His mother's name is Mijaro (密闍盧), also called Sufuguang (蘇富光). The Míngshū (明疏) states: in the north there is a king called Suijōda-ō (Water Crystal Mound King / 水精埵王), whose name is Vaiśravaṇa (Bishamonten).
