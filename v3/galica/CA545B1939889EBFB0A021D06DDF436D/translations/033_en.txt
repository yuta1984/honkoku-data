【Upper Right】
■Lower Grade, Inferior Birth (Gebon Geshō)

Those of the lower grade, inferior birth have committed the five grave offenses (pañcānantarya). At the time of death, they encounter a good spiritual teacher who recites the nembutsu [with them], and through [this recitation] the sins accumulating through birth and death are extinguished. Immediately, a golden lotus flower, like the solar disk, appears before this person, and they at once attain birth in the Land of Ultimate Bliss.

【Lower Right】
■Amida of the Wondrous Observing Wisdom (Myōkanzatchi no Mida)

As stated in the *Shokyō Yōshū* (Essentials Compiled from the Various Sutras): His father was the Wheel-Turning Sacred King Gatsu-jō (Monthrise), and his mother was Shushō Myōgan (Supremely Beautiful Countenance). Furthermore, [Amida's] body is the color of red crystal (kōhari). His name was called Kyōshika (Kauśika). He renounced his royal throne and his country, took monastic vows, and came to be known as the monk Dharmākara (Hōzō Biku).

【Upper Left】
■Amida of the Mandala (Mandara no Mida)

The mudra of the Three Bodies [of the Buddha]: the right palm is displayed openly while the left palm is concealed. On the chest there is the svastika (manji) symbol. The thumb is joined with the ring finger and the middle finger. This is the mudra of the Dharma Body (hosshin) and the Reward Body (hōshin) — the two bodies. [In another mudra,] the right hand joins the thumb with the index finger; this is the mudra of the Manifestation Body (ōjin).

【Lower Left】
■Amida of the Five Kalpas of Contemplation (Gokō Shiyui no Mida)

The three characters "Muryōju" (Infinite Life/Amitāyus) represent the Three Truths of Emptiness, Provisional Existence, and the Middle Way (kū, ke, chū) of all Buddhas throughout the three periods of time. The name and title [of Amida] formed through five kalpas of contemplation is the ultimate root and source of the eighty-thousand dharma treasuries.
