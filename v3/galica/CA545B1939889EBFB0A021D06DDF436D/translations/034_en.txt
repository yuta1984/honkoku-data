**Upper Right**
■ Yamagoe Nyorai (The Mountain-Crossing Tathāgata)
As transmitted, when the venerable form of Amida appeared on the ridge of Yokawa on Mount Hiei, the Monk Genshin (Eshin Sōzu) reverently beheld it and immediately drew and preserved the image on the sleeve of plain silk. For full details, the painted image he copied (along with its engi/origin story) can be found at the Eshin-in in Yokawa.

**Lower Right**
■ Tahō Nyorai (Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata)
The Lotus Sūtra states: "In the distant past, in a world of immeasurable tens of millions of billions of asaṃkhyeya worlds to the east, there was a land called Hōjō (Viśuddha). Within it there was a Buddha, whose name was called Tahō (Prabhūtaratna, 'Many Treasures')."

**Upper Left**
■ Dainichi (Mahāvairocana) of the Kongōkai (Diamond Realm)
The Kongōkai (Vajradhātu) arises from the wisdom of the Buddha-fruit of Vairocana. "Kongō (vajra)" signifies the nature of the realm of bodhi (enlightenment). The *Dainichi-kyō Kabushō* states: "Vairocana Buddha is another name for the sun; it carries the meaning of dispelling darkness and illuminating all things universally."
(Above figure) Chiken-in (Wisdom Fist Mudrā)

**Lower Left**
■ Dainichi (Mahāvairocana) of the Taizōkai (Womb Realm)
In Sanskrit, this is called Vairocana; in Chinese/Japanese it is rendered as "Saikō Kenkō Ganzō Nyorai" (Tathāgata of the Highest, Manifest, Vast, Eye-Treasury). The Taizōkai (Garbhadhātu) arises from the realm of the afflictions and desires of sentient beings, and employs the wisdom-seal of the Tathāgata.
(Above figure) Hokkai Jō-in (Dharmdhātu Meditation Mudrā)
