The Seven Kannon

**Upper Right**
**Senju Kannon (Thousand-Armed Avalokiteśvara)**

The "thousand hands" actually refers to forty arms. Since each of the Twenty-Five Realms of Existence (nijūgo-u) has forty arms, the combined total comes to one thousand hands.

[Figure]

The Root Mudrā; the Nine-Headed Dragon Mudrā. Also known as "Daihi Kanzeon" (Avalokiteśvara of Great Compassion).

---

**Lower Right**
**Batō Kannon (Horse-Headed Avalokiteśvara)**

The reason why this honored one's responsive benefits and efficacy are so profoundly deep is that it is like a horse that thinks of nothing but grass and water, with no awareness of anything beyond — that is the meaning being conveyed here.

[Figure]

---

**Upper Left**
**Jūichimen Kannon (Eleven-Faced Avalokiteśvara)**

The three faces at the front display compassion (jibo); the three faces on the left display wrathful fury (fundo); the three faces on the right have tusks/fangs pointing upward. The faces on the right laugh with scorn upon seeing evil, and rejoice upon seeing good. The main central face neither laughs nor grows angry (fushō-fushin), embodying the non-duality of good and evil. As for the buddha at the crown of the head, this is the Thus-Come One Shōhōmyō (Correct Dharma Radiance Tathāgata) of the past age, and this is called "taika gyōin" (embodying the cause while facing the result).

[Figure]

Also known as "Daikō Fushō Kanzeon" (Avalokiteśvara of Great Radiant Universal Illumination).

---

**Lower Left**
**Shō Kanzeon (Noble Avalokiteśvara)**

In the left hand, an unopened lotus bud (mifu renge) is held. This represents the form in which [the bodhisattva] is on the verge of causing the yet-unopened Lotus of the Wondrous Dharma of all sentient beings to bloom open.

[Figure]

The right hand forms the mudrā of Great Compassion, Bestowing Fearlessness (Daihise-muʾi-no-in). Also known as "Daiji Kanzeon" (Avalokiteśvara of Great Loving-Kindness).
