【Celestial Deities (Shoten)】

■The Solar Orb (Nichirin)
In the "Dictionary of Names and Meanings" (Myōgi-shū), it is called "Suriya"; in this land it is called the Solar Orb.
The "Sūtra on the Creation of Heaven and Earth" states that the Buddha caused the Bodhisattva Hōō (Precious Response) to create the sun.
The "Baihu Tong" (White Tiger Compendium) states: the sun's diameter is one thousand ri, its circumference three thousand ri, and it descends seven thousand ri below heaven.

■The Lunar Orb (Gachirin)
The "Dictionary of Names and Meanings" states: "Soma" — in this land it is called the Lunar Deity.
The "Sūtra on the Creation of Heaven and Earth" states that the Buddha caused the Bodhisattva Kichijō (Auspicious) to create the moon.

■The Northern Dipper (Hokuto)
The "Analects" refers to it as the "Northern Star" (Hokushin).
The "Treatise on Astronomy" (Tenmonshi) refers to it as the "Northern Pole" (Hokukyoku).
The palace-dwelling of the great star spans 720 ri in circumference; the middle stars span 480 ri; the small stars span 120 ri.

■The Morning/Venus Star (Myōjō)
The Morning Star is that which appears in the interval between night and day — when the moon has already hidden itself and the sun has not yet revealed itself. It is the complete whole of the One Suchness (ichinyō), undivided between light and dark, delusion and enlightenment.
Therefore, when the two characters are combined they make three: the character for "bright" (明) is composed of the sun (日) and moon (月) placed side by side, and so the two characters "Morning Star" (明星) together contain the three luminaries (sankō).
