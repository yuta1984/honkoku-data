## The Twenty-Five Bodhisattvas (continued)

---

### 【Upper Right】Kōmyōō Bodhisattva (光明王菩薩 / King of Radiant Light)

First, the term "kōmyō" (radiant light) represents the manifestation of wisdom. "Ō" (king) signifies that which is supremely exalted and excellent. The phrase reads: "May my radiant light surpass that of the sun, the moon, and all the Buddhas." It is for this reason that the name "King of Radiant Light" is given. This is also found in the *Engaku Ryakushō* (Abridged Commentary on the *Sūtra of Perfect Enlightenment*).

---

### 【Lower Right】Sangaie Bodhisattva (山海慧菩薩 / Wisdom of Mountains and Seas)

The *Gensan* (Commentary), volume eight, states: "Holding the wisdom of the treasury of treasures, profound as the sea and immeasurable; as difficult to look up to as a great mountain — hence the name Sangaie (Wisdom of Mountains and Seas)." The meaning of the passage is that, because the height and depth of this bodhisattva's wisdom and virtue resemble a mountain and the sea, the name was thus bestowed.

---

### 【Upper Left】Kongōzō Bodhisattva (金剛蔵菩薩 / Vajragarbha / Storehouse of the Vajra)

In Sanskrit, this is called *vajra* (伐羅闍 / *batsu-ra-sha*), which is rendered into Chinese as "kongō" (金剛). Kongō (vajra) is that which is both supremely hard and supremely sharp. The wisdom of this bodhisattva is likewise so. That neither afflictions (kleśa) nor external demons can violate or disturb it — this is the quality of being "hard." That it can shatter all obstacles and break through the doubts and delusions of people — this is the quality of being "sharp." Because this bodhisattva possesses inexhaustible merit, the term "storehouse" (藏/zō) is applied.

---

### 【Lower Left】Kegon Bodhisattva (華厳菩薩 / Adorned with Flowers / Avatamsaka)

In the passage concerning the upper direction in the *Amitābha Sūtra* (*Amida-kyō*), there appears the name "Zashshoku Jitsu Kegon Shin Butsu" (雑色実華厳身仏, the Buddha of Variegated-Flower-Adorned Body). This refers to the fact that, in the causes of one's own practice (jigyo), the flowers, like precious jewels, adorn the body of the fruit-virtue (the body of enlightenment). This interpretation is found in the *Daiyu Kuge* (大佑句解). Ultimately, the name of this bodhisattva-being reveals the principle of cause and effect.
