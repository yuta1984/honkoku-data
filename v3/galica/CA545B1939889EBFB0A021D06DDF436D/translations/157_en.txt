## The Ten Great Disciples

---

**#1 Śāriputra (舎利弗, Sharihotsu)**
[Translation: Śarīra-putra / "Body-born Son"]　Foremost in Wisdom

The *Nirvāṇa Sūtra* states: his mother's name was called "Śāri" (舎利). The name was established from the mother's name. Thus he is called "Śāriputra" (舎利弗). The character *futsu* (弗) in Chinese (Qin) means "son" (子). It is also translated as "Śarīra-putra" (身子, "son of the [beautiful] body"). The *Monku* (*Fahua wenju*) states: this woman (Śāriputra's mother) possessed a beautiful and shapely body. Because he was born from that body, he is called "Son of the Body" (身子).

**#2 Maudgalyāyana (目犍連, Mokukenren)**
[Translation: Caisher of Beans (採菽)]　Foremost in Supernatural Powers

Kumārajīva states: "Maudgalyāyana" (目連) is a Brahmin family name. His personal name was "Kolita" (拘律陀). "Kolita" is the name of a tree; he was a child obtained through his parents' prayers to the spirit of that tree.

The *Vinaya-nāmapada* translates the name as "Caisher of Beans" (採菽). His parents were fond of eating beans (*shuku*/豆), and this was used to mark (establish) the child's name.

**#3 Mahākāśyapa (大迦葉, Dai Kashō)**
[Translation: Drinking Light (飲光)]　Foremost in Dhūta (ascetic) Practices

The *Monku* translates this as "Great Tortoise Clan" (大亀氏). The *Myōgi* states: he was named "Pippalāyana" (畢鉢羅). He was a child born after his parents prayed to a tree spirit. Thus he took the name (of that tree).

The reason for the title "Great" (大): if one considers what it represents, it is because of the greatness of his wisdom, the greatness of his virtue, and the greatness of his mind—therefore he is called "Great."

The *Monku* states: Kāśyapa's bodily light (aura) was capable of outshining all things. Therefore he is translated as "Drinking Light" (飲光)—his light consumes/absorbs all other light.

---

**#4 Aniruddha (阿那律, Anaristu)**
[Translation: Without Poverty (無貧)]　Foremost in the Heavenly Eye (Divyacakṣus)

The *Myōgi* states: In a past life during a time of great famine, he once offered millet rice (稗飯) to a Pratyekabuddha (辟支仏). Because of this, he was reborn among gods and humans and received the pleasures of his wishes. From that time onward, he has never known want or lack. Therefore he is named "Without Poverty" (無貧).
