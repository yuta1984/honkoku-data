## The Seven Kannon (continued)

### **Nyoirin Kannon (Wish-Fulfilling Wheel Avalokiteśvara)**

The text of Nyoirin's original vow (*honsei*) states: "I vow that within [the realm of] great compassion, not a single person shall fail to fulfill the vow of two lifetimes; that I shall not return [any being] remaining amid false deception and sinful transgressions [without returning them] to Original Enlightenment (*hongaku*); and that I shall never abandon great compassion." Nyoirin is the Transforming Lord of the Celestial Realm (*tendō ke-shu*), and the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel (*nyoiju*) is accordingly the supreme treasure of the heavens.

[Illustration]

Also known as "Daibonshinen Kanzeon" (Avalokiteśvara of Great Brahmic Profound Depth).

---

### **Juntei Kannon (Cundī Avalokiteśvara)**

The *Shikan* (*Mohe Zhiguan*) states: "Entering into samādhis such as the Kannon-nyoyō samādhi and others, [this bodhisattva] causes all those in the human realm to break through the Three Obstructions (*sanshō*), subjugate arrogance and conceit (*kyōman*), and behold their Buddha-nature."

[Illustration]

Also known as "Ten'nin Jōbu Kannon" (Avalokiteśvara, the Great Man of Gods and Humans).

---

### **Fukūkenjaku (Amoghapāśa — Avalokiteśvara of the Unfailing Lasso)**

The *Senju-kyō* (*Thousand-Armed [Kannon] Sūtra*) states: "The Buddha said: this bodhisattva is named Kanzeon Jizai, and is also named Kenjaku (Lasso)." The meaning is this: [this bodhisattva] scatters the bait of the *Myōhō Renge* (Wondrous Dharma Lotus) upon the great ocean of birth and death, and with the mind-concentrated, unfailing lasso (*fukū no saku*), draws up the fish of sentient beings and sends them to the far shore of bodhi (enlightenment).

[Illustration]

---

### **Yōryū Kannon (Willow-Branch Avalokiteśvara)**

Kannon's responsive compassion (*ōshin jihi*) is such that, in whatever manner, it conforms to the inclinations and desires (*gyōyoku*) of each being's capacity — just as a willow branch sways and bends in the spring breeze. For this reason, [Yōryū Kannon] encompasses all of the Six Kannon (*roku kannon*) in its entirety.
