**Upper Right**
**Nyoirin Kannon (Cintāmaṇicakra Avalokiteśvara)**

The text of Nyoirin (Kannon)'s fundamental vow (honzei) states: "In my vow, within great compassion, I shall not leave a single person without fulfilling the wishes of both worlds [this life and the next]; I shall not remain without returning to Original Enlightenment (hongaku) even amid delusion and sinful transgressions; I shall never abandon great compassion." Nyoirin [Kannon] is the Lord of Transformation of the Heavenly Realm, and thus the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel (nyoi-ju) is the supreme treasure of the heavens.

[Figure]

Also known as "Daibonshin'en Kanzeon" (Avalokiteśvara of Great Brahmic Profound Depth).

---

**Lower Right**
**Juntei Kannon (Cundī Avalokiteśvara)**

The *Shikan* (Mohe Zhiguan) states: By entering samādhis such as the Kannon Samādhi, the Nyoyō Samādhi, and other such states of meditative absorption, [this bodhisattva] causes all beings in the human realm to break through the Three Obstructions (sanjō), to subdue arrogance and pride (kyōman), and to behold their Buddha-nature (busshō).

[Figure]

Also known as "Tennin Jōbu Kannon" (Avalokiteśvara, the Great Man among Heavenly Beings).

---

**Upper Left**
**Fukū Kenjaku (Amoghapāśa Avalokiteśvara)**

The *Senju-kyō* (Thousand-Armed [Avalokiteśvara] Sūtra) states: The Buddha declared, "This bodhisattva is named Kanzeon Jizai (Avalokiteśvara), and is also named Kenjaku (Pāśa / Lasso)." ○ The meaning is this: [the bodhisattva] casts the bait of the Lotus of the Wondrous Dharma upon the great ocean of birth-and-death (saṃsāra), and with the lasso of Mindful-Recollection and Unfailing Compassion (shinnen fukū no nawa), draws up the fish of sentient beings and delivers them to the far shore of enlightenment (bodhi).

[Figure]

---

**Lower Left**
**Yōryū Kannon (Willow-Branch Avalokiteśvara)**

Kannon, with a mind of responsive compassion (ōshin jibo), accords with and responds to the desires and inclinations (gyōyoku) of each being's capacity (ki) in all circumstances — just as a willow branch sways in the spring breeze. For this reason, [Yōryū Kannon] encompasses all Six Kannon in totality.
