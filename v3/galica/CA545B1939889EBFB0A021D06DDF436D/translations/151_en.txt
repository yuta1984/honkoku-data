**Tennin (天人, Heavenly Beings)**

The "pleasures" of the tennin are as follows: the floral garlands (keman) upon their heads never wither through the four seasons; their feathered robes never become soiled; they never perspire; and they never blink their eyes. But when the time of their death arrives, these pleasures all fade away, and they fall into the sorrow of the "Five Signs of Decay" (gosui).

**Karyōbinga (迦陵頻伽)**

Also known as "Kyarobinga" (竭羅頻伽). Translated into Chinese characters as *Myōsei-chō* (妙声鳥, "Bird of Wondrous Voice"), and also called *Kōon-chō* (好音鳥, "Bird of Beautiful Sound").

---

**Akusen Sennin (偓佺仙人, The Immortal Akusen)**

The *Ressenден* (*Liexian zhuan*, 列仙傳, *Biographies of Exemplary Immortals*) states: Akusen presented pine seeds to Emperor Yao (尭). Yao had no time to consume them himself, but the people of that time who received and consumed the seeds all lived to be two or three hundred years old.

---

**Sekishōshi Sennin (赤松子仙人, The Immortal Sekishōshi)**

The *Shinsen-den* (*Shenxian zhuan*, 神仙傳, *Biographies of Divine Immortals*) states: Kō Shōhei (黄初平, Huang Chūping) was fifteen years old when he was in the mountains herding sheep. A Daoist master, noticing something extraordinary in his eyes, immediately took him to Mount Kinkazan (金華山), where he remained for over forty years. He came to call himself "Shōshi" (松子, "Master Pine").
