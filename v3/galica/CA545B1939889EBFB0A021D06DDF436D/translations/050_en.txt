The Twenty-Five Bodhisattvas (continued)

**Shishiku Bodhisattva (Lion's Roar Bodhisattva)**
"Lion" is called "kara" (迦羅) in Sanskrit. This is translated into Chinese as "shishi" (lion), or alternatively rendered as "mui" (fearlessness). The reason this name is bestowed upon this bodhisattva is that, in preaching the Dharma and saving sentient beings, he has attained the majestic virtue of fearlessness. The lion is the king of beasts, for wherever it roams, it knows no fear.

**Daividoku Bodhisattva (Great Majestic Virtue Bodhisattva)**
"Great" (大) signifies universality and vastness. "Majestic" (威) signifies authority and power. "Virtue" (徳) signifies moral virtue and meritorious qualities. "King" (王) signifies sovereign freedom. Ultimately, because the great moral power of mighty authority clearly manifests in the boundless compassion for saving sentient beings, this bodhisattva is called "Great Majestic Virtue King."

**Jōjizaiō Bodhisattva (King of Sovereign Meditation Bodhisattva)**
"Jō" (定) signifies the meaning of meditation (禅定). Together, they convey the meaning of unhindered freedom and sovereign mastery.

**Daijizaiō Bodhisattva (Great Sovereign King Bodhisattva)**
The *Nirvana Sutra* states: "Great means that its nature is vast and expansive, just as empty space is." The *Sutra of Infinite Life* states: "With regard to all things in the ten thousand phenomena, one is freely sovereign according to one's will." This is because this bodhisattva is greatly sovereign and his miraculous transformations are without hindrance.
