Twenty-Five Bodhisattvas (continued)

【Upper Right】
■Shishiku Bodhisattva (Bodhisattva of the Lion's Roar)
"Lion" (獅子, shishi) is rendered in Sanskrit as "kāra" (迦羅). In Chinese translation it becomes "shishi" (lion), or alternatively it is translated as "fearlessness" (無畏, mui). This name is bestowed upon this bodhisattva because, in the act of preaching the Dharma and saving sentient beings, the bodhisattva has attained the awe-inspiring virtue of fearlessness (mui).
The lion is the king among beasts, for it moves and acts without any fear.

【Lower Right】
■Daīitoku Bodhisattva (Bodhisattva of Great Awesome Virtue)
"Great" (大, dai) signifies the meaning of universal and all-encompassing. "Awesome" (威, i) signifies the meaning of majestic power. "Virtue" (徳, toku) signifies the meaning of virtuous principle and meritorious virtue. "King" (王, ō) signifies the meaning of sovereign freedom.
In sum, because the moral virtue of great majesty is brilliantly manifest, and the great compassion for saving sentient beings is freely and boundlessly expressed, this bodhisattva is called "the King of Great Awesome Virtue."

【Upper Left】
■Jōjizaiō Bodhisattva (Bodhisattva of the King of Meditation's Sovereign Freedom)
"Jō" (定) signifies the meaning of meditative concentration (zenjo/dhyāna). "Jizai" (自在) signifies the meaning of unrestricted freedom and absence of hindrance.

【Lower Left】
■Daijizaiō Bodhisattva (Bodhisattva of the King of Great Sovereign Freedom)
The Nirvāṇa Sūtra (Nehan-kyō) states: "As for 'great' — its nature is vast and all-encompassing, like empty space, without measure."
The Sūtra of Immeasurable Life (Muryōju-kyō) states: "With regard to all things and phenomena, [this bodhisattva acts] freely according to will."
This is because [this bodhisattva] is greatly sovereign and free, with divine transformations unobstructed and without hindrance.
