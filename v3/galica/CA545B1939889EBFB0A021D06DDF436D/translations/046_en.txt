## 【Upper Right】Samantabhadra Bodhisattva (Fugen Bosatsu)

In Sanskrit, this bodhisattva is called "Viśvabhadra" (邲輸䟦陀) or "Samantabhadra" (三曼䟦陀), translated as "Universal Worthiness" (普賢 / Fugen). The *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka Sūtra* (悲華経) states: "My practice shall certainly surpass that of all other bodhisattvas."

The Buddha declared: "For this reason and through these karmic conditions, I now change your name and call you 'Samantabhadra' (Universal Worthiness)."

---

## 【Lower Right】Dhāraṇī Bodhisattva (Darani Bosatsu)

In Sanskrit, called "Dhāraṇī," rendered in Chinese as "Zōji" (総持, "All-Encompassing Retention"). According to the *Fanyi Mingyi Ji* (名義集 / Collected Meanings of Translated Terms): "All-Encompassing Retention means to hold fast to goodness so that it is never lost, and to hold back evil so that it never arises."

---

## 【Upper Left】Dharmeśvararāja Bodhisattva (Hō Jizai Ō Bosatsu)

The term "Dharma" (法) refers to the Buddha-Dharma, and "Sovereign of Self-Mastery" (自在王) signifies the quality of perfect freedom and unobstructed activity. This is an alternate name for Mañjuśrī. In Sanskrit, called "Mañjuśrī" (曼殊師利), translated as "Wondrous Virtue" (妙徳). In the *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka Sūtra* (悲華経), this bodhisattva is referred to as "Joyful Treasury Maṇi Jewel Accumulation Buddha" (歓喜蔵摩尼宝積仏), and in the *Śūraṅgama Sūtra* (楞厳経), as "Dragon-Lineage Supreme Venerable King Buddha" (龍種上尊王仏).

---

## 【Lower Left】White Elephant King Bodhisattva (Byakuzō Ō Bosatsu)

The white elephant symbolizes and manifests "principle" (理 / *ri*). "King" (王) signifies unobstructed, freely sovereign activity. This bodhisattva, in the essential nature of the bodhisattva-being (sattva), is fully endowed with all ten thousand dharmas, and freely wields wondrous function for the sake of liberating sentient beings. The name is derived from this bodhisattva's majestic and awe-inspiring appearance (威相).
