Twenty-Five Bodhisattvas (continued)

【Upper Right】
■Nisshōō Bodhisattva (日照王菩薩 — King of Solar Illumination)

With the sunlight of wisdom, this bodhisattva illuminates and dispels the darkness of sentient beings' delusion. Thus, the name is derived from this virtue of wisdom.

The *Avataṃsaka Sūtra* (Huayan jing) states: "The sun of wisdom illuminates the world and dissolves the clouds of birth-and-death."

【Lower Right】
■Shūhōō Bodhisattva (衆宝王菩薩 — King of Manifold Treasures)

"Shū" (衆) is read as *moromoro* (many, manifold). "Hō" (宝) is glossed as "precious" and read as *takara* (treasure). "Ō" (王) carries the meaning of sovereign freedom (jizai). Thus, the meaning is "the king of manifold treasures." "Treasure" here refers to the seven precious gems (the Seven Treasures), as well as the seven types of kingly treasures. Not limited to these, all treasures are combined to form this bodhisattva's name.

【Upper Left】
■Gakkōō Bodhisattva (月光王菩薩 — King of Moonlight)

The virtues of the moon-disc of this world — its threefold virtues and other qualities of excellence — are fully possessed by this bodhisattva. Therefore, these qualities are directly expressed in and embodied by the bodhisattva's name and title.

【Lower Left】
■Zanmai Bodhisattva (三昧菩薩 — Samādhi)

Because this bodhisattva attains perfect freedom within all the various samādhis, this name is taken. The word *zanmai* (三昧) is properly written *sanmaji* (三摩地, samādhi in Sanskrit), which is rendered into Chinese as *shōjō* (正定, right concentration) and translated as *shōshin-gyōsho* (正心行処, the place where the mind is rightly directed in practice).
