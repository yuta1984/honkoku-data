[Since Murakami had sought reinforcements from Kamakura,] Mochiuji granted the request and, appointing Momoi as commander, was about to dispatch troops. Upon hearing this, Uesugi Norizane, the Governor of Awa Province, remonstrated: "Shinano is not under the jurisdiction of the Kantō; it should be governed by the orders of the Shogun in Kyoto. How can it be appropriate for Mochiuji to involve himself in this matter?" As a result of this objection, the plan was abandoned. When the Lord of Shōjōin (Yoshimochi) passed away without an heir, Mochiuji had harbored hopes of going up to the capital and becoming Shogun. However, since Yoshinori had returned to secular life and assumed the position of Military Commander (Shogun), Mochiuji was constantly displeased and sought to attack Kyoto. Because Norizane repeatedly remonstrated with him, the relationship between lord and retainer became strained.

In the ninth year of Eikyō [1437], fourth month, Mochiuji of Kamakura secretly ordered Uesugi Norinao and Isshiki Naokane to kill Uesugi Norizane. The plot was discovered, and Kamakura fell into an uproar. Mochiuji himself went to Norizane's residence in Yamanouchi to effect a reconciliation. Norinao and Naokane were expelled from Kamakura, and for a time there was peace. On the twenty-second day of the tenth month, an imperial visit (gyōkō) was made to Yoshinori's residence, and the Kampaku and all the court officials attended in procession, while poetry and music (were performed at the banquet).
