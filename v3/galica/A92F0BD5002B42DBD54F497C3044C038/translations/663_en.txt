In the second month, Mochimoto resigned as Chancellor (Shōkoku/Regent). In the third month, he was restored to office and became Kampaku (Imperial Regent for an adult Emperor). In the fourth month, Yoshinori wrote in his own hand the founding legend (engi) of Ōjin and Jingū and presented it to the Konda Hachimangū shrine in Kawachi Province. In the sixth month, the Xuanzong Emperor of Ming China sent a letter to Yoshinori, addressing him as "Minamoto no Yoshinori, King of Japan." In the eighth month, Yoshinori resigned as Great General (Sei-i Taishōgun). In the tenth month, the Retired Emperor Go-Komatsu, now a Buddhist monk (Hōō), passed away at the age of fifty-seven.

In the sixth year of Eikyō [1434], fifth month, a Tang (Chinese) vessel came to pay court.

In the seventh year of Eikyō [1435], fourth month, Takatsukasa Dainagon Fusahira was appointed to the position of Naidaijin (Minister of the Center). In the eighth month, there was an incident of forceful remonstration (gōso) by the Sanmon (Enryakuji temple), and the divine palanquin (mikoshi) of Hiyoshi Shrine was moved in procession. Yoshinori dispatched warriors to attack Mount Hiei (Eizan).

In the eighth year of Eikyō [1436], there arose a conflict between Ogasawara Masayasu, the Military Governor (shugo) of Shinano Province, and a certain Murakami, a warrior of the same province, which led to armed battle. Murakami sought reinforcements from Kamakura (the Kamakura府).
