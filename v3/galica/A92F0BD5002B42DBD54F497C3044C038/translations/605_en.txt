In the ninth month, Manju-ji Temple was added to the ranks of the Gozan (Five Mountains). Tōfuku-ji Temple was to be stripped of its Gozan status due to its violation of the regulations of the warrior government. The temple monks apologized, but (the punishment) remained unchanged. In addition, regulations governing Zen Buddhism were established. In the eleventh month, Yoshimitsu affixed his seal (hanpan) for the first time, and the sacred landholdings (shinryō) of Iwashimizu (Hachiman Shrine) were donated. At this time, Yoshimitsu was fifteen years of age. This year, the Southern Court changed its era name to Bunchū.

In the third month of (Ōan) 6th year, Hosokawa Saemon no Sa Ujiharu encamped at Amagasaki for the suppression of the Southern forces. In the sixth month, the envoy-monks Chūyū and Muichi of the Great Ming (China) arrived in the capital from Chinzei (Kyushu) and were lodged at Saga. Their purpose was as follows: although the Great Ming had already dispatched envoys to Japan three times, they had been intercepted by the Kikuchi clan in Tsukushi (northern Kyushu) and were unable to reach the capital. Therefore, the two monks were sent (to accomplish this mission). Yoshimitsu was astonished upon hearing this. In the eighth month, the Southern Emperor Chōkei-in abdicated in favor of his younger brother, Prince Hiroshige (Kōgon-ō), and fled from Yoshino. The remnants of the Southern forces encamped at Amano in Kawachi Province and launched a night attack on the encampment of the Kyoto forces.
