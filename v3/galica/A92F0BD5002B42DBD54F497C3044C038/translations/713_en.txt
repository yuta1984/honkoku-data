In the first month of the second year of Meiō [1493], Regent [Kanpaku] Fuyuyoshi was appointed Grand Minister of State [Daijō-daijin]. In the third month, Yoshimura advanced toward Kawachi Province and struck down Hatakeyama Yoshitoyo. Hatakeyama Masanaga accompanied him. Yoshitoyo was the son of Yoshihiro [Yoshinari]. In the third month, Fuyuyoshi resigned as Regent. Konoe Minister of the Right [Udaijin] Hisamichi became Regent. In the fourth month, Yoshimura encamped at Shōgakuji Temple. Yoshitoyo attacked Shōgakuji. Hosokawa Masamoto, having been called upon by Yoshitoyo, joined forces and broke through Shōgakuji. Hatakeyama Masanaga was killed in battle. His son Naoyoshi fled to Kishū [Kii Province]. Yoshimura was captured by Masamoto and confined in the residence of his [Masamoto's] retainer, Mononobe no Kii no Kami. By Masamoto's arrangement, Yoshimichi was summoned from Izu Province and brought up to the capital, where he was installed as lord [of the shogunate]. Yoshimichi was elevated to Junior Fifth Rank Lower Grade [Jogo-i-ge].

In the same month, Tokudaiji Atsukiyo resigned as Minister of the Left [Sadaijin]. Kazan-in, former Minister of the Right [Udaijin], Masanaga was appointed Minister of the Left. In the sixth month, Yoshimura secretly escaped and headed to Etchū Province, and from there turned back again, proceeded to Suō Province, relied upon Ōuchi no Suke Yoshioki, and spent his days there.
