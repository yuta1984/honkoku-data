In the fourth month of the third year [of Kōtoku, 1454], Hatakeyama Iyono-kami (Governor of Iyo Province) Yoshinari and Hatakeyama Owari-no-kami (Governor of Owari Province) Masanaga disputed over the headship of the Hatakeyama clan. Originally, because Tokuhon had no children, he had adopted Masanaga — the son of his younger brother Mototomi — and made him heir. Subsequently, when a natural-born son, Yoshinari, was born, Masanaga was set aside and Yoshinari was made heir. Masanaga was expelled, and Yoshinari petitioned for and received a vermillion-sealed document (migyōsho) ordering Masanaga's punishment. Nevertheless, Hosokawa Katsumoto and Yamana Sōzen, among others, favored Masanaga. Therefore, Masanaga secretly hid himself in Katsumoto's residence, while his followers were concealed at Sōzen's place.

In the seventh month, Nijō Mochimichi resigned both as Kampaku (Imperial Regent) and as Udaijin (Minister of the Right). Takatsukasa Sadaijin (Minister of the Left) Fusahira was appointed Kampaku, and the former Naifu (Minister of the Center) of the Tōin clan, Sanehiro, was appointed Udaijin.

In the eighth month, as Yamana Sōzen and his associates were gathering Hatakeyama's retainers and rōnin (masterless samurai), the capital fell into unrest. Consequently, Yamana Sagami-no-kami (Governor of Sagami Province) Norichika, Hosokawa Hyōbu-no-shōyū (Junior Assistant Minister of Military Affairs) Katsuhisa, Takeda (of uncertain given name), and others stood guard over the Muromachi-dono (the Shogun's palace).
