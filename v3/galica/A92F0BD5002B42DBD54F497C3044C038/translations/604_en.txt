(Following Yoriyuki's withdrawal to Saihō-ji,) Yoshimitsu sent Akamatsu Risshi Sokuyu (Norisuké) and others to summon Yoriyuki back. Yoriyuki returned to the capital immediately.

According to one account, Yoriyuki, realizing that his own power had grown so strong that people feared him exceedingly, devised a scheme to diminish his own authority and build up Yoshimitsu's prestige. He secretly conspired and made an arrangement with Yoshimitsu, so that in the presence of the assembled great lords (daimyō), he would be harshly rebuked by Yoshimitsu, and then withdrew to Yamaguni in Tanba Province, feigning embarrassment and distress, for a period of seclusion. From this time on, it is said that all people came to hold the Shogunal house in high regard.

In the seventh month, Fujiwara no Tsuneaki resigned as Naifu (Minister of the Center). In the same month, Momonoi Naotsune advanced to Etchū Province and engaged in battle. In the eighth month, the forces of the Southern Court rose up and attacked the fortress of the Kusunoki (clan). A large number of reinforcements were dispatched from Kyoto. In the tenth month, the reconstruction (zōtai) of the Iwashimizu Hachiman Shrine and Mount Hiei (Enryaku-ji) took place. Yoriyuki was appointed as Sagami no Kami (Governor of Sagami). In the eleventh month, Akamatsu Norisuké died at the age of sixty.

In the third month of (Ōan) 5th year, Imagawa Ryōshun fought against Kikuchi Takemasa and others in Tsukushi (Kyushu). Ōuchi no Suke Yoshihiro led troops to rescue Ryōshun, and they achieved victory.
