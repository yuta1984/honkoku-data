(Meiō 6, continued) Naomoto was appointed Regent [Kanpaku]. Koga Dainagon Minamoto no Toyomichi was appointed Minister of the Center [Naidaijin]. In the seventh month, Ichijō Fuyuyoshi resigned as Grand Minister of State [Daijō-daijin]. In the ninth month, Minamoto no Shigeuji of Koga passed away. He was sixty-four years of age. He took the posthumous name Kenkyō-in. His son was called Masauji. The warriors of the Kantō region referred to him as "Gosho" [His Excellency], but it was a title in name only, and he held no real prestige or authority. In the tenth month, Regent Nijō Naomoto passed away. He was twenty-seven years of age. Ichijō Fuyuyoshi was reappointed as Regent.

In the sixth month of the seventh year of Meiō [1498], there was a great earthquake across the provinces. Around this time, it is said that the statue of Taika no Kaishin [Fujiwara no Kamatari, the "Daishokuikan"] at Tōnomine shattered.

In the fifth month of the eighth year of Meiō [1499], Koga Naidaijin Minamoto no Toyomichi was appointed Minister of the Right [Udaijin]. In the eighth month, Kujō Dainagon Hisatsune was appointed Naidaijin. In this year, Shogun Minamoto no Yoshihisa sent an envoy to Joseon Korea to request the printing blocks of the complete Buddhist scriptures [Issaikyō]. Korea printed and presented the sutras, but did not hand over the printing blocks.

In the third month of the ninth year of Meiō [1500], Koga Toyomichi resigned as Minister of the Right. On the twenty-eighth day of the ninth month, His Majesty [Emperor Go-Tsuchimikado] passed away. He was fifty-nine years of age. At this time, the Imperial Court had fallen into such decline that there were no funds for the Imperial funeral, and for forty-some days [the body remained uninterred…]
