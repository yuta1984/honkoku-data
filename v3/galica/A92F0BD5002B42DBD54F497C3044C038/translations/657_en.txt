## Nihon Ōdai Ichiran, Volume Seven

### The 103rd Reign: Emperor Go-Hanazono

His personal name (imina) was Hikohito. He was the great-grandchild of Sukō-in (Emperor Sukō). Sukō-in's son was called Prince Yoshihito, who took the posthumous title Daitsu-in. Daitsu-in's son was the unranked prince Sadafusa. This latter was indeed Hikohito's father. His mother was Fusei-mon-in, the daughter of the posthumously appointed Minister of the Left, Tsuneari.

During the Kannō Disturbance, Emperor Sukō was taken captive by the Southern Court, and his younger brother Go-Kōgon ascended to the throne. After returning from Southern Court captivity, Sukō-in moved to the Fushimi Palace. Because he was the legitimate heir of the Jimyōin-dono imperial line, it is said that Sukō-in wished for Prince Yoshihito to succeed to the throne after Go-Kōgon-in; however, as the warrior houses (the shogunate) would not permit this, the succession continued through Go-En'yū, Go-Komatsu, and Shōkō.
