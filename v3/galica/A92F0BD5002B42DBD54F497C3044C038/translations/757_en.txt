One was Kakukei, a monzeki (a monk of imperial or noble lineage) at Ichijōin Temple, and the other was Shūkō of Rokuonji Temple. As for Shūkō, Miyoshi and Matsunaga dispatched a messenger to summon him, and had him killed along the road as he was leaving the capital. Kakukei, sensing the danger early, crossed Mount Kasuga and made his way to Ōmi province, where he placed himself under the protection of Sasaki Yoshikata, the lay monk known as Jōtei. He then returned to secular life and took the name Yoshiaki.

In the sixth month, the posthumous rank of Minister of the Left (Sadaijin), Junior First Rank (Jūichii), was conferred upon Yoshiteru, and he was given the posthumous title Kōgen'in. He had been appointed shogun from the fifteenth year of Tenbun (1546), and had governed from the nineteenth year (1550) until this year — a period of sixteen years. In all, from the year Takauji entered the capital in the second year of Kenmu (1336) to the present year, with successions passing between fathers and sons, and between brothers, there had been thirteen generations of shoguns over a combined period of two hundred and thirty-one years.

In the twelfth month of the ninth year (of Eiroku, 1566), Minamoto no Yoshihide received court rank (joshaku).

In the eighth month of the tenth year (of Eiroku, 1567), Sasaki Jōtei's son Yoshimasu secretly colluded with the Miyoshi clan and plotted to assassinate Yoshiaki. Upon hearing of this, Yoshiaki fled to Wakasa province and sought refuge with Takeda Yoshimune, deliberating plans to pursue and destroy the Miyoshi.
