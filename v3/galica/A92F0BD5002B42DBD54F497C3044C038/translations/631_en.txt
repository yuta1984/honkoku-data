When Dōgi entered and departed, the attending Gesseikei and Unkaku (court nobles and senior courtiers) all descended to the garden and performed sonkyo (a crouching bow of obeisance). Those among them who were particularly close to the warrior house were called "Jikkinshu" (intimate attendants).

In the same year, Imagawa Ryōshun returned from Kyushu. In the beginning, during the time of Takauji, Ryōshun's father Kunino (Kuninori) had held the provinces of Suruga and Tōtōmi. He bequeathed Suruga to his eldest son Noriuji and Tōtōmi to Ryōshun. When Noriuji died, his son Yasunori and Ryōshun fell into discord with one another. Ryōshun had long been stationed in Tsukushi (Kyushu), while his son remained in Tōtōmi. Yasunori repeatedly slandered Ryōshun to the shogunate. Furthermore, Ōuchi Yoshihiro, coveting the position of Kyushu Tandai (Deputy Governor-General), made representations that, although Ryōshun was in Tsukushi, because his son resided in Tōshū (Tōtōmi), Ryōshun was in communication with Ujimitsu of Kamakura. Moreover, the Kanrei (shogunal deputy) Shiba Yoshimasa (known by the title Kageyukoji) harbored the intention of installing his kinsman Shibukawa as Tandai. For all these reasons, Ryōshun was relieved of his post as Tandai and returned to the capital. After this, the affairs of Tsukushi and the Chūgoku region were administered by Yoshihiro.
