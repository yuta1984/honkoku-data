Gyōkō and others also accompanied him in attendance. Waka poetry was composed. According to one account, Yoshinori already harbored an ambition at this time to destroy Kamakura (the Kamakura府), and it is said that his visit to view Mount Fuji was merely a pretext to observe and gather information about the situation in the Kantō region. In the tenth month, Kaneyoshi resigned as Regent. Nijō Mochimoto became Regent once again. In the twelfth month, Yoshinori performed the ministerial congratulatory ceremony (daijin haiga), and Takatsukasa Dainagon Fusahira and others attended in his retinue. In the same month, Yoshinori was appointed as the Chief Administrator (bettō) of the殿上 and the Grand Chief Administrator of the Retired Emperor's household, and became the bettō of both the Junwa and Shōgaku Academies, as well as the head of the Minamoto clan (Genji no chōja). He was granted the use of an ox-drawn carriage (gissha). This year, Hosokawa Ukyō-no-daibu Mochiyuki was appointed as Kanryō (Deputy Shogun). In the same year, an envoy was dispatched to Ming China. Koreans came to pay court.

In the fifth year of Eikyō [1433], first month, the Emperor came of age (genpuku) at the age of fifteen. The Regent and Chancellor (Shōkoku) Nijō Mochimoto served as the cap-crowning officiant (kakan). Left Minister Minamoto no Yoshinori served as the hair-dressing officiant (rihats). This followed the precedent set at the coming-of-age ceremony of Retired Emperor Go-Komatsu, when Nijō Yoshimoto performed the cap-crowning and the Lord of Rokuon-in (Ashikaga Yoshimitsu) performed the hair-dressing.
