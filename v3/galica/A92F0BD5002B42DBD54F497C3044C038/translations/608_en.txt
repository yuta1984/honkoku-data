[Yoshimitsu] granted [Bungo to the Ōtomo clan], and bestowed Nagato and Hizen upon Ōuchi Yoshihiro. Within Chikugo, Higo, and parts of Hizen, Kikuchi's soldiers had constructed fortifications at various locations and continued to hold out.

In the tenth month, Yoshimitsu returned to the capital. In the eleventh month, Uesugi Tomofusa returned to Kamakura. From this point on, Yoshimitsu's military prestige grew exceedingly great, and as the Southern Court (*Nanpō*) had also declined, the warriors of the various provinces all gathered in Kyoto.

In the twelfth month, the ceremony of the sovereign's enthronement was held. After the accession (*senzo*), the world had not been at peace; furthermore, the sacred *shinboku* tree of Kasuga Shrine had, for particular reasons, been brought into the capital and had remained there for some years, making it difficult for members of the Fujiwara clan to perform their public duties. For this reason, the grand ceremony had been postponed.

In the third month of the first year of Eiwa (1375), Yoshimitsu made a pilgrimage to Iwashimizu [Hachimangū]. The procession was splendidly arrayed, and offerings of swords, sacred horses (*shinme*), and gold dust were presented. Zenhōji Temple served as the lodging hall (*shukubō*). As this shrine had been the tutelary deity (*ujigami*) of the Minamoto clan since the time of Yoshiie, it was held in especial reverence.
