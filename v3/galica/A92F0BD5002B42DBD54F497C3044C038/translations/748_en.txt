(In Mino,) the Toki and Saitō were present. In Owari, Oda Nobunaga was present. In Ise, the Kokushi Kitabatake were present. In Ōmi, Sasaki Sadayori and Yoshikata were present. In the Kinai and Nankai regions, the Miyoshi and Matsunaga clans were present. In Harima, Bizen, and Mimasaka, the remnants of the Akamatsu clan and the Ukita clan were present. In the Chūgoku region, the factions of Sue Zenkyō (Harukata), Mōri Motonari, Amago Haruhisa, and Yoshimi Masayori contended against one another. In Bungo, the Ōtomo clan was present. In Hizen, Ryūzōji Takanobu was present. In Satsuma and Ōsumi, Shimazu Yoshihisa was present. The various provinces were divided among rival powers, and not a single day passed in peace.

In the first month of the twenty-first year (of Tenbun, 1552), the Shogun Minamoto no Yoshifuji returned to the capital from Sakamoto. Hosokawa Harumoto shaved his head and fled from Katada. In the second month, Hosokawa Ujitsuna and his younger brother Fujikata came up to the capital from Settsu Province, accompanied by Miyoshi Nagayoshi. In the third month, Ujitsuna took the title of Ukyō no Daibu, and Fujikata took the title of Uma no Kami. Though the Hosokawa maintained the name of hereditary Kanrei (Deputy Shogun) in title, they had declined in power, and the Miyoshi finally (came to dominate) the capital, the Kinai, and the Nankai regions...
