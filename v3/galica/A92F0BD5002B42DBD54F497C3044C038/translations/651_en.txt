The Hana Hakkō (a Buddhist ceremony of eight lectures) was conducted.

In the eighth month of the thirty-third year, Tōin Mitsuhide resigned from the position of Naifu (Minister of the Center). Konoe Dainagon Tokitsugu was appointed to the position of Naifu.

In the tenth month of the thirty-fourth year, Akamatsu Sakyō-no-daibu Mitsusuke and Akamatsu Echigo-no-kami Mochisada entered into a dispute over their landholdings. Since the time of Takauji, the Akamatsu clan had held dominion over five provinces: Settsu, Harima, Bizen, Mimasaka, and Inaba. Mitsusuke was the legitimate grandson of Norisuke. Mochisada was the grandson of Norisuke's elder brother Sadanori, and was therefore not a legitimate heir but rather of a cadet branch. Nevertheless, because Mochisada was a favored retainer of Yoshimochi, three of the five provinces were granted to Mochisada. Mitsusuke, enraged, set fire to his own mansion in Kyoto and withdrew to Harima. Yoshimochi, angered in turn, ordered Hosokawa Mochimoto and Yamana Mitsuhiro to suppress Mitsusuke. However, because Mochisada was an arrogant and discourteous man, the various daimyō all despised him, and together with Mitsusuke, they conspired to [expose/punish] Mochisada's wrongdoings...
