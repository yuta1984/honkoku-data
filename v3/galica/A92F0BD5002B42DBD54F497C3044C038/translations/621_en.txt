([Minamoto no Yoshinari was] a descendant of Retired Emperor Juntoku and a man of great erudition. The *Kakaishō*, a commentary on *The Tale of Genji*, was his work.

In the third month of the second year [of Kākyō, 1388], the Regent Kanetsugi passed away at the age of twenty-nine. In the fourth month, Nijō Yoshimoto became Regent once again — in total, he served in the office of Chief Minister (Regent or Kanpaku) on four separate occasions. In the fifth month, Yoshimitsu resigned from the position of Minister of the Left (Sadaijin). Tokudaiji Udaijin Sanetsugu was appointed Minister of the Left; Dainagon Minamoto no Tokomichi was appointed Minister of the Right (Udaijin); and Ichijō Dainagon Tsunetsugu was appointed Minister of the Center (Naidaijin). Tsunetsugu had originally been a son of Nijō Yoshimoto, but the late former Kanpaku Ichijō Tsunemichi had adopted him and made him heir to the Ichijō family. In the sixth month, the Regent Yoshimoto passed away at the age of sixty-nine. He was posthumously styled Fukōen-in. His son Morotsugu was again appointed Kanpaku. In this same year, Yoshimitsu traveled for pleasure to the shores of Kishū (Kii Province) and also went to view Mount Fuji.
