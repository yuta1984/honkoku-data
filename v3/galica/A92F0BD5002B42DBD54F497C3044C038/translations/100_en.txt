(Continued from the previous page) [Guards were posted to check travelers.] At this very point, when they were about to dispatch reinforcements to Baekje, Empress Saimei passed away in the seventh month of that year. Emperor Tenji, while still wearing mourning garments, continued to oversee the affairs of state. In the eighth month of that year, generals Azumi no Hirafu and Kawabe no Momoe and others were appointed as commanders and sent to aid Baekje, with provisions of food, military supplies, and weapons dispatched to them. In the ninth month, Prince Buyeo Pung (Hōshō) of Baekje was sent back to Baekje accompanied by Hata no Eichi, Ta no Kutsu, and five thousand soldiers. Fukushin came out to receive them, hailed Buyeo Pung as the King of Baekje, and accepted Japan's directives. Although Buyeo Pung had long remained in Japan as a hostage, his country had been defeated by Tang China and Silla and was left without a king, which is why this came to pass. Afterward, Emperor Tenji observed the mourning rites for Empress Saimei and returned to Yamato. The following year, three hundred tan of cloth were bestowed upon Baekje King Buyeo Pung, and one hundred thousand arrows, five hundred kin of thread, one thousand kin of cotton, one thousand tan of cloth, one thousand sheets of tanned leather (oshikawa), and three thousand koku of seed rice were bestowed upon Fukushin.
