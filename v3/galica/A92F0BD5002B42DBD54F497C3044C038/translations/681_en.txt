(Continued from the previous page) Ryōken was appointed as his deputy, tasked with handling matters relating to appointments of head priests at the Gozan (Five Mountains) and the Jissetsu (Ten Temples), and public appointment documents (kujō) were granted by the warrior government (bakufu).

In the second month of the fourth year [of Bun'an, 1447], Yoshinari was promoted to Senior Fifth Rank, Upper Grade (Jushō-i no Jō) and appointed as Chamberlain (Jijū). In the fourth month, Yoshinari held a performance of inuōmono (mounted dog-chasing archery). In the fifth month, Togashi Jirō and his uncle Yasutaka disputed the position of military governor (shugo) of Kaga Province. The kanrei (Deputy Shogun) Hosokawa Katsumoto supported Yasutaka, while Hatakeyama Tokuhon supported Jirō. Consequently, the province was divided, with each party administering half.

In the sixth month, Ichijō Kaneyoshi, desiring the position of Regent (Shippei/Kanpaku), appealed to Yoshinari's mother, the Lady Ōkata. As the warrior government then submitted this to the court, Konoe Fusatsugu was relieved of his current post as Regent, and Kaneyoshi became Regent (Kanpaku).

In the eleventh month, Yoshinari performed his first archery ceremony (yumi-hajime). In the same month, retainers of the Hatakeyama clan defeated enemies in the southern part of Kii Province, slew their commander — a secularized prince formerly of Enman-in — and presented his head to the capital. In the same month, the title of Retired Emperor (Daijō Tennō) was conferred upon the monk-prince Dōkin of Fushimi Palace.
