All those who bore ill will toward Norizane were killed. As for Mochiuji, Uesugi Mochitomo, Chiba-no-suke Taneyoshi, Ōishi Noriyoshi, and others were assigned to guard him, and a messenger was dispatched to Kyoto to request that Mochiuji's death sentence be reduced by one degree of severity.

In the second month of the eleventh year (of Eikyō, 1439), a command came from Yoshinori to Norizane to the effect that the crime of Mochiuji's rebellion could not be pardoned. Accordingly, Uesugi Mochitomo and Chiba Taneyoshi advanced on Eianji, and Mochiuji committed suicide. He was forty-two years of age and was posthumously titled Chōshūin. His uncle Mitsusada likewise killed himself. All those in close attendance also died in battle. Yoshihisa committed suicide at Hōkokuji. From the time Motouji had governed the eastern provinces during the Kannō era to the time of Mochiuji, four generations had passed, spanning some ninety years. As all the Kantō warriors submitted to Norizane, he was urged to take the title of Kantō Kanrei (Deputy Shogun of the Kantō). Nevertheless, Norizane, feeling that he could not escape the guilt of having caused the death of his lord, shaved his head and took the name Chōdō.
