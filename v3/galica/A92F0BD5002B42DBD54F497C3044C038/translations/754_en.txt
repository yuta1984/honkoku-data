Masaka's son, Yoshitsugu, was adopted and made his heir. Matsunaga's power and influence grew ever greater.

In the spring of that year, Uesugi Terutora persuaded the various warriors of the Kantō region and, riding the momentum of his victory against Hōjō Ujiyasu, pressed his attack into Odawara, pushing as far as the area of the castle gate and Lotus Pond (Hasuike). The castle was already in a perilous state. However, Terutora went to pray at Tsurugaoka in Kamakura, and there, angered by the disrespectful behavior of Narita Nagayasu, struck him on the cheek with his fan. Narita returned to Oshi Castle and rebelled. From this point on, the warriors of the Kantō region all turned against Terutora. Terutora returned to Echigo, and the Kantō region once again came under the authority of Ujiyasu. That autumn, Terutora advanced into Kōzuke and Shinano provinces, and on the tenth day of the ninth month, fought a battle against Takeda Shingen of Kai at Kawanakajima, after which both sides returned to their home provinces. Terutora and Shingen were evenly matched adversaries, and they faced each other in prolonged confrontation over the Jōshū (Kōzuke) region for a number of years.

In the spring of the fifth year (of Eiroku, 1562), Mōri Motonari and the Ōtomo of Bungo went to war. Yoshiteru sent Dōchō, the Monzeki of Shōgo-in, to Motonari, and sent the Major Counselor (Dainagon) of the Koga clan, Minamoto no Michioki, to the Ōtomo, and had them make peace.
