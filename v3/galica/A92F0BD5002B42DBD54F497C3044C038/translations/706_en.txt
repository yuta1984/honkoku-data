In the ninth year [of Bunmei, 1477], in the first month, Yoshihisa was appointed to the Senior Third Rank. In the eleventh month, the daimyō who were partisans of Yamana Sōzen all departed the capital and returned to their respective provinces. Yoshimi proceeded to Mino Province. The capital [Rakuchū] became peaceful. Hatakeyama Masanaga became the Deputy [Kanryō]. From the Ōnin [War] until this point, eleven years had elapsed. From this time onward, the various daimyō each seized control of their allotted domains, and the authority of the military commanders declined.

In the tenth year [of Bunmei, 1478], in the Kantō region, Ashikaga Shigeuji and Uesugi Akisada made peace. Shigeuji returned to Koga. Akisada succeeded to the headship of the Yamanouchi house, established a castle at Hiroi in Kōzuke Province, and exercised authority over eight provinces. At this time, the head of the Ōgigayatsu Uesugi was called Shurishidaibu Sadamasa. His chief retainer was called Ōta Dōshin, and Dōshin's son was called Dōkan. They resided in Musashi Province. Through the stratagems of the Ōta father and son, many of the warriors of the Kantō turned away from the Yamanouchi [Uesugi] and came to follow the Ōgigayatsu [Uesugi]. From this time, the two Uesugi houses fell into discord and came to engage in armed conflict.
