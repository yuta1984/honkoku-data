She was the daughter of the posthumously appointed Minister of the Left [Sadaijin], Fujiwara no Norihide. In the fourth month of the ninth year of Eishō [1512], at the age of seventeen, he [the future sovereign] underwent the coming-of-age ceremony [genpuku] and was proclaimed an imperial prince [shinnō senge]. In the fourth month of the sixth year of Daiei [1526], at the age of thirty-one, he acceded to the throne [senzo]. The regent [kampaku] was Konoe Ukie [Right Minister of State] Taneie. The shogun was Saisho no Chūjō [Major Counselor and Middle Captain of the Inner Palace Guards] Minamoto no Yoshiharu, and the kanrei [shogunal deputy] was Hosokawa Takakuni, who had taken Buddhist orders under the name Jōkan.

In the ninth month of the same year, Tokudaiji Kinkane resigned from the position of Minister of the Left [Safu]. In the twelfth month, Yoshiharu summoned skilled archers from nearby provinces and held the first archery ceremony of the season [matohajime].

In the seventh year [of Daiei, 1527], Miyoshi Nagamoto, who had taken Buddhist orders under the name Kaiun, advanced from Awa Province, arrived at Sakai in Izumi Province, and attacked Kyoto. He engaged Hosokawa Takakuni in battle at the Katsura River, and Takakuni was defeated. Asakura Takakage of Echizen Province entered the capital and engaged the Miyoshi forces in battle, and the Miyoshi forces were routed.

In the eighth month of the first year of Kyōroku [1528], the Konoe kampaku Taneie was transferred to the position of Minister of the Left [Safu]. Koga Naifu [Minister of the Center] Minamoto no Michikoto was appointed to the position of Minister of the Right [Ufu]. Kujō Dainagon [Major Counselor] Uemichi was appointed to the position of Naifu [Minister of the Center]. This year, too, the Miyoshi clan attacked Kyoto, causing widespread unrest, and Shogun Minamoto no Yoshiharu left the capital for Ō[mi Province]…
