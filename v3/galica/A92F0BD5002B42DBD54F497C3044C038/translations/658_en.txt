Sadafusa succeeded him, but the family's fortunes declined further and further. Nevertheless, by order of the Retired Emperor Go-Komatsu, (Sadafusa) was granted the proclamation of unranked prince. Having incurred the wrath of Shōkō-in, Sadafusa took the tonsure and assumed the religious name Dōkin.

In the first year of Shōchō (1428), Shōkō-in fell ill, and as there was no designated crown prince to succeed to the throne, on the twelfth day of the seventh month, Gyōtō, the Kunaiky (Minister of the Palace) of Seizonji, proceeded to Fushimi and met with Dōkin. He conveyed the wishes of Muromachi-dono, Minamoto no Gisen (Ashikaga Yoshinori), and informed him that Hikohito should come to the capital. The following day, four to five hundred retainers of the lay monk Hatakeyama Mitsuka (Mitsue), also known as Dōtan, who served as kanrei (shogunal deputy), came as an escort, and (Hikohito) entered the Nyakuōji (shrine) in the Eastern Hills. Akamatsu Sakyo no Daibu Mitsusuke provided a guard of honor. Through the warrior houses' intermediary, the Nijō Regent Mochimoto, a request was submitted to the Retired Emperor Go-Komatsu, and Hikohito was formally designated as his adopted son. On the seventeenth day, (Hikohito) proceeded from Nyakuōji to the Sentō (the Retired Emperor's palace).
