The remaining members of the Nitta clan, who were scattered in various places, were also tracked down by Motouji and pacified. Rather than residing in Kamakura, Motouji established his encampment at the Iruma River in Musashi Province, and because his military preparations were strict and thorough, the eastern provinces remained peaceful. In the eleventh month, Kikuchi Takemitsu, who had long been aligned with the Southern Court and resided in Higo Province, had been engaged in battles year after year with the Shōni and Ōtomo clans, and had also attacked and defeated the Tandai (Regional Deputy) placed there by Takauji. In this same year, upon hearing of Takauji's death, battles broke out across Kyushu on numerous occasions, and Kikuchi emerged victorious. Prior to this, a prince of the Southern Court had been sent down and was revered as the Sei-sei Shōgun (Shogun of the Western Pacification). Many warriors from the Nitta clan and other provinces traveled to Tsukushi (Kyushu) to seek Kikuchi's support. The greater part of Kyushu thus came under Kikuchi's influence.
