In the first month of the third year (of Kakitsu, 1443), Yoshikatsu was granted the rank of Junior Fourth Rank Lower Grade (Jushii-no-ge).

In the fifth month, an envoy from Joseon Korea arrived in Japan. The Kanrei (Deputy Shogun), Hatakeyama Tokuhon, declared: "They are surely coming for the purposes of trade, using tribute missions as a pretext. As the Shogun is still a young child, it would be wasteful to burden the various provinces with unnecessary expenses," and turned them away from Hyōgo. However, as they presented the matter as a visit of condolence for Fukōin (the late Shogun Ashikaga Yoshinori), they were permitted to enter the capital, and Shiba Chiyotoku was appointed to provide for their various needs and expenses.

In the sixth month, the Koreans proceeded to the Muromachi residence and had an audience with Yoshikatsu.

On the twenty-first day of the seventh month, Seii Taishōgun, Sakon'e-no-chūjō, Minamoto no Yoshikatsu died young, at the age of merely ten years. Although still a child, it is said that he was fond of horse-riding, and it is handed down by tradition that he died as a result of accidentally falling from his horse. His reign lasted three years. He was given the posthumous Buddhist name Keiunin. He was posthumously granted the titles of Minister of the Left (Sadaijin) and Senior First Rank (Jūichii). Hatakeyama Tokuhon and the others installed Yoshikatsu's younger brother Yoshinari as their new lord. He was eight years of age at the time.

On the night of the twenty-third day of the ninth month, a band of ruffians (kyōzoku) broke into the Imperial Palace. One group climbed up into the Seiryōden (the Emperor's residential hall), and another forced their way in from the Tsubonemachi quarter and set fire to it.
