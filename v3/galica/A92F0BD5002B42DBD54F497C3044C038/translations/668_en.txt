(Mochifusa and Noritomo were sons of Zenshū.) There were many warriors in the Kantō region who turned against Mochiuji. Miura(-no-suke) Tokitaka also revolted and, in turn, set fire to Kamakura. Mochiuji encamped at Ebina in Musashi Province and dispatched Uesugi Norizane to hold back the forces from Kyoto. A battle took place at Hayakawajiri in Sagami Province, in which Norinao was defeated. The Kyoto forces crossed over the Hakone pass and advanced. Mochiuji again dispatched Kido Mochisue to hold them back. Isshiki Naokanе and Tokiie were also defeated by Norizane and returned to Ebina. Norizane departed Kōzuke and encamped at Bumbai (Bubaigawara) in Musashi Province.

In the eleventh month, Miura Mochitaka and others attacked Kamakura, captured Yoshihisa alive, and confined him in Ōgigayatsu. Mochiuji, having no recourse, pleaded with Norizane for a reconciliation. Accordingly, Norizane's chief retainer, Nagao Hōden, came and accompanied Mochiuji to Kamakura, where they entered Eianji, and Mochiuji had his head shaved (took Buddhist vows).
