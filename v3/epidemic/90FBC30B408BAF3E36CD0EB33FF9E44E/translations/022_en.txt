...at times of epidemic, around the fifth or sixth month after birth, and during spring, summer, and autumn — inoculating healthy children free of illness is considered especially ideal. *(Setsu)* It is also transmitted that during the first two months after birth, the mechanisms of the skin are not yet fully developed, and there is a risk of producing *katō* (false/spurious pox). *(Fu)* It is further stated that during the period from the sixth to the eighth week after birth, because the body's reactive mechanisms are hypersensitive, dangerous symptoms may readily arise; moreover, since the skin's structural formation is not yet complete, it is impossible for the vaccine pustule to develop fully. *(Fu / Kō)* Although vaccination tends to take effect well in newborn infants, in some cases the skin of the upper arm has an abundance of fine down-like hair (*jomō*), causing the vaccine lymph to adhere to it and fail to produce a reaction. *(Mo)* It is also stated that, regardless of age, the period of teething and the six weeks following birth

...should be avoided for vaccination. Within six weeks of birth, the skin is lax and loose (*jūchi*), with excessive fine hair (*nenmō*), and the vaccination may be applied in vain. Furthermore, if vaccination is carried out during the teething period, or when the child has diarrhea, there will be an impediment to the maturation of the pustule. Also, if the weather is not fine and clear, the vaccine lymph is prone to deterioration, making it unfavorable to perform vaccination. *(Ho)* It is also said that for children who are frail and emaciated, or those who have a constitutional tendency toward *nōsui* (hydrocephalus), one should for the time being set aside the procedure and wait until the child has grown to three or four years of age. *(Mo)*

On careful reflection, the old method (*koho*) — that is, the traditional Chinese-derived practice — was most strict in selecting the proper season: it prized the time of the Three Springs (*sanshun*) when all things come to life and the weather is mild and harmonious, and it prohibited the procedure during summer, autumn, and the beginning of winter.
