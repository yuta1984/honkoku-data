**[Method of Drawing Out and Extracting the Vaccine, continued]**

...selecting neither [favorable weather], nor auspicious days, avoiding neither the wind, nor observing any prohibitions; making no distinction between male and female, and paying no heed to the size or age of the recipient. For infants from one hundred days after birth onward: provided they currently have no *sōrai* (severe skin sores), no *ruirekki* (scrofula / cervical lymphadenopathy), no *taidoku* (fetal toxin), and no blood-heat of the skin, no *kanshaku* (infantile digestive accumulation disorder), nor any other diseases — inoculation may be carried out at any time, as circumstances allow.

*(Supplementary note)* The *Sanka Menō* states — [synthesizing the works translated and compiled by Shohō Ikuzō, namely: the *Conradi Treatise on Therapy*, *Most's Universal Medical Dictionary*, *Bressler's Complete Book of Pediatrics*, *Galdsmith's Complete Book of Cowpox*, *Selius's Complete Book of Surgery*, *Consbühler's Treatise on Therapy*, *Karaman's Book on Skin Diseases*, *Hufeland's Treatise on Therapy*, and others] — that without selecting the year, season, or weather (whether cloudy or clear), and regardless of the nature of currently prevailing epidemics, it is acceptable to carry out the inoculation procedure. Of all times, spring and early summer are considered the optimal season for smallpox vaccination. Neither the spread of natural smallpox nor of other diseases presents any impediment.
