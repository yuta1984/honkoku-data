**[Left page]**

The fluid is a transparent, flowing substance (*ryūdōbutsu*), bearing a slight viscosity and a faintly salty taste (*kanmi*), with absolutely no odor. Its composition consists approximately of water and *tansei* [egg white/albumin]. When a sample of good-quality fluid is taken and examined under a microscope (*kenbikkyō*), numerous small globules (*shōkyū*) are found, constantly moving about within it. Although their shapes and sizes are by no means uniform, in the case of genuine pox (*seitō*) fluid, they are generally perfectly spherical. Moreover, the efficacy of the fluid corresponds to the abundance of these small globules. When the fluid becomes over-ripe, the globules decrease. If vinegar or other acidic substances are mixed in, the globules dissolve; if the fluid is warmed, the globules disappear — and consequently, the power of transmission is likewise diminished and destroyed. **(Pu)**
