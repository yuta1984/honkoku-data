Because the treatment is effective and the course of illness mild, it is not strictly necessary to adhere to seasonal timing; rather, one may carry it out as the occasion demands, and indeed there has never been a single failure from the outset. It is especially important to ensure that good-quality vaccine lymph (*kamyo*) is perpetuated without interruption. As for the various contraindications (*shoki*), one must certainly exercise caution; however, it is not necessary to be as severely strict as one must be with the serious cases of human smallpox inoculation — and there is no harm in this. One should not say that "the old method is meticulous while the new method is lax." In my private deliberations, *Tokugintō Shincho* (*Dokugyūtō Shinsho*) discusses how, in practicing this method, even the frail and weak become robust, and chronic ailments (*shukushitsu*) are likewise brought to resolution.
