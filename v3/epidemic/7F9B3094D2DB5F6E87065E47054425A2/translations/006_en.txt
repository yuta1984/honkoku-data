…(By cautiously avoiding it,) one may go through life without ever contracting this affliction and remain in good health. Those who by their innate constitution lack susceptibility may, even during an epidemic, live together with the afflicted and share the same bed, yet go their entire lives without contracting the disease. One might object, however, as follows: "If (smallpox) is a single externally-derived contagious disease, why is it that once a person has contracted it they never fall ill again, and why does no one become infected two or three times in the course of a single lifetime? Moreover, if it is not an innate fetal toxin, why would one deliberately administer the method of variolation (*shuhō*), thereby causing an innocent child to suffer illness?"

To this I respond: The disease of pox (*tōshin*) is among the most virulent of all contagious poisons, penetrating even into the innermost palace chambers and the most remote and distant corners of the land, and even among the smallest of birds and beasts there are those that encounter its calamity. Therefore, those who escape this

[Left folio]

poison throughout their entire lives number only a mere handful among tens of millions of people. Despite being so extraordinarily virulent, it does not infect a person a second time. The reason one is not reinfected is that once a person has been infected, their susceptibility is entirely exhausted and extinguished.

Similarly, other diseases such as *baisō* (syphilis) 〈Interlinear note: Among those in the profession of prostitution, once they have received this toxin, they are not re-infected. This is called "*toya*" (閉屋, "closed house/molting").〉, erysipelas (*sekiyū tantoku*), and scarlet fever (*kōkashin*) 〈Interlinear note: The original name is "Road Hong" (*rōdo honku*). "Road" means red, and "Hong" means fiery particles/rash. Its symptoms are fever, rapid pulse, and sore throat; over the course of days it spreads without limit, like (the scarlet fever epidemic) in Russia.〉, as well as *taii* (mumps/drooling sore), whooping cough (*hyakunichizeki*), and others — these too are all diseases that afflict a person only once in a lifetime. Furthermore, there are people who are sensitive to lacquer fumes: if they eat lacquer leaves and develop a severe rash, they do not suffer from it again. The world is full of such examples. Why should smallpox alone be treated as suspect? That said, for the other afflictions mentioned, those who fall ill are extremely rare. However, smallpox is a once-in-a-lifetime calamity from which no one is able to escape…
