…[If one takes care to avoid it,] one may pass through life without ever contracting this affliction, remaining in good health. On the other hand, there are those whose innate constitution (*tenshitsu*) lacks susceptibility: even if they live together with patients during an epidemic, sharing the same bed and sleeping side by side, there are those who are never infected throughout their entire lives.

Someone might object as follows: "If [smallpox] were a single externally-transmitted contagious disease, why is it that once one has been infected, one never falls ill again? Why is it that there is no case of being infected two or three times within a lifetime? Furthermore, if it is not an inherent fetal toxin, why is it necessary to deliberately administer variolation (*shuhō*) and cause suffering to innocent children?"

The fact is that the disease called *tōshin* (pox) is among the most violent of all contagious poisons: there is nowhere it does not penetrate, reaching even into the deepest palace chambers and the remotest, most secluded corners of the land, and even the humblest creatures — birds and beasts — have those among them that encounter its disaster. Therefore, those who go through their entire lives without encountering this

[Left folio]

poison number only a scant few among tens of millions. Despite spreading so extensively, it does not infect anyone a second time. The reason one does not become re-infected is that once one has been infected once, one's susceptibility is entirely and completely shed.

Furthermore, other diseases such as *baisō* (syphilis) — *[Interlinear note: Among the ranks of prostitutes, once they have received this poison once, they are not infected again. This is termed* toya *("closed cage/moulting").* ] — erysipelas (*sekiyū tantoku*), and scarlet fever (*kōkashin*) — *[Interlinear note: The original name is* Rōdo Honku *(Roodvonk). "Rōdo" means red; "Honku" means sparks/fire-flakes. Its symptoms are fever,]* *[Interlinear note: rapid pulse, and sore throat; spreading without limit over the course of days, like Russia [i.e., vast and boundless].* ] — as well as *taigi* (drooling disease / chin-dampening), whooping cough (*hyakunichizeki*), and the like, are also diseases that strike only once in a lifetime. Again, people who are sensitive to lacquer fumes may eat lacquer leaves, break out in a severe rash, and thereafter never suffer from it again. There are many such cases in the world. Why must one single out smallpox alone for suspicion?

Nevertheless, in the case of the other ailments mentioned, those who actually fall ill are extremely rare. Only smallpox is a once-in-a-lifetime affliction that none can escape…
