Zhang Xunyu's *Zhòngdòu Xīnshū* ("New Treatise on Smallpox Inoculation"), Zhang Luyu's *Yītōng* ("Comprehensive Medicine"), Xiangying's *Zhòngdòu Xiǎoyǐn* ("Brief Introduction to Smallpox Inoculation"), and in our country, Heian's Hōki's *Yōka Shutō Shinpō* ("The Heart of the Method of Smallpox Inoculation in Pediatrics"), Watanabe's *Shutō Hitsujunben* ("Discourse on the Essential Procedures of Smallpox Inoculation"), and others — the great majority of these are all grounded in the method of the *Jīnjiàn*, and praise the benefits of inoculation and its ability to save people from calamity. Nevertheless, the reason that one in ten, or one in a thousand or hundred, is lost, and that it is impossible to hold a guarantee of complete success, is that the practice still belongs to its early stages and there remain aspects not yet fully perfected. For this reason, Zhang Lu also said that "following nature and following the times, without forcing the procedure, is superior," and many others have likewise declined to trust it. However, compared to the naturally epidemic form of smallpox (*tenkō shizento*), its benefits are thirty- to forty-fold greater.

At that time, there were many in society who slandered and criticized it.
