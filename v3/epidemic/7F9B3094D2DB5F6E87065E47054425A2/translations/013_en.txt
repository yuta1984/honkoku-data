There happened to be a physician known as Jenner, whose fame was widely celebrated throughout the country, and who was regarded as a highly accomplished and benevolent healer. Upon observing small bluish pustules near the udders of cattle, which resembled the pustules of smallpox in form, he reasoned that perhaps they would be capable of dispersing the poison of human smallpox. He tried inoculating people experimentally, and it proved effective. Thereafter, the method spread successively to countries across the Atlantic, Asia, America, and elsewhere, where it was cultivated and administered according to this method. Among men and women, young and old, numbering several million, not a single person suffered harm, and not a single person contracted the disease again.
