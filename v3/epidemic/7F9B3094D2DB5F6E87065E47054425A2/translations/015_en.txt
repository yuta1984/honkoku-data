...covering the signs and symptoms — genuine and false — as well as other matters such as minor pox, fine rash, and complications arising from concurrent conditions, and even the methods of cultivating *yèmiáo* (liquid vaccine) and *gānmiáo* (dried vaccine) — there is nothing that is not fully covered. Moreover, it resolves the reader's confusion and clarifies doubts on their behalf; the writing is concise yet the meaning is thorough. Though it is but a slim pamphlet, one may say that the art of inoculation is exhausted herein. That the author titled it "Summary" (*lüe*) is merely an act of modesty. That later persons renamed it "Complete Treatise" (*quanshu*) is likewise no exaggeration of praise. Ah — among those who transmitted this method in China, Mr. Qiu [Qiu Xi] is regarded as the first advocate, just as Mr. Jenner (*Yen-shi*) is in England. And not only in those lands: even in our own country, those who came to know that there existed a method of cowpox inoculation did so because Mr. Qiu's book was published and circulated. good quality vaccine material (*karyō*, viable seed) was a source of deep regret among physicians. At this juncture, through the benevolence of Heaven, which cherishes life, and the gracious favor of the Sagely Court in its love for the people, physicians from foreign lands brought good vaccine material and it spread widely throughout the realm. The calamity of smallpox, which had hitherto been exceedingly difficult to manage, was transformed into a matter of the utmost ease and safety. Truly, there is nothing greater than this in the way of protecting infants and young children (*hōseki*).
