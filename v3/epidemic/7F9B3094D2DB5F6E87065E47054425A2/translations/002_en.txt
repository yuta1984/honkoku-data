# Supplement to Sanka Shinsho (New Writings on Scattered Blossoms)

**Orally transmitted by Master Namba Hōsetsu of Bizen Province; recorded in writing by disciple Hayashi Shū of Hizen Province**

## Questions and Answers

**Question:** It is said that in ancient times there were no people who suffered from smallpox (痘瘡). From approximately what period did it begin to spread in Japan and other countries?

**Answer:** As for Japan,

The disease was transmitted from overseas during the reign of Emperor Shōmu, in the seventh year of the Tenpyō era, the year of Kinoto-I (乙亥) [735 CE; according to this text, 1,116 years before the time of writing]. It originated in Tsukushi (Kyushu) and spread throughout the entire country. The *Shoku Nihongi* (Chronicles of Japan, Continued) states: "In the seventh year, the year of Kinoto-I (乙亥), on the day of Heigo (丙午) in the eighth month, epidemic sores (smallpox) broke out greatly across the provinces under the jurisdiction of the Dazaifu, and the common people all took to their beds. On the day of Jinin (壬寅) in the twelfth month: this year the harvest was quite poor, and from summer through winter, throughout the realm..."
