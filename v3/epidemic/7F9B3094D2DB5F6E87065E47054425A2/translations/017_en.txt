(Continuing from the previous page) ...waiting [for the wounds to dry], no additional covering plaster was applied. Thereafter, taking the eighth day as the appointed time for collecting the inoculation material, the vaccination was passed on and administered to more than one hundred children; in every case the pocks were pure, beautiful, red, and vigorous — the finest quality pustules. For the first time, it was confirmed with certainty that every word spoken and every word written [by the Dutch physician] had been not in the slightest degree false.

Therefore, the general outline [of these matters] was set down in writing and transmitted to fellow-minded men of learning, and imparted to those studying under [the author's] instruction. It is hoped that, with the modest spirit of one who preserves human life, this may make some small contribution to the benevolent grace of protecting the children of the nation, so it was said.

This was the result of Nara-shi's [Dr. Narabayashi's] fervent commitment and dedication to the Way, his profound compassion in preserving life, and Mon-shi's [the Dutch physician's] heartfelt and deeply sincere devotion of effort. When the whole country comes hereafter to adopt this method, one cannot know how many hundreds of millions of children will be lifted into the realm of benevolent long life.

[Left folio]

Ah — the meritorious efforts of these two men: how could one not feel the deepest gratitude? This very year [Kaei 3, 1850], in the first month, the lord of Ashimori in Bitchū Province summoned Ogata Kōan from Naniwa [Osaka]. Ogata-shi had administered the inoculation to his own two children in that city, then voyaged across the [Seto Inland] Sea and arrived [in Ashimori]. By order [of the lord], a Cowpox Eradication Hall (*jotōkan*) was established, and the samurai retainers and the many children of the domain were inoculated. Beyond these, those who came seeking inoculation arrived in an unceasing stream one after another. From the latter part of the first month through the third month, approximately one thousand five hundred persons in all received inoculation, and every one was safe, without a single loss.

When Ogata-shi first arrived, he passed through Okayama, and through the connection of an old acquaintance, the author's nephew Shinono Ryūsen sent word to inform the author of this matter. The author had been ardently longing for this opportunity for six years to this day; he immediately went to visit the Cowpox Eradication Hall, sought out Ogata-shi to learn the method in detail, and to perform the inoculation...
