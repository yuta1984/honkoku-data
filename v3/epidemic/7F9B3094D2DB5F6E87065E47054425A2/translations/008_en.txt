…[Women and girls who remain at home may escape the disaster,] but men who travel on journeys, like those who cannot avoid encountering places where smallpox is epidemic — once they encounter it, they inevitably become infected. Moreover, in great cities and busy towns through which people come and go, and in the case of lords, nobles, and persons of rank, there is no means for any of them to avoid it. This is a method that cannot be universally applied. Therefore, even in areas where smallpox has been prohibited [from entering], it gradually intrudes and cannot be held at bay. In some cases, by domain ordinance the prohibition has been lifted, and the method of inoculation (*shuhō*) has been adopted. Rather than forcing oneself to do what is difficult to carry out, it is far better to employ the method of inoculation — which is easy to administer widely — to eradicate the disease at its root. Even in the vast and boundless continent of Europe, inoculation has entirely suppressed the disaster of smallpox. Our homeland's soil is surrounded by sea; it stands independent, adjoining no foreign peoples on its borders. Wide though it may be, distant though places may be, if we broadly establish our medical encampments, rally the great forces of unified medicine far and wide, and advance with battle cries to subjugate [the disease], then the extermination of the fierce and poisonous demon of smallpox — which has wrought havoc for over a thousand years — is something we may await with a fixed date in expectation.

**Question:** From what time did the technique of smallpox inoculation (*shutō*) originate?

**Answer:** During the reign of Emperor Zhenzong of the Song dynasty, a divine person from Mount Emei took the scabs (*ka*) of a mild, epidemic form of smallpox on behalf of the child of Grand Counselor Wang Dan, made it into a vaccine preparation (*nae*), and stuffed it into the child's nostrils to induce a smallpox eruption. This is regarded as the founding origin of smallpox inoculation. That was over eight hundred years ago from the present. The Qianlong Emperor of the Qing dynasty issued an imperial command to compile the *Yīzōng Jīnjiàn* ("The Golden Mirror of Medical Orthodoxy"). In that work's chapters, this method was recorded, disseminated broadly throughout the world, and used to save the common people from untimely death.
