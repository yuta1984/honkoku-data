Many people suffered from "pea sores" (豌豆瘡; an early name for smallpox) and died young. What is commonly called "mo-kasa" (裳瘡) refers to this very disease. In the Kojidan and the Ainōshō and other works, it is recorded that a fisherman from Tsukushi drifted to Silla and was there first infected with this poison. As for China, all accounts agree that it arose during the Later Han dynasty. This is ultimately traced to an incident in which soldiers of Ma Yuan contracted "captive sores" (虜瘡) during his campaign against Nan-yang. However, the Book of the Later Han (後漢書) does not record this event. The Waitai Miyao (外台秘要), citing the Zhouhou Fang (肘後方), states: "In this year, a disease broke out. An epidemic erupted with spots spreading across the head, face, and body, covering the entire person in a moment. It resembled burn sores, each topped with white fluid. Those severely afflicted invariably died within several days. This was a malignant and toxic vapor. People say it was acquired during the campaign against the enemy at Nan-yang during the Jianwu reign period, and so it came to be called 'captive sores' (虜瘡)." Later scholars mistakenly interpreted the phrase "during the Jianwu period" in the Zhouhou Fang as referring to the Jianwu reign period of the Eastern Han dynasty.

[Left folio]

The Zhouhou Fang was compiled by Ge Hong of the Eastern Jin dynasty; since it states "in this year, a disease broke out," it is certain that smallpox originated in the Eastern Jin period. Li Shizhen's Bencao Gangmu (本草綱目), in the supplementary prescriptions for shengma (升麻), also cites the Zhouhou Fang, and the account is largely the same as that in the Waitai Miyao. It records that during the reign of Emperor Yuan of the Eastern Jin, this disease spread from the northwest and was named "captive sores" (虜瘡). There appears to be some basis for this view.

Among the Western nations as well, accounts differ somewhat. The Treatise on the General Treatment of Smallpox by Hufeland (扶氏) states: Smallpox first spread in Europe in the year 700 CE (corresponding to the fourth year of Emperor Monmu's reign, the year Kanoe-Ne [庚子], in our country; 1,151 years ago from the present); in America in the year 1500 CE (corresponding to the ninth year of the Meiō era, the year Kanoe-Saru [庚申], in our country; 351 years ago from the present); and in the Netherlands (依蘭土) in the year 1800 CE (corresponding to the twelfth year of the Kansei era, the year Kanoe-Saru [庚申], in our country; 51 years ago from the present).
