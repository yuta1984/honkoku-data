[Right folio]

Many people suffered from *wandòuchuāng* (smallpox pustules resembling peas) and died young. This is what is commonly called *mokasa* (裳瘡) in the vernacular. In *Kojidan* and *Ainōshō* and other works, it is written that a fisherman from Tsukushi drifted to Silla and was the first to be infected with this poison.

As for China, all accounts state that the disease originated during the Later Han dynasty. This is traced back to the occasion when Ma Yuan campaigned against Nanyang, during which his soldiers contracted *lùchuāng* (captive sores/barbarian pox). However, the *Book of the Later Han* (*Hou Han Shu*) does not record this event. The *Waitai Miyao* (*Arcane Essentials from the Imperial Library*), citing the *Zhouhou Fang* (*Emergency Prescriptions*), states: "In this year there was disease. A seasonal epidemic broke out with rashes spreading across the head, face, and body, covering the entire person in an instant. The appearance was like burn wounds, all bearing white blisters. Those severely afflicted would invariably die within a few days. This is the malevolent toxic *qi*. People of the world say it was contracted during the Jianwu reign period in the campaign against the barbarians at Nanyang, and so it is called *lùchuāng* (barbarian sores)." Later people mistakenly read the phrase "during the Jianwu period" in the *Zhouhou Fang* as referring to the Jianwu reign of the Eastern Han dynasty — but this is nothing more than an error.

[Left folio]

The *Zhouhou Fang* was compiled by Ge Hong of the Eastern Jin dynasty. When it says "in this year there was disease," it is certain that smallpox originated in the Eastern Jin period. Li Shizhen's *Bencao Gangmu* (*Compendium of Materia Medica*), in the supplementary formulas for *Shengma* (black cohosh), also cites the *Zhouhou Fang*, and is largely the same as the *Waitai Miyao*. It states that during the reign of Emperor Yuan of Eastern Jin, this disease arose from the northwest and spread, and was named *lùchuāng*. This account has merit and appears to be correct.

In the Western world as well, there are some discrepancies among various accounts. According to Hufeland's (*Fushi*'s) *Treatise on the General Treatment of Smallpox* (*Tōsō Tsūji-hen*), smallpox first spread in Europe in the year 700 CE (corresponding to the fourth year of Emperor Monmu's reign, the year *Kōshi* [庚子], which is 1,151 years before the present); in the Americas in the year 1500 CE (corresponding to the ninth year of Meiō, the year *Kōshin* [庚申], which is 351 years before the present); and in the Netherlands (*Iran-do* / Holland) in the year 1800 CE (corresponding to the twelfth year of Kansei, the year *Kōshin* [庚申], which is 51 years before the present).
