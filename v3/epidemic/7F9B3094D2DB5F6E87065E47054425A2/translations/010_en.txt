...from which country did it originate?

**Answer:** According to the account of Fussellrand [i.e., a source identified as such], in the year 1769 of the [Western] calendar (interlinear note: *corresponding to the seventh year of Meiwa, the year Kōin [庚寅], in our country; eighty-one years from the present*), a German physician discovered the method of taking cowpox and inoculating it into humans. In 1798 (interlinear note: *corresponding to the tenth year of Kansei, the year Bojutsu [戊午], in our country; fifty-three years from the present*), England's Edward (interlinear note: *given name*) Jenner (interlinear note: *family name*) further expanded the experiments and made them public to the world. In the book of Copleyratt [i.e., a source identified as such], on the fourteenth day of May 1796 (interlinear note: *corresponding to the eighth year of Kansei, the year Heishin [丙辰], in our country; fifty-five years from the present*), Jenner first tried [the inoculation] on one child, and thereafter administered it to many children, thereby establishing its reliability. In 1798, he had a new book on cowpox printed. In the annotations of Copleyratt, it is stated that the method of cowpox inoculation was originally

[Left folio]

discovered in Germany. In a book published in that country in 1765, it was recorded that cowpox has the effect of preventing epidemic smallpox (*tenkō-tō*); and further, in a book published in 1769, it was argued that servants and maids in households that keep dairy cattle who had contracted cowpox were thereby rendered immune to epidemic smallpox. Moreover, a person named Plett of Stakkentorf (interlinear note: *place name*) in 1791 (interlinear note: *corresponding to the third year of Kansei, the year Shin'gai [辛亥], in our country; sixty years from the present*) inoculated two young girls with cowpox and obtained good results; however, no one praised him for it, and the credit was attributed to Jenner alone, so it is said.
