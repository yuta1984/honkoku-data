...and was born in 1749 (interlinear note: *corresponding to the third year of Kan'en, the year Kōgo [庚午], in our country; one hundred and one years from the present*). In 1776 (interlinear note: *corresponding to the sixth year of An'ei, the year Teyū [丁酉], in our country; seventy-four years from the present*), he first examined the nature of cowpox and, following the method of smallpox inoculation, discovered how to take it from cattle and transmit it to humans, and then to transmit it from person to person. Thereafter, accumulating further experiments, he came to know with ever-greater certainty the reliable efficacy of its preventive power, and in 1798 he published a book on the subject. (Interlinear note: *The above three books are based on the translations and accounts of Mr. Ogata.*)

The *Gyūtō Shinsho* [New Book on Cowpox] states: Cowpox is a disease that has existed since ancient times, and its transmission to those who take milk from cattle is likewise a matter of antiquity. In general, those who take milk tend to have the soft and thin parts of the skin come into contact with cowpox, and those who happen to suffer abrasions or chapping of the skin cannot escape infection.

[Left folio]

It further states: Jenner, Pearson, Odier, and others of their circle personally investigated and studied this method in depth, and ultimately demonstrated clearly that cowpox, being mild in nature, would—upon a single inoculation—exempt one from the calamity of smallpox. They earnestly spread this technique. When they first began to practice it, they obtained the vaccine material either directly from cattle or from those who had naturally contracted the disease. Thereafter, they transmitted it from person A to person B, and from person B to person C, and in all cases only mild and favorable cowpox pustules developed, with not a single harmful outcome. In the Netherlands, cowpox was experimentally inoculated into fifteen thousand children, and after some time had passed, five thousand of those inoculated children were subjected to smallpox inoculation as a test; not a single child contracted it...
