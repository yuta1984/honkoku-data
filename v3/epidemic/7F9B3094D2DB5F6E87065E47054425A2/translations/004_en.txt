*Sirakus* [?] states: The disease of *tianhua* (smallpox) was originally absent from the western nations. In the year 1104 CE (corresponding to the first year of Chōji, the year *Kōshin* [甲申], which is 747 years before the present), it spread from eastern regions and became widespread throughout the nations of the west. (Based on *Gyūtō Kibō* [*Wondrous Methods of Cowpox Vaccination*].)

*Nieuwenhuis* (a book title) states: In the year 572 CE (corresponding to the first year of Emperor Bidatsu's reign, the year *Jinshin* [壬辰], which is 1,279 years before the present), the disease was transmitted from Ethiopia (a place inhabited by Black peoples) to Arabia; in the year 650 CE (corresponding to the first year of Hakuchi, the year *Kōjutsu* [庚戌], which is 1,201 years before the present), it was transmitted to Egypt; and around the years 1200–1300 CE, owing to military campaigns in the eastern regions,

Question: The ancient accounts all say that smallpox originates from congenital fetal toxin (*taidoku*). Is this correct or not?

Answer: It has been said that the source of smallpox lies in the "fire of desire" (*yokka*) of the parents, or alternatively, that it is caused by the fetus consuming the impurity of the mother's blood while in the womb. However, these are merely commonly held traditional theories and do not accord with actual principle.
