The Treatise on the General Treatment of Smallpox (痘瘡通治篇) by Most (謨斯篤) states: Smallpox first spread to the nations of the East in the year 1200 CE (corresponding to the second year of the Shōji era, the year Kanoe-Saru [庚申], in our country; 651 years ago from the present). At that time it was called "childhood epidemic toxic disease" (小児天行疫毒). (The foregoing is based on the Yōyō Seigi [幼幼精義].)

Sidang (嘶噹㖦) states: The disease of smallpox (天花) did not originally exist in the nations of the western regions. In the year 1104 CE (corresponding to the first year of the Chōji era, the year Kinoe-Saru [甲申], in our country; 747 years ago from the present), it spread from the eastern regions and became widespread throughout the nations of the western territories. (Based on the Gyūtō Kibō [牛痘奇法; "The Wondrous Method of Cowpox"].)

The work Nieuwenhuis (ニユエンホイス) states: In the year 572 CE (corresponding to the first year of the reign of Emperor Bidatsu, the year Mizunoe-Tatsu [壬辰], in our country; 1,279 years ago from the present), the disease spread from Ethiopia (the land where Black peoples dwell) to Arabia; in the year 650 CE (corresponding to the first year of the Hakuchi era, the year Kanoe-Inu [庚戌], in our country; 1,201 years ago from the present), it spread to Egypt (阨日多). Around the years 1200–1300 CE, owing to military campaigns in the eastern regions,

Question: The ancient explanations uniformly hold that smallpox originates in congenital fetal toxin (先天之胎毒). Is this correct or not?

Answer: The notion that the source of smallpox is caused by the "fire of parental desire" (父母慾火), or alternatively that it is caused by the fetus consuming the impurity of the mother's blood while in the womb—these are views that have become established through common habitual transmission, and they are not grounded in actual truth. The reason for this is as follows: this disease did not exist in ancient times, and even today, when a person successfully avoids and escapes exposure, they go through their entire life without falling ill and remain unharmed.
