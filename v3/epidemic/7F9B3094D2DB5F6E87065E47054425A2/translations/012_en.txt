...without exception. In London as well, an official order was issued, and a new cowpox vaccination center was established to practice this technique exclusively. Those who came to request inoculation were so numerous as to be almost beyond counting. In France too, an order was issued from the hospital in Paris, and cowpox vaccine material was sought from the Netherlands and administered on a trial basis, but without effect. As a result, Dr. Odier, a physician from England, was summoned and entrusted with the task of inoculation. He promptly inoculated one hundred and fifty people, all of whom developed favorable cowpox pustules. From this, everyone came to know with certainty the remarkable efficacy of cowpox. The *Gyūtō-sho* [Book on Cowpox] by Pearson (a personal name) states: When those who take milk contract cowpox, they develop inflammation and swelling in various parts of the hands, initially producing small blisters resembling burns,

[Left folio]

which quickly suppurate. Generally, the appearance is like a Kreutzer (a coin), with the outer edge raised higher than the center, taking on a vivid bluish color. Fever then follows, and the axillary lymph nodes swell and rise. These various symptoms persist for one or two days, and even in the most prolonged cases do not exceed four days, gradually subsiding thereafter. Because those infected with cowpox transmit it back to cattle while milking, and because of this cycle of transmission, the vaccine material was able to continue without interruption. (Interlinear note: *Comparing this to the ordinary course of disease, it appears to be the most rapid. Perhaps there is some difference between this and the case of those in whom it develops after inoculation.*)
