## Famous Places of Edo: Witty Wordplays on Measles

This page presents a collection of humorous jiguchi (地口) — comic wordplay poems that use the names of famous Edo locations as punchlines, creating double meanings related to the measles epidemic.

**[Right Page]**

**[Column 1]**
Neither measles nor cholera can stay long — Nihonbashi ("Japan's Bridge," implying things cannot "bridge over" into permanence)

Everyone under thirty gets the measles — Surugachō ("As surely as Suruga," wordplay on "suruga gotoku" = just as one would expect, everyone gets it)

Everyone over thirty — Sumidagawa ("The Sumida River," punning on "sumida" = it's already over/done with)

Don't eat oily things — Warishimizu ("Split drainage water," warning against rich foods during illness)

**[Column 2]**
The face of someone with a high fever — Akasaka ("Red Hill," punning on the red face of measles patients)

Everyone's complexion — Aoyama ("Blue/Green Mountain," punning on the pallid complexion of the sick)

Call the doctor quickly — Koishikawa ("Small Stone River," where the Edo public medical clinic, the Yōjōsho, was located)

**[Column 3]**
Even a quack doctor can get lucky — Teppōzu ("Musket Sandbar," punning on "teppō" = musket, i.e., hit or miss)

The medicine seller's profits — Ōhashi ("Great Bridge," punning on making a "great" profit)

The palanquin bearers get paid upfront — Naitō Shinjuku (new lodging area; patients need carrying)

With rice porridge and pickles, one soon gets hungry — Sukiyabashi ("Sukiya Bridge," punning on "suku" = to become empty/hungry)

**[Left Page]**

**[Column 1]**
I want to drink but should I not? — Suidōbashi ("Waterway Bridge," punning on water being all one can drink)

Entertainers are in a pitiful state — Ōji ("Prince," but also suggesting "ōjō" = dying/in great difficulty, as performances are cancelled)

Take good care of your health — Shinagawa (punning on "shina yoku" = gracefully/properly)

Even young couples must sleep apart — Nezu ("Nezu Shrine area," punning on "nezu" = not sleeping together)

Even if the measles lets up, still — Yoshiwara ("The pleasure quarter," punning on "yoshi" = alright, good — implying one can return to the brothels once recovered)

**[Column 2]**
Food for the sick — Takanawa ("High Rope," suggesting meager, sparse food like walking a tightrope)

The proprietors of brothels complain — Iidamachi ("Iida Town," punning on "iida" suggesting "no rice/income")

Is it as dangerous as it seems, will costs run high, one must consider — Shian-bashi ("Deliberation Bridge," punning on having to "think carefully" about the situation)

Practice halls are silent with measles, not even a response — Shamisen-bori ("Shamisen Canal," punning on "shamisen" = to play dumb/give evasive answers; also the music has stopped)

Even just the appearance of relief from above — Ureshi no Mori ("Joyful Forest," the very sight of official aid is cause for happiness)

**[Column 3]**
After measles, one's eyes become — Kasumigaseki ("Misty Barrier," punning on "kasumu" = to become hazy/blurry, a known after-effect of measles)

The hearts of people from other provinces — Ryōgokubashi ("Bridge of Two Provinces," punning on those caught between their homeland and Edo)

The doctor goes from house to house — Mimeguri ("Three turns/circuits," punning on the doctor making rounds, or "hōhō" = crawling away in defeat)

The benevolence of the authorities — Fukagawa ("Deep River," punning on "fukai" = deep, profound — expressing gratitude for official relief measures)
