"Even if the parents are somewhat negligent in their nursing care, even if the children have ailments or internal complaints — is it not your duty to watch over them and ensure the smallpox passes lightly? If you cannot fulfill this duty, then why do you sit upon the household shrine, proudly and arrogantly eat the red offerings placed before you, and expect to be venerated as the

[Upper left folio]
Smallpox Deities? Whatever the circumstances, from this point on, see to it that no harm comes to the children!" — so declared [Tametomo], with great vigor and heroic spirit.

○ Now, these words reached the ears of the grandchildren and legitimate heirs of [the smallpox deity] Yunootomine before long. [They said among themselves:] "Whether he be a god or Tametomo, we simply cannot oppose him outright. We must see to it that [the illness] passes without delay before the twelve days are up, and that [the children] recover as swiftly as possible. However, since Lord Tametomo is so quick to act, even if only our own kind go to plead our case, if our lord [Tametomo] is displeased with us, then it would be best to call upon our familiar companions in play — those associated with us — and ask them to intercede with Tametomo on our behalf." And so,

[Lower right folio]
straightaway, they put forth Daruma first among all, along with other comic and endearing figures whose very faces bring delight — such as the owl —

[Lower left folio]
and also the auspicious cart, the papier-mâché tiger, Kintarō, and the young monk, and others who are easy to call upon — for even this is a matter of the gods. Without resorting to complicated arguments, stepping nimbly and swiftly, keeping it informal, in their own fashion, [they headed] toward Tametomo. →[continued on next page]
