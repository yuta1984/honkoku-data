**Main Text**

**Upper Right Page**

(Daruma and the others having arrived,) at this moment, Tametomo addressed Daruma, saying: "You are always at the side of the smallpox deity like a caretaker, never leaving their side, and yet you allow children to be harmed — are you not ashamed? Or perhaps, having attained enlightenment, you consider yourself above such concerns? I would hear your explanation." At this, Daruma fell into a moment of thoughtful deliberation. ○ At this time, Daruma was devising a way to win back the favor of the great deity Tametomo. Yet whatever he might say, faced with the fierce power of the smallpox deities — who with a single glare could make one vanish, and who, even if one fled hiding under a *sandawara* (rice-straw lid), could stop one dead with a single shout — it seemed that there was nothing to be done but submit, with no time even for *sasayu* (bamboo-grass hot water), and no recourse but dried cod (*hidatsu*). Deciding therefore that argument would be futile, Daruma thought the best course was simply to comply without fuss, and said: "Indeed, your grievance is entirely reasonable. From this moment forward, we shall work alongside the smallpox deities with all our might and dedication, so that not a single soul among ten thousand shall come to harm." ◐

**Lower Right Page**

◐ Having pledged this as firmly as a posted seal-notice, Daruma and the owl exchanged glances, blinking their eyes repeatedly, and [remained thus].

**Upper Left Page**

Meanwhile, the smallpox deities had gathered at the Handa Shrine, making all manner of excuses among themselves. As a result, there was no smallpox deity properly guarding the children anywhere, and so the worms of chronic childhood ailments — beginning with *kan no mushi* (the worm of irritability) — along with fetal toxins (*taidoku*) and all manner of disease-worms began to run rampant. Furthermore, gods of pestilence and epidemic, seizing upon the negligence of the smallpox deities, attached themselves to children in order to enjoy their own aggrandizement. This caused great suffering to the children, and so their parents, recognizing this as a critical moment, called for doctors, while others gathered miraculous medicines to nurse their children, sparing no effort whatsoever.
