When I further questioned him on other matters concerning smallpox, his answers were exceedingly careless and not worth taking seriously. The Netherlands is, after all, a barbarian land (*ikoku*), and its people are close to birds and beasts. Perhaps such treatments may exist there, but they are ill-suited to the people of China or Japan. I once gathered and read those books, and further pressed and inquired into their theories, but found them likewise not worth taking seriously. They are not to be trusted.

One note: After the fifth or sixth day, when itching appears, one must pay especially close attention. If the facial pustules are scratched open,

**[Left folio]**
the vital energy (*ki*) will escape, the pus will fail to form properly, the eyes will suddenly open wide, the swelling will subside, and various deteriorating complications (*henshō*) will arise—all because the poison turns inward and attacks the interior. If the critical areas are broken open, no medicine or physician can retrieve the situation. Laypeople think only that scratching will leave scars, but this is a grave misunderstanding. What I fear is not the leaving of scars; it is that death will surely follow—and this is precisely why I so strictly admonish against scratching the pustules open. It is only a matter of four or five days. I implore you, with the utmost earnestness, to remain vigilant day and night, and ensure that the pustules are absolutely not broken. This is to be regarded as the foremost duty in nursing care.
