Encouraging Good and Chastising Evil — Government Licensed — Nishiki-e Newspaper
No. 28

No one has ever seen a square egg — yet as though beholding the moon on the thirtieth night (when there can be no moon), I have heard a tale of a courtesan of true sincerity.

A prostitute kept by a certain Takahashi of Shinmachi-dōri 2-chōme, named Wakatsuru, had her family home at Awaza Shimo-dōri 1-chōme. Her father had died early, her mother was aged and her elder brother sickly; pressed by poverty, she became a prostitute. Then, by the government edict of years past releasing prostitutes, she unexpectedly was able to return to her parents' home. But, as the saying goes, "if the people increase, the water increases" — her return only added one more mouth to feed, and their poverty grew worse. So she took up the work she knew, laundering for hire, and, caring nothing for her own appearance, devoted herself to her mother and brother. A certain gentleman, admiring such sincerity, offered to take her under his protection; but Wakatsuru would not consent. "If I were to take customers now," she said, "I should fail in my obligation to my former master." Thus even in bitter poverty she kept faith. When Takahashi heard of this true-heartedness he was deeply moved: he called her back to his house, made her a courtesan again as before, and gave over to her every coin of what she earned. By this, it is said, Wakatsuru now supports her mother in comfort. Surely this may be called the virtue of a filial child.

　　Narrated by the Master of Jishū-sha
