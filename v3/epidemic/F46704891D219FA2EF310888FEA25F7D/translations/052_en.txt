Nichinichi Shimbun, No. 20

In Funakoshi-chō, in the prefecture of Osaka, there lives a woman named Matsumoto Ai, who makes her living as a bonesetter. In private she has trained in jūjutsu, but as she is only twenty-six and of fair appearance, her valor lay hidden from view.

At the beginning of the First Month of Meiji 8 (1875), she was walking along the Nagara embankment in company with a young woman of the neighborhood when, in the chill river wind of dusk, four ruffians sprang upon them, seized the two women and began to molest them. At first the women meekly tried to talk their way free, but the men's rudeness only grew bolder, until they knocked the two down and threw themselves upon them. At this Matsumoto Ai thought, "Shall I let them off any longer?"—and single-handed she flung the four men aside and scattered them, and with the swift skill of her practiced art she even hurled one of them splashing into the river. The villains, terrified, cried out "This is too much!" and all together fled—leaving only a white wake behind them—scattering along the riverbank like ashes flung to the wind.

Whose descendant, one wonders, can this woman be? Her father must surely have been a man of note in swordsmanship and jūjutsu. The old tale of the human pillar buried at Nagara Bridge has long since passed away, yet all who heard of it marveled, saying that this girl's courage was surely a keepsake of that ancient valor.
