Whenever one speaks of newspapers, it is always love-suicides, robbers, adulterers and elopements — not a single good story among them. The author, therefore, taking this to heart, presents today for a change the tale of a virtuous woman.

This is Oharu, the widow of Ono Kamekichi of Nagao Village, Asakuchi District, in the province of Bitchū. She is twenty-eight this year. Having married Kamekichi at the age of sixteen, she bore a child in the second year of Meiji (1869). But from about that time Kamekichi contracted the dread disease of leprosy, and though his body rotted away day by day, Oharu's heart never rotted — such was her fidelity. Carrying her nursing infant, she dressed other people's hair for a pittance, and with that thin wisp of smoke (a bare livelihood) she supported herself while keeping her chastity intact. Her parents, pitying her lot, urged her to divorce him; but she kept to the way of a wife and would not consent, and so passed her days.

Last autumn her husband Kamekichi died, and once again her parents urged her to remarry. But she said, "This little child is my husband's keepsake. If I do not let this child succeed to the family name, with what face could I meet my husband in the world beyond?" — and she would by no means agree. Surely this is what should be called a beauty of the heart.
