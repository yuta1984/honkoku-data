In the summer of the seventh year of Meiji (1874), two robbers broke one night into the house of Jūzō, a village-office errand man of Urae Village in Osaka Prefecture. Seizing Jūzō, they shouted abuse at him, demanding, "Show us the wealthy houses in this village!" But when they saw Jūzō quickly snatch up a seaweed-cutting sickle, the villains at once flashed their naked blades and cut him down. His wife Yuki struggled desperately to save her husband, but all in vain: pitilessly, at the age of forty, her husband's life came to its end, and he perished for nothing.

Their son Yoshimatsu, taking his little brother with him, ran through the village from house to house and as far as the residence of the kochō (village headman), calling for help; but while the crowd was hurrying to the scene, the robbers made good their escape. The wife Yuki, who had collapsed near the gate, managed to say, "I was shot in the belly with a gun by the other robber, the one who was guarding the doorway just now" — and before she had finished speaking, she breathed her last.

Thus "Jū" — long life — vanished away like "yuki" (snow), and husband and wife together crossed the Mountain of Death and the River of Three Crossings. As for the murderous robbers who had thus washed blood with blood, they could not escape the net of Heaven: before long they were arrested and sentenced to death by beheading.
