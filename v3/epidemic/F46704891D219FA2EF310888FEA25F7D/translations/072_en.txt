Konobu, now the second-generation
　Sadanobu, artist

Published by Mori Bunzō

Published by Awabun
Engraved by Hori Tora

A certain day-labourer employed by the Hyōbushō (Ministry of Military Affairs) received a five-yen gold coin from the ministry and took it home, putting it away under his bedding that night. The next morning, however, he clean forgot about it and went off to work. Suddenly remembering, he took fright, hurried home and searched — but the coin was nowhere to be found. He had a daughter of seven, and when he questioned her she said, "This morning I picked up five rin and bought sweets with it." He went at once to the confectioner and explained the matter, whereupon the confectioner said, "I never noticed either — I used that coin to buy turnips." So off he went again, hunting here and there for the turnip seller, and at last found him and told him the whole story. The turnip seller said, "I have no idea whether I took in a gold coin or not," and opened his purse — and there, sure enough, it was. Greatly astonished, he said, "Since this belongs to you," and promptly handed it back, asking only for his five rin. Moved by such honesty, the labourer gave him one shu as a token of thanks.

Had this gold coin never turned up, it would have been — no turnip (kabu), but a "kabu-shimai," a complete ruin. That it was all settled so roundly (en, a pun on "yen") was thanks indeed to that lucky five yen he had on hand. Ah, money must be handled honestly and with care!

Nichinichi Shimbun, No. 240
