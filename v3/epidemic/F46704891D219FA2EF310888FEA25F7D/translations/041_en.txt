"The demon that dwells within one's own heart — ah, how fearful it is to behold! Only the man himself truly knows that terror." This is the story of an Osaka robber, one Shōkichi of Kodenbō.

In the second month of the eighth year of Meiji (1875), Shōkichi was arrested in Oda Prefecture and sent under escort to Osaka, where he was already subjected to severe interrogation and confessed in full, without holding anything back, the whole course of the crimes he had committed.

Then he spoke as follows: "The people I have killed up to now are beyond counting. At the time, none of it troubled me in the least. But from the days when I was in hiding in Oda Prefecture, every night the ghosts of the men and women I had killed appeared before me — now raging at me, now heaping abuse upon me — and at last they began to show themselves even in broad daylight. Words cannot describe how terrifying it was."

This shows that while a criminal's nerve is still strong, such vengeful spirits work no harm; but once he comes at length to fear his own guilt and is reduced to fleeing and hiding, they appear. Such cases, they say, are many in this world.
