Shimbun Zue (Illustrated News), No. 8

　　Drawn by Sadanobu II, formerly Konobu
　　　　　　　Published by Yao Zen

Not in the sense of the proverb "the blind man fears no snake" [i.e. reckless through ignorance], but as a truly stout-hearted man who would not yield even to robbers — such is the person who takes the studio name Hinhin-an ("Hut of Poverty upon Poverty") and who has won fame for his virtuoso playing (*kyokubiki*) of the shamisen; he is commonly known as Ikuhisa Bokuichirō.

On the eleventh day of the Fourth Month of this year, at the northern end of Tamae Bridge, three robbers blocked the way before his vehicle and were on the point of stripping him of his clothes, when Bokuichi spoke up:

"The clothes of a poor man such as myself would fetch but a trifling sum even if you sold them. If you will let me off from having my clothes taken, I shall offer to your ears one piece of music, which is my livelihood."

Even these ruffians were won over by the reasonableness of his words, and thinking, perhaps, that there was something more to it, they presently asked him to strike up a tune. Bokuichi thereupon played a well-practised piece of his own speciality, and, moved as they must have been by its exquisite tones, the robbers not only went off empty-handed but even gave Bokuichi twenty sen in coin before departing.

　　　　　　　　Recorded by Shōjōdō Kyūka
