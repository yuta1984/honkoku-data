Yūbin Hōchi Shinbun (Postal News), No. 551

　　　　　　　　　　　[Illustration by] Taiso Yoshitoshi

In Funakoshi-chō in Osaka there is a woman named Matsumoto Ai, whose trade is bonesetting. She is still only twenty-six years old, and she had long been skilled in jūjutsu as well; but because her face was so comely, no one realized how formidable she was.

Not long ago she was passing along the embankment of the Nagara River, accompanied by the daughter of a neighboring household. It was twilight, and the wind off the river was cold, when four ruffians sprang out and seized them — two men to Oai and two to the neighbor's girl — intending to rape them. At this Oai flew into a great rage: she hurled into the river the man who had grappled with her, knocked another sprawling with a thrust, seized by the scruff of the neck the man who had pinned the neighbor's girl down and was straddling her, and twisted him to the ground, then clenched her fist and struck another about the eyes. At this they all took fright and fled in every direction.

　　　　　　　　　　　Recorded by San'yūtei Enchō
