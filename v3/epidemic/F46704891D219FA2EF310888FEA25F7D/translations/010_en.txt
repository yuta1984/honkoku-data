Meiji 8 [1875]. Osaka Nishiki-e Shimbun, No. 24

"At Nan'endō the wife goes up in flames" — that is only a slurred rendering of the pilgrims' hymn. But no; this time it is no play on words: on a spring day two men were actually burned to death. It happened on the fourth day of the third month of this year, on Mount Wakakusa in Nara Prefecture — and there is nothing "unclear as a deer" about it (the facts are certain).

To be sure, the burning of Mount Mikasa (Wakakusa) is a yearly custom, and so, having set fire to the grass at the foot of the mountain, the two men supposed the grass could not possibly burn itself out so quickly and settled down to rest on the very summit. But the wind blew up far more fiercely than they expected. They struggled frantically to escape, yet the raging flames blocked them on all four sides, and there was nothing for it — they perished in the fire. A pitiful thing indeed.

Nor is this the only such case: whoever one may be, and in whatever matter, one may lose one's life or one's property through a moment's carelessness, and examples of this sort are by no means rare in the world. Take care of yourself; take care with fire.
