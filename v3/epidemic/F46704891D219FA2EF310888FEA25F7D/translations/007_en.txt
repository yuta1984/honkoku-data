Genjirō, son of Chōemon of Komatsuda village in Nagato Province, was originally the child of a timber dealer near Higashi-Torii in Toyoura-chō. Abandoned at the age of two, he was picked up and raised by Chōemon.

Being a gentle, obedient lad, he went into service as an apprentice at a certain house, where he so won the favour of his master and mistress that he kept none of the money he was given for himself, but sent it all home to the parent who had taken him in — for as the saying goes, "if one god casts you away, another god picks you up." At this single lamp of filial devotion his foster father rejoiced, saying it was "the sevenfold radiance that rewards the trouble of raising him."

Meanwhile, however, his true parents, hearing that he had grown into such a fine young man, sent an intermediary to negotiate, saying they wished to have him returned to their own house and raise him themselves. But the honest, single-minded Genjirō replied that the debt of gratitude he owed to the parent who had raised him to this day was deeper than any owed to the parents who had cast him off at the age of two — and deep though the pool may be, he would have nothing to do with his true parents' shallow scheming. To this the real parents could make not a single word of reply, and the matter was dropped just as it stood.

Poor though one may be, to abandon the treasure that is a child is no proper human way. Raise your children, and see — a day will surely come when it profits you.

Meiji 8 [1875], sixth month. Reported in the Yomiuri, No. 121.
