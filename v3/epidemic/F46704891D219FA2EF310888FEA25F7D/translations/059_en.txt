Hiragana Ehiri Shinbun (Illustrated Newspaper in Kana), No. 16

On the night of the fourteenth of the fifth month, a burglar broke into the house of Hara Sanpei, a dealer in household sundries in Shibaguchi Nichōme.

He wore a papier‑mâché mask of Asahina, wrapped his face in a dark‑blue furoshiki, and, holding a drawn sword, demanded in the usual formula, "Hand over your money!" The wife, trembling, brought out a trifling sum. "That's not enough — bring out your clothes!" he said. "Yes, yes," she answered, and took three or four garments from a chest of drawers. As the burglar set his sword down to bundle up the clothes, Sanpei instantly seized the sword and slashed the robber's face five times.

"Asahina" cried out and collapsed. Just then a policeman came running up; Sanpei, mistaking him for the robber's accomplice, struck at him, but the officer parried the blow with his staff (jitte), calling out, "I am Hidemori of the patrol!" — at which Sanpei at last came to his senses. Meanwhile the burglar made his escape.
