Osaka Shimbun Nishiki-e, No. 3　　　　　　　Konobu, now Sadanobu II, painted this

At Takaido village near Tokyo, on the twentieth day of the fourth month of Meiji 8 [1875]: a seven-year-old girl of a certain household had been minding a baby born the previous year, but toward evening she came home alone. When her mother asked after the infant, the girl replied, "Just as you always say — 'If you cry too much I'll throw you in the river' — well, he cried so hard there was nothing to be done, so I threw him into the river and came back."

Hearing this, the mother went nearly mad with distraction and rushed to the riverside, but the baby had already been carried off by the current and there was no trace of him. Returning to the house, she seized the girl and beat her without mercy — and the blows fell so badly that this child, too, died.

Aghast at the calamity, the mother thought, "With my husband away from home, how can I ever answer for this?" — and she likewise threw herself into the water.

Thus does karma come round: in a single day, three of them set out together on the journey of death — a "shallow" affair at the shallows of the Sanzu River, and rumour of it ran as high as the name of Takaido itself. Pitiful indeed it is; yet was it not all owing to the thinness of natural affection?

You who have children, clean out your ears and listen well!

Lamented in the Yomiuri, No. 88.
