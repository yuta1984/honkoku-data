Tokyo Nichinichi Shimbun, No. 322

It is said that "for teaching the people love and affection, nothing is better than filial piety." Thus, faithfully keeping to the old teachings — of the man who lay upon the ice and obtained a carp, and of the man who dug bamboo shoots out of the snow — this man, during his aged mother's long illness, attended to her meals, her decoctions of medicine, and to both her bodily necessities, without borrowing another's hand in the slightest; his care was utterly thorough. Moreover, on fine, clear days he would carry her on his back for a stroll, and to see his mother's delight was his own greatest joy. And so it went, year after year, as though it were all a single day.

Once, though the autumn was still young, his mother expressed a wish to eat ice. He therefore went several ri away to Naganuma Mountain, descended deep into the bottom of a ravine, and at last managed to find some ice, which he gave to his mother. This filial son is a farmer of Kagaminuma village, Iwase district, Iwashiro province, in Fukushima prefecture, and he has been granted a sum of money in commendation.

　　Recorded by Tententendō Donton
