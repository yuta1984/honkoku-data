**[Left Page: Comparative Table of the Risks of Various Pox Diseases (*Shoto Ken'i Hyō*)]**

**Categories:** Signs & Symptoms / Mortality / Danger / Pustulation

---

**[Great Misfortune] Natural Smallpox (Smallpox)**

*Nature:*
It is a contagious disease that spreads from a single location. It is ordinarily extremely virulent, and the suffering is unbearable.

*Mortality:*
One in six patients dies. When it spreads through a major city, the death toll typically reaches over 3,000 per year.

*Danger:*
One in three patients will inevitably fall into a critical condition.

*Pustulation:*
Ordinarily, numerous pustules break out across the entire body, and their odor is utterly foul.

---

**[Misfortune] Variolation by Insufflation (*Shuto Funbi-hō*) — i.e., the method practiced at Kinegawa**

*Nature:*
While there are cases where patients recover without incident, sometimes various dangerous and severe symptoms appear.

*Mortality:*
Roughly one in thirty patients dies.

*Danger:*
One in ten patients develops adverse symptoms. In some cases, true pox does not develop and false pox appears instead.

*Pustulation:*
Occasionally, throat tumors or scrofula (*ruirekki*, swelling of the cervical lymph nodes) may develop.

---

**[Middling] Vesiculation Variolation (*Shuto Happō-hō*)**

*Nature:*
There is a risk of contagion. While it is generally considered benign, it can occasionally cause life-threatening conditions.

*Mortality:*
One in one hundred patients dies.

*Danger:*
One in fifty patients is prone to developing abnormal symptoms.

*Pustulation:*
Rarely, the site of inoculation may become severely inflamed, with significant suppuration and rupture of pustules.

---

**[Fortunate] Human Pox Inoculation by Scarification (*Shuto Jinpō Sashi-hō*)**

*Nature:*
Compared to natural smallpox, all cases are benign and there are no dangerous symptoms. However, granules (pox lesions) do appear across the entire body, and the duration of the course varies — this is where it falls short of the cowpox method.

*Mortality:*
One in three hundred patients dies. However, I have administered this method since Tenpō 9 (1838) over a period of twelve years, applying it to seventy or eighty children per year and cumulatively reaching several thousand children, yet not a single child suffered a critical condition or died. This is well known to all. Perhaps this is entirely due to the great care with which the procedure was performed.

*Pustulation:*
(No particular adverse notes.)

---

**[Most Auspicious] Cowpox Vaccination (*Gyūtō-hō*)**

*Nature:*
When administered with due care, it is invariably mild and calm, producing no symptoms that could be considered alarming, and there is absolutely no doubt that it protects against natural smallpox.

*Mortality:*
There is absolutely no mortality.

*Danger:*
There are absolutely no dangerous or severe symptoms.

*Pustulation:*
Only a single vesicle forms at the site of inoculation.
