According to "Bencao Gangmu" (Honzo Komoku):
　　　Chicken Intestine Herb (Keichōsō) ○Kawarakena ○Ohakoben ○Tabirako
　　　(Murasaki Hakoben) — One of the seven herbs of Jinjitsu (New Year's Day)

　　　The chicken intestine herb (keichōsai) tastes slightly bitter, and when chewed produces a slippery saliva; its stem has no thread-like fibers and is slightly purple in color. Its flowers are also said to be purple — this must be it.
　　　The goose intestine herb (gachōsai) tastes sweet, its stem is hollow with thread-like fibers, and its flowers are white. This is considered a separate species.

According to "Nōsei Zensho" (Complete Book of Agricultural Administration), the chicken intestine herb has a square stem and purple color, leaves that are opposite-paired and resemble water-chestnut leaves, and bears pink flowers. Could this be the same plant?
　　　No, it is not. The slightest difference has already been noted.

Recorded in the relevant volume of "Gunpōreki."

According to "Kyūkō Honzō" (Famine Relief Materia Medica): Cotton Thread Herb (Tabirako/Kawarakena), Earth Bowl Herb (Kawarakena), Tabirako, Hotoke-no-za (Buddha's Seat).

Among the seven herbs, what is called Tabirako and Hotoke-no-za is most numerous. Yellow-flowered plants are also called Tabirako (according to "Yamato Honzo"). This herb is commonly called "umanokoyashi" (horse manure herb). The name "Kawarakena" (Earth Vessel Herb) is not found — what exactly is it based upon? "Gunpōreki" provides a detailed explanation of this herb. Looking now at "Kyūkō Honzō," the name Tabirako does not appear under Cotton Thread Herb, and "Baba-shikomuna" (Kawarakena) should be investigated separately.

According to "Wakan Sansai Zue," in the section on soft and slippery vegetables:
　　　Yellow Quail Herb (Tabirako)

According to "Zoho Tashiki-hen," in the section on soft and slippery vegetables:
　　　Chicken Intestine Herb — Japanese name, per current examination: "murasaki hana no hakoben"

　　　In "Tashiki-hen," the meaning of the name "Earth Bowl Herb" is absent.
　　　In "Gunpōreki," the Chicken Intestine Herb is identified as Kawarakena. Baba-shikomuna is also called Earth Vessel Herb. These two things likely share the same name. The Chicken Intestine Herb in "Kyūkō Honzō" does not match in terms of flowers and leaves.

　　　　　　　　　　○"Nōsei Zensho"
　　　　　　　　　　○"Gunpōreki" also says: Baba-shikomuna (Kawarakena)
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　(Habashuusai)

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　I note that:
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　"Nōsei Zensho" lists Baba-shikomuna and Chicken Intestine Herb separately, but these are likely the same plant. The Baba-shikomuna (Habashuusai) in "Wakan Sansai Zue" corresponds well to the Earth Bowl Herb (Kawarakena).
