## Hasu (Lotus) — continued

**Same species:**
　There is one variety with white flowers.

---

**From *Honzō Kōmoku* (本草綱目, *Bencao Gangmu*, Compendium of Materia Medica):**

　　菡萏 (*Kantан* / the unopened lotus bud)
　　　[Japanese name: *Hasu no hana*]

---

**From *Imyō Bunruishō* (異名分類抄, Classified Collection of Alternative Names):**

Alternative names for the lotus (*ren* / 蓮):
- *Tsumanashi-kusa* (妻無し草, "husbandless herb")
- *Tsurenashi-kusa* (連れ無し草, "unaccompanied herb")
- *Mizu no hana* (水ノハナ, "water flower")
- *Tsuyu-kan-kusa* (ツユ堪クサ, "dew-bearing herb")
- *Mizukan-sō* (水堪草, "water-bearing herb")

　Upon reflection, Cui Bao's *Gujin Zhu* (崔豹『古今注』, *Commentary on Things Ancient and Modern*) states:
　"*Fukyō* (芙蕖); another name is *kōka* (荷花, lotus flower); another name is *suika* (水花, water flower)," and so on.

---

**The author further states (in addition to what was said earlier):**

Concerning "*Sekiren-shi*" (石蓮子, "stone lotus seeds"): According to Su Song (蘇頌) in the *Honzō Kōmoku*, the [seeds], upon reaching autumn, turn black and sink into the water, becoming *sekiren-shi*. Li Shizhen (李時珍) further states: "What remains after cutting away the black husk of the stone lotus is called *rennniku* (蓮肉, lotus flesh/meat)."

○ The *Kenmei-en* (兼名苑, *Jianming Yuan*) states: A lotus flower that has already opened is called "*fuyō*" [芙■; character partially illegible], while one that has not yet unfurled is called "*kantан*" (菡萏).

○ That which grows on land (*oka*) is called "*Moku-fuyō*" (木芙蓉, "woody *fuyō*" / *Hibiscus mutabilis*), and it has long been the custom in this country to simply call it "*fuyō*." That which grows in water is called "*Sui-fuyō*" (水芙蓉, "water *fuyō*"), and this is the lotus flower (*renka*).
