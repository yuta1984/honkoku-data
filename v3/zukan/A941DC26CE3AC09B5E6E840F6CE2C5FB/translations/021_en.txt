The "Wakan Sansai Zue" (Illustrated Sino-Japanese Encyclopedia), in the section on moisture-loving plants, states:
　　　Dukengusa (hototogisu) [toad lily] — the common name is the same as the proper name;
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　the proper name is unknown.

　　　　　　　　　(Listed under the 【Flowering Plants】 category in the "Yamato Honzō")

The "Bukō Sanbutsu-shi" (Record of Products of Edo) states:
　　　Aburatten-so (hototogisu) [oil-spotted plant]

　　　　　　　　　On the fifth day of the first period of Nanryo (the eighth month), year Hinoe-Inu [1826],
　　　　　　　　　a stem was gathered at Dōkanzan,
　　　　　　　　　and one illustration was made.

(Annotation by the author, Tamaki [Baien]):
The dukengusa (hototogisu) has two varieties of flower. The one with white flowers bearing pale purple spots is called "yama-dukengusa" (mountain toad lily). Upon examining the illustration in the "Jikinsho," the specimen depicted therein corresponds to the yama-dukengusa. It differs slightly from what is simply called "dukengusa" (toad lily). Nevertheless, they are not to be regarded as entirely separate species.
