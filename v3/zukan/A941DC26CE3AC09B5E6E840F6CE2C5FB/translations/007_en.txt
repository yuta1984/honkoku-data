## Section on Water Plants

**[Inscription on illustration]**
On the Bon festival day of the seventh month of the year *Kinoto-Tori* (乙酉, 1825), drawn from life (*shasei*) while viewing the water's edge at Asai (Shallow Well/Shore).

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　[WATER PLANTS]

---

**From *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草):**

　*Omotaka* (arrowhead / *Sagittaria trifolia*)
　　*Hime-Omotaka* (小ヲモダカ, dwarf arrowhead)

---

**From *Honzō Kōmoku* (本草綱目):**

　睡蓮 (*Suiren*, water lily) → Japanese name: *Hitsujigusa* (未草, "ram's-hour grass")
　　[Alternative name: *Ko-renge* (小蓮花, "small lotus flower")]

---

**The author states:**

The correct Chinese character(s) for *Omotaka* have not yet been determined. Common usage has, erroneously, universally adopted the character 澤瀉 (*Takusha*) for this plant. However, this is incorrect. *Takusha* (澤瀉) is a medicinal plant: it produces shoots in spring, grows in shallow water, has leaves resembling an ox's tongue, and opens white flowers in autumn. The reason *Omotaka* has long been mistakenly written as 澤瀉 is that the Japanese reading of 澤瀉 happens to be "*Omotaka*," and this confusion has been perpetuated over a long period of time.

There is also a plant called *Sashi-Omotaka* (薢草, "piercing arrowhead"). Its leaves resemble those of *Takusha*, but its root is different from *Takusha*.

Furthermore, there is a plant called *Aginashi*, which resembles *Omotaka*. Its flower, too, is similar to that of *Omotaka* — white, with three flat petals. It has been said that this is what is referred to as "small *Omotaka*" (*Ko-Omotaka*), and this view carries some credibility.
