**From the *Yamato Honzō* (Japanese Materia Medica):**

**True Lan (*Shin-ran*)**: The Japanese name is *Fujibakama*. It is also called *Araragi*. In ancient poetry it was composed as "*ran*," and in the *Yakumo-shō* it is also written: "蘭 is called *fujibakama* or *ran*." This is the true *ran*. In ancient poetry, *fujibakama* appears frequently. In the poem by Kuninobu:

　　*Aki no no ni　muramura tateru　fujibakama*
　　　　*murasakifukaku　tare ka some ken*

　　(In the autumn fields, the fujibakama rise in clusters—
　　　deeply purple, who has dyed them so?)

○ The *lan* mentioned in the *Shijing* (Book of Odes) and *Chuci* (Songs of Chu) also refers to this plant.
In ancient times, aromatic woods such as *jinko* (aloeswood) and *danko* (sandalwood) had not yet arrived from China, so *lan* was greatly prized.
What is today called "orchid" (*ran*) has leaves resembling those of the broad-leafed mondo grass (*Ophiopogon japonicus*), and bears fragrant flowers—but it is not the true *lan* / "*yūran*" (hidden orchid) of antiquity.

**From the *Wamyō Ruijushō* (Japanese Names Classified and Collected):**

Lan: According to the *Kenmeien*, "蘭, also named 蕙."
(*Fujibakama*)
Two readings: *ran* and *kei*. According to the *Wamyō Honzō*: "*Fuchihakagama*" (布知波賀萬).
In the *Shinsen Man'yōshū*, the two characters 藤袴 (*fujibakama*) are used separately/distinctly.

**Lan (*Fujibakama*): One of the Seven Flowers of Autumn**

From the *Man'yōshū*, Book Eight — Two poems on the flowers of the autumn field, by Yamanoe no Okura:

　　*Aki no no ni　sakitaru hana wo　oritarite*
　　　　*kakiatsubureba　nanakusa no hana*

　　(Plucking the flowers blooming in the autumn fields
　　　and counting them — the seven flowers of autumn)

**First poem:**

　　*Hagi no hana　obana　kuzuhana　nadeshiko no*
　　　　*ominaeshi　mata fujibakama　asagao no hana*

　　(The bush clover, pampas grass, arrowroot flower, pink,
　　　maiden flower, and also fujibakama, and the morning glory)
