**From the *Honzō Wage* (Japanese Explanation of the Materia Medica):**

**Hujang** (虎杖, *Kojō*; Japanese knotweed, *Reynoutria japonica*)
- Alternative names: Kujō (Bitter Staff), Daichūjō (Great Worm Staff), Hanjō (Spotted Staff), Sanjō (Sour Staff)
- Japanese name: *Itadzuri* (伊多豆里)

---

**From the *Kyūkō Yafu* (Wild Plants for Famine Relief):**

**Sōtōsō** (*Itatori*): Grows along the waterways of the Hu-Xiang (Hunan/Xiang River) region. Its leaves resemble those of the woody *hibiscus* (*Hibiscus mutabilis*), but it is a different plant. [Note: this point requires further investigation.]

---

**From the *Kyūkō Honzō* (Materia Medica for Famine Relief):**

**Santōjun** (*Itadori*)

　According to the *Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica), *Hujang* blooms in the seventh month, and upon inquiry, its flowers are said to resemble those of the peach — but this account is inconsistent with other entries. The statement that its flowers resemble the chrysanthemum is also suspicious and uncertain.
　The *Shu Bencao* states that the plant grows to over one *zhang* (approx. 3 meters) in height, but Kou Zongshi doubted this and held that it is not so.
　In all likelihood, variation according to region of production is by no means unique to *Hujang* alone (this is true of many plants).

**Written on the first day of Nanryo (eighth lunar month) in the year of Kinototori (Yiyou)**

**From the *Imyō Bunruishō* (Classified Collection of Alternative Names):**

　　*Futaba momiji* — [reading:] *Tachihi*

---

　From the *Hanzei-ki* (Chronicle of Emperor Hanzei): "There was a well here, called the *Mitsui* (Auspicious Well). When the Crown Prince was washed with its water, *tachihi* flowers fell into the well. Because of this, the Prince was named *Tachihana no Hana* (多遅花). What is today called *itadori* (虎杖) is that same plant." And so on.

---

　In the *Yamato Honzō*, [Li] Shizhen states: "*Zhang* (杖, 'staff') refers to the stem; *hu* (虎, 'tiger') refers to the speckled markings [on the stem]."

　〇 In Sei Shōnagon's *Pillow Book* (*Makura no Sōshi*), it is written: "*Itatori* is written with the characters for 'tiger's staff' (虎杖). It seems it could do without the 'staff' — for it is rather slender and graceful." This is said in jest.

　The mature *itadori* ought to be usable as a walking staff — it is extremely light. However, it breaks easily.
