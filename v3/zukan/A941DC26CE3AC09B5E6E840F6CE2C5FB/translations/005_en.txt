## Hasu (蓮 / Lotus, *Nelumbo nucifera*)

**From *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会, Illustrated Encyclopedia of Japan and China), section on Water Fruits:**

"Upon reflection, the lotus grows from mud and yet remains pure and clean, and by nature it abhors filth — excrement, urine, oils, and offal. As Zhou Maoshu (Zhou Dunyi) said: 'The chrysanthemum is the flower of the reclusive hermit; the peony is the flower of wealth and nobility; the lotus is the flower of the gentleman of virtue (*kunshi*).' Furthermore, that all the Buddhas delight in using the lotus blossom as their seat is also simply because they draw upon this same meaning of purity."

**The Author (Baien) states:**

The lotus, though a single plant, goes by many names depending on which part is used. The root is called *gū* (藕); the seed/fruit is called *ren* (蓮), and also: *gūjitsu* (藕実), *teki* (菂), *kyū* (薂), *sekiren-shi* (石蓮子), *suishi* (水芝, "water mushroom"), and *takushi* (沢芝, "marsh mushroom"). The leaf is called *ka* (荷); young leaves are called *kasen* (荷銭, "leaf-coins"); those that lie flat on the water surface are called *kūka* (藕荷); those that rise above the water are called *kika* (芰荷) — and it is these that produce flowers. *Renzui-shu* (蓮蕊鬚, "lotus stamen filaments") refers to the stamens of the lotus flower, also called *butsuzashu* (仏座鬚, "Buddha's throne filaments"). *Renbana* (蓮鼻) refers to the receptacle/calyx of the flower. *Renbo* (蓮房, "lotus chamber") refers to the seedpod surrounding the seeds; because it resembles a beehive (*hachisu*), the plant came to be called *hachisu*, of which *hasu* is an abbreviated form. The stem, moreover, is called *gūsetsu* (藕節, "lotus-root node"). Among all plants and trees, the one from which the greatest number of parts — flowers, leaves, stems, roots, seeds, and more — are put to use is, first and foremost, the lotus.

**From *Tashikiden* (多識編), section on Water Fruits, under the entry for *Ren'ō* (蓮藕):**

Lotus flower (*renke* / 蓮花)
　Japanese name: *Hachisu no hana*; also said as *Hasu no hana*
　Alternative names: *Fuyō* (芙蓉) [from *Kokon-chū* (古今注)]; *Fukyō* (芙蕖) [same source]; *Suikwa* (水華, "water blossom")
