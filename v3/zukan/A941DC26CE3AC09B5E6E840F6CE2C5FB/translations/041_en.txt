## Record of Autumn Senbon-yari (Autumn Ladle-head Herb)

　　　　　　　　　　　　23rd day of the 7th month of autumn, Year of Bosshi (1828) — Drawn from life
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　【Herb Category】

---

**Autumn Ladle-head Herb (Senbon-yari / 秋匙頭菜)**

I (Baien) state the following:
　　That which opens its flowers in spring is called *Hитōzai* (匙頭菜 / "Ladle-head Herb");
　　That which sends up flower stalks in autumn but does not open its flowers is called *Senbon-yari* (千本槍 / "Thousand Spears").
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　(As given in the *Chikinshō*)

23rd day of the 7th month of autumn, Year of Bosshi (1828) — Drawn from life
　　　　　　　　　　　　【Herb Category】

The Senbon-yari is indeed none other than the *Hitōzai* (匙頭菜). In spring, seedlings emerge in the first month, and by the third month the plant bears flowers. Its flowers most closely resemble those of the dandelion (*Taraxacum*), being very small, and of a pinkish-white color. Once the flowers fall, they produce plumed seeds (a cottony *hō*). The seedlings grow most vigorously when summer arrives, and by the sixth month they send up flower stalks numbering in the tens or more. Their form is as shown in the illustration. The buds reach the point of being about to open, yet stop of their own accord without ever opening. It is from this form that the name "Thousand Spears" (千本槍) was given. There is no other plant that flowers twice within a single year. As for the regular (spring) flowers, they have been recorded in Volume 3 of the Spring Section. This is one species with two names; it is also one species in which the flower changes [between seasons], differing slightly. The *Chikinshō* has not yet attempted this explanation. I, Baien Chikkaku, planted several types of this herb in my own garden plot, observed the form of their flowers, examined and identified each plant, and recorded their names. The one for which I was able to determine the correct identification with precision—the one I would designate as a rare and unusual variety—is most likely the *Hitōzai* (匙頭菜).
