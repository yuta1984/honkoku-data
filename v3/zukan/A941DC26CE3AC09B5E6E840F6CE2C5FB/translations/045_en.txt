## Record of Tree Category (木類)

　　　　　　Year of Boshi (*Bosshi*), 21st day of Early Autumn — a branch was taken from the garden and one illustration was drawn from it.

---

The ***Tonkō-sho*** (遁甲書, *Book of Escaping the Celestial Stems*) states: "The *Aogiri* (梧桐, Chinese parasol tree) can reveal the regular and intercalary months of the solar-lunar calendar. It produces twelve leaves, one on each side; counting from the bottom, one leaf corresponds to one month. If there are two [leaves] at the top, it signifies an intercalary year, with thirteen leaves in total. The smaller leaf is considered the intercalary month." And so forth.

*Seitō* (青桐, *Aogiri*), that is to say, *Gotō* (梧桐) without fruit — and the one resembling *Seitō* with forked (*mata*) leaves — is named ■Tō (■桐). ○ This explanation requires further investigation.

## Source: *Honzō Kōmoku* (本草綱目) and *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会), Fragrant Wood Category

**Gotō (梧桐)** — *Firmiana simplex* (Chinese Parasol Tree)
　Alternative names: *Shin* (櫬, pronounced "shin") · *Yūgaku* (橐鄂)
　　　　Also known as: *Ao-ni-yorori* (青如桹梩)
　　　　　《marginal note: Aogiri (アヲギリ)》

---

When its fruit is old (ripe), it splits open, and seeds form along the edges. What is referred to as being "about the size of *Gotō-shi* (五桐子)" in pill-making refers to these seeds.

Hence the saying: "The phoenix (*hōō*) will not perch except on the *Aogiri* (梧桐)." Does it not also eat the tree's seeds?
