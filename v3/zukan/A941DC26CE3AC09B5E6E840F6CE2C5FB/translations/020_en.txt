【Herb Category】

The "Wakan Sansai Zue" (Illustrated Sino-Japanese Encyclopedia), in the section on climbing plants, states:
Sekichiri (ishimikawa) [Persicaria perfoliata] — also known as: sekiheirei, gokō, godokusō
《Ushinohanatsura》, jabichigo, yamaSoba
Japanese name: "ishimikawa," also called "Ushi no ■ (hanatsura)"

The "Kōeki Jikinsho" (Expanded Garden Plant Compendium) states:
Sekichiri (ishimikawa), also called "kaeru no tsura kaki" (frog-face scratcher)

In the "Yamato Honzō" (Japanese Materia Medica), sekichiri and kaeru no tsura kaki are listed as separate entries.
However, these are fundamentally the same plant, and the two names may simply be two designations distinguishing large and small forms.
Nevertheless, the size of plant varieties varies according to local conditions — the fertility or poverty of the soil, as well as how richly people cultivate them — and there are instances of remarkably large, well-nourished specimens arising. This much can be understood from the above; however, one must also bear in mind that there exist varieties belonging to the categories of Chōryōsō (Zhang Liang herb) and Bankaīsō (alum-associated herb).
