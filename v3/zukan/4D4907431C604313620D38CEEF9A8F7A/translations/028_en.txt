## 【RINGO (林檎 — Apple, Malus pumila)】

From the **Zōho Tashikihen** (Supplemented Encyclopedia), section on Mountain Fruits:
　Apple flower (林檎花)　Japanese name: Ringō (利牟古宇); commonly also called "Ringo (利牟古)."
　　Alternative name: Raikin (来禽, "coming bird") [from the Hōjō / Model Calligraphy Texts]
　　[Supplementary] alternative name: Bunrinrōka (文林郎果, "Fruit of Lord Bunrin")

From the **Wamyō Ruijūshō** (Japanese Names Encyclopedia):
　Ringo / Riūkō (林檎)　The annotations to the Honzō (Materia Medica) state: the character 檎 is read "kin (禽)"; the Japanese name is "Riūkō (利宇古宇)."

From the **Saijiki** (Almanac of Annual Events):
　In Japan, what is called "Kaidō (海棠)" refers to this plant (the apple).
　At first, [the fruit or seed] floated down from a river; a man named Bunrin (文林) picked it up and planted it, and the name "Bunrin" accordingly became associated with it. [From the Wakan Sansai Zukai]

　　Transcribed faithfully on the 10th day of early Kosen (3rd lunar month), Mizunoto-Hitsuji year (Bunsei 6, 1823).
　　【Seal】Trees

## 【NASHI (梨 — Pear, Pyrus pyrifolia)】

From the **Zōho Tashikihen**, section on Mountain Fruits:
　Pear (梨, ri)　Japanese name: Nashi (那志)
　　Alternative names: Kaika (快果, "refreshing fruit")；Kasō (果宗, "foremost of fruits")；Gyokunyū (玉乳, "jade milk")；Mitsufu (蜜父, "honey father")

　Pear blossom (梨花) — also from the same source, and Tōri-ka (棠梨花, wild pear blossom) [Note: the "棠梨" (wild pear) shares its name with the "three-leafed kaidō (三葉海棠)"].

　　　　　　[From the Kokin Wakashū or related anthology:]
　　　　　　　　In the bay of Ō, a pear tree extending its single bough —
　　　　　　　　Whether it bears fruit or not, let us lie together beneath its spreading branches.
　　　　　　　　(Anonymous)

From the same text (Tashikihen), same category:
　◦ Gari (鵞梨)　Japanese name: as I judge, "Kokanashi (古加那志)"　Alternative name: Menri (綿梨, "cotton pear")
　◦ Kōsuiri (香水梨, "fragrant water pear")　Japanese name: as I judge, "Tsunashi (豆那志)"　Alternative name: Shōri (消梨, "melting pear")
　◦ Kanshōri (含消梨, "melt-in-the-mouth pear")

　　Transcribed faithfully the following morning of the same spring, after washing the inkstone.
　　【Seal】Fruit trees

From the **Wamyō Ruijūshō**: Pear (梨子, nashi)
From the **Tōin** (Tang Rhyme Dictionary): The character 梨 has the fanqie reading "dāo-zhǐ (刀指)"; Japanese name "Nashi (奈之)."
From the **Kenmeien** (Chinese synonym dictionary): Pear (梨子); alternative name "Kanshō (含消, 'dissolving in the mouth')."

## 【HEBIICHIGO (蛇苺 — Snake Strawberry / Mock Strawberry, Duchesnea indica and related species)】

From the **Jiuhuang Bencao** (Materia Medica for Relieving Famine):
　Cockscomb Fruit (鶏冠果, Kuchinawaichigo / "snake strawberry")
　　Alternative name: Wild bayberry (野楊梅, Yaōbai)

**Jaichigo / Snake Strawberry (蛇苺)**
　　From **Chikinsho** (Landscape and Plant Reference):
　　　The flower of the hebiichigo (snake strawberry) is called "hebiichigo." The name "蛇含 (jagan / snake-holding)" is also used for the same plant.

　　From the **Zōho Tashikihen**, section on Creeping Plants:
　　　Snake strawberry (蛇苺)　Japanese name: as I judge, "Hebi-ichigo (倍比伊知古)"
　　　[Supplementary] alternative names: Jahō (蛇藨, "snake bramble")；Jiichigo (地苺, "ground strawberry") [from the Kaien / encyclopedic compilation]；San-ichigo (蚕苺, "silkworm strawberry")

　　　Transcribed faithfully on the 25th day of Kosen (3rd lunar month), Mizunoto-Hitsuji year (Bunsei 6, 1823).
