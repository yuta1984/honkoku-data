## Rengyo (連翹 / Forsythia, *Forsythia suspensa* / *F. viridissima*)

---

### According to *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会), section on moisture-dwelling plants (湿草類):

　**Wa-renkyō (倭連翹 / "Japanese Forsythia")**

　　What the *Honzō* (本草 / Chinese pharmacopoeia) refers to as "Small Rengyo (*Ko-renkyō*), which originates from the southern regions," is the same as what is today called Wa-renkyō in Japan. The "true" Rengyo (*Forsythia*) once existed in Japan but is no longer found here. Across all provinces, what is commonly called simply "Rengyo" refers to this plant.

> [Author's note / marginalia]: In my own supplementary examination, I find that what the *Honzō* calls "Ko-renkyō (小連翹 / Small Forsythia)" is in fact an herb, and is the same as what is called *Sōgiyo* (草義陽). The shared account of Rengyo in Chinese and Japanese sources that corresponds to the Japanese usage is precisely this. The matter of *Wa-renkyō* warrants further investigation.

---

### According to *Honzō Ikkagen* (本草一家言):

　**Rengyo — second entry**

　　The Small Rengyo (*Ko-renkyō*): its branches droop downward.
　　The Large Rengyo (*Dai-renkyō*): its branches grow upright.

---

### According to *Honzō Wage* (本草和解):

　**Alternative names:** Ren (連), Ikyō (異翹), Kanrenshi (旱蓮子), Ranka (蘭華), Sanren (三廉)
　The root is called *Renshō* (連軺); it is also called *Chikukon* (竹根 / "bamboo root").
　**Japanese name:** Itachigusa (伊多知久左 / "weasel grass")

　　　　[Note]: The entry on Rengyo in *Kyūkō Honzō* (救荒本草) accords well with this.
