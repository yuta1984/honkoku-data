## 【STRAWBERRY (Ichigo / Rubus species)】

According to Zōho Tashiki-hen, Creeping Plants section (増補多識篇蔓草類):
　Fùpénzǐ (覆盆子, Fukubon-shi) [Chinese raspberry / Rubus species]:
　　Japanese names: Kāfuri-ichigo (加宇布利伊知古); upon investigation, Kusa-ichigo (久左伊知古, "grass strawberry")
　　Additional alternative names: Kei (茥) [Erya]; Ketsubon (鈌盆) [same]; Saikoku-sō (西国草) [Tujing];
　　　　　　Hireuka (畢楞伽) [same]; Taibaку-bo (大麦苺); Sōten-hō (挿田藨); Uhō-shi (烏藨子) [Bencao Gangmu]

According to the Bencao Gangmu (本草綱目), there are five types of berry-like plants [Péng-lěi (蓬蘽), Fùpénzǐ (覆盆子), Biāo (藨), Shù-méi (樹苺), and Shé-méi (蛇苺)] in total. Li Shizhen himself collected and compared them, and for the first time established a definitive account. The divergent theories put forward by various scholars are numerous and none can be fully trusted.
　　[Author's note:] Even when tallied the same way as Li Shizhen's five types, the actual varieties number more than ten. In China there may indeed be five. According to the views of earlier scholars, "fùpén" (覆盆) is the name for the fruit of the berry (苺).

The Yamato Honzō (大和本草) records this plant as Fùpénzǐ (覆盆子).
The Jikikin-shō (地錦抄) identifies it as Xuángōu-zǐ (懸鈎子). This warrants further investigation.
[The author] includes an illustration of one type of Fùpénzǐ in his true-to-life drawings.
This is based on the illustration in the Jikikin-shō.
For this plant, the Yamato Honzō is followed as the primary source.
The illustration in the Wakan Sansai Zue (和漢三才図会) differs greatly, though its description is in agreement.

## 【JAPANESE YEW (Ichii), KYARABOKU, AND INUGAYA】

According to Yamato Honzō (大和本草):
　Ichii-no-ki (一位の木, Japanese yew / Taxus cuspidata)　　Kyaraboku (伽羅木)
　　　The flowers are yellow-red in color, shaped like balls, and grow clustered together in one place.
　　　The fruit resembles [thorns?] and is very small.

According to Wakan Sansai Zue (和漢三才図会), Fragrant Woods section:
　Kyaraboku (伽羅木, also called Otsuko) [common name]
　　　Otsuko (於豆古) [Ezo/Ainu name]
　The indigenous people of Ezo-Matsumae call this tree "Otsuko." Because the grain of this wood resembles that of aloe wood (奇楠, Kyara / agarwood), it is commonly called Kyaraboku (aloe-wood tree).

　Abōsan (阿勃参)　Bebegaya (辺々榧) / Inugaya (狗榧 / イヌカヤ)
　　　　　Kyaraboku [also] Ichii-no-ki

　In the barbarian (southern foreign) language, this oil is called "oil of Portugal" (ポルトガルの油); it is also called "Aceite de una" (アセトウナ).

[According to Toyoda Yōkei (豊田養慶)'s Shakuben Jyoroku (赭鞭徐禄):]
　Abōsan (阿勃参)　[Bebegaya / Inugaya]
　　　　　　　　According to Wang's Huiyuan Xiang-zhu (王氏彙苑詳注): Abōsan comes from the kingdom of Furin (払林国). It grows to over one jō (approx. 3 meters) in height; the bark is greenish-white in color; the leaves are slender and grow in opposite pairs, resembling turnip greens (蔓菁). The fruit resembles pepper and is red. When a branch is pressed and ground, it exudes a juice like oil. Applied to scabies and ringworm, it cures without fail. The oil is extremely precious, worth a thousand gold pieces.
　○ This entry also appears in the Kyokuseki Benran (曲籍便覧). The Bebegaya tree of Ōmi Province is this very plant. Bebegaya is also called Inugaya (犬カヤ / 狗榧); it grows throughout mountain areas. Mountain folk press oil from it, which is used by surgeons and practitioners of external medicine.
