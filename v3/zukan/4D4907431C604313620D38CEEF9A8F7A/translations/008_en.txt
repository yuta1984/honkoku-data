## [Tsukushi / Sugina — Field Horsetail: *Equisetum arvense*]

Spring of Kanoto-Mi (1821)　　[Seal] Vegetable category

　　Tiānhuācài (Tenkwasai) — Tsukutsukushi (Tsukushi / horsetail shoots)

From the appendix of the *Yamato Honzō* (Japanese Natural History):
　　It grows in mountain valleys and wild fields. In spring it first sends up flower shoots resembling writing brushes.
　　Separately, it grows stems and leaves, which are called "Sugi-na" (cedar grass), as they resemble cedar. The Chinese name
　　is not yet clear, and it has not yet been encountered in Chinese books. However, some say
　　that in the appendix to the entry on "Mokuzan" (scouring rush / *Equisetum hyemale*) in the *Honzō Kōmoku* (Bencao Gangmu), there is an entry for "Wènjīng" (Mondokei),
　　also known by the alternate name "Setsuzokusō" (jointed-grass), and that this may well be what we call Tsukushi.
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　(And so on.)

The *Honzō Ikkagen* (Personal Views on Materia Medica) states:
　Sugina has eleven names: Niúchún, ■Dan, Xiāngsīcǎo (Lovesick grass), Duànchángcǎo (Heartbreak grass),
　　　　　Chóufùcǎo (Sorrowful wife grass), Shuāngcǎo (Frost grass), Liáoshāwèn, Àn​xùcǎo (Jointing grass),
　　　　　Shuǐxièhuā, Bǐtóucài (Brushhead vegetable). (The name "Shuǐxiè" is said to be an error.)

　My (the author's) assessment:
　　　The leaves are called Sugina (スギナ), and the flower shoots are called Tsukushi (土筆).
　　　Ephedra (*Mahuang*) is the male, and Sugina is the female — so it is said, etc.

## [Hirado Azalea: *Rhododendron* × *mucronatum*]

[Seal] Flowering tree
　　　　Kōshin spring (1820)

The *Wakan Sansai Zue* (Japanese-Chinese Illustrated Encyclopedia), under "Poisonous Plants," states:
　　Hirado-tsutsuji (Hirado Azalea)
　　　　Ryūkyū-tsutsuji (Ryūkyū Azalea)
　　　On examination: Hirado is a place name in Kyushu.
　　　It originally came from Ryūkyū (the Ryūkyū Islands), and was named accordingly.
　　　The white-flowered variety is called "Ryūkyū," and the purple-
　　　flowered variety is called "Tsutsuji" (azalea).

## [Kirishima Azalea: *Rhododendron obtusum*]

The *Wakan Sansai Zue*, under "Poisonous Plants," states:
　　　　Yìngshānhóng (Kirishima)
　　　　　Hóng Zhízhú (Red Azalea)
　　　　　　Colloquially called "Kirishima" (岐利之末)

　　The *Honzō Kōmoku* treats Shān Zhízhú (Mountain Azalea; also: Mountain Pomegranate / Cuckoo Flower) and
　　Yìngshānhóng (Red Azalea) as a single species;
　　but now they are regarded as two distinct species.

The *Zōho Tashikiben* (Expanded Polyglot Dictionary), under "Poisonous Plants," states:
　　Mountain Azalea (*Shān Zhízhú* / Santekichoku)　Japanese name: Upon reflection, "Tsutsuji"　Additional alternate name: Hóng Zhízhú (Red Azalea)
　　　Shān Shíliu (Mountain Pomegranate)　Yìngshānhóng　Dùjuānhuā (Cuckoo Flower)

　　　　　My assessment: The *Honzō Kōmoku*'s treatment of Mountain Azalea and Yìngshānhóng as a single species is
　　　　　not without basis, as can be understood from the *Tashikiben* — they were originally one and the same.
　　　　　Yìngshānhóng (literally "reflecting red across the mountain") has flowers of
　　　　　a deep crimson color, and when in bloom the color spreads across an entire mountainside — it is from this that the name derives.
　　　　　Whether it is a distinct variety of Mountain Azalea whose alternate name was later separated out
　　　　　and made its own name remains to be examined in later texts.
