【Sendaihagi (Thermopsis lupinoides / False Lupine)】

According to herbalists: Yě juémíng (Sendaihagi)
　　　　Sendaihagi (Sendai Bush Clover)

According to Yamato Honzō:
　It is suggested that Mùxù (alfalfa/Medicago sativa) may be the Sendaihagi, but this is incorrect.
　The Chikinsho states that the illustration of Mùxù is clearly a different plant; the Sendaihagi rather resembles a moisture-loving grass.

【Enishida (Common Broom / Cytisus scoparius)】

According to Imyō Bunruishō:
　Juémíng (Enisuta)
　In Chikinsho: Enisuta
　In Gunsho: read as Enishita

In Gunsho:
　Jīnquèhuā (Enishida)　〔Requires further investigation〕

The Yamato Honzō also states that there are two varieties of Juémíng.
One of these may be this plant (Enisuta). The two characters for Juémíng (Enisuta) refer to Ketsumei, which is a separate plant altogether. There are few texts that record Ketsumei as "Enisuta." In the Wamyō Ruijushō, there is a Japanese name "Ebisukusa," but this refers to the Ketsumei (Senna obtusifolia) that blooms in autumn. This matter should be further examined and distinguished by later scholars. Moreover, upon consideration, the number of plants given the name "Juémíng" is not limited to two varieties but is in fact very numerous. This entry has become too complex to summarize comprehensively.


According to Honzō Ikkagen:
　It is said that the flower of Enisuita (Enishida) resembles that of Redama〔Jīnquèhuā (Common Broom / Genista)〕.
　　The author states:
　　　Enisuta and Renju share the same classification by name,
　　　and it is difficult to distinguish their flowers from one another.
