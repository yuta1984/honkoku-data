## 【Kibushi (木藤花 / 旌節花; *Stachyurus praecox*)】

*Kibushi* (木藤花; "wood wisteria flower") / *Seisetsuka* (旌節花; *Kibushi*)

[Two varieties depicted]

Sketched from nature on the sixteenth day of *Kosei* (姑洗; third lunar month), year Kinoe-Saru (甲申) [Bunsei 7, 1824], at *Kaegaku Shūō* (華萼集桜？).

The author remarks:
*Seisetsuka* (旌節花) is in fact the original Chinese name for *Sakurasō* (桜草; primrose). The account of *Kibushi* (木藤花) requires further investigation.

## 【Sumire (スミレ; Violet) / Yabu-geman (ヤブゲマン; *Corydalis incisa*)】

[Note: The illustration appears to depict *Murasaki-keman* (*Corydalis incisa*). The discussion conflates it with *Sumire* (violet). "*Sumōtori*" is a folk name for violet, derived from a children's game (*sumō*) in which flower stalks are hooked together and pulled. *Kankin* (旱芹; "dry celery/parsley") is today assigned to celery.]

**According to the *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草; *Japanese Materia Medica*):**

　The flower of *Kinsai* (菫菜; *noseri* / *sumire*)
　　　Is called *Kankin* (旱芹; "dry parsley/celery").
　　　Some identify this with *mama-hikigusa* (ママ引草), which is incorrect.

---

**According to the *Zoho Tashikiden* (増補多識編; *Expanded Nomenclature Compendium*), in the section on Pungent Vegetables (葷菜類):**

　*Kin* (菫; violet/cress)
　　[Japanese names:] *Sumire* (須美礼); upon further consideration, *Noseri* (乃世利; water parsley)
　　[Alternative names:] *Kukin* (苦菫; "bitter violet") [source: *Erya* (爾雅)]
　　[Supplementary alternative names:] *Kōki* (黄葵; "yellow mallow") [source: Tang Chinese sources]
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　*Kankin* (旱芹; "dry parsley") [source: *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目)]
　　　　　　　　　　　[Note: The *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会) refers to *Kukin* as *sumire* (菫).]

---

**According to the *Wamyō Ruijushō* (和名類聚抄; *Japanese Names Classified and Assembled*):**

　*Kinsai* (菫菜) = *Sumire* (スミレ; violet)

---

**According to the *Honzō* (本草; *Materia Medica*):**

　*Kinsai* (菫菜): commonly also called *kinkei* (菫葵; "violet mallow").
　[The character 菫 (*kin*) is pronounced *kin* (謹). Japanese reading: *Sumire* (須美礼).]

---

**The author (Baien) remarks:**

In the *Wamyōshō* (*Wamyō Ruijushō*), *kinsai* (菫菜, read *noseri*) is identified with *sumire* (菫菜); however, *sumire* is in fact the *shikajōtei* (紫花地丁; Siberian violet / *Viola mandshurica*). The plant called *sumōtori* (相撲取り; "sumo-wrestler") discussed here corresponds well to the *kinsai* as described in the *Yamato Honzō*. Its leaves resemble those of *Keman* (*Corydalis* / 荷苞牡丹) but are smaller, and resemble parsley (*seri*) leaves but are larger. The flowers also resemble those of *Keman*, and it grows in thickets. Its stems and leaves are soft. The flower is purple, and it grows on dry land. The plant called *kankin* (旱芹; "dry parsley/celery") is indeed this plant. The reading of 菫菜 (*sumire*) requires further investigation. This plant is also called *yabu-geman* (藪荷苞; "thicket *keman*").

**According to the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会; *Illustrated Sino-Japanese Encyclopedia*), in the section on Moisture-Loving Plants (湿草類):**

　*Tōshujutsu* (桃朱術) — also called *Tōshujutsu* (陶朱術)

**According to the *Bukō Sanbutsushi* (武江産物志; *Record of Products of Edo*):**

　*Tōshujutsu* (桃朱術) = *Yabu-geman* (ヤブゲマン)

**According to the *Bencao* (本草綱目; *Compendium of Materia Medica*):**
*Tōshujutsu* is a variety of *seisō* (青葙; *Celosia argentea*) that grows in gardens. Its leaves are fine and resemble parsley (*seri*). Its flowers are purple; its fruits form pods (角). If one holds a mirror to its side and confronts it [the mirror], the seeds burst open of their own accord. On the fifth day of the fifth month, one collects the seeds and wears them, causing a woman to be loved by her husband. One account says the flowers are yellow [and so on].

The author (Baien) remarks:
The flowers, leaves, and fruit of *Tōshujutsu* do not differ from the description in the *Honzō*. When a person touches the ripe fruit, the pods burst open spontaneously, splitting apart in rings like thread wound in circles. The account that the yellow-flowered variety is *Seiyō-kinteki* / *Momochidori* (芹葉鉤吻; *Cardamine* sp. or similar) refers to a related but distinct species. The description in the *Sansai Zue* (三才図会) of a plant resembling lily flowers (*hyakugōka*) does not agree with this plant.
