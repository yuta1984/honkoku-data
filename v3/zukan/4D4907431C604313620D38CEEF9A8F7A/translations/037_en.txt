**Flowers of the Purple Wisteria (紫藤 / Shitō / Fuji)**　　*Fuji* (不之)

---

**According to the Supplemented *Tashiki-hen* (増補多識編), section on Vines (蔓草類):**

　Purple Wisteria (紫藤 / Shitō)
　　《Japanese name》Upon my consideration: *Fujinohana* (布知乃波那 / フヂノハナ; "wisteria flower")

---

**According to *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会), section on Vines (蔓草類):**

　Purple Wisteria (Fuji) — Chinese pronunciation: *Zǐ Téng* (ツウ・テアン)
　　Also: *Shōtōtō* (招豆藤)
　　　Japanese name: *Fuchi* (布知 / Fuji)
　Only the single character 藤 (*fuji*) is used, and its Japanese reading is taken as the plant's name.

---

**According to the *Wamyō Ruijushō* (和名類聚抄):**

　*Fuji* (藤 / フヂ)
　The commentary to the *Erya* (爾雅) states: The character 藟 〈a fanqie spelling derived from "Dāo Yà"; also written as 蘽〉
　　Japanese name: *Fuchi* (布知 / Fuji)

---

**According to the *Imyō Bunruishō* (異名分類抄):**

　Alternative names for *Fuji* (藤):
　*Nikigusa* (二季草; "two-season plant"), *Murasakikusa* (ムラサキクサ; "purple plant"), *Matsumigusa* (松見草; "pine-viewing plant"), *Sanokata no hana* (サノカタノ花), *Matsunakusa* (マツナクサ)

---

**According to the *Nanfang Caomu Zhuang* (南方草木状), section on Trees (木類):**

　Purple Wisteria (紫藤)
　　The leaves of the purple wisteria are slender and elongated; the stem resembles bamboo; the roots are extremely hard and dense/heavy. It has bark; its flowers are white and its seeds are black. If placed in wine (sake), it will not rot or decay even after two or three thousand years. When its stem is cut and placed in soot (煙炱), over time it takes on a purple color. The fragrance (of this purple material) can be used to invoke the descent of deities.
