## 【Akebi (Akebia quinata / Five-leaf Akebia)】

According to *Honzō Wage* (Japanese Interpretation of Materia Medica):
　　　Mùtōng (木通) — Alternative names: Tōngcǎo (通草), Fùzhīdīngwēng (附支丁翁), Mannen-fuji (万年藤 / "Everlasting Wisteria")
　　　　　　　　　　The fruit is called Yānfù (燕覆 / "Swallow Cover")
　　　　　　　　　Japanese name: Akebi-kazura (阿計昆加豆羅)

【Seal】Medicinal herb

According to *Wamyō Ruijushō* (Japanese Names for Categories of Things):
　Tōngcǎo (通草) [read as: Akebi-kazura]
　　As stated in *Wamyō Honzō*: In the commentary by Táo Hóngjǐng it says:
　　"The stem has fine pores running through it, and the two ends communicate with each other."
　　Japanese name: Akebi-kazura (阿介比加都良)

According to *Yamato Honzō* (Plants of Japan):
　Mùtōng (木通) [read as: Akebi-kazura]
　　It is a climbing vine. The vine is long, large, and firm.
　　The vine itself is what is called mùtōng (木通), while the leaves and stem are called tōngcǎo (通草).
　　The "Kinome-zuke" (wood-bud pickles) of Kurama are made from this tōngcǎo.

Kinoe-Saru year, last days of Kosei (the third lunar month), on the third day.
Painted from life.
