## Karatachi (枸橘 / Trifoliate Orange)

[Seal: Flowering tree category]

*From "Zōho Tashiki-hen" (Expanded Polyglot Encyclopedia), shrubs section:*
　　枳 (Ki) — Japanese name: Karatachi (加羅多知) — with alternate names:
　　　The fruit is called "枳実 (Kijitsu)" in "Honkyō (Bencao Jing)," and "枳穀 (Kikoku)" in "Kaibō (Kaibaō)."

*From "Wamyō Ruijū" (Japanese Names Classified):*
　　枳椇 (Karatachi) — "Honzō" states:
　　　枳椇 (two syllables: "ji" and "ku"; Japanese name: Karatachi)

*"Gyokuhen (Yupian)" states:* "枳 is a form of citrus (橘) that grows in a twisted, bent manner." The "Shichikan Shokkyō (Seven-volume Food Classic)" states: "枸櫞 (here, '枸' is the same character as '椇')."
I (the author) say: In "Yamato Honzō," 枳椇 is recorded as "Kenpononashi (Kenponashi)." Which account is in error is unclear.

**Yamato Honzō:**
　枸橘 (Karatachi-hana / Karatachi) — also known as 臭橘 (Shūkitsu, "stinking citrus")
　　　Japanese name: Karatachi
　　In Tsukushi (Kyushu), it is called "Gezu."
　　From old times, it has by common custom been mistakenly used as 枳殻 (Kikoku) or 枳実 (Kijitsu) in medicine — this is incorrect.
　　"Sansai Zukai" states: "In Japan as well, when one plants mikan (citrus) in Ōshū (northern Japan), it transforms into 枳殻. This is one remarkable thing."
　　In "Yamato Honzō," 枸橘 (Gesu) is identified as Karatachi. Karatachi is thus 枳殻, and the illustrations and explanations in "Wakan Sansai Zue" agree with this. A certain scholar's view is worthy of consideration.

**Genju (the author) notes:**
　There are two kinds of 枳殻. The 狗橘 (Karatachi) used as a hedge plant has leaves, wood, and fruit smaller than the ordinary Karatachi. There is no doubt that this is the "狗橘" described by Li Shizhen.
　The true 枳殻 has larger wood, leaves, and fruit, and is the same type but a different variety. What is now sold at pharmacies as "小枳実 (Ko-kijitsu)"
　　　is the fruit of the 狗橘 (Karatachi).
　　　　It should not be used as medicine.

## Yamabuki (山吹 / Japanese Kerria)

荼蘼 (Tomi) flower
　　Also called "Yamabuki"
　Also, 酴醿 (Tobi) is called "Yamabuki" (也末布岐) in the "Man'yōshū."
　In "Rōeishū," the character 欵冬花 is written and used to compose poems about Yamabuki — but this is an error.
　欵冬 refers to Fukinotō (butterbur shoots). (From "Chikinsho")

[Seal: Medicinal herb]

**Yamato Honzō states:**
　棣棠 (Yamabuki / Japanese Kerria)
　　　　Chinese names: 金碗 (Kinwan) · 喜水 (Kisui)
　　Reading 酴醿 (Tobi) as "Yamabuki" is incorrect.
　　Tobi refers to Goyaagi (茶麋, a type of climbing rose).
