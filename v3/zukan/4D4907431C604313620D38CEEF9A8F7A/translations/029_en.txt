## 【KASHIWA (Oak — *Quercus dentata* and related species)】

From the *Zōho Tashikihen*, section on Fragrant Woods (香木類):
　Haku (柏)　Japanese name: Kae (加恵); commonly in the present day also called "Sohatate" (曽浪多天).
　Alternative name: Sokuhaku (側柏, Chinese arborvitae, *Platycladus orientalis*)
　Additional alternative name: Kiku (椈)

　[Author's note:]
　　Haku (柏) is not the same as kashiwa (カシワ). The timber of haku is called "Asunaro" (阿須奈呂, *Thujopsis dolabrata*).
　There is also "Kotegashiwa" (児手柏). "Kashiwa" refers to "hō/pao" (枹), while "柏" is called "Toe" (刀ヱ).
　It has been associated with pine and also with cedar, and records confusing these are erroneous.

Same season (3rd lunar month), 25th day,
Hastily transcribed at the Usōan (Rain-Grass Hermitage).

---

From the *Wamyō Ruijūshō* (Japanese Names Encyclopedia):
　Haku/Kae (柏)　From the *Kenmeien* (兼名苑): The alternative name for haku (柏) is "kiku" (椈) [with the two readings "hya ku" and "ki ku"; Japanese name: Kahe (加閉)]
　Kashiwa (槲)　From the Honzō (Bencao): "Kashiwa" (槲) [read with the sound of "to/斗"; meaning: separate/unloose]
　Koku (斛)　Japanese name: Kashiwa (加之波)
　From the *Tōin* (唐韻, Tang Rhymes dictionary): Haku (柏) [read with the sound of "haku/帛"; Japanese name: as above]
　These are names for trees.

【Seal】Medicinal tree

From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会, Japanese-Chinese Illustrated Encyclopedia of the Three Powers), section on Mountain Fruits:
　Hō/Kashiwa (枹) [characters for sound and form]: Ōba-ichi (大葉檪, large-leafed oak), Boku (樸, unworked wood), Kaku (槲), Soku (樕)
　The *Wamyō-shō* (Wamyō Ruijūshō) reads the character 蔛 as "Kashiwa" (加之波).
　The character 柏 is sometimes also used, but neither is strictly correct.

[Author's own examination:]
　Various texts are mixed and confused, and it is difficult to be certain. However, what is today called "Kashiwa" (加之波) is this plant. It is not the "Sokuhaku" (側柏) of the *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草). The collected explanations in the *Wakan Sansai Zue* are the most coherent.

◦ From the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目): Kunugi (檞, *Quercus acutissima*) has two varieties. One variety grows in clumps and the larger form is called "Hō/Kashiwa" (枹), also known as "Ōba-ichii" (大葉檪, large-leafed evergreen oak); alternative names: Boku (樸) and Kaku (檞).

◦ Kaku (檞), Hashaso (柞), Ichii (檪), Kashiwa (枹), and Nara (楢): the various commentaries on these are mutually confused, and so I shall now establish separate entries for each.
　　Kaku [large-nutted big oak], Kashiwa [Quercus dentata], and Nara [Japanese oak] all have similar leaves but differ in size. Kaku is intermediate between Kashiwa and Nara. Hashaso (柞) belongs to the same group as these three but is a distinct species; although its leaves are similar, they differ. Hashaso (柞) and Ichii (檪) are two names for a single tree. Maruguri (丸栗, round chestnut), Hashaso (柞), Ichii (檪), Kashiwa (枹), Kaku (檞), Nara (孛落) trees, and Buna (橅, Japanese beech) all belong to one group; their leaves fall in winter.
◦ Master Cao (曹氏, Cao Shuya) states that "the new and old leaves of kashiwa (柞) alternate with one another" — this point merits further consideration.
