## Sumire (Violet / *Viola mandshurica* and related species)

**Shikwachitō (紫花地丁)** — Japanese name: Sumire — Alternative names: Dokkōko (独行虎) [from *Bencao Gangmu*]
　　　　　Heitei (米布袋) [from *Zōho Imyō*]　Sentsu-sō (箭頭草, "Arrowhead Herb") [from *Bencao Gangmu*]
　　　　　Yōkaku-shi (羊角子, "Goat Horn Seed") [from *Hiin*]

***Jiuhuang Bencao*** (*Kyūkō Honzō*, Manual of Famine Relief Plants) states:
　Kinkun (菫菫, Sumire) vegetable
　　Alternate name:
　　　Arrowhead Herb (箭頭草)

**Sumire flower** — alternate names are recorded in the earlier entry where illustrations were made.
　　Two varieties exist.

***Mao Shi*** (Book of Odes):
　　Kin (菫) — Tsubo-sumire (壺菫, small violet)

At this time, the thirteenth day of the Gusho month (the third month) in the *kinoto-saru* year of Bunsei 7 (1824).
Drawn from life in my garden.

[Seal] Flowering Grasses

***Wakan Sansai Zue***, section on moisture-loving grasses, states:
　　Kin (菫) — Sumire / Sumō-gusa [Chinese reading: *qín*]　Bitter Kin (苦菫)　Bitter Mallow (苦葵)
　　[Japanese name: Sumire; colloquially also called Sumō-gusa (相撲草, "Sumo Herb")]

***Bencao Gangmu*** — Su Gong states: "The *juncai* (菫菜) is [namely the Sumire]." Liu Yuyi, citing the *Shuowen*, says: "The *jun* (菫) has a root like shepherd's purse and leaves as fine as willow, etc." — This refers to the bitter violet (*kujun* / 苦菫). The ancients used inverted speech (*sakagoto*), just as they called licorice root (*ganzō* / 甘草) "greatly bitter" (*daiku* / 大苦). [The author notes: the one described as having "leaves like willow" was illustrated earlier.]
　Children play by picking two stems, hooking them together, and pulling against each other; the stem whose calyx is cut off and falls is declared the loser (*make*), and so the plant was named *Sumō-gusa* (角力草, "Sumo Herb / Wrestling Grass"). Among the three species — Kenno-shōko (*Geranium thunbergii*), violet (菫), and *Tōsō* (刀草) — the violet's name is the most renowned. [#1]

## Hakobe (Chickweed / *Stellaria media*)

　　　　　　***Shokkyō*** states:
　　　　　　　　繁縷 flower (Hakobe)

　　　　　　***Wamyō Ruijushō*** states:
　　　　　　　　Hanurō / Hakubera (蘩蔞) — The *Bencao* says: 蘩蔞 [the two readings: *Fan* and *Lou*]
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Japanese name: Yakubera (八久倍良)
　　　　　　　　Tao Yinju (陶隠居, Tao Hongjing) states: this is the same as *Jichō-sō* (鶏腸草, "Chicken Intestine Herb").

　　　　　　　　First day of Yayoi (the third month) in the *mizunoto-hitsuji* year. [Medicinal herb] [Vegetable]
　　　　　　　　Sketched with two brushes in a casual style.

　　　　　　　　***Zōho Tashiki-hen***, section on soft and smooth plants, states:
　　　　　　　　　Hanrō (繁縷) — Japanese name: Hakubera (波久倍良), also called Hakobe (波古倍) [#4]
　　　　　　　　　　Supplementary entry: Tsuruha kobe (豆留波古倍, trailing chickweed)
　　　　　　　　　Alternative names: Jisō (滋草) [from *Qianjin*]; [*Zōho Imyō*] ■縷 (Sōro) [#2] [from *Erya*]
　　　　　　　　　Gō (蔜) [reading: *áo*]　■縷 (Ryōro) [#3] [Guo Pu's commentary]　Gachō-sai (鵞腸菜, "Goose Intestine Herb")

　　　　　　　　***Engishiki***, ***Ribu-ō-ki***,
　　　　　　　　***Chōmei Shiki Monogatari*** and other
　　　　　　　　various texts all list it among the seven herbs of *Jinjitsu* (人日, the seventh day of the first month / Nanakusa no Sekku).

## Kakidōshi (Ground Ivy / *Glechoma hederacea* var. *grandis*)

　　　　　　***Yamato Honzō***:
　　　　　　　　Sekisetsu-sō (積雪草, Kakitōshi / Shakusetsusō)
　　　　　　　　　In the *Bencao*:
　　　　　　　　　Kohakka (胡薄荷, "Foreign Mint")
　　　　　　　　　Tsuruhatsuka

　　　　　　***Kyūkō Yafu*** (Manual of Famine Relief Plants):
　　　　　　Chisen-ji (地錢兒, Kakitōshi)　　　　　　　　[Creeping herb]
　　　　　　　One name: Sekisetsu-sō (積雪草)

　　　　　　***Zōho Tashiki-hen***, section on fragrant herbs:
　　　　　　　Sekisetsu-sō (積雪草) — Japanese name: Tsubokusa (豆保久佐); upon examination, also Tsuruhaka (豆留波加)
　　　　　　　Alternative names: Kaiso (海蘓) [*Zōho Imyō*]　Kohakka (胡薄荷)
　　　　　　　[Taihō-hō] Chisen-sō (地錢草) [from Chinese edition]
　　　　　　　Rensen-sō (連錢草, "Linked Coins Herb") [from Herbal Illustrations]

　　　　　　***Wakan Sansai Zue*** states: The *Sekisetsu-sō* is commonly called "*Kaki-tōshi*" (籬通し, "hedge-passer"). The illustration in *Honzō Shinzoku* which depicts it with branching twigs is incorrect. The reading of *Tsubokusa* (豆保久佐) in the *Wamyō Ruijushō* (Wamyō Shō) is also incorrect. *Tsubokusa* is listed separately. That the *Bencao Gangmu* does not record it as flowering is a matter that should be investigated further.

　　　　　　***Wamyō Ruijushō*** states:
　　　　　　　　Sekisetsu-sō (積雪草) (read as: Tsubokusa) — In the notes to the *Bencao*, Tao Yinju (Tao Hongjing) states: "The Japanese name for *Sekisetsu-sō* is *Tsubokusa* (豆保久佐). It is named thus because of its cold nature (*kanrei*)." Su Jing states: "Its leaves resemble coins (*zeni*), and therefore it is also called *Rensen-sō* (連錢草, 'Linked Coins Herb')."
