## Kodemari (Japanese Snowball / *Spiraea cantoniensis*)

[Seal] Flowering Trees

**Fentuan-hua (粉団花, Kodemari)** — Japanese names: [Kotemari / Susukake] — [Source: *Chikinsho*; also recorded in *Shoruiki*]

　　Chinese name:
　　　Yudaihua (御帯花, "Honorable Sash Flower")

　　　　　壬午 year, the second望日 (17th day) of the lower period of Gusho (the third month / Kisaragi)

**Yamato Honzō** states:
　　Suzukake [also called Kotemari]

**Chaseki Sōka Shū** (Anthology of Flower Arrangements for Tea Ceremonies):
　　Mayū Jukyū (麻葉繍毬) — (Kotemari)

## Kinsenkō (Pot Marigold / Calendula / *Calendula officinalis*)

[Seal] Flowering Grasses

**Saijiki** (Almanac) states:
　Kinsenka (金錢花) — Kingaika (金蓋花)
　　　　　Chōshunkiku (長春菊) [Chikusuji Rokujitsu]
　　Various alternative names exist.

　　Copied on a day in mid-spring of the 壬午 year [#1]

**Yamato Honzō** states:
　　This is the Kinsen flower. It blooms most prolifically in spring, and as it succeeds through all four seasons, it is also called
　　　　　Tokoharu-hana (常春花, "Everlasting Spring Flower").

**Kyūkō Yafu** (Manual of Famine Relief Plants) states:
　　Kinsen-ji (金銭児) — (Kinsenkō)

**Wamyō Ruijushō** states:
　　Kinsenka (金銭花)
　　　Emperor Jianwen of Liang composed a rhapsody (賦) on the Kinsenka
　　　The Kinsenkō is colloquially known as "Komononashi (古無軟)"
