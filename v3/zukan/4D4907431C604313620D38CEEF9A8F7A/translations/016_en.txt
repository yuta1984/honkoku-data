## 【RENGE-SŌ (Chinese Milk Vetch / Astragalus sinicus)】

**Suiheisai (砕米薺) / Renge-bana**

Children in the Kinai region (around Kyoto and Osaka) call this plant "Renge-bana" (lotus flower).
In Tsukushi (Kyushu), it is called "Tehōzō-bana."
In Musashi province, people commonly call it "Renge-sō" (lotus grass).
In the *Saijiki* (almanac of seasonal events), it is classified as "gokei" (五形, one of five forms).

**According to the *Taihō Baryōhō* (Great Treasure Horse Medicine Formulary):**
"Kiran-sō" (喜蘭草)
　Commonly known as "Suiheisai" (砕米薺).
　　Also called "Kenke-bana"
　　And vulgarly called "Renke-sō"

**From the *Jieziyuan Huazhuan* (Mustard Seed Garden Manual of Painting):**
　Purple Vetch (紫雲英, Renge-sō)
　At the end of the third month (koshin/姑洗) of the Mizunoto-Hitsuji year (癸未),
　on the ninth day, painted directly from life at Matsubara,
　using a portable inkstone (koshisuzuri).

**The *Jiuhuang Bencao* (救荒本草, Famine Herbal) entry on "Suiheisai"**
does not explain the name based on the plant's form. Looking at the illustration in that work today, it resembles a lotus flower (renge). In any case, the meaning and origin of the name "Suiheisai" (砕米薺, "broken-rice shepherd's purse") has not yet been elucidated. In the *Bencao Yijiayan* (本草一家言), "Zihua Diding" (紫花地丁, violet) is listed under the alternate name "Suiheisai," and so forth.

## 【DŌKŌ / SHUNGIKU (Crown Daisy / Glebionis coronaria)】

**According to the *Zoho Tashiki-hen* (Expanded Encyclopedia of Knowledge), Aromatic Vegetables Section:**
Dōkō (同蒿 / 茼蒿, Crown Daisy) — Japanese name: Unknown/unclear — Expanded alternate name: Hōkō (蓬蒿)

**According to *Shoku-kyō* (食競, A Treatise on Foods):**
Tōkō (茼蒿)
　Kōrai-giku (高麗菊) — popular name
　Shun-giku (春菊, Spring chrysanthemum)　Mujin-sō (無尽草) (anecdotes omitted)
　Painted on the fifth day of mid-spring (second month), Mizunoe-Uma year (壬午)

[Seal] Vegetable category
[Seal] Medicinal herb

**The author's own view:**
In the *Bencao* (Materia Medica), it states: "Wild chrysanthemum (野菊), also called bitter-taste herb (苦意), is spring chrysanthemum (春菊)," while asserting it is not the "wild chrysanthemum of shepherd's-purse wormwood" — and so forth. However, this view is incorrect. Wild chrysanthemum blooms with small yellow flowers in autumn, whereas Dōkō (Crown Daisy / Shungiku) blooms in spring with flowers resembling chrysanthemums, hence the name. The form of their flowers and leaves is greatly different from each other.

　　　also called "Enkō-sō"
　　Names from *Wamyō Ruijushō*: "Fuhohote-gusa" and "Sosoki"
