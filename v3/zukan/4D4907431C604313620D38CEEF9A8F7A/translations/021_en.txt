## 【TANPOPO (蒲公英 / Dandelion, *Taraxacum* spp.)】

Dandelion (Hokōei / 蒲公英) — According to the *Saijiki* (歳時記):
　◦ [Reading: Tsutsumigrass (鞁草, Tsutsumikusa)]
　◦ The yellow-flowered variety is called [Yellow Hokōtei (黄鞁釘) / Yellow Flower Diding (黄花地丁)]
　　The White Hokōtei (白鞁釘) [refers to the white-flowered variety, as noted in the *Kyūkō Yafu*]

　　　　　　　　　　Re-copied on the 26th day of Kisaragi (3rd month), Mizunoto-Hitsuji year

According to *Kyūkō Honzō* (救荒本草):
　[Reading: Bōbōteisai (孛孛丁菜) = Tanpopo]　Also called: Kōkabyō (黄花苗, "Yellow Flower Seedling")

According to *Wamyō Ruijushō* (和名類聚抄):
　Hokōsō (蒲公草). The *Honzō* says: "Hokōsō (蒲公草)." Japanese name: *Fujina* (不知奈); one account says *Tana* (太奈).
　　　Alternate name: Kōdokusō (耩耨草). ["耩" is read with the fanqie of *Kō-Kō* (上声江項反); "耨" with the fanqie of *Nu-zu* (奴豆反)]

According to the *Zōho Tashikiben*, Soft and Smooth Plants Section (増補多識篇柔滑類):
　Hokōei (蒲公英)　[Japanese name] Tana (多那); on reflection, Tanpopo (多牟保々)
　[Other names] Kinshinsō (金簪草, "Golden Hairpin Herb") [from *Bencao Gangmu*]
　[Additional names from Supplement] Kōdokusō (耩耨草) [pronunciation: Kō (搆)]
　　Ōkachichō (黄花地丁, "Yellow Flower Diding") 　[Colloquial] Fukōei (鳬公英) [from *Qianjin*]
　　Hōkōei (僕公罌) [from *Tujing*]　Hokkōei (鵓鴣英) [from *Yuce*]　Hokōchō (蒲公丁)
　○ Chitansō (地胆草, "Earth Gall Herb")

According to *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目):
　There is a purple-flowered variety called Daitei-sō (大丁草).
　That which is similar but bears no flowers is called
　Chitansō (地胆草).

According to *Nōsei Zensho* (農政全書):
　It is called Bōbōteisai (孛孛丁菜).
　Its flowers open in the morning and wither from noon onward,
　doing so every day—and yet they endure through the long season.

## 【HAHAKOGUSA (鼠麹草 / Jersey Cudweed / *Pseudognaphalium affine*)】

According to *Kankai Shokkyō* (巻懐食競):
　Hahakogusa (鼠麹草)　Reading: Hahoko-gusa (波々古草). Also called:
　Gogyō-yomogi (五行蒿), and it is one of the Seven Spring Herbs of *Jinjitsu* (人日, the 7th of the 1st month).

According to *Imyō Bunruishō* (異名分類抄):
　On consulting the *Bencao Gangmu*, the explanatory names for Hahoko (鼠麹) include
　"Butsuji" (仏耳, "Buddha's Ear") and "Soji" (鼠耳, "Mouse's Ear") — it is an ear-shaped herb.
　The *Wamyōshō* gives: Anroshi (菴蘆子 = Hahoko). The *Honzō Ruihen* gives: Basenkō (馬先蒿).
　It is also added to the Seven Herbs of Jinjitsu as Gogyō (御形) or Gogyō (五行蒿).
　[Author's note:] Mimina (耳菜) is a separate species. To be copied later.

According to *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草):
　Gogyō-sō (鼠麹草 / Gogyō)
　　Alternate name: Butsuji-sō (仏耳草, "Buddha's Ear Herb")
　　Japanese name: Hahakogusa (母子草, lit. "Mother-and-Child Grass")
　Some consider it the same as Kōkakō (黄花蒿, "Yellow-Flowered Wormwood"),
　but identifying it as Jakō (邪蒿) is incorrect.

According to *Tashikiben*, Moisture-Loving Grasses Section (多識篇湿草類):
　[Reading: Sōkiku (鼠麹草)]　[Japanese name] On reflection: Hahakokusa (保於己久左)
　[Other names] Mushinsō (無心草, "Mindless Herb") [from *Bielu*]
　[Additional names from Supplement]:
　Beikiku (米麹, "Rice Malt") [*Gangmu*]　Soji (鼠耳, "Mouse's Ear") [*Bielu*]
　Butsuni-sō (仏耳草, "Buddha's Ear Herb") [*Faxiang*]
　Kōmō (香茅, "Fragrant Sedge") [*Shiyi*]　Kōkō (黄蒿, "Yellow Wormwood") [*Huibian*]
　Jōni (茸母, "Fungus Mother")
　[There exists a poem on *Jōni* by Emperor Huizong of the Song dynasty.]

In former times in Japan, on the third day of the third month, hahakogusa was used to make *kusa-mochi* (草餅, herb rice cakes).
For this reason, in Japanese custom, it is called *Mochigusa* (餅草, "Mochi Herb").
