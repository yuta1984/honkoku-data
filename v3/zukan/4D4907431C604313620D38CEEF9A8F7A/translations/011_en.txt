## Mitsumata (三胯 / Oriental Paperbush, *Edgeworthia chrysantha*)

[Seal] □ category

*Chikinsho* states:
　　*Mitsumata* (三胯, "three-forked")
　　　　The bark of this tree is harvested and used to make paper;
　　　　the finest quality paper is said to be made
　　　　from this bark. And so on.

　　　　Mid-second month (February), Mizunoe-Uma year (文政 5, 1822)

*Honzō Kōmoku* (*Bencao Gangmu*, Compendium of Materia Medica) states:
　　　*Kekkō* (結香, "bound fragrance"): *Mitsumata-yanagi*; from Bōshū (防州, present-day Yamaguchi Prefecture)
　　　　　　　*Juzufusa* (ジュズフサ); from Sanshū (三州, Mikawa Province, present-day Aichi Prefecture)

## Ume (梅 / Japanese Plum/Apricot, *Prunus mume*)

[Seal] Flowering trees

Copied in full over ten days, from the end of the intercalary first month following the New Year, Mizunoe-Uma year (文政 5, 1822).

*Yamato Honzō* states:
　*Ume* (梅): In the *Man'yōshū*,
　　　　　written as "*Mume no hana*" (ムメノ花)
　　　and also read as "*Ume no hana*" (ウメノハナ).

　The plum flower alone opens ahead of all other flowers under heaven.
　For this reason, it is called:
　　　*Hyakka no sakigake* (百花魁, "the vanguard of a hundred flowers")
　　　*Hana no ani* (花兄, "elder brother among flowers")

The fact that the *ume* is called *Kōbunboku* (好文木, "tree that loves learning") originates in the *Kikyochū* (起居注, Court Diary) of the Jin dynasty. In Japanese popular tradition,
the name *Kōbunboku* is said to refer specifically to the white plum (*hakubai*)
that has an especially fine fragrance.

*Wamyō Ruijushō* states:
　　　*Ume* (梅): The commentary on the *Erya* (*Jiga*) states: *Ume* (phonetic gloss: *Mobai* 莫杯, fanqie)
　　　Japanese name: *Ume* (宇女)

## Ōbai (オウバイ / Winter Jasmine, *Jasminum nudiflorum*)

[Seal] Flowering trees

　*Geishunka* (迎春花, "flower that welcomes spring"), called *Ōbai* (ウォウバイ) [from *Yamato Honzō*]
In common popular usage,
　it is called *Ōbai* (黄槑, "yellow plum").
　　Copied throughout an entire day during the intercalary first month (大蔟),
　　Mizunoe-Uma year (文政 5, 1822).
