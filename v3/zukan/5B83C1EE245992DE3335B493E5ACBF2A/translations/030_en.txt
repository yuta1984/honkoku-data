Kinugasasō (Sōkyū / Paris) - Compendium of Materia Medica
《Japanese name: Kinugasasō; Chinese name unidentified; one variety》
It has toxicity. It grows in mountain areas. In spring, one stem grows from one root, reaching a height of about one shaku. Eight or nine leaves grow at the top of the stem, forming an umbrella-like shape. In summer, a stem grows from the center, bearing a single flower. The flower has ten petals of pale purple with deep purple patterns, and drooping yellow stamens. As for Japanese varieties of Sōkyū (Paris), details remain unclear.

Hakurakkuai (Macleaya / Plume Poppy) - Compendium of Materia Medica, Appendix to Castor Bean Entry
《Takenigusa》
It has great toxicity. It grows in mountain areas. In spring, seedlings grow from old roots. The round stems have no nodes and are hollow inside. It reaches a height of five to six shaku. The leaves resemble chrysanthemum leaves in shape but are not pointed at the tips, and are nearly one shaku in size. Both stems and leaves are white-green in color, and when cut, a reddish-yellow juice flows out. In summer, small white flowers open at the top of the stems, forming spikes. Later, pods are produced that are flat and thin, pointed at both ends, containing small black seeds inside. This plant is used in boiling bamboo.
