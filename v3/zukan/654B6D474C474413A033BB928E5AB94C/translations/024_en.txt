**【CHESTNUT (栗, Kuri)】**

　　　　　　　　　　　　　5th day of the 5th month, Mizunoe-uma year (1822)
　　　　　　　　　　　　　Painted faithfully while sheltered from the early summer rains

---

From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (Japanese-Chinese Illustrated Encyclopedia), Fruits section:

　　The flowers of the chestnut
　　　fall during the early summer rains (梅雨) of the 5th month. For this reason, it is popularly written
　　　as "Zuiritsu-ka" (隋栗花), and this is used as a gloss for the reading "tsuyu" (梅雨, rainy season).

　The *Wamyō Ruijushō* states:
　　　Kurishiko (栗子) — chestnut

　The *Kenmyōen* states:
　　　The chestnut is "a companion of the strength" (力質の友); alternate name: Senshi (撰子)
　　　　　　Japanese reading: Kuri (久利)

---

**A certain book states:**

In the 4th year of Emperor Keikō's reign, in the province of Ōmi (近江), there was a single withered tree whose treetop pierced the sky and entered the clouds. When the elders of the province were asked about its origins, they said: "This is a chestnut tree from the age of the gods. Long ago, the branches of this tree rivaled the mountain peaks, and so it was called 'Hie-yama' (並枝山, 'Mountain of Rivaling Branches'). And because it stood connected in parallel with the high peaks, it was also called 'Hira-yama' (並聯山, 'Mountain of Parallel Peaks'). Every year the fallen leaves decompose into soil, and throughout the soil there is nothing but chestnut leaves," and so forth.
The name of Kurimoto District (栗本郡) may also have arisen from this.

　　【Seal】　Tree Category (木類)

---

There is a variety called "Sawa-guri" (沢栗, marsh chestnut), a name given in local custom. It grows abundantly along the stream banks in the mountains of Kiso. Its fruit and leaves are no different from those of the ordinary chestnut. The grain of its wood is finer and more beautiful than ordinary chestnut and is used as a material for utensils and implements. Its fruit is small but the trees grow large. It belongs to the pole/rod (棒) category of trees.

"Sasa-guri" (栭栗) refers to the smaller variety — it is a small chestnut, also called "Shiba-guri" (柴栗). The commentary to the *Erya* (爾雅) states: "In the Jiangdong region, the small chestnut is called 'Yuritsu' (栭栗)." In Cui Yushi's (崔禹錫) *Shokukyō* (食経), it is called "Kōshi" (杭子).

The shiba-guri is rarely sold commercially in the same way as large-fruited chestnuts; rather, it is most often prepared as *kachiguri* (搗栗, dried and pounded chestnut) and widely used as a traditional food offering for the New Year season. The preparation method for *kachiguri* is described in detail in the *Jitsupū* (実譜).
