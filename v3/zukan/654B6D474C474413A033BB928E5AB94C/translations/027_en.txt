## 【Yabu-kanzō / Daylily (Hemerocallis fulva)】

**Kanzō (萱草)**　*(Alternative name: Rokusō [鹿葱, "Deer Onion"])*

　　Transcribed together with leaves
　　　　Suihin (fifth lunar month), Mizunoe-uma year
　　　　　　Transcribed on the last five days of the month

【Seal: Vegetable category】【Seal: Medicinal herb】

> Recorded in the *Yamato Honzō*, section on Flowering Plants.

---

**Kango Shō** (Sino-Japanese Glossary) states:
　　Japanese name: Wasuregusa (Forget-me plant)

**Kemyōen** (Encyclopedia of Names) states:
　　Kanzō (Daylily)
　　Alternative name: Bōyū (忘憂, "Forgetting Sorrow")

The author's
　own collection of books records it as: *Kansō* (藼草)

Also recorded as:
　*Kensō*, *Kensō* (蕿草・諼草), and
　*Bōyūsō* (忘憂草), etc. — all refer to the same plant.

---

Also, in the author's collection of books, it is recorded as:
　*Ginan-sō* (宜男草, "Herb for Bearing Sons")　and　*Gijyo-sō* (妓女草, "Courtesan Herb")

The author remarks:
　The *Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica) states that the Kanzō has three varieties: red, yellow, and purple. The purple-flowered variety remains uncertain (not confirmed by the author).
　Nevertheless, there are several varieties of Kanzō, and the author has personally seen six kinds.

---

**Zōho Tashikiben** (Supplemented Encyclopedia of Knowledge), Section on Marsh Grasses (*Shitsusō*), states:

　Kanzō (萱草)
　　*Japanese name:* Wasuregusa (わすれぐさ)
　　*Alternative names:*
　　　　Tankyoku (丹棘)　《Kokon-chū》
　　　　Rokusō (鹿葱, "Deer Onion")　《Kayū》
　　*Additional alternative names:*
　　　　Bōyū (忘憂, "Forgetting Sorrow")　《Setsumon》
　　　　Ryōshū (療愁, "Curing Grief")　《Kōmoku》
　　　　Roken (鹿剣, "Deer Sword")　《Doshuku》
　　　　Gijyo (妓女, "Courtesan")　《Go-Shin》
　　　　Kinan (宜男, "Auspicious for Sons")
