**【Goosegrass / Crowfootgrass (オヒシバ)】**

**Sumō-kusa (角力草, "Wrestling Grass")**
　　　　*Hakuji-sō* (白慈草) 《According to a stored text, this may refer to *Sumire-sō* (菫草, violet)》

　　Copied during the *Sanpuku* period (the hottest days of summer) of the Mizunoe-uma year (1822)
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　【Seal】 Grass Category

From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (Japanese-Chinese Illustrated Encyclopedia), Marsh Grasses section:

　　《Also known as: Sumō-tori-kusa (相撲取草) / Chikara-kusa (力草)》
　　　　*Kakutei-sō* (角觝草, "Sumo Wrestling Grass")
　　　　*Chikara-kusa* (力草, "Strength Grass") — original botanical name unknown

Children take the stem, bend the spike into a loop like a coin-string ring (*zenisashi*), and use two of these; one is inserted into the other's loop, and two players each hold a stem and pull against each other — the one whose stem breaks loses. This is a popular game, and the plant is commonly called *Sumō-gusa* ("wrestling grass"). The plant grows vigorously of its own accord and becomes a nuisance in farmland and gardens. Because the root is so strong and difficult to pull out, it came to be named *Chikara-kusa* ("strength grass"). Originally, the violet (*sumire*) was called *Kakutei-sō*, and a game of pulling flowers against each other to determine a winner was played with it — the spirit of the game is exactly the same.

There is also a female variety, whose stems and leaves are weak and soft. It will be described in a later volume.
