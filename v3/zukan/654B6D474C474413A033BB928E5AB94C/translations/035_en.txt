**From the Bencao Gangmu (Honzō Kōmoku / Compendium of Materia Medica):**
　The Gōkan (Albizia julibrissin / silk tree) is planted in the courtyards of homes so that people will not feel anger.
　It is said that the Gōkan dispels anger, while the Kanzō (daylily / Hemerocallis) causes one to forget sorrow.

**From the Wamyō Ruijushō (Japanese Name Thesaurus):**
　Gōkan-boku (Nemuri-no-ki / sleep tree)

According to the Tōin (Tang Rhymes dictionary), the character 棔 is pronounced "kon" (hun), and its Japanese name is recorded as "nefuri-no-ki."
　　　　　　The Benshoku Rissei (color-classification text) calls it "Mutsubi-no-ki" (tree of harmony/affection).
　The Gōkan-boku is a tree whose leaves open in the morning and close at dusk.
