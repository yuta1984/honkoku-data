## 【NADESHIKO — Japanese Pink / Dianthus】

Written on the sixth day of early summer, in the year Jingo (壬午)
[Seals:] "Medicinal Herb" / "Flowering Plant"

**Honzō Wage (Commentaries on the Materia Medica)**

*Kubaку* (瞿麦, *Dianthus superbus*) #1 *(marginal notes: Tokonatsu / Sekichiku)* *(note: not listed in the Honzō)*

**Alternative names:** Kyobaку (蘧麦)　Kyokubaку (巨句麦)　Tengiku (天菊)
　　　Tairan (大蘭)　Sekichiku (石竹)　Nantenjikusō (南天竺草)

**Japanese names:** Nadeshiko (奈天之古)　Yamato-nadeshiko (也末跡那天之古)
　　　Sekichiku (世岐知句)

---

**From the Yūsei Shō**

*The Ōkagami Uragaki* states:
"'Kubaку' (Nadeshiko) is the original name of this plant. Because the consort of the Somedono Palace was referred to as the 'Nadeshiko Lady,' out of respect for her taboo name, the flower came to be called 'Tokonatsu' (Ever-Summer). Since the Tokonatsu blooms across three seasons — summer, autumn, and winter — it is called the 'Tokonatsu flower.' The word 'tokonatsu' carries the meaning of 'eternal' or 'constant.'"

- *Bakuden Shō*: listed as "Katami-gusa" (memento plant)
- *Fuboku Shō*: listed as "Katami-gusa" (same)
- *Shinome Shō*: listed as "Natsukashi-gusa" (plant of fond longing)

---

**From the Yamato Honzō**

Kubaку (Nadeshiko / Sekichiku) 【Kubaку】
　　This is the stone bamboo (*Dianthus chinensis*). Its Japanese name is Nadeshiko.
　　It is also called Tokonatsu.
　　Further details are given in the lower section of the same record.
　　In Gao Lian's *Treatise on Flowering Plants*, the double-flowered variety is said to be named "Luoyang Flower" (洛陽花).

**From the Imyō Bunrui Shō**

Nadeshiko (撫子) — also called "Hikurashi-gusa" (day-passing plant)
The *Honkō* gives "Sekichiku" (石竹) as the explanatory name for Kubaку.

---

**From the Wamyō Ruijushō**

Kubaку (Nadeshiko / Tokonatsu):　The *Bencao* states: "Kubaку, also named Tairan (大蘭)"
*(marginal notes: Japanese name — Natenoshiko / alternatively called — Tokonazu)*

　　Tamaki comments:
　　"The wild-growing Nadeshiko is classified as Kubaку (瞿麦), while those cultivated from seedlings in private gardens are called 'Luoyang Flower' (洛陽花). The Kubaку (Nadeshiko) that appears in the poem about the Seven Autumn Plants in the *Man'yōshū* refers to the wild variety."
