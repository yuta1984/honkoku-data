**Upper Section**
The "*hagi*" of Gokasho in Ise Province (also called "*kōmuki*") — The *kawahagi* (filefish/leatherjacket) is brown in color, with spines on both sides.
In Iki Island, the *kawamuki* is found in shallow waters (in Suibara District of Echigo Province, it is called "*hachiyu*").
The *kawahagi* of Geshū (present-day Hiroshima Prefecture) includes varieties that are particularly delicious. In Izumo Province (present-day Shimane Prefecture), people do not eat it, treating it as if it were as poisonous as pufferfish (*fugu*). However, unlike pufferfish, it is actually not poisonous and is eaten [elsewhere] without particular reverence. For this reason, it is dried pufferfish (*fugu*) — rather than this fish — that is sent as a tribute from Unshū (Izumo Province). This fish inhabits sandy areas and is a gentle-natured fish. It is caught using fine-mesh nets or "*tamashi*" nets. The taste is good in summer and autumn. It grows to about seven or eight *sun* (approximately 21–24 cm) in length, and spawns in spring.

**Middle Section**
*Kuchiboso* — A variety of *kawahagi*, of which half the body is head.

(Life drawing labeled "Kuroki")

In Matsumae (Hokkaidō), it is called "*hitode*" ("human hand").
"*Utakararippu*" (possibly an Ainu word) — [a type of] seaweed/tidal grass.
Its flavor surpasses that of *sayori* (halfbeak). The skin is also easy to peel, and the flesh is firm, closely resembling *hata* (grouper).

**Lower Section**
*Tsunoko* — Entirely black, with spines on the skin. It belongs to the *kawahagi* family.
