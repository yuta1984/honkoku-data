**[Upper section]**
*Nansan-shi* (Records of Southern Products) states: The black ray (*kokukō*) has a shape resembling a round fan (*uchiwa*). Because its head resembles that of a swallow, it is called the "swallow ray" (*enkō*).

The Konpei-yokosa [ray?] from Nagaoka in Echigo Province has a [similar fan-like] shape, is black in color, and has a long tail.

**[Lower section]**
According to *Moppin* [*Buhin*/*Honzō Moppin*]: The *sa-ei* (a type of skate/ray) is small in shape, reddish-black in color, and the largest specimens reach over one shaku (~30 cm) in length.
*Moppin Mokuroku* states: The *Bandanei* (坂田鱣) measures two to three shaku in length; its head is round and flat, resembling a round fan, and its body is narrow and elongated.
This is called *Uchiwa-zame* (fan shark/ray).

**[Sticky note text (see previous frame)]:** Katatabori (カタタホリ)

**[Upper section]**
*Ushi-ei* (Bull Ray / Black Ray) — Source: *Moppin*

*Gin-ebi* (Silver Ray): Its color is darker/blacker than the *Aka-ei* (Red Ray/Common Stingray). It resembles the *Aka-ei* but is not as good (in taste).

The *Nabe-ebi* from Tajiri in Hitachi Province is considered the least tasty and is black in color.

People of Matsumae (Hokkaido) dry the *Kasube* (ray) as dried fish (*ho*), treating it similarly to *Aka-ei*.

People of Sendai call it *Karakai-ei*. It is a type of ray with no spine (tail barb), has a wisteria-purple (*fuji*) hue, and smaller ones are called *Kasebi*.
　Dried specimens are called *Renden*. It is eaten as sashimi. In taste, it does not measure up to *Aka-ei*.

A merchant/source from Ise called *Tadashi* states: It resembles the *Aka-ei*; the flesh is thick, largely dark/black, with a slightly dark area on the belly side. The taste is slightly inferior to that of the *Aka-ei*. The belly side resembles *Aka-ei* after it has cooled. The flesh is also thinner than *Ma-ei* (Common Ray).

*Kaimō* [*Honzō Kōmoku Keimō*] states: Another name for *Ushi-ei* is *Kuro-ei* (Black Ray). In Sei Province (Ise), it is called *Ushieu*; in Sendai, [another name is used]. Its color is black, and large specimens measure about six shaku (~180 cm). It is considered low-grade. It corresponds to the "Bull Fish" (*gyūgyo*) in the Chinese text *Minsho* (*Fujian Records*).

**[Lower section]**
The *Katatabori* (Black Ray) of Geishū (Aki Province / present-day Hiroshima area): Preparation methods are the same as for *Aka-ei*. Large specimens caught by fishing measure about six shaku.
