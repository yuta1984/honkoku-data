**Right Folio (with illustration)**

**Upper Section:**
The Red Ray (*Akaei*) lives at Susaki and lies buried in the sand. If a hollow depression is found where one has burrowed out, search nearby, step on it, and spear it with a *yasu* (a pronged spearing tool). One must always ask for helpers, and thank them with purchased sake.
When stung by the spine, pour boiling water over the wound and apply rice bran.
When struck by a Red Ray's spine, wash the wound with hot miso soup.
When stung by a Red Ray, looking at green grass is said to increase the pain.
It is said that chewing hackberry berries (*enomi*) relieves the pain.
Red Rays are caught using sardines as bait.
The trap used for rays is called "*ha*," and is used around June.

A fish merchant says: A large *Hirata* (flat ray) is called *Hirata-Tōran*; a large *Etari* (branched ray?) is called *Nitari-Tōran*. Some refer to all large rays generally as *Tōran*. The *Nitari* has slightly yellow-tinged edges on its fins, resembling a faded version of the *Maei*.

**Middle Section:**

**Left Folio (with illustration)**

**Upper Section:**
The River Red Ray (*Kawa-Akaei*) from the Hino River in Echizen Province is superior to sea-caught specimens.
However, its disposition is worse than that of sea-caught rays, and it frequently impedes the progress of boats.
Large Red Rays live in deep pools (*abuna*). It is said that one once blocked a hole in an embankment, and so people do not kill them.
When one attaches itself to a boat, attaching a piece of iron about three *shaku* long to the tip of an oar and pushing against it will make it let go.
They live in the Nishi River (*Nishikawa*).

A person from Ise named Chū (possibly a merchant) says: The small *Tōran* is called *Hirata*. It resembles the Red Ray but its flesh is extremely thin. The back is bluish-white and the belly is white. Large ones can grow to the size that requires three or four people to carry. The flavor is not good. When the flesh is boiled, it shrinks and becomes like konjac (*konnyaku*).

Shirata Ray from Kujūkuri (99-ri Beach): one specimen — not tasty; white belly.
Blue Ray (*Aoei*) from Shima Province (*Shishū*): the most common; white belly, long tail.
*Konpei* from Nagaoka, Echigo Province: resembles a *Yokoza Ray*, with a black color and a long tail.
*Rendei* from Tajiri, Hitachi Province: small, with a refreshingly clean flavor — good for *kinome-ae* (young sansho pepper leaf dressing) and *nuta* (vinegar-miso dressing). The skin color resembles that of a flounder (*hirame*).

**Middle Section:**
When rays in Geishū (Aki Province) swim up to the surface, they move in overlapping layers stacked above each other, one upon another. When harpooning them, one should target the one at the front — it will yield large, plentiful catches. If one targets the rear, the fish at the front will sink to the bottom and become harder to catch. Similarly, the Red Ray clings to muddy bottoms. They surface during the rainy season (*tsuyu*). At night, a fire is lit and they are taken by harpooning. Young are found around March. Length: three *shaku*.
