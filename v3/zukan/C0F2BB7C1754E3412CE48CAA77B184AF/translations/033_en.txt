【Upper section】
Umikashika (sea deer) from Matsumae (Hokkaido):
It habitually dwells among rocks in shallow waters of rivers and the sea. It can be caught from around the fifth and sixth months, when it is two to three sun (approx. 6–9 cm) in length. From autumn through winter it reaches about one shaku (approx. 30 cm), which is its peak season. In both taste and appearance, it resembles a cross between anglerfish (ankō) and scorpionfish (okoze). It is said that there is only one place where this fish appears.
A rare variety of okoze.
　　　Umikashika

Arrived from Ōbuchi on the twenty-seventh day of the fifth month of the first year of Bunsei (1818), the Year of the Tiger.
The entire body is grayish-white; the belly is white, with black spots scattered all over.
Upon tasting it, the flavor was mild and not particularly delicious.

【Lower section】
Iwa-okoze (rock scorpionfish).

Oni-okoze (demon scorpionfish / Inimicus japonicus): The spiny variety, when simmered in miso soup, has the medicinal effect of promoting urination and reducing swelling. It is also said to be effective against tetanus and beriberi.
