**Right Folio (with illustration)**

**Upper Section**

**Madobiki-ei (Window-pulley Ray):** Its form is thin and flat, with a tail that is slender and long, resembling the rope used to pull a sliding rain shutter (*madodo*); hence its name. Its flesh and bones are somewhat hard, and its flavor is inferior.

**Ushi-ei (Ox Ray):** Black in color, the flesh is white and plump, but the taste is wholly unpleasant.

**Nobusuma** (#1)
Resembling the *Musarahi-mae-ei* (a type of ray),
Its color is black, both wings (pectoral fins) are long, and there are many spines on the tail.
Its flavor is inferior to the *mae-ei*. Its price is also low.
In Edo, fish merchants call it "Karasu" (Crow).

According to *Rinkai Ibutsu-shi* ("Records of Strange Things from the Coast"): "The *tobi-gyo* (kite/eagle ray) has the shape of a kite (*tobi*, the bird), except that it has no tail or feet. On rainy days it can fly to a height of several *jō*."

○ According to *Nansan-shi* ("Records of Southern Products"): "The *kaien-gyo* (sea-swallow fish) has fleshy fins and is capable of flight."

According to *Zensei-shi* (#2): "The *𩶈-gyo* (#3), also called *kaien* (sea swallow), can grow large enough to fill a cart. Its head resembles that of a bat, and its body posture is like a flying swallow. Its tail has a crest (*tategami*), and the forked tips of the tail also resemble the forked tail of a swallow (*ensho*). Between the forks there is an additional elongated oval tail that winds sinuously. On each flank there are flat holes in five layers stacked in alternation. In general appearance it resembles the *kama-boko* ray (*Rhinobatidae*), and it also lacks scales and armor." 

[Note:] According to the *Wuxing-zhi* section of the *Book of Southern Qi* (*Qi Shu*), in the first year of *Yongming* (483 CE), at Shipu in Yanguang County (Yangguan-xian), a sea fish arrived on the tide and, when the water receded, could not leave. It was over thirty *zhàng* in length, black, without scales, and its voice sounded like an ox. The local people called it "sea swallow" and caught and ate it. Could this not be the very same fish? In *Keifu Kyūshi* ("Old Records of Hainan"), there is a "swallow fish" whose dorsal skin is rough (sandy) and whose flesh is white and delicious. A newer record states: "There are three varieties—red, white, and yellow—with wings resembling those of a swallow, capable of soaring above the sea, and hence named after the swallow. Popularly called *Rōa-gyo* ('old crow fish')." Neither old nor new records give full details. All are suspected to be the same fish as the *𩶈-gyo* (#3).

**Lower Section**

According to *Kaien-ken Zukyō* ("Illustrated Gazetteer of Haiyan County"): "The *𩶈-gyo* (#3) is round and as large as a winnowing basket (*mi*), smells fishy, and has soft bones. Its tail is long, and at the tip there is a sharp spine. When a person tries to seize it by hand, it deftly moves its tail and strikes. When struck by the spine's poison, the wound festers all the way to the bone before finally stopping."

**Left Folio**

*Ise-chū* (Iseya Chūbei) (#6) says: "The *Yokosa* (#4) has something bone-like within its flesh that is hard, but the flavor is good."

　　A fish merchant says: "When fresh *Yokosa* is sliced crosswise, and the flesh — which comes apart in flakes — is eaten with vinegar-miso (*su-miso*), they say it tastes just like eating sashimi of swordfish (*kajiki*)."

　　Item description: *Amikasa-ei* — the back is mouse-grey, and the belly on both sides is yellow.

　　*Yokosa* — triangular with a short tail; the fins (*yokosa*) resemble those of the *Aka-ei* (red ray / common stingray). Made into *kamaboko* (fish paste cake).

　　Hitachi Province, Tajiri — *Takabu* (#5): the most delicious. Large, mouse-colored. Eaten raw.

　　*Yokosa* of Geshū (Aki Province) — also called "Great Ray (*Ōei*)." Over one *jō* (approx. 3 meters) long, dwelling in the open sea. It always appears in one fixed location. Caught by angling.
