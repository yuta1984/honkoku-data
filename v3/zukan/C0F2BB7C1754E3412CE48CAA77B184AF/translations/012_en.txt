**Right Folio (with illustration)**

**Upper Section**

**Madobikie-i (Window-Cord Ray):** Its form is thin and flat, and its tail is slender and long, like the cord used to pull open a window shutter — hence its name. Its flesh and bones are somewhat hard, and its flavor is inferior.

**Ushi-ei (Ox Ray):** Its color is black, its flesh is white and fatty, and its taste is utterly unpleasant.

**Nobusuma** [#1]
Resembles the *Musarabi Mae-ei* (?); its color is black, both wings (pectoral fins) are long, and its tail has many spines. Its taste is inferior to the *Mae-ei*, and its price is also lower. In Edo, fishmongers call it "*Karasu*" (Crow).

According to *Rinkai Ibutsu-shi* ("Records of Strange Things from the Sea Coast"): "The *Tobi-gyo* (Kite Fish) resembles a kite (*tobi*) in form, except that it has no tail or feet. On cloudy and rainy days it also flies to heights of several *jō*." ○ According to *Nansan-shi* ("Records of Southern Products"): "The *Kaien-gyo* (Sea Swallow Fish) has fleshy wings and is capable of flight."

According to *Zensai-shi* [#2]: "*Kō-gyo* [#3], also known as *Kaien* (Sea Swallow). Large specimens fill an entire cart. The head resembles that of a bat, and the body's bearing is like a soaring swallow. The tail has a mane-like appendage, and its forked tips also resemble the forked tail of a swallow. Between the forks there is additionally a long, oval tail, the shape of which undulates like a serpent. On each side there are five layers of flat, stacked holes. In general it is similar to the *Kamegaei* (*Pufu*, Skate) [raja type ray], and likewise has no scales or armor. Note: the *Qi Shu* (*Seisho*), in its "Five Phases" section (*Gōgyō-shi*), records that in the first year of *Yōmei* [483 CE], at Shihpu in Yanguan County, a sea fish rode the tide ashore and could not return when the water receded. It was over thirty *jō* in length, black in color, scaleless, and made a sound like a cow. The local people called it '*Kaien*' (Sea Swallow) and caught and ate it. Perhaps this is indeed that very creature." According to *Keifu Kyūshi* ("Old Gazetteer of Qiong Prefecture"): "The *En-gyo* (Swallow Fish) has rough, sand-like skin on its back, white flesh, and a delicious flavor." According to the *New Gazetteer*: "There are three varieties — red, white, and yellow. Both wings resemble those of a swallow, and it is capable of soaring over the sea, hence the name 'swallow.' It is commonly called *Rōa-gyo* (Old Crow Fish). Neither the old nor new gazetteers provide detailed accounts." All of these are suspected to be the same as the *Kō-gyo* [#3].

**Lower Section**

According to *Kaien-ken Zukyō* ("Illustrated Topography of Haiyan County"): "The *Kō-gyo* [#3] is round and large, like a winnowing basket (*mi*). It has a fishy odor and soft bones. Its tail is long, and at the tip there is a sharp spine. If a person tries to grasp it by hand, the fish deftly swings its tail and strikes; where the spine's venom enters, the flesh rots down to the bone before it stops."

**Left Folio**

*Ise no Tadashi* [a fishmonger named Tadashi from Ise] says: "Inside the flesh of *Yokosa* [#6] there are bone-like structures (hard), but the taste is good."

A fishmonger says: "If you take fresh *Yokosa*, cut it sideways, and the flesh comes apart in flakes, eating it with *su-miso* (vinegar-miso dressing) is like eating *kibata* [#5] sashimi — the flavor is excellent."

**Species listings** [#4]:
- **Amikasa-ei** — back is mouse-gray (*nezumi-iro*), both sides of the belly are yellow.
- **Yokosa** — triangular, short-tailed; the tail spine of *Yokosa* is like that of *Aka-ei* (Red Stingray). Used to make *kamaboko* (fish cake).
- **Hitachi, Tajiri — *Takabu*:** Most delicious; large, gray (*nezumi-iro*) in color; suitable for eating raw.
- **Geishū *Yokosa*:** Also called *Ōei* (Large Ray); over one *jō* (approx. 3 meters) in length; inhabits the open sea. Always appears at the same spot; caught by line fishing.
