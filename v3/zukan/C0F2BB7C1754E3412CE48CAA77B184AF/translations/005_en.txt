*Ningbo Fuzhi* (寧波府志, Gazetteer of Ningbo) states: "It is small and yellow, with a black back and yellow belly."

*Youyang Zazu* (酉陽雑俎), Supplementary Collection, states: "The yellow ray (*Huánghóng yú*) is yellow in color, has no scales, a pointed head, and a body resembling a large oak leaf. The mouth is located beneath the jaw. Behind the eyes are ear openings that connect to the brain. The tail is one *shaku* long, with three spines at the tip, which are extremely venomous."

Aichi kelp (*aichikonbu*), seaweed and tidal grass.

At a depth of twenty-four to twenty-five *hiro* in the sea around Iki Island, one finds the Red Ray (Akaei), Black Ray (Kuroei), and Nakaken (reddish-brown with a long tail).
The eggs of the Red Ray are first white, then turn yellow. Three out of four individuals are male, one is female. The eggs are white and resemble Buddhist prayer beads. They are not particularly tasty. The Red Ray has a vivid, striking coloration.
Large rays can be as big as a six-tatami-mat room; they are caught with twelve or thirteen harpoons over a period of about three days.
Their form is round and their color is black; they are not slippery even when stepped on. They fan sardines and mackerel with their two wings (fins) to stun them before eating. They yield a large amount of oil. They are harmless to eat. The *Maei* (front ray) is reddish-brown tinged with blue, and greenish-red on the underside — considered the finest quality.
Rays around the Gokasho area of Ise burrow holes in the sand. They come out into the open sea at low tide and emerge at night when the moon rises.
Rays from Shima Province (*Shishū*) are caught in summer. It is good to extract and eat the ray's liver oil.
The Red Ray eats clams. Where Red Rays are present, people cannot enter holes to gather abalone.
The eggs of the Red Ray are entirely white, resembling the eggs of a large soft-shelled turtle. In February, it is said they are eaten in Bingo Province using a stick (or rod).
The male ray is called "*Sambotchiri*" (possibly referring to the claspers) and has two roots (two claspers). The female is yellow. Young born around the summer *doyō* season are called "*teirigo*." Among creatures that move their eyelids are: the Red Ray, sharks, and pufferfish — all of which steal food at night.

**Lower section:**
According to people from Sendai, the fins of the Red Ray are yellowish with a tinge of red.
The *Maei* is reddish-yellow, the color of *hatsutake* mushrooms (*Lactarius hatsudake*), with a darkish tinge. Its teeth are soft. In spring and summer, the Red Ray is preferred; in winter, the Kite Ray (*Tonbiei*) and *Yokosa* (possibly *Yokozame*, a species of shark or ray) are preferred.
