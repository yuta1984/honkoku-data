**[Left folio (with illustration)]**

The **ray (ei)** burrows holes in the seabed and lives there.
When the tide goes out, it comes out toward the open sea. At night, it comes out on moonlit nights.

According to the *Ningbo Prefectural Gazetteer*: The "kōsa" (yellow ray) is small, with a black back and yellow belly.

According to the *Youyang Zazu* (Miscellaneous Morsels from Youyang), Continued Collection: The yellow ray (*kōkōgyo*) is yellow in color, has no scales, a pointed head, and a body resembling a large oak leaf. Its mouth is located under the jaw; behind its eyes are ear openings that connect to the brain. Its tail is one *shaku* (approx. 30 cm) long, with three spines at the tip, which are highly venomous.

**Aichi Korube** (seaweed / tidal grass)#2

In the waters off Iki island, at depths of twenty-four to five *hiro* (fathoms, approx. 44–45 m), one finds the red ray (Akaei), black ray (Kuroei), and Nakaken (reddish-brown with a long tail).
The eggs of the red ray are initially white and later turn yellow. Those with three tails are male, and those with one tail are female. The eggs are white and resemble prayer beads. The taste is not delicious. The red ray is vivid in color.
The largest rays are about the size of a six-tatami room, and are caught with twelve to thirteen harpoons, roughly every three days.
They are round in shape, black in color, and not slippery even when stepped on. They use both wings to fan and pin down sardines and mackerel before eating them. They yield a large amount of oil. Safe to eat.
The *maei* (front ray / manta-type) is reddish-brown with a bluish tint, and greenish-red on the underside — considered the finest quality.
Rays in the Ise Gokasho area burrow into the sand and live there. They come out toward open water when the tide recedes, and emerge on moonlit nights.
The rays of Shima Province are caught in summer. It is good to extract and eat the liver oil of the ray.
The red ray eats clams. When red rays are present, people cannot enter the holes to collect abalone.
The eggs of the red ray are entirely white, resembling the large eggs of a soft-shelled turtle. It is said that they are eaten in Bingo Province in the second month using [sticks?].
The male ray's organ is called "sambotchiri" and has two roots (claspers). The female is yellow in color. They give birth during the midsummer (dog days). The young are called "teirigo." Some have moving eyelids. Red rays, sharks, and puffer fish steal prey at night.

**[Lower section]**

According to people from Sendai: the red ray's fins are yellowish with a tinge of red.
The *maei* is reddish-yellow, the color of *hatsutake* mushrooms (*Lactarius hatsudake*), with a dark tinge. Its teeth are soft. In spring and summer, the red ray is most desirable; in winter, the *tonbiei* (kite ray) is found abundantly alongside.
