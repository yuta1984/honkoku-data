**Right Page (with illustration)**

The "*hōwo*" (swordfish/billfish) of Tsushima Island resembles the illustration in the *Yamato Honzō* (Japanese Materia Medica): its upper beak is long and sword-like, capable of shaving wood and easily piercing through boats. Its skin is black and resembles that of a tuna (*maguro*). Its flesh is poisonous, yet people eat it nonetheless. The internal oil is strained, and the intestines are grilled and eaten — the flavor is supremely delicious. The liver resembles that of an otter, with several lobes hanging in a row. This is simmered to extract the oil.

The flesh of *kajiki* (marlin/billfish) is at its best in summer. The *mekajiki* (swordfish) has white flesh with a flavor resembling shark. It is best marinated in miso and then grilled. In Tosa Province (present-day Kōchi Prefecture), it is called "*nairake*."

The *bashōkajiki* (sailfish) has an extremely long mouth and large dorsal fins.

**Middle Section**
In Ise Province, *kajiki* is called "*ōiwo*" (大魚, "great fish"). In Nagasaki, the white *kajiki* (shiroka jiki) is called "*sawara*."

*Kawahagi* (filefish, brown in color): It has spines on both sides. In Suibara District of Echigo Province (present-day Niigata Prefecture), it is called "*hachiyu*."

**Lower Section**
[This fish] inhabits large rocks/reefs in Geshū (present-day Hiroshima Prefecture). It is a pelagic fish. Some have long bills and some short ones. The hardness [of the bill] is comparable to iron. The flesh is white and rich in oil. It is commonly eaten as salted fish called "*nashira*."

*Kachikoruhe*: [A type of] seaweed/tidal grass.

According to "Tadabeē" (or "Chū") of Ise: The flesh of the *bashōkajiki* is uniformly red. Its body is flat. The *mekajiki* has white flesh. A subspecies of *mekajiki* whose flesh is greenish in color is commonly called "*kusamochi*."

The flesh of *kajiki* is reddish, with variations in depth of color. The skin varies from thin to thick. The flesh of *mekajiki* is white, and comes in two types: plain gray (*nezumiro*) and gray with mottled markings. The *bashōkajiki* may be black.

In the Hino River area of Echizen Province (present-day Fukui Prefecture), the large fish called "*ōiwo*" is the *kajiki*. It resembles tuna but has scales. In the northern provinces, it is called "*yoshinuta*." When cooked, it melts away. Its eyes are large; if the eye socket, roughly the size of five *gō* (approximately 900 ml), is hung up, a fine quality oil of five *shō* (approximately 9 liters) can be extracted from it. If one eats the meat of the head, one will remain intoxicated for six or seven days.

According to the *Sansai Zue* (Illustrated Compendium of the Three Powers): The fish "*jin*" (鱘) is commonly called "*kachitōshi*." Upon investigation: the bill of the *jin* is sharp and pointed like iron, and ships at sea that encounter it may be pierced through. Hence it is popularly called "*kachitōshi*" (柁通, literally "rudder-piercer").
