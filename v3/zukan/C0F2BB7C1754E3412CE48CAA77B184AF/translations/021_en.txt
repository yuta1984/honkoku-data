**Kinkagamidai (Golden Mirror Bream):** Has no circular halo markings; silver in color.

**Kagamidai (Mirror Bream):** In shape resembles the Aigo (Siganus fish), and belongs to the same group as the Manakaatsuo. The head is large; the dorsal fin is long, extending to the tail, and the underside of the belly is also long. Fins flank the [?] on both sides.#1 The tail is not forked. Both the dorsal surface and the ventral surface have serrated spines. At the center of the body there is one circular spot, black in color. Around the eye is golden, as if gold leaf had been applied over lead. The area around the abdominal organs is white.

Kagamidai is ground into a paste to make *shinjō* (a fish dumpling/cake).

**Hage:** Has round star-like markings along the back.

**Kagamidai:** Thin like a flounder (*karei*), with no scales. Color is pale blue, with black circular markings on the belly, about the size of a large coin. The tail resembles that of a flounder (*hirame*). The mouth is large, the skin is smooth, the fins are small, and it has no bones. Caught and fished in autumn and winter.

**Entry — Batōgyo (Horse-head Fish):** Called "*Matōo*" in Ise Province. Also known as "*Tatakigane*" (Striking Bell).【Matōo / Tatakigane】

**Wase** [Nagasaki]: Resembles a flounder (*hirame*), black on both sides. The snout is long, with a bone at its tip.

According to the "Index/Catalogue": Wase is [caught] in the western provinces. Its form resembles the Manakaatsuo but has no scales; the back is pale black with white tie-dye-like markings. The mouth resembles that of a horse mackerel (*aji*), and the body length is one to two *shaku* (approximately 30–60 cm).

Kagamidai is ground into a paste to make *shinjō*.

**From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (encyclopedia):** The "*Hōgyo*" (魴魚), commonly called "*Kagamiuo*" (mirror fish) or "*Matōo*" (target fish).#2

[Note:] Hō (魴) and Shō (鯧) are two distinct creatures; their shapes are similar and their descriptions make them difficult to distinguish. However, by the illustration in the *Wakan Sansai Zue*, one can distinguish them well. Generally, the Hō has a fin running from the nape to the tail, with a single filamentous streamer (mane). It is best eaten grilled; its flesh is white, and superior to that of the Shō.
