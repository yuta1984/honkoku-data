**Right folio (with illustration)**

**Middle section:**
*Netsutspau* (Netsutspou): The head is pointed, resembling a mouse in appearance. The back has round spotted markings; the color is bluish and the body is small and elongated. It stings/pricks people. The chest is white, appearing as if pale blue light shines through it. There are markings on the flanks. Compared to the *kochi* (flathead fish), it has no scales and its color is deeper. There are two hook-like projections on the upper jaw, with teeth at their tips. It belongs to the *karakaki* group (?). The supraorbital region (area above the eyes) of this fish is exceptionally thick.

**Lower section:**
*Netsutspou*: Large specimens are called *Numerigochi*.
*Netsuzako*, *Megochi*, also known as *Netsuhō*, and sometimes called *Anesan*.
(This fish) is extremely mucous, flat in body shape, and has little flesh. It inhabits the same areas as *kisu* (sillago) and *haze* (goby), and larger specimens are found around Namamugi (in present-day Kanagawa Prefecture).
*Netsutspou* will prick a person's hand when caught on a hook. It is also caught in nets.
*Mimikochi*: Found in Etchū Province (present-day Toyama Prefecture).

**Left folio (with illustration)**

**Upper section:**
*Kawahagi* (filefish): There are three varieties. One is the *Umazura* (*Umazurahagi*, longnose filefish), one is a rounded variety (*Kawahagi*, filefish proper), and one is a hexagonal variety (possibly a type of boxfish/*Hakofugu*). The taste is excellent; the mouth is pointed and small.

**Lower section:**
*Ishidai* (striped beakfish), *Kawahagi*:
Toxic. Has black markings.※1 Gray in color.
Both its nature and taste are poor. The skin is thick; do not eat it raw.
