## [From the Sansai Zue: On the "Old Woman Fish," "Ribbon Fish," and "Biwa Fish," commonly known as Ankou (Anglerfish)]

According to the *Senshu Fushi* (Prefectural Gazetteer of Quanzhou): "It is also called the 'Belly-Button Fish' (Kasaigyō), and has a band resembling a *hishi* (a type of sash) on its belly, to which it attaches other organisms, hence the name 'Ribbon Fish' (Jūgyō). Its shape resembles a tadpole, and large specimens can be as big as a serving dish." According to the *Wudu Fu*: "This fish has no scales and its shape resembles a *biwa* (lute), and so it is also called the 'Biwa Fish.'"

**[Author's note]:** This fish is found in abundance in the Eastern Sea, but is rare in the southwestern seas. Those that appear in early October are most prized; from March onward they become scarce, and in summer and autumn they are entirely absent. In shape it is round and flat like a dish, thick in the middle with a large belly; the back is black and the belly white. Its eyes and nose face upward, its mouth is wide and large with whisker-like appendages. The fins are short and weak, and the bones are also extremely soft. The tail is undivided and long. [It is used in soups and dishes], and its flavor is [mild and slightly sweet, of neutral character]. [Only the stomach and head are discarded]; the rest is entirely edible and makes for excellent cuisine. There is a proper method for preparing it, which is called ["tsuri-giri" (hanging cut)]. A rope is passed through [the lower lip] of the mouth and the fish is suspended from a [roof beam]. Water is poured into the mouth until [five or six shō] overflow from the mouth. First the skin of the [throat and neck exterior] is cut, then the skin is peeled away [around the entire body]. The fins and [flesh] are then separated, and the gallbladder, intestines, and bones are removed. Piercing the stomach sac with a blade causes the accumulated water to flow back out. If the fish is not butchered according to this proper method, the flesh will not separate cleanly from the skin and bones.

**Field Notes (in Japanese vernacular):**

*Ankou* in Izu Province: Caught from winter through spring using nets. The *Kutsu-ankou* (shoe anglerfish) is small, about one *shaku* (approx. 30 cm) in length, red in color, and delicious. [No "nuno-ko" (egg ribbons)?]—perhaps a male fish.

Usami, Izu: In October, anglerfish live at a depth of about seven *shaku* (approx. 2 meters) on the sea floor. They are caught using flatfish nets (*hirame-ami*).

Geishū (Aki Province, present-day Hiroshima area): Anglerfish are rare in the western provinces. They burrow into mud and can also be caught by fishing lines.

"Shōkoru Setsuppu" — a type of seaweed / tidal grass [see annotations]

The purple anglerfish (*Murasaki-ankou*) is considered the finest quality.
