**Right folio (with illustration and attached slip/note)**

Illustrations of *Onikochi* (Demon flathead/scorpionfish), *Shishikochi* (Lion scorpionfish), *Akagochi* (Red scorpionfish), and *Hebikochi* (Snake scorpionfish).

The *Oni-okose* (onikochi; spiny scorpionfish/devilfish) has numerous spines. When cooked in miso soup, it is effective as a diuretic (reducing edema/swelling). It is also said to be beneficial for tetanus and beriberi.
In Geishū (Aki Province; present-day Hiroshima Prefecture), it dwells among seaweed. It has spines on its back, and if one is stung, the pain continues from the time the tide begins to ebb until high tide.
Conversely, if stung at high tide, the pain does not cease until low tide. It is caught using nets.
In Iki (present-day Nagasaki Prefecture), it lives in shallow waters. This fish is the *okose* (okoze).

*Shachihoko-okose* (shiachi-hoko scorpionfish):
It has a mud-colored, thin, smooth skin;
when peeled, the flesh beneath is white.
It is a fish of the *okose* (okoze) family.
