**Upper section:**

**Middle section:**
*Ishidai* (striped beakfish) — The shape resembles that of the *aigo* (rabbitfish), broad-bodied, with an angular head resembling the *koshōdai* (grunt). The tail is small, similar to the *aiko*. The tail resembles that of the bonito and is flattened. The skin is tough and it has teeth. It can reach one *shaku* two to three *sun* (approx. 37–40 cm). There are horizontal black stripes, with the spaces between them in gray (*nezumi-iro*), and those areas lack scales. The *ishidai* lies somewhere between the *kachiyama* and the *tsubakuradai*, with a forked tail, a round body, a long banded pattern, and three distinct stripes. It tapers in accordance with its body shape. There is strong musculature in the jaw.
*Shimadai* (striped fish) — The shape resembles the *tanago* (bitterling); the scales are similar to those of the *ishidai*; the vertical stripes are bluish-white to indigo.

**Lower section:**

*Suzume-dai* (damselfish) — illustration from life
*Shima-ishidai* (barred knifejaw)
　　According to the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (*Three Powers Illustrated*): The *shima-dai* resembles the *umibuna* (sea crucian carp) but is smaller, not exceeding four to five *sun* (approx. 12–15 cm), and has black and white horizontal stripe patterns that follow one another in layers without mixing.
In Geishū province, it is called "*Kurokuchi*" ("black-mouth").
The scales are fine and hard, difficult to remove. It is a fish of the Seto Inland Sea, caught by rod and net. It produces young in spring. It becomes best to eat in summer. It is rich in oil. Large specimens measure about one *shaku* (approx. 30 cm); the flesh is tough, and it has two teeth like those of a pufferfish.

**Left folio (with illustration)**

**Upper section:**
In Kujūkuri (Chiba), it is called "*Shō*."
Also called *Shimadai*. Blackish-blue in color, without scales.
*Kurigome-gomaishidai* (*Gomishidai*) — A type of *ishidai* (rock porgy), with a finely rough texture. It also has extremely fine scales, like sharkskin. The flavor of the flesh is not good. It is considered food for people of low status. When dried in cold winds during midwinter, the color changes. It has two teeth on top and bottom, like the beak of a bird.

**Middle section:**
*Ishidai* — Resembles the *tanago* (bitterling) in shape, with a black color; the back resembles that of a *karei* (flatfish), with an indigo-tinged jet-black coloration. For this reason, it is said to resemble the *kurodai* (black sea bream).
