**Right folio, upper section:**
From Odawara — "Watamuki" (also called "Umi-mamushi," or Sea Viper)

Its shape resembles a snake; it is black in color, the flesh is white, and the taste is delicious. When prepared as kabayaki (grilled with sauce), there are no bones. It has three firm joints/nodes, and if these nodes are not removed, even after cutting off the head and tail and removing the intestines, the body will spring/jump about. The nodes are like those of the camphor tree (kusunoki). No scales. Around early spring (haru-na, etc.), it is generally used as bait for small fish. The girth is about the size of a thumb, and the length is one shaku five or six sun (approximately 45–48 cm).

　　*Sea Snake (Umi-hebi)*

　　Its shape resembles a conger eel (anago),
　　length about three shaku (approx. 90 cm),
　　girth about four sun (approx. 12 cm),
　　the flesh has many fine bones throughout,
　　and few people eat it.
