## Right Folio (with illustrations and sticky note)

### [Upper Section]

(Upon reflection,) the form and color of mackerel (*saba*) largely correspond to the descriptions given in the *Honzō* (Chinese materia medica) texts. However, it is found abundantly in the northern and western seas, and one does not hear of it inhabiting lakes. Nor can we confirm here that its cranial bones (otoliths / skull bones) are used as implements. Its shape resembles the grass carp (*kokurei* / *koi*), and its scales are extremely fine. Large specimens reach 1 shaku 4–5 sun (approximately 42–45 cm). The back is a true blue-green color, with faint dark bluish-black mottled patterns, and some have markings resembling a rope wound around the body. Near the tail, on both sides symmetrically, there are fins with spine-like projections. The flesh has a sweet flavor with a slight acidity. It spoils easily, and eating fish left overnight can intoxicate the eater. However, fresh fish prepared with vinegar (*su*) is excellent. Off the coast of Noto, in mid-April, hundreds of thousands of fish drift with the waves, and can be caught without hook or net. They are dried (like dried abalone) and transported to various provinces. People of all classes prize them, and they are used as offerings for *Chūgen* (the Bon season midsummer gift-giving) and New Year's celebrations. The fish is split open along the spine from the back, and two pieces placed together as one layer, called "*hitosashi*" (one skewer). Those of a reddish-purple color are considered the finest quality. Coating with sardine oil before drying improves the color. Noto-produced mackerel is considered the best, followed by Sado and Etchū.

### [Lower Section]

In Satsuma Province (Sashū), large mackerel are called "*Gongen-saba*."
*Shaba* (a type of seaweed / tidal grass)

From the *Wakan Sansai Zue*: Japanese name *Aosawa* (阿乎左波).
The *Shokukyō* (食経, "Classic of Foods") states: "Mackerel has a pointed mouth and a blue-green back." The *Honzō* accounts are roughly the same.

The *Gozatsuzo* (五雑爼, "Miscellaneous Notes in Five Categories") states: "Eating mackerel (*saba*) already calms madness; eating *ei* (鮆, a type of small fish) stops arrogance; eating leftover fish does not cause intoxication; eating *ningyo* (人魚, mermaid/dugong?) cures stupidity; eating the *kōrai uguisu* (黄鳥, oriole) for the first time, and eating the *ketsukuri* bird (鶢鶋) prevents hunger — these are ancient sayings, though their veracity has not yet been confirmed."

### [Middle Section — continues from right folio]

**Mirror Fish (*Kagami-dai*)**

There exists a fish called *Nuka-Kagami-dai*, which is a different species from this one. Among the fish called "mirror fish" (*kagami*), there are in fact several different kinds. This particular fish is extremely flat like a board, has no scales, and has a blue-green (*hekisoku*) back with faint dark spots. The fin below the eye is exceptionally transparent, as beautiful as white glass. The fins below the belly are thin and soft, resembling five cords, and are bluish-green in color. This fish is luminously clear throughout its body, transparent like a mirror — hence the name. Regarding this fish: larger specimens are about 5–6 sun (15–18 cm) in size, silver in color, with the fine bones visibly showing through from outside. Its shape resembles a tobacco-cutting knife (*tabako-hōchō*). For this reason, I have heard that fishermen at Tama-no-ura (璚浦) call it "*Tabako-hōchō*" (tobacco knife). The body is flat and thin, with very little flesh. It is difficult to use as food.
