## Right Folio

**Upper Section**
**"Tōjin Tachi-no-uo" (Belt fish as called by foreigners):** Gaudy/wild in color, often brought back [to port] in large quantities. Tachiuo is good prepared with ankake sauce (starchy, thickened sauce). In Geishū (Aki Province, modern Hiroshima), tachiuo are large and tend to school together. They are most abundant in winter; those with high oil content are caught with nets.

**Middle Section** (Quotation from *Igyō Sanki, Utsuwa-gata Sakana no Bu* [Section on vessel-shaped fish]):
"The *kōgai-gyo* (hairpin fish): Its color is white like a kōgai (a hairpin ornament). It is a little over one shaku in length. There is also another variety called the 'Goō kōgai' (King Yue's hairpin [fish]), which is about the size of a hairpin and white in color. It lives at the seaside and in ponds. When one spots it, one should catch it immediately; if one waits, it burrows into the soil."

**Lower Section**
Tanjū (Kurimoto Tanjū) states: In Edo it is called *Umi-hebi* (sea snake); in Kishū (Kii Province) it is called *Tatami-bari*, and also *Yōji-uo* (toothpick fish). The largest specimens reach five or six sun in length. In Amagasaki in Sesshū (Settsu Province), it is called *Suzu-anago*. The body is slender, the mouth small and pointed, four to five sun in length. It resembles sayori (halfbeak) and has ridges (edges) along the body. Its color is dark/black. Fishermen dry it and use it as a toothpick (牙杖, *yōji*). It is also said to cure tooth decay. It is reportedly not used as a food item. This is precisely the fish in question. There is also a variety about five sun in length; when dried, it is perfectly straight and slender, pointed at both head and tail. The underside of the belly is long and hollowed out as if scooped, as if the intestines have been removed. This may be the trace left behind after the brood-pouch young have detached; it is difficult to judge. It is a most unusual specimen among fish.

## Left Folio

**Upper Section** (Quotation from *Wakan Sansai Zue* [Illustrated Japanese-Chinese Encyclopedia]):
"*Ji* (鱭), commonly known as tachiuo (belt fish). Upon examination, the *ji* is born in rivers and seas, but it is wrong to say it lives in rivers and lakes. It appears in greatest abundance in the eighth and ninth months, at the same time as sardines (iwashi). It is especially numerous in Senshū (Izumi Province) and Banshū (Harima Province). Large specimens reach three to four shaku; smaller ones reach one to two shaku. It resembles sea eel (umi-unagi) but is thin and flat, bluish in color, covered above with a white gloss like mica—this is in fact fat. It has no scales; those who say it has fine scales are in error. The eyes are round and large; the snout, gills, and hard spines below the navel are all as described in the *Honzō* (Chinese materia medica texts). Scraping off the white fat, it may be braised or grilled. The flesh is tender, and the flavor is excellent. Small bones cross the spine like the teeth of a comb."

**Middle Section**
*Hama-hebi* (*Yōji-uo*, pipefish): Under the Funabashi bridge, there are large specimens reaching one shaku. They enter firm sand tail-first. They also enter inside clam (hamaguri) shells.
In Ise, it is called *Yōji-uo*, and also *Hashi* (chopstick/bridge).
The *hama-hebi* of the Kasai coast has a back color like that of a *kochi* (flathead fish), a yellow belly, a hexagonal body, about seven or eight sun long, with a pointed beak like that of a sayori.
From the second-third month through the ninth month, the *hama-hebi* enters the sand tail-first. It can enter as much as five sun at a time. It has many bones, jointed like a herring (shibu-shi).
In Geishū (Aki Province), it is called *Namaida* and lives in sand.

(From the entry on *anago* [conger eel] in the *Shokuryō Seiyō* [Essential Principles of Food Therapy]):
There is also a *Suzu-anago* [annotated with "a" in red ink to clarify reading], found in Amagasaki. It resembles *anago* (conger eel) and is three to four sun long. The body has ridges, the beak is slender, and the color is black.

(From the commentary on a certain fish in *Senkinraku*):
There is also a *Suzunago* (annotated in red as *Suzu-anago*) in Amagasaki, without scales [annotated with "fish" beside it]. It is slender and small, four to five sun in length. It resembles sayori with ridges on the body. The beak is slender and long, the color is black. Fishermen dry it and use it as a toothpick (*tekiga-jō*). It is said to cure tooth decay. It is not included as a food item [the original character 云 is struck through and replaced in red with 入れず].
