**Upper Section**

According to *Nan'etsu-shi* (南越志, "Records of Southern Yue"): "The *𩽳-gyo* (ray) resembles the *䱃𩺷* (loach; pronounced *hitsu*); on its tail there are spines like the thorns of the *tōdai-gusa* (spurge/castor-oil plant, *Euphorbia* or similar thorny plant)."

*Tōhoko-ei* (Spear Ray) — [illustration]

**Kujūkuri — *Sanbonshiri* (Three-tailed Ray)**
Its color is red and most beautiful. It has three tails. The skin is rough near the head.
Toward the tips [of the body], at about one *sun* (approx. 3 cm), there are needle-like spines, and the color there is pale.
The back has much sandpaper-like texture, and the flesh is somewhat firm. Both tips of the "sleeves" (pectoral fins) also measure about one *sun*, bear needle-like spines, and are pale in color.

According to *Wakan Sansai Zue* (三才図会, "Illustrated Encyclopedia of the Three Powers"): "The Japanese name of the *Kaiyō-gyo* (sea kite fish, i.e., ray) is *Komeebi* (*Ko-me-e-bi*). It has saw-toothed spines at the base of its tail and can swing them left and right to sting people. When one wishes to catch it, one should grasp the sunken hollow beside its eye. If one fails to do so, the wound from the sting will be greatly painful, and in severe cases may even cause death."

[Note:] The form of the ray (*ei*) is as described above. Large specimens have skin with a rough, shark-like texture (*samehada*), which can be used to decorate sword scabbards. Its mouth is set very far underneath; large specimens exceed one *jō* (approx. 3 meters) in length. The liver can be rendered to extract lamp oil.

*Aka-ei* (Red Ray / Common Stingray, *Dasyatis akajei*): This is the true ray. Its flesh is red. According to popular tradition: "When boiled and eaten, it stops diarrhea and dysentery. Its gallbladder treats night blindness (*jakumoku*, lit. 'sparrow-eye') in children. Tried many times with effect. Though not yet recorded in *honzō* (materia medica) texts, it is fine as long as one does not eat too much."

*Yō-fun* (Kite Ray / Eagle Ray, *Myliobatidae*): Its snout is pointed and its color is black. It has fleshy wings (pectoral fins), and its shape closely resembles that of a kite (*tobi*) or hawk (*hai*, a type of raptor), and so it is named *Tobi-ei* (Kite Ray). Its original name is *Kaiyō-gyo*, and this name likely derives from the same source. Its flesh is tender and the flavor is excellent. *(Continued on next page.)*
