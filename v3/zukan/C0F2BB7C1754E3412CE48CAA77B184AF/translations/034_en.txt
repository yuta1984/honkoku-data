Line-caught sawara (Spanish mackerel): looks fine when caught, but deteriorates quickly upon exposure to sunlight.
Net-caught sawara: keeps for one day, until the following day.

Sawara from Geishū (Aki Province, present-day Hiroshima area): It is an offshore fish that forms large schools near the surface. It has fixed grounds from winter through spring and is caught by net. Large specimens reach three shaku (approx. 90 cm).

【Quotation from Wakan Sansai Zue (Illustrated Encyclopedia of Japan, China, and the World)】
Makō (Chinese: mǎjiāo; Japanese: sawara): The common name is "sawara." The smaller fish is called "seisengyo" (blue arrow fish) in Chinese, and commonly "sagoshi" in Japanese.
Upon examination: the makō fish has a pointed head, large eyes, hard gill covers, no scales, and a blue color, with blue-spotted round markings on the back (some individuals have no back markings). The belly is white, the fin spines are hard, the tail is forked, with spines at the tail tips; the dorsal finlets resemble the teeth of a large saw. Large specimens are about three shaku (approx. 90 cm). Because they appear most abundantly in spring months (春月), the character "鰆" (combining fish and spring) is used colloquially. Due to its narrow, elongated shape, it is also called "sarabara" (narrow-bellied) or "sagoshi" (narrow-waisted). Smaller specimens are about one shaku (approx. 30 cm), particularly blue in color, with white flesh [annotation: sweet in flavor, warming in nature], rich in fat, with a thick and delicious taste. Whether prepared as sashimi (raw), grilled, simmered, or salt-cured, it makes an excellent dish.

Karasumi (salted, dried roe): This is made from the ovaries (roe sac) of the makō. The shape of the roe mass resembles the pod of the sword bean (nata-mame), only larger. When dried, it turns brown and slightly resembles karasumi (the famous product made from mullet roe), hence the name. It is produced in large quantities in Tosa (present-day Kōchi), Awa (present-day Tokushima), and Sanuki (present-day Kagawa). The flavor [annotation: sweet with a slight astringency] is good, but it does not compare to karasumi made from mullet roe.
