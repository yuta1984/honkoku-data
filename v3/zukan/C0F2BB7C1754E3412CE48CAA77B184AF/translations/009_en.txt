**Right Folio (with illustration)**

The Tonbi-ei (Kite Ray) has a distinct head shaped like a ladle (*shakushi*), thick in form. The mouth is located on the underside. Its color is *tobi-iro* (kite-brown, a dark reddish-brown).
In overall form it is circular, and the head closely resembles that of a toad (*hikigaeru*/*sen*). The back is dark bluish-black, the belly is white, and the fins extend along toward the tail. Its flavor is considered low quality, and its price is cheap.

**Left Folio (with illustration)**

In Kujūkuri (Chiba), it is called *Tonbi-ei*, and alternatively *Takamaru*.
"*Hato-ebi*" is another name for *Tobi-ebi* (Flying Ray).
The *Tobi-ei* in Geishū (Aki Province, modern Hiroshima) can be caught at sizes of up to about five shaku (approx. 150 cm).
In *Buppin* (物品, "Material Goods"), it is recorded under the names *Hatsu-ei*, *Tonbi-ei*, *Tobi-ei*, and *Tori-ei*.
According to *Ise no Tadashi*: "The flesh is thick and somewhat firmer than that of the *Mae-ebi*."
A fish merchant says: "The Tonbi does not exceed about three shaku (approx. 90 cm) even at its largest."
According to *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草, "Japanese Materia Medica"): "The *Tobi-ei* is mouse-grey in color and its head is shaped like that of a turtle."
