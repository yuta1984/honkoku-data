　Kawahagi (filefish; lit. "skin-peeler")
　People of Buzen province corruptly call it
　"Kofuhagi"

**Middle section:**
　　*Aigo* — Lives along rocky shores. Also called *Isokusare* ("shore-rotting"). Body is flat, coloration blackish-yellow.
　　The Aigo resembles the *gichi* (surgeonfish) in form, with an elongated body, shaped like a *tanago* (bitterling), and a forked tail. The large ones —
People of Usami in Izu province
call it "*Kittsupa*." The body is round, the mouth small, and the fins long.
It has no scales; the skin is tough and rough. The skin is peeled off and discarded. The color is black, and the flesh is light. Sick people eat it.
There is a spine on the jaw.
　　　*Makai* — the name used in Etchū province

**Lower section:**
In Geishū (Hiroshima domain), it is called "*Aiko*" or simply "*Ai*."
It lives among seaweed and does not venture elsewhere. It always eats seaweed and nothing else. The flesh is tough, has an unpleasant odor, and is not tasty. It bears young in spring. It is caught with a fine fishing line.

**Left folio (with illustration)**

**Middle section:**
A fish of the ray (*ei*) family.
About six to seven *sun* (approx. 18–21 cm) in size. Bluish in color, eaten as sashimi.
Perhaps a type of flat *aji* (horse mackerel)? The flavor is good.
Local people brush it with sesame oil and eat it salted.

**Lower section:**
*Aiko* — Resembles the *manakatsuo* (pomfret), with a round and elongated body, large eyes.
The scales are extremely fine, as if there are none at all. The intestines are long. The tail resembles that of the *aji* (horse mackerel). Those that live in deeper waters have a bluish-black color; those in shallower waters can be yellowish-red.
