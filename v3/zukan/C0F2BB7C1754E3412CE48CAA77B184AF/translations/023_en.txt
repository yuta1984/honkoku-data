## Right Folio (with illustrations)

### [Upper Section]

**Tsushima — *Tanabōchō***

This fish resembles the *Shimakatsuō* (striped bonito) and appears like a flat wooden lid left to dry in the sun. The body is thin, approximately 1 shaku 4–5 sun (roughly 42–45 cm) in length. The flesh is scant, and the color of the skin resembles that of mackerel (*saba*). There is no pattern on the body; the color is like that of the tail, which tapers gradually toward the end. The flavor is similar to the large horse mackerel (*ma-aji*), but lacks fat. It is good for making *kamaboko* (fish cake).

**Manakatsuō (*Pampus argenteus* / silver pomfret)**

The whole body comes up (when caught) covered in a silvery sheen [perhaps: it comes up glistening with a silver powder].

### [Middle Section]

**Gira** — Approximately 1 sun (ca. 3 cm) in size. Delicious, but the bones are hard; the back is flat like a rice cracker (*senbei*).

**Gichi (I)** — Resembles the *Gira* but larger. Beautiful in appearance. Makes a "*gichigichi*" sound.

Large *Gichi* are mixed in with horse mackerel and sold at Samukawa.

At **Kasahi Ura** (Kasahi Bay), this fish is called "*Hira-aji*" (flat horse mackerel).

Its shape resembles a camellia leaf (*tsubaki*), with an arrow-shaped tail (*ya-o*). About 2–3 sun (6–9 cm) in length. It has short fins that run around the body. The ventral fins are reddish-yellow. The eyes are large; the head is narrow. Some specimens have mackerel-like patterns on the back, resembling the surface of an old bronze object lightly patinated. The fish has many fine bones, and the mouth is pouch-like. It is silver-white in color, with a single row of markings running along the spine.

- The area around the gills is marked with dark bluish-black spots
- The overall form is close to that of the horse mackerel (*aji*)
- No *sengo* (scutes/hardened lateral-line scales); the lateral line is thin
- The lateral line begins at the jaw (*ago*)
- Curves in an arc
- And runs to the tail
- Also known as "*Gindabira*"
- It is said to make a "*gichigichi*" sound when rubbed
- When caught in large quantities, oil is extracted from it for the first time (i.e., it is used as a source of oil)

---

### [Lower Section]

**Kigiū of Geshū (Aki Province, present-day Hiroshima area)** — A fish of the open sea, abundant in autumn. Its flavor is exceptionally fine. It has no scales; the bones are hard and fine. It is a small fish that lives at the base of submerged tree roots, and is caught in nets.

**The *Gichi* of Izu** has soft bones and is delicious — it is said this is because of the rough seas there.

In **Numazu and Fuchū**, the *Gichi* (*Gite*) is called "*Jintahira*."

**Hirogi** — Tosa Province (Tōshū)

**Giji**, **Hira-aji**, **Seregonashi** — Caught by beach seine (*jibikinami*) in the fifth and sixth months.

**Kohada** (gizzard shad) — Found in the same area, residing in the shallows where the water reaches standing-person depth.

## Left Folio (with illustrations)

### [Upper Section]

**Kagami-dai (Mirror bream)** — Also known as *Kiriko-dai*. The body is laterally flattened (*hira*), with extremely long dorsal and ventral fins.

**Kiriko-dai** (found at Kujūkuri) — Rarely encountered; occasionally enters beach seine nets. Its color shines like the light of the *Tachiuo* (largehead hairtail/swordfish), and powder-like scales fall off easily. The shape is flatly rounded, like the *Manakatsuō*. The first (dorsal) fin is extremely long — as much as 1 shaku (ca. 30 cm). Overall length is about 5 sun (ca. 15 cm). No scales.

**Quoted from the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会) [Annotation #1]:**

"*Ubashō-no-uo* (嫗背魚 — 'Old Woman's Back Fish'), commonly called *Ubose*. This fish is so named because its humped shape roughly resembles the bent back of an elderly woman; it is thus popularly called *ubashō-no-uo*, and further corrupted in pronunciation to *Ubose*.

Examining this: The form of *Ubose* resembles *Manakatsuō* (*Pampus* / Chinese silver pomfret) but is smaller. Above the head there is a protrusion continuing along the spine, forming a hump or wart-like ridge. The scales are extremely fine. In color it is white and luminous, like mica (*unmo*). It appears in the ninth and tenth months in the provinces of Settsu, Izumi, Kii, and Harima. [Size ranges from] 2–3 sun to 5–6 sun. It is delicious when grilled, and also excellent made into *sushi* (*nare-zushi*)."
