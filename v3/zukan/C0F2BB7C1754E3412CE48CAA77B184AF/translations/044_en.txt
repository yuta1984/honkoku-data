## Right Folio

**[Illustration present]**

(From *Buppin Shikryaku* [物品識略]) It resembles the *hokotachi no uo* (a type of cutlassfish/ribbonfish) but is broader; the tail is thin and pointed, the body is pale black, and the dorsal fin is red.

The fish known as *Nawakarami* in Echigo Province is rarely caught in hand-drawn nets (*tekuri-ami*). Its form is five to six *shaku* in length, six to seven *sun* in girth, with a long beak resembling that of a bird. It has one or two fins on its back; the tail resembles that of a mackerel (*saba*), is pale reddish in color, and has scales.

According to the Kurimoto Bunko (Kurimoto Collection), this is a strange fish found in the sea off Hamada in Iwami Province (石州). Ono Ranzan's theory identifies it as "a *yakara* with a short beak," though the accuracy of this is as yet unknown, and for now it is illustrated here, awaiting the learned judgment of a knowledgeable gentleman at some future date.
This illustration came from Lord Hotta.
Copied on the twenty-fourth day of *mōka* (early summer, the fourth month) of the year *Bunka Teichū* (文化丁丑, 1817).

**Upper Section**

《Next illustration》

○ Kurimoto Tanshū writes: On the twenty-eighth day of the fifth month of the twelfth year of Kansei, *kōshin* (1800), this fish was caught in the waters of Aneko-no-Nada off Karatsu in Hizen Province. The total body length measured five *shaku* and two *sun* by carpenter's square (*kanejaku*), approximately two *sun* in width, and approximately three *bu* in thickness.

This was borrowed from the Mankōtei Master (万香亭主人) on the eighteenth day of *chūtō* (mid-winter, eleventh month) of Tenpō 2 (1831).

My own examination (Tanshū): Might this not be of the same species as the strange fish from Cape Hinomisaki in Unshū (Izumo Province) shown in the following illustration? Their forms closely resemble one another, and the dates are the same as well. The only differences lie in the place of capture and the measurements. With a creature of such an unusual appearance, errors in observation are possible, and it is likely that this is the same animal — the discrepancies in provenance and measurements may be due to lapses of memory, causing deviations from the original record. It is impossible to say which account is correct. I have recorded it here for the purpose of comparison (*kōgō*), and have copied the illustration as it is. This is merely a modest attempt to provide material for future scholarly verification.

Copied by Tanshū on the nineteenth day — the day of the winter solstice — of *chūtō*, Tenpō 2, *kanoto-u* (1831).
