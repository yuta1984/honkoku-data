## Right Page (Description of Hatahata / Sailfin Sandfish)

### Upper Section

The hatahata of Dewa Province has a head resembling that of an iwashi (sardine), with a silver-luminous body, no scales, and thin skin, like the tachinouo (largehead hairtail / beltfish). Its belly contains abundant roe (ko/hara-ko). It ranges in size from five or six sun to one shaku. Its flavor is not particularly fine (not considered delicious).

It is said that in former times this fish dwelt near Mito, but followed the lord of Akita to Dewa Province.

The hatahata resembles the konoshiro (dotted gizzard shad), with a tapered tail end, a white and lustrous color, and a dark back. Specimens caught in the eleventh and twelfth months measure about three or four sun. The taste is mild and it has a toxic quality — eating it can cause scabies or skin rashes (kaisen).

The hatahata is caught in the sea of Akita in Usha Province (羽州). It is said that this fish first appears on the day of the Ox (ushi no hi) after the winter solstice.

## Left Page (Description of Managatsuo / Silver Pomfret)

### Upper Section

In Geshū (Aki Province / present-day Hiroshima area), the managatsuo is caught by net. It is available throughout all four seasons, but is most plentiful in the ninth and tenth months. Large specimens measure about one shaku; they move through the water in schools and are also found in muddy areas.

The managatsuo is also fished in Bizen Province. The fishing season runs from the end of the fifth month through the sixth month.

### Lower Section

The *Sansai Zue* (Illustrated Encyclopaedia of Heaven, Earth and Man) states: "The 鯧 (managatsuo / silver pomfret): short-necked, with a flat body resembling the fang (bream) but flattened. The scales are fine and the color is white."

The Japanese vernacular name is "Manakatuwo" (末奈加豆乎).

Upon examination, the form of the 鯧 is as described above. It is most abundant in Settsu, Izumi, Kii, and Harima Provinces, and is not found in the seas to the northeast. It grows to over one shaku in length. It is white with a tinge of blue, and is most excellent when made into dried fish (kannabi/uomaki). However, it carries a faint blue-green odor; grilling it is also fine. It can also be made into sushi (narezushi) or pickled in sake lees. The one preparation not recommended is boiling. Its scales are so fine and white that it appears almost as if it has none.
