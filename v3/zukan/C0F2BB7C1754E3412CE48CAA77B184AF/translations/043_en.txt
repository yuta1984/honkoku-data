## Right Folio

**Middle Section** (Quotation from *Wakan Sansai Zue* [Illustrated Japanese-Chinese Encyclopedia]):
Commonly called *yakara*.
Upon examination, the *kan-gyo* (簳魚, "arrow-shaft fish") resembles in form the sayori (halfbeak, 鱵) but is longer and more cylindrical, named after the shaft of an arrow (矢幹, *yagara*) due to its shape. The beak is long, symmetrical above and below. The scales are fine, patterned, and slightly reddish. The tail is forked, and between the two forks hangs a single thread-like streak of red. The flesh is white, sweet, and of a warming nature (*kan-on*), though not especially delicious. It is found in the Tōkai region, Suruga Province, and Izu Province.
It is said that those who suffer from hiccups or obstructions of the esophagus will be cured if they eat and drink using its beak, but this is often exaggerated and cannot be taken as certain.

**Lower Section**
Smaller *yakara* inhabit areas where the tidal current is at its strongest.
In Geishū (Aki Province, modern Hiroshima Prefecture), *yakara* is eaten as sashimi (raw fish).
