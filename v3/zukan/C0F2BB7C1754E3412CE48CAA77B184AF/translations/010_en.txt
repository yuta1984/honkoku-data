**Upper Section**
*Gin-ei* (Silver Ray) 《Kujūkuri》
Its color is darker than that of the *Aka-ei* (Red Ray/Reddish Stingray).
It resembles the *Aka-ei* but is not as tasty.
Note ② from *Buppin* (物品): The *Kagami-ei* (Mirror Ray) is round in shape; large specimens reach about five shaku (approx. 150 cm).

**Lower Section**
The *Dōran* is large and dark in color. In Sendai it is called *Renten* or *Karakai*, and it has recently come to inhabit (this region as well).
*Kashi-ebi* refers to a type of *hie* (ray), specifically the rounded variety. It resembles the *Aka-ebi* in shape, but is pale whitish in color with a certain sheen; even when boiled, the color remains the same. The flavor is mild, with a distinctive, slightly sharp taste.

　　*Nansan-shi* (南産志) states: "The *kōgyo* (ray) and the black ray (*kurō*) are shaped like a round fan (*uchiwa*).
　　Because its head resembles that of a swallow (*tsubame*), it is named the Swallow Ray (*enkō*)."

**Upper Section**
*Igyo-zusan* (異魚図賛) states: "The *Saisei-gyo* (Star-bearing Fish) has a star-like pattern on its back, with points gleaming white. It is named after this characteristic." (Note ①)
*Rinkai Suido-ki* (臨海水土記) states: "The *Saisei-gyo* is shaped like the *Tobi-ei* (Kite Ray); on its back are two white markings the size of a finger, and it is named after these."
*Yamato Honzō* (大和本草) states: [text appears incomplete]
Note ② from *Buppin*: The *Zanki-ei* (*Sanki-ei*) and *Sufuta-ei* are pale blackish in color, with many short spines on the tail.
According to a fish merchant: "The *Kangi* (*Gangi-ei*) has many varieties. It tastes best in the winter months. It is good when skinned, boiled, and made into a cold aspic (*nikogori*)."

The *Kangi-karakai* (also known simply as *Kangi*) is called *Reden* in Mito (Ibaraki). In summer it dwells around Chōshi (Chiba). The skin of its back is brown, resembling that of the stone flounder (*Ishi-garei*), but with raised spines. Its flavor is light (mild).
The *Kangi* has scales resembling the wood or thorns of a *sansho* (Japanese pepper) tree, is pale blackish in color, and white on the belly. Its flesh is slightly thin and flat. In the fifth and sixth months it is used for *namasu* (vinegared dish); it is not eaten boiled. It is also called *Gangi-ebi*. Its tail is single but branches out and may have more than twenty divisions. The *Karakai* is thin on the inside, with a few spines on the fins. The back is close to mouse-gray in color. The *Karakai*'s color is the same as the *Aka-ei*.
The specimen from Tajiri in Hitachi (Ibaraki Prefecture): in terms of both flavor and size, it is second only to the *Takabu* (another species of ray). It is suitable for eating raw.
