**Right Folio (with illustration)**

**Upper Section**
*Gin-ei* (Silver Ray) — [from] Kujūkuri
Its color is darker than that of the *Aka-ei* (Red Ray/Common Stingray).
It resembles the *Aka-ei* but is not attractive in appearance.
In *Buppin* (物品, "Material Goods"), it is recorded as *Kagami-ei* (Mirror Ray); its shape is round, and the largest specimens reach about five *shaku* (approx. 150 cm).

**Lower Section**
*Dōran*: Large in size, with a darkish color. In Sendai it is called *Renten*. "*Karakahi*" (*Karakarei*, a type of flatfish-like ray) has begun appearing in this area recently.
*Kashi-ebi* (Ray): The rounded variety is called by this name. It resembles the *Aka-ei*, with a pale whitish color; its flesh has texture, and even when boiled, the color remains the same. The flavor is mild, and it has one spine.

　　According to *Nansan-shi* (南産志, "Records of Southern Produce"): "The *Kōgyo* (ray) and the *Kurokō* (black ray) are shaped like a round fan (*uchiwa*). Because their heads resemble a swallow, they are named *Tsubame-kō* (Swallow Ray)."

**Left Folio (with illustration)**

**Upper Section**
According to *Igyo Zusan* (異魚図賛, "Illustrated Praises of Strange Fish"): "The *Taisei-gyo* (Star-bearing Fish) is named for the star-like patterns on its back, which glisten white (*gyōreki*) in dotted formation."
According to *Rinkai Suido-ki* (臨海水土記, "Records of Coastal Waters and Land"): "The *Taisei-gyo* is shaped like the *Tobi-ei* (Kite Ray); on the upper part of its back there are two white ornament-like markings (*tō*), each about the size of a finger, from which it takes its name."
According to *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草):
In *Buppin*, it is recorded as *Zanki-ei* and *Sufuta-ei*: pale black in color, with many short spines along the tail.
A fish merchant says: "*Kangi* (Gangei/Skate) comes in many varieties. Its flavor is best in the winter months. It is good to peel off the skin, boil it, and serve it chilled."

*Kangi-karakahi*: Also known as *Kangi*. In Mito it is called *Reden*. In summer it inhabits Chōshi (Chiba). The skin on its back is brown, resembling *Ishi-garei* (stone flounder) in appearance, with prominent spines. The flavor is light (mild).
*Kangi*: Its scales resemble those of the prickly ash (*sansho*) tree. Its color is pale dark, belly white. The flesh is somewhat thin and flat. In the fifth and sixth months it is eaten as *namasu* (sliced raw fish dressed with vinegar); it is not eaten boiled. Also called *Gangi-ei*. The tail is single but branches at the tip into more than twenty forks.
*Karakahi*: The inside of the body is thin. There are a few spines on the fins. The back is close to mouse-grey in color.
*Karakahi*: Its color is the same as the *Aka-ei*.
From Tajiri in Hitachi Province (modern Ibaraki): In both flavor and size, it ranks second to *Takabu*. It can be eaten raw.
