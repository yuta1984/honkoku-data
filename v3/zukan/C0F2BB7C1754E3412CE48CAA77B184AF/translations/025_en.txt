## Right Page (Description of Saba / Mackerel)

### Upper Section

Upon examination, the form and color of the saba (mackerel) largely accord with the descriptions in the Chinese materia medica texts (honzō). However, while it is abundant in northern and western seas, we have not heard of it being found in lakes. Nor have we seen one whose occipital (pillow) bone could be fashioned into a vessel. Its form resembles the koi (carp), with exceedingly fine scales; large specimens reach one shaku four or five sun in length. The back is a true blue, with faint patches of dark bluish-black, and some bear markings resembling wound rope. Near the tail, paired fins stand opposite each other, bearing spiny protrusions. The flesh [is sweet with a faint sourness], spoils easily, and eating fish left overnight can intoxicate a person. However, cooking fresh fish with vinegar is excellent. Off the coast of Noto, in the fourth month, tens of thousands drift on the waves, and one can obtain them without rod or net. They are made into dried preparations (like abalone-style dried fish) and shipped to various provinces, appreciated by people of all classes, and used for Bon Festival celebrations. Split open along the backbone, two pieces are made into one layer, called "hitosashi." Those of reddish-purple color are considered finest; coating them with sardine oil and drying improves their color. The product of Noto is the best, followed by that of Sado and Etchū.

### Lower Section

In Satsuma Province, large mackerel are called "Gongen-saba" (Gongen mackerel).
"Shiyaba" refers to the seaweed called mososhiokusa.

[Referenced in] the *Sansai Zue* [encyclopaedia]; the Japanese name is "Aosaba" (blue mackerel). The *Shokukyō* (Classic of Food) states: "The saba has a pointed mouth and a blue-grey back." The explanation in the *Honzō* (materia medica) is omitted here.
The *Gozatsuzu* (Five Miscellanies) states: "Eating mackerel cures madness; eating halfbeak (sayori) stops arrogance; eating other fish does not intoxicate; eating mermaid flesh cures foolishness; eating the golden oriole stops jealousy; eating the wonggū bird prevents hunger." Such sayings have existed since ancient times, but their truth has not been confirmed.

## Left Page (Description of Kagami-uo / Mirror Fish)

### Upper Section

Mirror fish (Kagami-uo): This fish has no scales; its back is blue-green, with pale dark spots. The fin below the eye is transparent like white glass; the fin below the gills is slender and cord-like, blue-green in color. It measures approximately five or six sun in length.

### Middle Section

Mirror fish — There is also something called "Kagami-dai (Mirror bream)," but it is a different creature. There are several species called "Kagami fish." This fish is extremely flat like a board and has no scales. Its back is blue-green with pale dark spots. The fin below the eye is extremely transparent, as beautiful as white glass. The fins below the belly are fine and soft, like five cords, of a blue-green color. This fish is translucent throughout its body, like a polished mirror — hence it acquired this name. Large specimens are about five or six sun [approximately 15–18 cm] and are silver in color, with fine bones visible on the exterior. Its form resembles a knife used for cutting tobacco. For this reason, the fishermen of Tama-no-ura call it "Tabako-bōchō" (tobacco knife), as the author has heard. The body is flat and thin with little flesh, making it difficult to use as food.
