**[Right folio — continued from previous page: 食茱萸, *Zanthoxylum ailanthoides*]**

This tree is found abundantly in warm regions throughout the country, including Bōshū (Awa Province, present-day Chiba) and Zushū (Izu Province, present-day Shizuoka). It grows to a height of about two *jō* (approx. 6 meters), with a trunk circumference of two to three *shaku* (approx. 60–90 cm). The branches, trunk, and leaf stems are all densely covered with thorns. The leaves resemble those of *Phellodendron amurense* (Japanese cork tree) but are finer, and are of a violet-brown (*kinshu*) color. In autumn, flowers bloom at the tips of the branches, forming clusters of small, pale yellow blossoms that develop into fruit. The fruit resembles that of *Zanthoxylum bungeanum* (Chinese pepper, *shinjō*). When ripe, the fruit turns red; its flavor resembles that of *shinjō* but is inferior. Because crows gather to eat the berries, the plant is known as *karasu-no-sanshō* ("crow's sanshō pepper").

**塩麩子 (*Enfu-shi*; *Rhus javanica* / *Rhus chinensis*, Chinese sumac)**

*Vernacular names:*
- *Nurute* [Nōshū (Mino Province, present-day Gifu)]
- *Nurite* [Edo; Sashū (Sado Province, present-day Niigata)]
- *Katsunoki* [Ōshū (Mutsu Province, present-day Tōhoku region)]
- *Fushinoki*
- *Shōgunboku* ("Commander's tree")
- *Saiwainoki* ("Auspicious tree") [Kōshū (Kai Province, present-day Yamanashi)]
- *Futenoki* [Bishū (Owari Province, present-day Aichi)]
- *Fubokushi* (浮木子) [cited in *Waitai Biyao* (外台秘要)]
- *Shu* (主) [cited in *Zhenglei Bencao* (証類本草)]

**[Left folio]**

- *Uen* (鳥塩) [cited in *Suisho Kō* (萃処考)]
- *Ensakushi* (塩醋子) [cited in *Guangdong Xinyu* (広東新語)] *(the above are names for the fruit)*
- *Enfuboku* (塩膚木) [cited in *Zhengzi Tong* (正字通)] *(name for the tree)*

This tree is found abundantly in the hills and fields throughout the country. It grows to over one *jō* (approx. 3 meters) in height. The leaves are arranged in opposite pairs of nine or eleven leaflets per stem, resembling the leaves of the lacquer tree (*Rhus verniciflua*). The leaf stems have wing-like projections (rachis wings) shaped like arrow feathers. In summer, a panicle grows from the tips of the branches, drooping about one *shaku* (approx. 30 cm) and bearing numerous small white flowers clustered together. Afterward, flat fruits resembling those of the lacquer tree develop, and the outer skin produces a white, salt-like coating. The taste is salty — this is known as *moku-en* ("wood salt"). The seed kernels are also flat and smaller than lacquer tree seeds. These are the *enfu-shi* (塩麩子, "salt bran seeds").

In autumn, white bag-like growths appear on the leaf stems. Their shape varies and resembles small deer antlers; they are hollow inside and hard. These are insect galls, and are called *goba-ishi* (五倍子; Chinese galls). In common parlance they are called *kifushi*, and when ground into a powder, the product is called *fushi-no-ko* ("gall powder"). It is used by women for tooth-blackening (*ohaguro*). A full entry on this subject can be found under the "Insects" section. In autumn, the tree's leaves turn red before falling. The name *nurite* is also rendered as *momishi*.
