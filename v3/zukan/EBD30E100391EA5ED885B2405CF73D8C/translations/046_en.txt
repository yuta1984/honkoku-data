*Kansho* (sugarcane)　*Satōkibi*　*Satōnoki*　*Okisatō*
　　　　　*Shosha* 《Annotation: From the commentary on *Wen Xuan* (文選)》　*Kansha* 《Annotation: From *Runan Pushi* (汝南圃史)》　*Shosha* 《Annotation: From *Tong Ya* (通雅)》　*Kansho* (sweet potato — a homonymous name) 《Annotation: A different plant shares this name》
　　　　　*Kansho* 《Annotation: Both from the same source above》　*Kansho* 《Annotation: From *Zhengzi Tong* (正字通)》　*Alikont* 《Annotation: Latin》
　　　　　*Sattsukarihera* 《Annotation: Dutch》

　According to *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草), sugarcane did not exist in Japan in ancient times, and therefore it is not recorded in such old texts as the *Engishiki*, the *Ryō no Gige*, or the *Wamyō Ruijushō*. It is said to be listed in later works such as the *Setsuyōshū* and the *Kagakushū*. Since this plant is originally native to warm regions, it is vulnerable to cold and difficult to cultivate in cold areas. Today it is widely cultivated in Shikoku, Kyūshū, Kishū (Kii Province), Sunshū (Suruga Province), and Zushū (Izu Province). Because the plant does not ordinarily produce flowers or fruit, one should, during October, select stalks that are not too thin and are well ripened, and bury them in a south-facing (sunny) location. In March, dig them up, cut them into sections each retaining a node at both ends, and plant them in ridged furrows in the field. Moisture causes buds to sprout from the nodes. Thin and weak buds are removed, while robust ones are allowed to grow. The seedling resembles *hagi* (bush clover) or *morokoshi* (sorghum), with leaves that are somewhat narrow.

　As the seedlings grow, they reach a height of more than one *jō* (approximately three meters or more). The leaves are like those of *morokoshi* (sorghum) and grow abundantly. The stalk is large and resembles bamboo. The outer skin is hard, while the inner pith is soft, similar to sorghum. The nodes are closely spaced and gradually become more widely spaced toward the tip. In October, stalks that are well ripened are cut and placed in a press (*rokuro*) to extract the juice, which is then boiled down to produce sugar.

*Konronsha* (崑崙蔗) 《Annotation: From *Jijie* (集解)》　Purple sugarcane (*Murasakisatō*)

　According to the account of Master Tamura, this variety is widely cultivated in Satsuma Province (Satsushū) and is rarely seen elsewhere. It is said that while it can be used to make brown sugar (*kurozatō*), it is difficult to produce white sugar from it. In form it resembles sorghum, the stalk is thick and tinged with red, and the leaves are also similar to those of sorghum.
