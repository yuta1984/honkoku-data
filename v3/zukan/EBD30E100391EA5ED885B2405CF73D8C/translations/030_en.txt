**Tiánguā (Sweet Melon / Oriental Melon)**
Japanese names: *hosochi* 《Wamyō-shō》; *makuwa-uri* 《Kyoto and Edo》; *ama-uri* [sweet melon]; *achi-uri* 《Unshū / Kyushu》; *te-uri* 《Ōshū / Sendai》; *chinmyō* 《Sado Island》
Korean: *Zamuoi*; Dutch: *Meloensijn* [Meloen]; *Toen* (菟延) 《cited from Honzō Wamyō and Shinsen Shokuji-hō》
[Additional Chinese names:] Dōnglíng Chúwèi; Zhūlíng Xiānshí 《both from the same source above》; Xiāngguā 《Shèngjīng Tōngzhì》;
Niǎnqīngguā 《Níngbō Fǔzhì》; Huáng Ráng Guā 《Mínshū》; Jīn'é Pǐ 《Shíwù Běncǎo》;
Xiāng Tiánguā 《under the heading "Guā Dì" in Li Shizhen's *Explanations of Names*》

According to the *Yamato Honzō*, fine specimens are produced in various places including Kyoto, Edo, Sakai in Senshu (Izumi Province), and Nōshū (Mino Province). It is said that because particularly fine melons came from Makuwa Village in Nōshū, good melons came to be called "*makuwa*." In Edo,

**[Left page]**

the finest specimens are those grown in Naruko Village in Yotsuya. The variety called "*hon-yamato*" measures over three *sun* (approx. 9–10 cm) in length and three to four *sun* (approx. 9–12 cm) in circumference. When ripe, it turns golden yellow with fine silk-like streaks running along its surface. The flesh is green. When eaten, the fragrance and flavour are excellent — exceedingly sweet — and it is the same shape as those produced in Makuwa Village in Nōshū. In Chinese sources, the *Runan Pǔshǐ* refers to this as "Suàntóng Guā" (蒜瞳瓜). In general, the sweet melon (甜瓜) type is distinct from other melons: its leaves are about the size of a palm, with shallow serrated edges, and both the upper and lower surfaces are covered with fine hairs; the vines are also hairy. The flowers are five-petaled and yellow, resembling those of the *magawake* (醤瓜 / pickling melon). Regarding the soil for cultivating this melon: growing it in the black earth of hills and uplands yields a superior product, while growing it in low, damp sandy ground yields an inferior one. In the worst cases, the flavour becomes like that of a pickling melon, and the fruit cannot be eaten without being salt-preserved. The "Yīnguā" (陰瓜) mentioned in the *Jíjiě* (Collected Commentaries) section is in fact the *nánguā* (南瓜, pumpkin/squash), which is what is called *bōbura* or *henitōka* in Japanese.
