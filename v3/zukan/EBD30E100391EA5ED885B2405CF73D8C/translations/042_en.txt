　　　Eiiiku (*Vitis ficifolia* / wild grape)
　　　　*Inu-ebi*
　　　*Ebi-tsuru*　　*Kanebu*
　　　　　*Kuro-budō* (black grape)

【Left Page】

　　　　*Tō* 《Annotation: Standard character | Common》　*Ōiku* 《Annotation: Common | Elegant》

　This plant grows wild throughout mountainous and open fields in various regions. Its climbing vines resemble those of grapes (*budō*), and the leaves are likewise similar, being either five-pointed or three-pointed. The undersides of the leaves are covered with yellowish-brown hairs. In summer, it blooms in panicles and bears fruit. The fruit resembles grapes but is somewhat smaller; when ripe it turns black and tastes sour. In Ōshū (the Tōhoku region), the fruit is used to make wine (*budōshu*). The pith of the vine produces insects, which are called "*kama-ebi* insects." These are used as food for peacocks. A separate variety found on Mount Nikkō in Shimotsuke Province (Yashū) and in the Kiso mountains of Shinshū has vines like those of grapes, with leaves resembling those of the luffa (*hechima*), thick in texture and covered with yellowish-red hairs on the underside. It blooms in clusters of small flowers and bears fruit resembling *eiiiku*, but of a purple-black color.
