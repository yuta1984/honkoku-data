**Left folio:**

**Names:** *Mezamashikusa*, *Chanoки* (tea plant); *Sōjinboku* (艸人木; Ranzan's note: a hidden name used in the way of tea); *Tea* (Latin); *Thee* (Dutch); *Yōsaku* (楊責; cited in *Honzō Wamyō* from *Kenmeien*); *Kansō* (甘草; from *Seijitsū*); *Shigyokuketsu* (紫玉缺; same source); *Sōkwai* (草魁; from *Meibutsu Hōgen*); *Tatsusō* (龍草; same source); *Kushū* (苦茱; from *Tsūga*); *Fuyakō* (不夜侯; from *Tenseki Benran*); *Rakusōtō* (酪蒼頭; from *Gunpōfu*); *Seijinshu* (清人樹; same source).

Tea should be cultivated in mountain soils of yellow-black color; it is difficult to grow in low-lying paddy fields. The leaves resemble those of the *sasanqua* (*Camellia sasanqua*), while the flowers resemble white plum blossoms — thick, large, and fragrant. The fruit also resembles that of the sasanqua.

Regarding the distinction between *cha* (茶, tea picked early) and *mei* (茗, tea picked late): in the commentary of the *Shakumei* (釈名), citing the theory of Guo Pu (郭璞), it states: "That which is picked early is called *cha*; that which is picked late is called *mei*." In the Yamashiro Province (Kyoto) area of Uji and elsewhere, tea picked after twenty-one days following the onset of the third-month seasonal node is called *hatsumukashi* (初昔, "first mukashi"), and tea picked after that is called *nochimukashi* (後昔, "later mukashi"). The character 昔 (*mukashi*) can be decomposed into the components meaning "twenty days" (廿) and "one day" (一日), which together were fixed into a single character. What is named *hatsumukashi* corresponds to *cha*, and everything from *nochimukashi* onward corresponds to *mei*. Among tea-growing regions, Uji is regarded as the finest. Other noted regions include Yoshino in Yamato Province, Goshō in Ōmi Province, Tanba, Kumano in Kishū, Suruga Province, Iyo Province, Hyūga, Yanagawa in Chikugo, and various other provinces throughout the country. As the origins of tea cultivation in Japan and its methods of production are described in detail for each region elsewhere, they are omitted here.
