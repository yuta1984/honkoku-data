**醎草 (*Kansō*) [Supplementary entry]**

*Vernacular names:*
- *Hachijōsō* [cited in *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草)]
- *Ashitaba* [local dialect of Hachijō Island]

This plant grows abundantly on Hachijō Island in Zushū (Izu Province), and is also found in great numbers along the coastlines of Bōshū (Awa Province) and Zushū. The leaves resemble those of *udo* (*Aralia cordata*) and are deep green in color. When the stem is cut, a yellow sap flows out. Plants that have grown for three years reach a height of about two *shaku* (approx. 60 cm). Small white flowers bloom in umbel formations resembling those of *byakushi* (*Angelica dahurica*). They subsequently bear fruit with longitudinal ridges, approximately the size of a grain of barley. On Hachijō Island, this plant has long served as a regular dietary supplement alongside rice.

---

**A related variety: *Oni-udo* (鬼独活)**

This variety grows along the coastal areas of Kishū (Kii Province, present-day Wakayama and Mie Prefectures). It resembles *Hachijōsō* but does not wither in winter; for this reason, it is called *Tokiwa-no-Hachijōsō* ("Evergreen Hachijō plant") in Edo. Its leaves are thicker and more lustrous than those of *Hachijōsō*. Its flowers and fruit are the same as those of *Hachijōsō*. The root does not die off even over many years. It is said that this plant is poisonous.
