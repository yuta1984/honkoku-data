Entry 38: *Hisui-kai* (翡翠介, Kingfisher Shell) — per *Nansan-shi* — ○ *Sui-bō* (翠蚌, Emerald Mussel) — common name — ○ *Kawa-karasu* (川カラス, River Crow) — per *Kaimono Keimō*

○ *Kaimono Keimō* states: A species approximately one *sun* (approx. 3 cm) in length, of pale green with a slight blackish tint, is called *Kawa-karasu* (River Crow Shell).

○ Sekiju states: The shape is elongated horizontally, resembling the *Kuro-ōen-kai* (Black Mandarin Duck Shell), with the area near the beak (umbo) resembling the *Matsukasa-kai* (Pine Cone Shell). The dorsal surface is black, the lip (ventral margin) is green, with a beautiful luster. The interior is no different from that of the Pearl Shell. Smaller specimens are pale black, with wickerwork-like wrinkles near the beak, and brownish lips. It is largely similar to the *Matsukasa-kai*, differing only in its length. Specimens are approximately one *sun* in size. Many have a whitish, worn appearance near the beak. It must not be confused with the *Matsukasa-kai*. It is a bivalve.

Entry 39: *Matsuba-kai* (松葉介, Pine Needle Shell) — common name — ○ *Kō-sei* (蛤青, Green Clam) — per *Nanzan-shi* — ○ *Hisui* (翡翠, Kingfisher/Jade) — same source — ○ *Sui-kai* (翠介) — ○ *Ao-dōhin* (アヲドウヒン) — Owari Province dialect

○ Sekiju states: The shape is round and swollen, the shell is thin, one end (anterior) flares outward, the other end (posterior) is long, the umbo is straight and level, and the texture is coarse with mixed horizontal striations. From the beak area to the dorsal surface, it is tea-brown in color; from the lip area to the dorsal region it is dark bluish-black with a greenish tint. Lustrous specimens show pale black and dark-brown horizontal bands near the beak, while near the lip the coloring is brownish with dense light-and-dark horizontal bands; some have fine yellow vertical striations. Others have reddish-black horizontal bands of varying shades with a greenish tint, and dense yellowish-black vertical markings. Those with more green coloration are considered the finest quality. Larger specimens are often dark-brown or reddish-brown. All have a smooth, glossy exterior, while the interior is indistinguishable from that of the Pearl Shell. In size, they range from approximately one *sun* to as large as five or six *sun*, though larger specimens are rare. Newly born specimens are pale black with a light greenish tint, and the shell is extremely thin and fragile. These belong to the *Dofū-kai* (土負介) group. This is likely what Li Shizhen refers to as "*gé*" (蛤, clam) in his writings.
