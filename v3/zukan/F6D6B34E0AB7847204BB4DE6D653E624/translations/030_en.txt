Entry 36: Ekaki-kai (Painter's Shell)　[Medicinal name / Notes] ○ Kujira-kai [According to Dōtei] ○ Ekahi [Japanese name] ○ Neshi-kai [According to Gentatsu]
　○ Eboshi-kai [Kanmon Kaifu] ○ Mine no Yuki [Kanmon Kaifu] ○ Setsuya [Nagisa no Nishiki]

○ According to the *Honzō Kibun* (Notes on Materia Medica): This shell grows in lake waters or in coastal marshes. It reaches about one foot (shaku) in length. The outside is black, while the inside shines greenish and appears white. As the shell ages, the outer skin becomes pitted and worn as if eaten by insects, exposing white areas beneath. It is for this reason that it is called "Ekaki-kai" (the Shell That Draws Pictures). It is of inferior quality and not used as food.

○ The *Butsurui Hinshitsu* (Classification of Natural Objects) states: In Japanese it is called Ekahi, also Karasu-kahi (Crow Shell), and also Ekaki-kahi. Upon reflection, the Chinese character 蚌 (*bō*) is a general term for elongated bivalves of the clam type, but the specific shell referred to as 蚌 in the herbals is the Ekahi. Specimens from Lake Biwa can exceed seven or eight *sun* (approximately 21–24 cm) in length.

○ The *Kaimō* (Enlightenment/Illustrated Guide) states: Inside the black outer skin there is white powder; it is thick. The interior is extremely hard, white in color, tinged with a faint blue-purple, and has a lustrous sheen. In older specimens, the black skin becomes mottled and worn away, exposing the white powder beneath in patterns resembling characters carved by insects. Because these patterns appear equally on both shells, it is called Ekahi or Ekaki-kahi.

○ Sekiju states: This refers to large specimens of the Dofu-kai (土負介). The shape differs slightly. One wing (flange) is rounded and short, the other wing is long with a pointed tip. One ear (auricle) has an angular projection. The shell is thick and has the appearance of an eboshi (nobleman's court cap), hence the name "Eboshi." Around the beak (umbo), the black skin is thick and exhibits a bronze-gold or silver luster, sometimes presenting an appearance like flowers. The surface is black with a reddish tinge; some specimens have dull brown or grayish-white mud-rust deposits arranged in horizontal stripes. The texture has coarse horizontal striations mixed throughout, with a marbled pattern and a polished luster. The interior is water-white or water-brown with mottled patterns, with a luster resembling that of the Seiju-kai (生䗯介) or the Abalone (石決明 / *Haliotis*). There is also a variant in which one wing is rounded and the other wing is slightly longer, with a narrow, short tip — compared to the specimens described above, this variety is more rounded in form. It may generally be a blackish variant of the *Hamaguri ao* (蛤青). The shell reaches three to four *sun* (approximately 9–12 cm) or more in size.
