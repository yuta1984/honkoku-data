**[Right folio]**

**Rinshi (醂柿) — Tarunuki ("Barrel-drawn Persimmon")**

In China, this term refers to the removal of astringency by soaking the fruit in various types of water. In Japan, astringent persimmons are placed in a sake barrel or sake vessel, sake is sprinkled over them, and when left thus, the astringency is removed and the flavor becomes sweet.

---

**Shikō (柿餻) — Kaki-kozuki ("Pounded Persimmon Cake")**

In China, glutinous rice (*mochigome*) is used. In Japan, non-glutinous rice (*uruchigome*) or barley is roasted and ground into a coarse powder, and then firm astringent persimmons or ripened persimmons (*jukushi*) are added, pounded, and mixed together to form a cake (*kō*). When made in this way, the astringency is removed and the result is sweet.

---

**[Left folio]**

**Hishi (椑柿) — Minogaki ("Mino Persimmon")**

Among persimmons, this is considered an inferior variety. When planted in mountain fields and wilds, it grows into a large tree. The fruit is small, roughly one *sun* (approximately 3 cm) in length, elongated, and comes to a point at the tip. Even after frost, the astringency is difficult to remove. The fruit is harvested while still green and soaked in water to produce astringent persimmon tannin liquid (*kakishibu*, persimmon lacquer). It can also be made into dried persimmons (*tōshi*).

---

**One variety — Mizuhachiya ("Water Hachiya")**

The shape is elongated, resembling the *minogaki*. It is made into *tarunuki* (alcohol-treated persimmon) or alternatively into dried persimmons (*kanshi*).
