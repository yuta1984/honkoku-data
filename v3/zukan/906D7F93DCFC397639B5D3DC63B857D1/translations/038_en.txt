**Right folio:**
*Shukan* (Vermilion Mandarin — 朱柑) [from the *Honzō Shūkai* / *Bencao Jijie*]
　　*Beni mikan* (Red Mandarin)

　　The skin is yellow-red in color, the flavor is strongly sour, and the fruit is of inferior quality.

**Left folio:**
*Shitōkan* (Lion's Head Mandarin — 獅頭柑) [from the *Honzō Shūkai* / *Bencao Jijie*]
　　*Unshū tachibana* (Citrus of Izumo Province)

　This variety is widely cultivated in Suruga Province. The fruit is large in shape, resembling a yuzu, and somewhat flattened. When ripe, it turns reddish-yellow, and its surface bears green wart-like protuberances resembling the back of a toad. The taste is sweet. The above three varieties [i.e., the *Tōteikan*, *Shukan*, and *Shitōkan*] all share the following characteristics: the leaves are large and similar to those of the bitter orange (*daidai*); there is no small leaflet at the base of the leaf; the branches and trunk bear thorns. The flowers have five petals and are white, and are larger than the flowers of the common mandarin orange.
