*Wō* (黄, "Yellow") [same source as above]　　*Yù-jú* (玉局, "Jade Bureau") [ref: *Hana-goyomi Hyakuei*]　　*Qīng-lù* (青露, "Green Dew") [ref: *Meibutsu Hōgen*]

What is today called *tachibana* is a different plant from what was anciently called *tachibana*. What is currently called *tachibana* in the western provinces (Saigoku)

[Left folio]

is slightly smaller than the *kan* (柑, mandarin). This corresponds to the *hóu-jú* (猴橘, "monkey orange") of the *Bā Mǐn Tōng Zhì* (八閩通志, *Comprehensive Gazetteer of the Eight Min Regions*). The term *jú* (橘) is a general name for the *kōji* (citrus) family. The *jú*-peel (*jú-pí*, 橘皮) and aged peel (*chén-pí*, 陳皮) used in medicine should properly be made from the peel of *kōji*. The distinction between *jú* (橘) and *kan* (柑) is discussed in detail in the "Jí-jiě" (集解, Collected Explanations) section [of the *Bencao Gangmu*]. According to the theory of Li Shizhen (李時珍): "The fruit of *jú* is small; the flavor of its segments is slightly sour; its peel is thin and reddish, with a pungent and bitter taste. *Kan* is larger than *jú*; the flavor of its segments is sweet; its peel is somewhat thicker and yellow, with a pungent and sweet taste." Following this explanation, there is no doubt that *jú* is indeed *kōji*. Varieties of *jú* and *kan* are widely cultivated in warm regions such as Shikoku and Kyushu. The *jú* tree can reach a height of over one *jō* (approx. 3 meters). Its leaves resemble those of the *tōsei* (冬青, Chinese holly / *Ilex*) and come to a point. The flowers are five-petaled and white, resembling those of *utsugi* (空疏, *Deutzia*), and the branches and trunk bear thorns. The fruit is smaller than *kan* and has a thin peel. It is therefore incorrect to use *kan* peel as a substitute for medicinal purposes today.

*Huáng-jú* (黄橘, Yellow Orange) [ref: *Jí-jiě* / Collected Explanations]　　*Shira-wa kōji* [regional name, Tōtōmi Province]

This variety originates from Shirawa (白輪) in Tōtōmi Province (遠州). Its form is slightly larger than *kan*, and the flesh fills the peel completely. The flavor of the flesh is sweet, the peel is very thin, and there are no white pith strands between the peel and the flesh. For this reason its peel is considered of superior quality. In recent times, a variety produced in Tōgane (東金) in Kazusa Province (上総) has been found to be identical in both form and flavor.
