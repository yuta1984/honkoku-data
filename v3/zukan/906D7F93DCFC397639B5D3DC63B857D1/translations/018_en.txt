**[Right folio]**

**One variety — Hachiya ("Hachiya Persimmon")**

The leaves are large and long, and the fruit is likewise large. The shape resembles that of the *minogaki* (Mino persimmon), with a length of approximately three *sun* (about 9 cm) and a circumference also of about three *sun*. The fruit, skin and all, is dried over fire until a white powdery bloom forms on the surface, producing what is called *hakushi* ("white persimmon"). This is considered the finest quality. Alternatively, half-ripened fruit harvested in autumn is placed in a vessel and left for about ten days, after which it naturally ripens, becoming soft and sweet in flavor. In the eastern provinces of Japan, this variety is called *hachiya*.

**[Left folio]**

**Kunsenshi (君遷子) — *Sarugaki* / *Susugaki* 《Yamato Honzō》 / Reisō (櫺棗)《Tenkei Benran》**
**Kunsenshi (桾櫏子)《Seiji Tsū》 / Nansō (軟棗)《Kyūkō Honzō》**

The form is the same as that of the common persimmon, but the fruit is small, round like a fingertip, and somewhat elongated. The seeds are also small. The *Yamato Honzō* states: "This persimmon is found in Tanba (present-day Kyoto/Hyogo area), and its sweetness is comparable to that of an ordinary persimmon." The fruit is harvested when fully ripe and eaten. Alternatively, it is harvested while still green and soaked in water to produce persimmon tannin liquid (*kakishibu*). There is one variety called *miyamagaki* ("deep-mountain persimmon"), found in the mountains of Bōshū (present-day southern Chiba Prefecture). Its leaves resemble those of *mamegaki* but are narrower and longer, and its fruit also resembles that of *mamegaki* but is flattened.
