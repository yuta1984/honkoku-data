**Nai (柰)** — A type of apple-like fruit

Names and regional terms:
- *Nai* (from *Wamyōshō*)
- *Rinkin* (from *Yamato Honzō*)
- *Nagami ringo* ("long apple") — Edo
- *Heni rinko* — Edo and Kashū (Kaga Province)
- *Beshyago* — Kashū
- *Bebego* — Same region
- *Heikoko* — Bizen Province
- *Funahe* (from *Honchō Shokkan*)
- *Hinba* (頻婆) — cited from *Yamato Honzō*
- *Eika* (頴菓) — from *Sancai Tuhui* (三才図会)
- *Tōkan* (荅還) — from *Tōngya* (通雅)
- *Heiha* (平波) — from *Tenseki Benran* (典籍便覧)
- *Heiha* (苹坡) — from *Runan Pushi* (汝南圃史)
- *Kuwaraihin* (火頼賓)
- *Tai* (㮈) — from *Honzō Wamyō*, citing *Kenmyōen*
- *Reikinttan* (冷金丹) — from *Huajing* (花鏡)
- *Hikihakuta* (比疑縛多) — Sanskrit term

- *Sha-kashi-ju* (沙果子樹) — from *Jiuhuang Bencao* (救荒本草)

The tree grows to about one to two *jō* (approximately 3–6 meters) in height. Its leaves resemble those of the *rinkin* (林檎, apple) but are considerably more elongated. In spring it produces flowers with five petals of pale pink. It subsequently bears fruit, which also resembles the apple but is longer and larger. When ripe, the fruit turns a beautiful crimson red. It is said that when grafted onto the *kaikō* (海紅, a variety of flowering crabapple), the tree tends to wither and die. According to Wakamizu, this tree is found in both Tsugaru and Shinano provinces, and its fruit is sun-dried and sent to distant places. The *Yamato Honzō* records that the flavor resembles that of the apple — sweet and fragrant — and that it is effective in treating ailments caused by excessive drinking.
