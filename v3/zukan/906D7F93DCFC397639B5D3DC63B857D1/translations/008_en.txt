**Rinkin (林檎) — Apple / *Malus* sp.**
*Japanese names and variant names:* Ryūgō [from *Wamyōshō*]; Rinko; Awo-rinko [from Yamato Province]; Kakō (花紅) [from *Runan Pǔshǐ*]; Bunrin (文林) [common name]; Mitsukuwa (蜜果, "honey fruit") [from *Qúnfāng Yǔ*]; Reikintan (冷金丹) [same source]; Bunkankuwa (文官果) [from *Hanagoromo Hyakuei*]; Rinyaku (淋瀹) [from *Meibutsu Hōgen*]

The tree resembles the *kaikō* (海紅, Chinese crabapple / *Malus spectabilis*), with the shaded side of the bark being notably dark. Height exceeds one *jō* (approx. 3 meters). The leaves are also similar to those of the *kaikō*, being large and thick, without any red coloration. In spring, clusters of five-petaled, pale pink flowers bloom among the young shoots, producing five or six fruits per cluster. In size, the fruit resembles the *ishinashi* (a type of pear) and is round in shape. When ripe, the side facing the sun turns red, and the flavor is sweet.

**Shi (柹) — Persimmon / *Diospyros kaki***
*Japanese names and variant names:* Kaki [from *Wamyōshō*]; Hiiso [variant name]; Sekijitsukuwa (赤実果, "red-fruit fruit") [from *Tenpeki Benran*]; Teikyūran (頳虬卵) [from *Meibutsu Hōgen*]; Shichikikuwa (七奇果, "seven-wonder fruit") [same source]; Kin'yō (錦葉, "brocade leaf") [from *Honzō Wamei*, citing *Kenmyōen*]; Mitsukuwan (蜜丸); Shūjitsu (朱実, "vermilion fruit") [both from same source]; Shiju (柹樹) [from *Jiuhuang Bencao*]

**[Left page]**

Persimmon trees are widely cultivated in the gardens of residences. The bark is black, and in old age it forms a scaly, armor-like texture. The leaves resemble those of *goshitsu* (牛膝, *Achyranthes bidentata*) and are thick. In spring, four-petaled yellow flowers bloom between the leaves, with a shape like a jar (*tsubo*).

**Kōshi (紅柹) — Red Persimmon** [from *Jijie* (集解)] — *Gosho-gaki* (御所柿)
Also known as: *Yamato-gaki* (大和柿); *Koneri-gaki*

This variety originates from Gosho village in Yamato Province (present-day Nara Prefecture) and is a renowned local specialty. The fruit is round in shape with slight indentations on four sides. When green at first, it is astringent. When fully ripe and red, its sweetness is incomparable, making it the finest quality.
