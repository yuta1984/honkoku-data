**[Right folio]**

**Tōshi (塔柿) — Kushigaki ("Skewered Persimmon")**

Whether the astringent persimmon is long or round in shape, the skin is removed, the fruit is threaded onto skewers, and then dried. This is the product known as *kushigaki*.

---

**Kōshi (烘柿) — Jukushi ("Ripened Persimmon")**

Rice straw is spread inside a container, and astringent persimmons are placed within it. Left thus, the persimmons naturally ripen on their own, the astringency is removed, and the flavor becomes sweet.

---

**[Left folio]**

**Hakushi (白柿) — Korogaki ("Rolled Persimmon")**

The skin of astringent persimmons is removed, and the fruit is dried to make dried persimmons (*kanshi*). When these are stored inside a container, a white powdery bloom naturally appears on the surface.

---

**Ushi (烏柿) — Fusubegaki ("Smoked Persimmon")**

The skin of astringent persimmons is removed, the fruit is tied with rope, hung over a fire, and dried to produce dried persimmons (*kanshi*).
