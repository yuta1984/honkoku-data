**Right folio:**
*Nyūkan* (Breast/Milk Mandarin — 乳柑)

According to the theory of Mr. Tamura: In former years, there was a Korean who brought *kanshi* (mandarin oranges) to Japan, and this variety came to be called "*kansu*." Its shape resembles that of *kunenbo* but is smaller. The color of the skin is also more vivid than that of *kunenbo*, and it resembles *mikan* while being slightly larger in size. Its fragrance is extremely pleasant, and its taste is exceptionally sweet. The thickness of its skin is slightly thinner than that of *kunenbo*. He suggested that this is likely the variety known as *nyūkan* (乳柑).

**Left folio:**
*Kaikōkan* (Sea-Red Mandarin — 海紅柑) [from the *Honzō Shūkai* / *Bencao Jijie*]
　　*Jagatara mikan* (Javanese/Batavian Mandarin)

The fruit is about the size of a *kunenbo*, the skin is thick and red, the surface is fine-grained (smooth-textured), and the flesh is sweet in taste.
