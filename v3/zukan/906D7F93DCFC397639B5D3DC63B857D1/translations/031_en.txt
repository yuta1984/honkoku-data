One variety:
　　*Tō-mikan* ["Chinese/Tang mandarin"]

　　Produced in Chikuzen and Chikugo provinces. It is said to be
　　originally a Chinese variety (*kanshu*). Larger than the common
　　*mikan* (mandarin), its peel has a fine, dense texture,
　　resembling that of the *tō* (*kunenbo* / Citrus grandis).
　　The peel is red, the flesh is sweet in taste, and it has
　　few seeds.

*Ryoku-jú* (緑橘, Green/Unripe Orange) [ref: *Jí-jiě* / Collected Explanations]

　　　*Ao-mikan* ["Green mandarin"]

　It came from Joseon (Korea),
　and is not of the *mikan*
　(mandarin) family. It can be
　eaten while still green.
