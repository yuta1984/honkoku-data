**[The following text continues the description of *Tokiwagaki* from the previous folio]**
　　It grows wild in the deep mountains of Kii Province (present-day Wakayama Prefecture). It retains its leaves throughout all four seasons. The shape of the leaves resembles those of *mamegaki* but is somewhat smaller and thicker; they do not fall even after two years. It bears fruit among the leaves. The fruit resembles that of *mamegaki* but is smaller, and turns black when ripe.

**Pomegranate** (安石榴, *ansekiyu*)　*Zakuro*　*Shakuro* (a corrupted reading of 石榴)　*Granaat boom* (Dutch name)
　　　　*Jinbō* 金龐 (from *Qunfangpu* 『群芳譜』)　　*Zǐfáng gèn yán* 紫房艮研 (from *Mingwu Fayan* 『名物法言』)　　*Sekisaku* 石醋 (from *Hanakoyomi Hyakuei* 『花暦百詠』)
　　　　*Jian hong jian* 剪紅絹 (a name for the flower in *Mingwu Fayan*)　　*延年花* (*Ennenka*, a name for the flower cited in *Honzō Wamyō* from Cui Yushi's *Shijing*)
　　　　*Tanenma* 娜捻磨 (Sanskrit name)

　It is widely cultivated in people's gardens. The tree reaches over one *jō* (approx. 3 meters) in height. The leaves resemble those of *mizohagi* (purple loosestrife, *Lythrum salicaria*), and at the end of spring, red flowers open at the tips of branches among the young leaves. At the base of the flower there is a calyx, resembling that of *sarusuberi* (crape myrtle). It later bears fruit, which ripens in autumn. The fruit resembles the seed capsule of the poppy but is larger. When ripe, it turns reddish-yellow and splits open naturally. Inside, dozens of seeds fill the pericarp. There are two varieties in flavor: sweet and sour. The sweet *zakuro* corresponds to the *Tiānjiàng* (天醤, "heavenly sauce") mentioned in *Youyang Zazu* (酉陽雑俎).
