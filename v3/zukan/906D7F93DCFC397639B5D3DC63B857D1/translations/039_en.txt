**Right folio:**
*Manjūkan* (Steamed-Bun Mandarin — 饅頭柑) [from the *Honzō Shūkai* / *Bencao Jijie*]

　The shape is smaller than the *Kinokuni mikan* (mandarin of Kii Province). The skin is thick and hard. The area where the stem attaches rises distinctly into a high protrusion, resembling the "nose" (raised center) of a *boke* (Japanese quince) fruit.

**Left folio:**
One variety:　　*Kiku mikan* (Chrysanthemum Mandarin)

　　　The leaves resemble those of the *kunenbo* but are smaller. The fruit is smaller than that of the common mandarin. On its yellow skin, vertical green stripes run lengthwise, giving the fruit an appearance like that of a chrysanthemum flower.
