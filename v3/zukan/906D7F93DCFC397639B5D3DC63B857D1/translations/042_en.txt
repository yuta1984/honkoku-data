**Right folio:**
*(Continuation of the previous entry)*
The tree and leaves resemble those of the *kunenbo*, with many thorns. The fruit is somewhat small, yellow like the *yuzu*, bitter in taste, and has an unpleasant odor, making it unfit for eating. However, because it is dried and used as a fumigant to repel mosquitoes, it bears this name (*kafusu*). In Chōshū, it is said that there are specimens with less bitterness and more acidity that can be eaten.

**Yū (柚)** — *Yu* [from the *Wamyōshō*] ／ *Yusu* [Chikuzen; Unshū] ／ *Kōtō* [China (Chūgoku)]
　　　　　　*Daikitsu* (大橘, "Great Tangerine") [from the *Daicōshūshi*]

　The tree is tall and large, with many thorns between its branches. The leaves form a two-segmented shape. In summer, it opens five-petaled white flowers. The fruit is larger than a *kan* (mandarin orange) but smaller than a *daidai* (*kunenbo*), yellow in color, with a rough skin, and sour in flavor.
