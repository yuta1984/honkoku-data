【Right Folio】

　　[Those] possessing blue-green [coloring]

**Fú Dié** — the Qīngfú Butterfly (*fuchō* / *chōchō*)

　　Alternate names: *Yé'é* [野蛾, "wild moth"; source: *Shìwù Yìmíng*]; *Tàmò* [撻末; same source]; *Tànhuāshǐ* [探花使, "flower-seeker envoy"; same source]; *Lǔlǐbógē* [魯里伯哥; same source, Mongolian name]

　　Explanation of Names: Li Shizhen states — The *jiédié* (蛺蝶, butterfly) is light and delicate; it flies with its wings pressed close together, fluttering in an airy manner. The butterfly is distinguished by the beauty of its antennae (*須*).

【Left Folio】

**Fěn Dié** — the Powder Butterfly (*funchō*; i.e., white butterfly)

　　Alternate name: *Cǎihuāshǐ* [採花使, "flower-gathering envoy"; source: *Diǎnjí Biànlǎn*]

　　Collected Explanations: Li Shizhen states — Butterflies and moths are of the same category. The larger ones are called *dié* (蝶, butterflies); the smaller ones are called *é* (蛾, moths). Their varieties are exceedingly numerous; all have four wings covered in powder (scales), love the fragrance of flowers, and use their antennae in place of a nose.
