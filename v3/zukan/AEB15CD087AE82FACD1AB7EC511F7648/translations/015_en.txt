【Right Page】

The *Guangdong Xinyu* (New Discourses of Guangdong) states: Among the things seen in Mount Luofu, there is nothing that cannot be likened to the phoenix. It is not only birds of five colors — red, kingfisher-green, and jade-blue — that are considered phoenixes; even the large butterfly is regarded as a "Little Phoenix" (shō hōō).

The *Senchūfu* (Illustrated Compendium of a Thousand Insects) states: On the 15th day of the 5th month of the 6th year of Bunka (1809), a chrysalis found inside a *sukashi-tawara* (a transparent cocoon-like casing) was obtained and stored as-is. On the 9th day of the 6th month, the chrysalis broke open and a butterfly emerged. Eighty-three days had passed in total. This butterfly is the very one illustrated here. The cocoon is shown under the entry for the *tenksan* (wild silkworm). It is commonly known as "tegus" (gut thread), and is said to be made into fishing line. When shown to the Dutch physician Siebold, he said he had never seen it before.

*Genbu-semi* (Black Warrior Cicada)　《marginal note: *yama-jōro* /》　*Kaminari-chōchō* (Thunder Butterfly)　《marginal note: Mino / Ōmi》dialect

The *Kōmoku Shōsetsu* (Detailed Explanation of the Bencao Gangmu) states: There is also a black-winged butterfly, commonly called "*yama-jōro*." The edges of its wings have a reddish tinge. Some have wings that are entirely jade-blue or green. This butterfly also has red markings along the wing edges. Its Chinese name is "*Genbu-semi*" (玄武蝉, Black Warrior Cicada).
