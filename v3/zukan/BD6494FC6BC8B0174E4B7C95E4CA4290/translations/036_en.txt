Two varieties. Sketched from life on the first day of the fifth month, in the year Kōshin.

According to the "Chaseки Sōkashū" (茶席挿花集, "Collection of Flower Arrangements for the Tea Room"):
　　Tanshunka (探春花) 《 Mokkōbara 》
　　Mokkōka (木香花 / Rosa banksiae)


　　　　　　The author's note:
　　　　　　　What is called "Mokkō" (木香) is in fact an herb. Its leaves are wrinkled,
　　　　　　　resembling those of Kakushitsu (鶴虱 / Torilis japonica, hedge parsley)
　　　　　　　and burdock (gobo), being narrow and long. In shape, they resemble
　　　　　　　the leaves of Shion (紫苑 / Aster tataricus). It bears yellow flowers,
　　　　　　　which resemble chrysanthemums. It is also called Aomokko (青木香)
　　　　　　　and Minami-mokkō (南木香). The plant Umanosuzukusa
　　　　　　　(馬兜鈴 / Aristolochia debilis) is also referred to as Aomokko.
　　　　　　　These are medicinal plants. Although the name "Mokkō"
　　　　　　　　　　　being applied to this particular tree is not recorded in any
　　　　　　　　　　　classical text, people commonly call it Mokkō.
