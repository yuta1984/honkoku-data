### From the *Jiuhuang Bencao* (Famine Relief Materia Medica):
**Water Smartweed / Floating Heart** (荇菜)　Also called: *Jinlian'er* (Gold Lotus Child)　Another name: *Ǒushūcài*

## From *Yamato Honzō* (Japanese Materia Medica):
**荇** (also written 莕) — this is *kōsai* / Nymphoides.

The *Wamyōshō* calls 荇菜 "*asaza*." Listed in the section below. *Yamato Honzō* records two varieties; in the *Jiuhuang Yepu*, the *asaza* character is given as 莕菜. This is, after all, 荇. Worthy of further investigation.

## From the *Wamyō Ruijushō* (Japanese Names Classified and Collected):
**荇 (*asaza*)** — The *Erya* commentary states: 荇菜 (the upper reading is the character *kyō* / 杏, also written 莕; Japanese name: *asaza*) grows in clusters in the water; its leaves are round and positioned at the long ends [of the stalks], following the depth of the water.

*Yamato Honzō* states: it is probably an error to read 荇菜 as "*asaza*." *Asaza* is an aquatic plant resembling *kawahone* (yellow pond-lily, *Nuphar japonica*). *Asaza* shows its roots above water, but 荇菜 does not. [荇菜] resembles *junsai* (water shield, *Brasenia schreberi*).

### (From *Kan* [壌?]):
**荇 — *asaza***

The roots grow on the bottom of the water; the stems are [fork-shaped], green above and white below. The leaves are green, also tinged with purple. [The surface] is smooth; the leaves float on the water's surface. According to *Meibutsu Benkai* (A Discernment of Famous Things), Du Fu's poem which says "水荇牽風翠帯長" (*shuǐ xìng qiān fēng cuì dài cháng* — "The water smartweed trailing in the wind, a long jade-green ribbon") refers to this plant. The *Bencao Gangmu* gives alternative names: Water Mirror Grass (水鏡草) and Dimple Plant (靨子菜). Today, it grows abundantly in marshy ponds throughout the country. It resembles *junsai* (water shield) and has notched [leaves]. It also opens five-petaled yellow flowers resembling small lotus blossoms; hence Luo Yuan's *Erya* calls it Gold Lotus Seed (金蓮子). There is also a white-flowering variety. Kui Yuandu's *Meibutsu Kai* confusingly mixes 荇 and 蓴 together in its explanation. **Floating duckweed** (*fuping*) is [a different plant entirely] — it is the one whose leaves, about the size of a bean, cover the water's surface. **Water clover** (田字草, *tenjisō*) is also [a separate plant]. Its leaves are called *tsumi*. Many books err and mix these up. Please consult the author's illustrations for the correct appearance of each plant.
