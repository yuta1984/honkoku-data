*Written on the 8th day of the latter part of the 4th month of summer, in the year Bunsei Kōshin (1824); drawn from life in the rear garden.*

**From the *Yamato Honzō* (Japanese Materia Medica):**

罌粟 (Keshi; opium poppy, *Papaver somniferum*)
[Auzoku]

---

**From the *Honzō Wage* (Japanese Interpretation of the Materia Medica):**

罌粟穀 (Auzoku-koku): This refers to the seed capsule (empty husk) of the keshi poppy.

In the same text, under the same entry: Alternative name — 米嚢子 (Heinōshi); 御米象穀 (Gomiishōkoku); Japanese name: 介志 (Keshi)

---

**From the *Zōho Tashikiben* (Supplemented Encyclopedia of Identification), Section on Hemp, Wheat, and Rice:**

罌子栗 (Afushizoku) — Japanese name: Keshi (計志); Note: this is the name in current use.

Alternative name: 御米 (Gyohei) [from the *Kaibao Bencao*]

象穀 (Shōkoku); Supplemented alternative name: 米嚢子 (Beinōshi) [from the *Kaibao Bencao*]

---

There are several varieties of this flower. The double-flowered (many-petaled) variety is called 麗春花 (Rishunka; "Beautiful Spring Flower"). It is also named 賽銭牡丹 (Saisen-botan; "Offering-Box Peony"). What is called 錦被花 (Kinhika; "Brocade-Covered Flower") is what is today known as 花罌粟 (Hana-keshi; the ornamental poppy, *Papaver rhoeas* or similar).
