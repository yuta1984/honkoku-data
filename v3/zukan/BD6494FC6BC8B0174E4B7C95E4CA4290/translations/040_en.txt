## Summer Chrysanthemum Flower
*(Copied on the 7th day of the 5th month, Kōshin year)*

**[Grass Category]**

---

**From *Yamato Honzō* (Great Japanese Herbal):**
Today, in Kyoto and throughout the various provinces, the plant known as *Butsukouso* (佛甲草, *Sedum lineare*) is called "*Ichigasou*" (一夏草, "one-summer plant"), and is also known as "*Mukonkusa*" (無根草, "rootless plant").

---

**From the *Zoho Tashikiden* (Expanded Encyclopaedia of Knowledge), section on Rock Plants:**
The Japanese name for *Butsukouso* (佛甲草, pronounced *Butsukōsō*) is, upon investigation, "*Hotoke no tsume*" (保土計乃豆米, lit. "Buddha's claw/nail").

---

**From the *Chikinsho* (地錦抄, a horticultural compendium):**
An alternative name is "*Busshikō*" (佛指甲, "Buddha's fingernail"), and the name "*Ichigasou*" (one-summer plant) is also recorded.

*(Copied on the 17th day of the 5th month of the Kōshin year, drawn from life among the rocks in the garden)*

---

**From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (Illustrated Sino-Japanese Encyclopedia), section on Rock Plants:**
"*Ichikusa*" (以知草) — the correct written form is unknown.

> The Japanese name for *Keitensō* (景天草, *Sedum*) is said to be "*Ichikusa*" (以知久佐); this plant belongs to the *Sedum* family. Therefore, could it be that it is mistakenly called "*Ichikusa*"?

---

**[Baien's Commentary/Investigation]:**
Upon examination, according to one account, [*Ichikusa*] is among the very smallest of the *Sedum* varieties. It also resembles the seedling of *Tsumerenka* (*Orostachys japonica*), with weak, vine-like stems that grow in profusion, blooming with small yellow flowers in the fifth month. It is said to be cultivated in people's homes — could the ones found in garden settings be this plant? *Butsukouso* (佛甲草) is listed separately, but its flowers and leaves do not match the illustration in *Yamato Honzō* and differ from it. It is unclear which should be considered the authentic name. The description of "*Ichikusa*" appears to be applicable here.
