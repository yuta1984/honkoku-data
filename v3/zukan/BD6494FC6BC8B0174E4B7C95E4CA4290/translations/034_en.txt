【Karasubishaku (Pinellia ternata / Crow's Ladle)】
According to the "Jikinsho" (Chikinsho):
　　Hange (半夏, read: Hange)

　　　　In the "Yueling" (Monthly Ordinances), it states: "In the fifth month, Hange sprouts."
　　　　This is because it grows in the middle of summer,
　　　　hence the name.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Sketched from life in the middle of the first summer month,
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　the fourth day, in the year Kōshin (1824 or 1764).
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Both species drawn from life.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　[Seal: Herb Classification]

According to the "Zōho Tashiki-hen" (Expanded Encyclopedia of Plant Knowledge), section on Poisonous Plants:
　Hange (半夏, Pinellia ternata)
　　《Japanese names》 Katahozo-hikakurichi; alternatively called Karasubishaku (Crow's Ladle); and in the expanded edition, Batohisuhi
　　《Alternative names》 Shūden (守田, "Protector of the Field") [from Bencao Gangmu]; Suigyoku (水玉, "Water Jewel") [same source]; Chimon (地文, "Earth Script") [from Bieelu (Supplementary Records)]
　　《Additional alternative names in expanded edition》 Wako (和姑) [from Bencao Gangmu]

Hange is also called Karasu no Bishaku (烏の柄杓, "Crow's Ladle"), or Mizutama (水玉, "Water Drop"), and another name is Tamori (田守, "Field Keeper").
　　　Genju states: There are three varieties — Chinese Hange, Ryukyu Hange, and Korean Hange.
