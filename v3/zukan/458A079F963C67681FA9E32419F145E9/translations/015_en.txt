**Eibō (衛矛)** — *Euonymus alatus* (Winged Spindle Tree)

Japanese names: *Kawakumatsudzura* (from *Wamyōshō*), *Kusomayumi* (from *Wamyōshō*), *Nishikigi*, *Yahazu-nishikigi*, *Mayumi*, *Yaba-nishikigi*

This plant grows in the mountains and is also cultivated in gardens. The tree reaches a height of about one *jō* (approximately 3 meters). The branches and leaves grow in opposite pairs. The leaf shape resembles that of *Eucommia* (*tochū*), being about one *sun* (approx. 3 cm) long and five to six *bu* (approx. 1.5–1.8 cm) wide, with fine serrations along the margins. In spring, small pale-green flowers open among the new leaves, and fruits develop afterward. The fruits are somewhat flattened, about three *bu* (approx. 0.9 cm) across, and upon ripening in autumn,

they turn red, then split open naturally to reveal bright red seeds within. The shape of the fruit resembles that of *Eucommia*. The leaves also turn red or purple in the winter months before falling. Both the branches and trunk develop longitudinal ridges forming wing-like projections. These wings are initially green, later turning a dark brownish color (*urumi*-color). These wing-like corky projections are harvested and used medicinally.
