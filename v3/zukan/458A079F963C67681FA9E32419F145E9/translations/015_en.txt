**Eibō (衛矛)** — *Euonymus alatus* (Winged Spindle Tree)
- *Kawakumatsuzura* [from the *Wamyōshō*]
- *Kusomayumi* [from the *Wamyōshō*]
- *Nishikigi*
- *Yahazu-nishikigi*
- *Mayumi*
- *Yaba-nishikigi*

It grows in the mountains and is also cultivated in gardens. The tree reaches about one *jō* (approx. 3 meters) in height. The branches and leaves grow opposite each other. The leaves resemble those of *Eucommia* (*tochū*, 杜仲) in shape, being about one *sun* (approx. 3 cm) long and five to six *bu* (approx. 1.5 cm) wide, with fine serrations along the edges. In spring, small pale-green flowers bloom among the new leaves, and berries follow afterward. The fruits are flattened, about three *bu* (approx. 1 cm) across. By autumn, they ripen and

turn red, splitting open naturally to reveal bright red seeds (arils). Their shape resembles the fruit of *Eucommia*. The leaves also turn red or purple as winter approaches and then fall. Both the branches and trunk develop longitudinal ridges that form wing-like projections. These are initially green, later turning brownish. These wings are harvested and used medicinally.
