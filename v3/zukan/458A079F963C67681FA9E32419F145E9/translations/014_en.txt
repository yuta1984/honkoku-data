**[Right folio]**
**Kukotsu (枸骨)** — Found abundantly in the mountains, and also planted in gardens as hedges. The leaves resemble those of *Ligustrum* (nezu-mochi), being thick and stiff, with spines along the margins; they do not wither through winter.

Japanese names: *Hiiragi* (also known as *hiraki*, *oni no metsuki*, *onishiba*)

Alternative names: *Byōtōshi* (猫頭刺) [from *Gatsu*], *Byōshi* (猫刺) [same source as above]

**[Left folio]**

In the winter months, small white flowers cluster and bloom among the leaves, giving off a faint fragrance. Afterward, berries are produced. They are roughly the size of soybeans, somewhat elongated, initially green, and turning black-purple upon ripening. In common usage, specimens without spines are called *me-hiiragi* ("female holly"), but this is not a separate species.
