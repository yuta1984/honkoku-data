**Kukotsu** (拘骨)
　　Hiiragi
　　Hiraki
　　Oni-no-metsuki (鬼の目突き, "demon's eye-poker")
　　Onishiba (鬼芝, "demon grass/turf")
　　Byōtōshi (貓頭刺, "cat's-head thorn") [from the *Tsūga*]
　　Byōku (貓刺, "cat's thorn") [same source]

This plant is found in abundance in the mountains, and is also cultivated in people's gardens as a hedge. Its leaves resemble those of *Jotei* (女貞, *Ligustrum* sp. / privet), being thick and hard, with spines around their edges. It does not wither through winter.

[Left folio]

In the winter months, small white flowers cluster and bloom among the leaves, with a faint fragrance. Berries form thereafter, roughly the size of soybeans but somewhat elongated. Initially green, they turn black-purple upon ripening. In common parlance, specimens without spines are called *me-hiiragi* (雌柊, "female hiiragi"), but they are not a separate species.
