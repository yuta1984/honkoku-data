**Kukotsu (拘骨)**
- *Hiiragi* (Japanese holly / *Osmanthus heterophyllus*)
- *Hiraki*
- *Oni no metsuki* (鬼の目突き, "eye-poker of demons")
- *Onishiba* (鬼芝, "demon grass")
- *Byōtōshi* (貓頭刺, "cat-headed thorn") [from the *Tsūga*]
- *Byōshi* (貓刺, "cat thorn") [from the same work]

It grows abundantly in the mountains and is also planted in gardens to form hedges. Its leaves resemble those of *Ligustrum* (女貞, *nezumimochi*), being thick and hard, with spines around the edges, and do not wither through the winter.

**Left page:**

In the winter months, small white flowers bloom in clusters among the leaves, with a faint fragrance. Berries follow afterward. They are about the size of a soybean but somewhat elongated. At first they are green, and upon ripening they turn dark purplish-black. Commonly, the spineless form is called *me-hiiragi* ("female holly"), but it is not a separate species.
