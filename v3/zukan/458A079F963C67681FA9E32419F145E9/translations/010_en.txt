**[Right folio]**
*One variety (an additional species)*

The leaves are rounded with pointed tips, and the areas between the veins are raised, giving a wrinkled appearance. Flowers open in spike-like clusters at the tips of the branches, resembling those of *nezumimochi* (Japanese privet, *Ligustrum japonicum*). Afterward, the plant bears fruit. The fruit is round, resembling a bean, and turns black upon ripening.

**[Left folio]**
**冬青** (*Tōsei*) — Japanese name: *mochi-no-ki* (*Ilex* spp., Japanese holly/Chinese holly)
*Alternative names:* Mannnenshi 万年枝 [*Jiwu Yiming*], Chōsei 長生 [*Huiyuan Xiangzhu*], Mannenjū 万年樹 [*Qunfang Pu*], Mannenboku 万年木 [*Zhengzi Tong*], Tōshō 冬牆 [*Runan Pushi*]

The species is highly varied. The tree reaches a height of over one *jō* (approximately 3 meters). The leaves grow alternately; they are broader, thicker, and more lustrous than those of *jotei* (glossy privet, *Ligustrum lucidum*). The surface is smooth with no transverse wrinkles or raised veining, and the leaves do not wither even through winter. In summer, small white flowers with five petals bloom in dense clusters among the leaves, after which fruit is produced. The fruit is initially green and turns red when ripe, resembling the fruit of the cherry (*sakura*) in shape.
