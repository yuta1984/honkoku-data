[Right folio]
A variety:
　Nanaminoki (name from *Yamato Honzō*)
　Nanamimochi

This plant is commonly cultivated in gardens. Its leaves resemble those of tsukubanemochi (a type of holly) in shape, being thick, and do not wither in winter. The fruits grow in clusters, bearing six or seven berries per cluster.

[Left folio]
A variety:
　Fukuramochi

The leaves are rounder and larger than those of the preceding entry (nanaminoki), tapering to a point at the tip. The flowers grow between the leaves, and their form is likewise the same as the preceding entry.
