This plant is commonly cultivated in people's gardens and used as a hedge. The tree grows to a height of over one *jō* (approximately three meters). Its branches and leaves grow alternately (*alternate arrangement*). The shape of the leaves resembles those of the *Fūsō* (*Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*), but with large lobes (incisions), and they are slightly hairy. Throughout the summer months, buds form in the axils between the leaves along the branches, opening into flowers. The flowers resemble those of the *Shokki* (hollyhock; *Alcea rosea*) in shape, are pale purple in color, and the base of each petal is deep crimson. The flowers open in the morning and wither by evening. For this reason, the *Shakumyō* (*Shiming*, a Chinese etymological dictionary) records it as the "morning-opening, evening-falling flower" (*chōkai boraku ka*; 朝開暮落花). The fruit is about the size of a fingertip, and contains small seeds within.
