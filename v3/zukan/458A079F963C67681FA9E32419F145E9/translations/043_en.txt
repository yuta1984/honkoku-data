Sekinan (Stone Southern Plant)　　Shakunage (Rhododendron)
　　Shakunagi　Shakunan

　It grows in deep mountains and secluded valleys, with trees reaching heights of six to seven shaku (approximately 180–210 cm).
　The leaves do not wither throughout all four seasons; their shape resembles that of loquat (Eriobotrya japonica) leaves,
　though smaller, with a deep green upper surface and soft brownish hairs on the underside.
　In early summer, branches extend from the tips of the trees, producing clusters of numerous flowers.
　Each flower resembles that of an azalea (Rhododendron/Tsutsuji) but is larger, forming five petals, or sometimes six or seven.
　When in bloom, the flowers take on a pale pink color.
