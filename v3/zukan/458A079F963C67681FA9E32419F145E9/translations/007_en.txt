**Sori (鼠李)** — *nabakōshi*

Regional names: *kuroumemodoki* (Edo); *ushikoroshi* (Kōshū); *nigaki* (so named because of its bitter taste); *torinobōrazu* (Echizen)

Chinese/literary names: *Ryokushi* (緑子) [from *Honkyō Hōgen* / *Bencao Fengyuan*]; *Shokushū Sori* (蜀州鼠李) [from *Bikyū Honzō* / *Beiji Bencao*]

---

**[Left folio]**

This plant grows in the fields and meadows of Kogane in Shimōsa Province and Toda in Musashi Province, among other locations. It reaches a height of seven to eight *shaku* (approximately 210–240 cm). The leaves resemble those of the plum (*sumomo* / *Prunus salicina*), are large, and grow alternately (*gosei*). The branches are sharp and resemble thorns. In spring, small yellowish-white flowers bloom in dense clusters between the branches. By autumn, the berries grow large; their shape resembles those of *sarsaparilla* (*Smilax china* / *hatsukkatsu*), and upon ripening they turn a deep indigo-black color. Inside the berries are seeds, which contain a great deal of resin. The leaves are said to be astringent, bitter, and toxic. The *Bencao Gangmu* (*Kōmoku*) also states that the plant possesses a slight toxicity.
