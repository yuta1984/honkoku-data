***Yōro***　***Tani-utsugi***　***Yama-utsugi***
　　***Satsuki-bana*** *(interlinear note: Etchū)*　***Saotome-utsugi*** *(interlinear note: Unshū)*

　It grows wild at the foot of mountains, and is also widely cultivated in private gardens. The tree reaches a height of six to seven *shaku* (approximately 180–210 cm). In spring, young shoots emerge. The leaves resemble those of *ajisai* (hydrangea) but are smaller and thinner, with serrated edges around the margins. While the leaves are young, they bear a reddish tinge. In the summer months, flowers open among the leaves. The form resembles that of the common *utsugi* (*Deutzia*) but is larger, measuring over one *sun* in length (approximately 3 cm), and is pale pinkish-red in color. It subsequently bears fruit. When the fruit ripens, it splits open into four or five sections, resembling a flower.
