**A Variety (of Sanban)**

It is said to be found on Ōshima, at Todoroki Pass (轟峠), in Chikuzen Province. The shape of the leaves is smaller than that of the preceding entry (Sanban), and the form of the flower spikes is likewise similar. The fruit is slender and elongated, and yellowish-brown in color.

**Nandina (南燭, *Nandina domestica*)**

[Regional names]: Nanten (Edo); Natsuten (Kyoto); Naruten

[Literary alternative names]: Sanyō 三葉 [Wahō-sho]; Nantenchiku 南天竹 [Tsūga]; Usai 烏菜 [Honkei Hōgen]; Usō 烏草 [Ruisho Sanyō]; Nansōmoku 南草木 [Mukeihitsudan/Mengxi Bitan]; Nantenchiku 南天竺 [Hiden Kakyō]; Taichin 大椿 [same source]; Kokuhanju 黒飯樹 [Kokon Hien]; Yōtōsō 揚桐草 [Kōmoku Sekinan Hatsumei]

This plant is commonly cultivated in residential gardens and thrives best in shaded locations. It is said that in the western provinces (*Saigoku*) there are large trees of this species, some large enough to serve as pillars. The leaves grow in tens on a single stem. The shape of each leaflet resembles that of the *ligustrum* (*nezumimochi*, *Ligustrum japonicum*) but is smaller, and the stalks are slender. The leaves do not fall throughout the four seasons; in spring, new leaves emerge that are not red but rather a pale bluish-green color. In summer, flower spikes of five to six *sun* (approximately 15–18 cm) emerge from the tips of the branches, opening dozens of flowers. Each flower is approximately three *bu* (about 9 mm) across, white, and six-petaled. When in bud, several sepals surround the bud, giving it the appearance of a double (*yaezaki*) flower, but when the flower opens, the sepals fall away, leaving six petals. The flowers are truly prone to scattering and are difficult to observe closely. Afterward, the plant bears fruit: initially green, it ripens to a deep crimson color by winter. With good care, the berries can persist until spring. Among varieties of this plant, there is one kind that bears white fruit and another that bears yellow fruit.
