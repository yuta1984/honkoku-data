【Right folio】

In the early morning, one should use one's hand bearing the oily residue from having just combed one's hair, and stroke the plant — this is the most beneficial method. Hence the popular saying that "orchids love licentiousness (淫)." In tending to them, one should water them with rainwater, pond water, or alternatively the oily water remaining after washing one's hair. Freshly drawn water is not appropriate. Bath water will cause worms and earthworms to breed. However, if the water used to wash eels is poured in, it will eliminate worms and earthworms. When care is insufficient, white spots (bumps) appear on the leaves. These are called "ran-shitsu" (orchid lice) 《Reference: Hanashi Sahen (花史左編)》. If one brushes and washes them away periodically with the tip of a writing brush, they will disappear naturally. Orchids are exceedingly difficult to cultivate, yet their form is one of quiet, refined elegance (yūga).

The 『Flower Mirror』 (花鏡, Huajing) states: The secrets of nurturing orchids are as follows — In spring, do not bring them outside 《lest they suffer from frost, snow, or cold winds》; in summer, do not expose them to direct sunlight 《the summer heat and fierce sun are to be most strictly avoided》; in autumn, do not let them dry out 《water frequently with enriched water or soybean liquid》; in winter, do not let them become wet 《it is appropriate to store them in a warm room or in a pit in the ground》. Methods for planting orchids can also be found in 『Zunsheng Bajian』 (遵生八牋) and 『Runan Pushi』 (汝南圃史), among others. In winter, they are stored in a warm room (muro), and care must be taken in the amount of water applied. At the time of Risshka (立夏, the start of summer)...

【Left folio】

...and around Shōman (小満, "Grain Buds," mid-sixth month), the plants should be divided (propagated by root division). If one takes the old bulbs and tends to them, leaves will sprout; if not taken, they will not sprout. The 『Kōchū-shū』 (行厨集) states: "If the leaves of kenran (建蘭) are steeped in oil, they will darken the hair and prevent it from falling out." Its nature delights in oily residue, and it is also beneficial for the hair. However, there is also a theory that it is poisonous. The 『Jibutsu Kanju』 (事物紺珠) states: "Orchids are extremely beautiful but have poison. A person from Shu (Sichuan), once drunk and thirsty, drank the flower water from a vase in which orchids had been placed, and died from vomiting and diarrhea." In general, the water in which flowers have been arranged is entirely poisonous, and this is not limited to orchid flower water alone.

Kenran (建蘭, "Cymbidium sinense"; popularly called をらん) has leaves that reach two to three shaku in length, are firm and hard, deep green in color, and grow upright. It has no fragrance. Its roots are called "dozokuldan" (土続断) 《Reference: Kokoku Tei Ransetsugen (虚谷訂蘭説言)》. Its qi (vital energy) carries an oily quality. In summer, young shoots emerge from beside the root bulbs. The sheaths (bracts) are tinged with purple and in form are roughly like bamboo shoots. Four or five leaves emerge, and by late autumn they have fully grown.
