From the Bencao Gangmu (Compendium of Materia Medica):

　Mint (Hakka/薄荷)　Alternative names: Bahe (菝■), Wuba (呉菝), Banheye (蕃荷葉)
　《Side note: Chinese pronunciation: Pohō》　Other names: Southern Mint (南薄荷), Coin Mint (金銭薄荷)
　　○Tatsuhakka (Dragon Mint)

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Kōin year, Muya month (9th month), 8th day
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Cut stem from the garden garden, true sketch

　When cats eat mint, they become intoxicated. Therefore, when one is bitten by a cat, squeezing the juice of mint and applying it to the wound is effective. Also stated: Mint is the wine of cats. Japanese knotweed (Itadori) is the wine of tigers. Mulberry berries (Kuwa no mi) are the wine of pigeons. Rui grass is the wine of fish. In all things, there is a mutual resonance that induces intoxication. Mountain mint is eagerly eaten by cats — like silvervine (Matatabi) and lindera (Unyaku), these are all medicines for cats.

　Wild Mint (Yauhakka)　Wild-growing variety　Dog Mint (Inuhakka)　Those with a strong, pungent smell

　There are several varieties referred to as "Hakka" (mint). Both the purple-stemmed and green-stemmed types are ordinary mint.
