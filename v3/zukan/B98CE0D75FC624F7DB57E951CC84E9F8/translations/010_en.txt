From the "Shabenchilu" (赭鞭徐禄):
　　　"Akasha" (赤車使者) — There are two varieties. The small-leafed one is called Kuchinawa-jōgo.
　　　　　　　　The large-leafed one is called Hamitsu (葉水).

From the "Tashikiden" (多識編), section on Aromatic Plants:
　　　Sekisha (赤車使者)
　　　　　Akana
　　　　　　　○Mitsuna
　　　　　　　Mitsukusa
　　　　　　　　　(Origin: Sado Island)

　　　　　　　　　　　　　The author [Baien] states:
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　This plant grows in wetlands. Its leaves and stems
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　are particularly lustrous, soft, smooth, and
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　moisture-laden — it is a common weed
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　not especially remarkable in appearance.

From the "Kyūkō Yafu" (救荒野譜):
　　　Jakuji Menden (雀兒綿單) — Japanese name: Nishikikusa
　　　　　　Menkenzu (綿繭頭)

　　　Additionally, there is a separate variety of Jikkin (地錦) which is a kind of ivy (Tsuta). Regarding this,
　　　the description of its plant form as recorded in the "Bencao Gangmu" (本草綱目) — in the
　　　Jijie (集解) commentary there is a differing explanation — when the stem is broken,
　　　white sap exudes. It grows as seedlings in wastelands, fields, temple grounds,
　　　and between small stones. According to the "Jiuhuang Bencao" (救荒本草),
　　　might it be a variety of Jihuai-cai (地槐菜)?
