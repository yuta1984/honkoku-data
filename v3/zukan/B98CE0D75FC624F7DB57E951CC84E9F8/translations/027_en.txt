Kijin-ran [Miraculous Orchid] — According to Kanen's explanation
　　　　Fuyō-ran [Hibiscus Orchid] — Tōpanya


　　　　The fruit of the Fuyō-ran resembles a gourd; when it ripens, its pod splits open and releases cotton-like fibers (wadded floss). For this reason, it is called Tō-han-ya-nai (Chinese Hanya). It differs from the Japanese variety of Hanyai.



　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Both species, Kanoto-U year, 9th month,
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　26th day (the day after the full moon), at Sōkyū,
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　drawn from life in the medicinal garden and courtyard.
