*Recorded from a stem broken from a perennial root in my own garden, drawn from life, on the third day after the first full moon [*shobō*, around the 1st–3rd] of early autumn, in the year Kōin (庚寅, 1830).*

---

**Sources: *Honzō Wage* (本草和解) and various other herbals (諸本草)**

**Yokuyi (薏苡)** — *Job's tears* (*Coix lacryma-jobi*)
Alternate names: Gairei (觧蠡), Ki-no-mi (芑実), Kanbei (簳米) [※ written with the character "艹＋贛" in the original manuscript], Kanju (簳珠), Kaikaimai (回々米), Yokujushi (薏珠子)

Japanese name: Juzudama (数珠陀麿, "prayer-bead jewel")

---

○ Tōmugi (唐麦, "Chinese wheat")
Jezugotama (ジエズゴタマ, variant reading of Juzudama)

"It is the custom of the country [Japan] to use these seeds as prayer beads (*juzu*); hence the name Juzudama."

Tsushitama (芋珠) [from the *Wamyōshō*]

---

There are two varieties of Yokuyi. One variety has large, round seeds with a thick husk. This is named *Bodaishi* (菩提子, "Bodhi seeds").
