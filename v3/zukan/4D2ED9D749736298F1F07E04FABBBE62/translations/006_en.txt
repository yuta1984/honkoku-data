...and freed from the labor of conjecture and investigation. From this it can be seen how precious illustrations truly are. Therefore, illustrations must be executed with precision and skill. If they are not precise and skillful, how can one distinguish things that appear similar but are in fact different? And where, then, would their value lie?

The physicians of the world are solely intent upon making profit, and give no thought to what it is that truly makes medicine what it is. They regard human life and death as no more consequential than that of dogs and horses, or as a mere weed in the dirt. If people rebuke them, they simply say, "It was fate," or "I have exhausted my skill," and trouble themselves no further. What leisure do they have to carefully select their medicinal substances? How much less do they concern themselves with illustrations and explanations — and still less with whether those illustrations are precise and detailed or coarse and

summary! Even in matters that are urgent and essential, they have no time to make careful selections. Carelessly and haphazardly, they leave everything to the dispensary (*yakushi*), never troubling to inquire whether the medicines are genuine or adulterated, refined or crude. Even if one were momentarily moved to try to distinguish them, having never studied and mastered the subject, how could one suddenly be capable of telling them apart? Alas — when a patient fails to recover, is it truly all a matter of fate, or of the limits of one's skill? It is like a case at law where the presiding official does not personally hear the matter himself, but instead relies entirely upon the falsified case record of a corrupt clerk to render his judgment. Can what he decides to be right truly be right, and what he decides to be wrong truly be wrong? How can the common people be free from wrongful injustice? This is truly a matter for the deepest indignation and lament.
