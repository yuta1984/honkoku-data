Preface to the Honzō Zufu (Illustrated Manual of Medicinal Plants)

Among all the things that people cherish, there is nothing more cherished than life; among all the things that people abhor, there is nothing more abhorred than death. What is there, other than the art of medicine, that can spare people from regret at the critical junctures of illness, life, and death? The Way of medicine bears responsibilities that are exceedingly weighty and urgent. Moreover, in the practice of treatment, one cannot accomplish anything without recourse to medicinal substances. The relationship of medicine to medicinal substances — in terms of urgency and necessity — is like that of a general to his soldiers. Should one abandon them, even the finest general or most gifted physician would have no recourse but to fold their hands and await destruction and death.
