　　　　　Haku (Konotegashiwa / Oriental Arborvitae)　[From the Bencao Gangmu]

It is regarded as the chief among the hundred trees (all trees). It grows in deep mountains. While young, its branches are dense and numerous; after several decades, it stands as a single trunk with branches extending obliquely, and it is very difficult for it to grow large. It is long-lived, sharing longevity with the pine. The color of the upper and lower surfaces of its leaves is the same. In spring it produces small, fine flowers and bears fruit. The shape of the fruit resembles a small bell. When winter comes (after the frost), it splits into four parts, containing small seeds. It is difficult to propagate from seed. In general, all trees follow the sun (facing south), yet the branches of this tree face west — this is its nature. It is presumably a tree of yin character. According to an old account, when a woodcutter enters the mountains and loses his sense of east and west, he can look at the haku tree to determine direction.
