(Continued from the previous page: on the veins of both the upper and lower surfaces of kunugi leaves) something resembling fruit is produced. At first these are extremely small, but later they become deep crimson like the fruit of the autumn olive (Elaeagnus / gumi), and are dotted with white spots. Those on the undersides of the leaves are paler in color, but when exposed to sunlight they deepen in hue. These are insect galls (虫窠). Their interiors are hollow (dongara), and by autumn, something resembling a kernel forms within the center, inside which resides a single insect. In winter it undergoes metamorphosis and emerges.

Furthermore, the **Seidonjū (斉墩樹 / chisha-no-ki, *Styrax japonica* or similar)** is abundant in mountain fields. Its leaves are deep green with serrated margins. In summer, five-petaled white flowers open at the tips of slender branches and among the leaves, clustered together. After flowering, fruit is set, hanging downward. In autumn, the fruit splits into three, revealing seeds that are brown and exceedingly hard.

At the tips of slender branches, something resembling fruit is produced and hangs downward. In color it is pale green with a slight yellowish tint, covered with a white powder. This is a transformation of the flower, growing clustered together in a single bunch. The interior is hollow (dongara) and generates insects. In autumn, once the insects metamorphose and disperse, the husk withers and shrivels. Those that produce insect galls (虫窠)—

—of one or two kinds are listed below.

**Hō (枹 / konara, *Quercus serrata*)** [From the *Bencao Gangmu*, collected explanations under *Hu* (槲)]

It grows in nearby mountains. It is a small tree, not exceeding three or four *shaku* (approximately 90–120 cm) in height. Its leaves emerge in spring and resemble those of the *nara* oak (槲). It produces flowers among its leaves and sets fruit; both flowers and fruit resemble those of the oak (樫/kashi). During summer, something resembling a chestnut burr (毬/iga) is produced among the leaves or on the branches. This is called "naragauto." At first it is green, and later turns brown. The inside is filled with something resembling a kernel, within the very center of which resides a single insect. In winter it metamorphoses and emerges. Its form resembles that of a fly (蠅). For this reason, the tree is also called "hahi-no-ki" (the fly tree).

**Fuboku (膚木 / fushi-no-ki, *Rhus chinensis*, Chinese gall tree)** [From the *Bencao Gangmu*, collected explanations under *Yanfuzi* (塩麩子)]

It grows in mountain fields. The form of its leaves resembles those of the lacquer tree (漆/urushi), and small leaflets grow along the stem.
