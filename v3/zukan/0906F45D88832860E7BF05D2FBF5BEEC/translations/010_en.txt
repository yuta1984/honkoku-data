　　　　　榕樹 (yōju / ako, Ficus microcarpa)　[From the Nanfang Caomu Zhuang]

A plant of southern regions, it is sensitive to cold. In winter it should be stored in a heated greenhouse (muro). Its leaves are deep green and glossy, and they wither and fall in winter. In summer, new leaves sprout from the tips of the branches, which then elongate. During the summer months, it is uncommon, but the plant will occasionally and suddenly produce numerous fruits along its branches without first flowering. The fruit is initially blue-green with white spots; when it ripens in late autumn, it becomes reddish-purple with yellowish spots. The inside of the fruit is purple and hollow — without flesh — and is entirely like a fig (ichijiku). When a branch is broken, white sap exudes. The plant propagates readily through cuttings. Occasionally it produces roots from the base, but unless the soil is proper for it, it cannot generate many roots, producing only one or two at most. Also, "gatsumaru" (Japanese name; Chinese name unknown) is likewise a variety of this group, a product of the southern lands, and is quite sensitive to cold. Its nature closely resembles that of the榕樹 (ako), and—

—in summer it too suddenly produces fruits along its branches without flowering. The shape of the fruit resembles that of the榕樹 (ako). It propagates well by cuttings. It generates roots from the base and also from among the branches. Although it is said to root more easily than the榕樹 (ako), if the soil is not suited to it, it is difficult for the plant to grow. Below, one or two species that produce fruit without flowering are listed:

**天仙果 (tensenpka / inubiwa, Ficus erecta)**　[Listed under "Appendix to the Ichijiku entry" in the Bencao Gangmu]
It grows no taller than one jō (approx. 3 meters). Its leaves are arranged alternately, without serrations. In summer it bears fruit among the leaves without flowering. The fruit resembles a fig but is smaller.

**木蓮 (mokulen / itabikadzura, Ficus pumila / creeping fig)**　[From the Bencao Gangmu]
It climbs over trees and rocks (spreading along them). Its leaves are thick, hard, slender, and long, arranged alternately, and do not wither even through winter. In summer it bears fruit among the leaves without flowering.

**無花果 (mukwaika / ichijiku, Ficus carica / common fig)**　[From the Bencao Gangmu]
The tree reaches more than one jō in height; its leaves are arranged alternately with branching lobes. When a branch is broken, white sap exudes.
