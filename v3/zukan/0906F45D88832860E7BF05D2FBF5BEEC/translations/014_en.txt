**Hackberry (朴樹, *enoki*)** [From the commentary on *Erya*]
Found abundantly in mountains, fields, and various places. Its leaves emerge in spring, and in summer it produces flowers and bears fruit. Within its leaves, things resembling grains of wheat appear, or the leaf surface may become shriveled. These are insect galls. Beyond these, there are cases where branches or leaves swell up, or where insects are produced in transformed flowers, but these are omitted here.

[*Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica), entry on the Mosquito-Mother Bird (蚊母鳥), Collection of Commentaries: Zangqi says: This bird is about the size of a chicken, black in color, and lives in ponds and marshes in the southern regions and among madder plants; it is also common in the Jiangdong area. Its cry resembles the sound of a person retching, and each time it vomits out one or two *sheng* of mosquitoes. Mosquitoes are born from the metamorphosis of insects in stagnant water; in Jiangdong there is the Mosquito-Mother Bird, in the northern frontier there is the Mosquito-Mother Tree, and in Lingnan there is the Horsefly-Mother Grass. These three things are of different kinds but serve the same function.]

[The same work, Appendix on Gadflies (蜚虻): Zangqi says: In Lingnan there is the Mosquito Tree (蚊子木), whose leaves resemble those of the Winter Green (*Ilex*), and whose fruit resembles loquat; when the fruit ripens, mosquitoes emerge. In the northern frontier there is the Mosquito-Mother Grass, within whose leaves blood-worms transform into mosquitoes. In Jiangdong there is the Mosquito-Mother Bird, also called—]

[The same work, entry on Acorn (橡実), Collection of Commentaries: Su Song says: The acorn is the fruit of the oak tree (*Quercus*). It is found throughout mountain valleys everywhere. The tree grows two to three *zhang* high, opens yellow flowers in the third and fourth months, and bears fruit in the eighth and ninth months, forming the acorn cups called *zaodou* (皁斗). Both *hu* oak (槲) and *li* oak (櫟) have acorn cups, but those of *li* oak are considered superior.]

[*Youyang Zazu* (酉陽雑俎) says: The *Qidun* tree (齊墩樹, olive) grows in Persia (波斯) and the country of Fulin (払林国, Byzantine/Syria). It reaches two to three *zhang* in height, has blue-white bark, and bears flowers resembling those of the pomelo, with an exceptional fragrance. The fruit resembles the star fruit (五歛子), and ripens in the fifth and sixth months. People of the Western Regions press it for oil, which they use to cook cakes and confections, in the same manner as people of China use sesame (巨勝).]
