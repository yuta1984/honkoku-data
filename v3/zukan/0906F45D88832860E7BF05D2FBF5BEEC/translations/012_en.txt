**Bumbojū (蚊母樹, Distylium racemosum / hiyonoki)**　[From the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目), under the collected explanations of the "mosquito mother bird" (蚊母鳥)]　
**Appendix: Benshiboku (蚊子木 / maruha-no-kihiyon)**　[From the same work's supplementary record on gadflies (蜚虻附録). Personal consideration: likely a variety of 蚊母樹]　
**Rekiboku (櫟木 / kunugi, sawtooth oak)**　[Same work, tree name under "acorn" (橡実)]　
**Seidonjū (斉墩樹 / chisha-no-ki)**　[From the *Youyang Zazu* (酉陽雑俎)]

---

The 蚊母樹 (hiyonoki) grows in the mountains. When planted in front of a garden, it is said to raise a wind and repel fire in the event of a conflagration. Its leaves remain green throughout the four seasons. In summer, new leaves appear, and from within the leaves, gall-like growths emerge; from inside these, tiny insects undergo metamorphosis and disperse. Furthermore, without producing visible flowers, it produces something resembling fruit directly on its branches. This is what is called "kodoshi (古度子 / hiyon)" [recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* under the supplementary entries for flowers and fruits (無花実附録)]. At first these are green, and they later turn brown. The interior is hollow (dongara), and the shell, though thin, is exceedingly hard. Insects are also generated within and emerge after metamorphosis. This is called "in-no-koto." These are insect galls (虫窠, nests of insects). Occasionally in summer, branches emerge from between the leaves and bear clusters of red flowers.

【Left folio】

After flowering, fruit is set. It is extremely hard, covered with brownish powder, and bears two spines at its apex. After autumn, it splits into two, revealing two seeds inside resembling grains of wheat, also extremely hard. This is the variety that is the female (雌) plant. There is another kind that bears neither flowers nor fruit—this is the male (雄) plant.

There is yet another variety whose leaves are somewhat rounded, and whose overall form does not differ much from the ordinary type, but which produces on its branches what appear to be perfectly round fruit. In color, these are first pale green and later turn brown. Their interior is hollow, and they likewise generate insects. This is thus an insect gall (虫窠). This corresponds to what is described in the supplementary record on gadflies (*Feibang fuji*) in the *Gangmu* as "the 蚊子木 (mosquito-offspring tree) found in Lingnan (嶺南)."

As for the **Rekiboku (櫟木 / kunugi, sawtooth oak)**, it is abundant in the mountains. Its leaves emerge in spring and broadly resemble those of the chestnut, with serrated (saw-toothed) margins. In summer, flowers appear among the leaves, resembling those of the oak (樫). Fruit is borne separately. At the base of each fruit there is a cupule (窠柏, hoso / involucre) that encloses half the fruit. This too resembles an acorn but is larger.
