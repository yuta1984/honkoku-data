**Mokuren (Magnolia liliiflora / Purple Magnolia)** [From *Bencao Gangmu* (Honzō Kōmoku); alternate name: Mokuren; Japanese-Chinese common name] — Appended entry: **Gyokuran (Magnolia denudata / White Magnolia)** [From *Mitan Huajing* (Hiden Kakyō)]

**[Right folio]**

It is cultivated in various places. In spring, at the tip of each branch, flowers with deep purple on the back (underside) and white on the front (upper side) open together with new leaves. Their shape resembles lotus blossoms. They have stamens/pistils and emit a fragrance. The petals number seven or eight. They do not bear fruit. Generally, all flowers open facing the sun (south); however, the flowers of this plant face entirely toward the shade (north). This is presumably because it is a yin-natured tree. In autumn, buds form. All face northward, their shape resembling the tip of a writing brush. After autumn (in winter), the leaves fall.

As for Gyokuran (White Magnolia), it flowers before its leaves appear. It blooms earlier than Mokuren, with eight or sometimes nine petals, pure white on both front and back surfaces, and its form resembles that of Mokuren. It has stamens/pistils and also emits a fragrance. It does bear fruit.

The fruit is reddish-purple in color, with a scale-like texture. At the top there is a skin of deep purple, and when split in two,

**[Left folio]**

inside there are seeds, which are vermilion in color. The overall shape resembles an insect holding a red ball in its mouth. They are very difficult to ripen fully, and many fall during summer. The flowers of this plant also face entirely toward the north (yin direction). In autumn, buds form at the tip of each branch and likewise face northward. After autumn, the leaves fall.

Furthermore, there is a variety whose petals are purple at the base, and which blooms at the same time as Mokuren. All of these propagate well by grafting. Their nature is wholly similar to that of Mokuren.

However, the old theory classified Gyokuran as a variety of Shinyi (辛夷, *Magnolia kobus*, Kobushi). Yet the fact that the flowers of Gyokuran face the north (yin direction) is a characteristic of Mokuren; Shinyi flowers do not face the north. Therefore, it is not appropriate to classify Gyokuran as a variety of Shinyi.
