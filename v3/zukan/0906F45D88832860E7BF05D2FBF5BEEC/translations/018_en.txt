**Left Page (左丁):**

**Citron (枸櫞, *kōen* / *marubu-shukan*)** 　[From the *Bencao Gangmu* / *Honzō Kōmoku*]

There is no domestically produced variety (Japanese-grown). Seeds transmitted from China are cultivated as potted plants. Its nature is particularly sensitive to cold. It flowers in summer, sets fruit, and after autumn ripens to a yellow color with a pleasant fragrance. The interior of the fruit contains neither pulp-sacs nor seeds, and when cut it resembles a daikon radish. One variety, the Buddha's hand citron (仏手柑, *busshukan*) [an alternate name for citron], likewise contains no pulp-sacs or seeds.

In general, fruits that contain no seeds have lost the natural principle by which plants propagate and perpetuate themselves. If they are not grafted or propagated by cuttings, the variety will eventually become extinct. This is something quite unlike the natural constitution of all ordinary plants and grasses.

The citrus fruits — kan (mandarin),橘 (tachibana/citrus), 橙 (daidai/bitter orange), and yuzu — all have small accessory leaves (wing leaves) at the base of their leaf stems; however, these two varieties (citron and Buddha's hand) lack such small leaves, bearing only the faintest trace of their vestigial presence.
