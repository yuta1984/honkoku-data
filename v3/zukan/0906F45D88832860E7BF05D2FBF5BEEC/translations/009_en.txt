All of these are varieties of haku (konotegashiwa / Oriental Arborvitae). Yōrakuhaku (itohiba / thread-leaf arborvitae) [from the Mitsuden Huajing] and Waikai (haibyakushin / creeping juniper) [from the Kotoyo Gengen] are likewise varieties within this group. Above all, henhaku (hinoki cypress) is regarded as the most prized — it is indeed the finest timber of Japan (Wakoku).

　　　　　　　　　[The Bencao Gangmu, in its section "Explanations of Names," states: Li Shizhen says, examining Wei Zicai's Liushu Jingyun: "All the ten thousand trees face the yang (south), but the haku alone points west. This is because it is a yin tree possessed of steadfast virtue; hence the character is written with the element 白 (white), as white corresponds to the west." Lu Dian's Piya states: "The haku pointing west is like a compass needle pointing south."

　　　　　　　　　The same work, in its "Collected Explanations" section, states: Li Shizhen says, "The Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian) refers to pine and cypress as the chiefs of the hundred trees. The tree stands tall and upright; its bark is thin; its grain is fine and smooth; its flowers are minute and delicate; its fruit forms a sphere, shaped like a small bell. After the frost, it splits into four, containing several seeds inside, each about the size of a grain of wheat, fragrant and delightful."]
