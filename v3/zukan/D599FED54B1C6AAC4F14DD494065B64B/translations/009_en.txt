　　[Appendix] Whales in icy seas eat *kawa-gaiko* (*mina*) [a small shellfish or crustacean]. Furthermore, *Seihonto* [referring to the water leopard, i.e., seals] steal the food of whales. It is said that when white fish (*shiro-uo*) are plentiful, whales gather in large numbers. According to the account of the Dutch, since the whale is such a fat-rich creature, it could hardly become so [large and fat] merely by eating *kawa-gaiko*; the details of its diet remain unknown.

**Teeth**

- *Hige* (baleen / whalebone)

　　[Appendix] *Hisubeen* [Dutch]; *Balein* [same (Dutch)]

**[Left Page]**

[The baleen] is found in the middle of the gill-cheek area, growing within the mouth in the manner of teeth. The color is black or pale-black, tinged with a faint green, and it has longitudinal and transverse grain patterns, resembling the horn of a water buffalo. [*Geishi* ("Records of Whales")]

Two species—the *ko-kujira* (gray whale) and the *iwashi-kujira* (sei whale)—have baleen that is white in color, with pale black longitudinal striations. [*Santei Geishi* ("Revised and Edited Records of Whales")]

The small ones measure one to three *shaku* [approx. 30–90 cm], the large ones up to one *jō* two *shaku* [approx. 360 cm]; thickness is three to four *bu* [approx. 9–12 mm] or as much as one *sun* [approx. 3 cm]; width is three to four *sun* [approx. 9–12 cm] or as much as one *shaku* [approx. 30 cm]; the total number is approximately over three hundred plates. The baleen grows on the upper jaw and is absent from the lower jaw. [*Geiki* ("Record of Whales")]
