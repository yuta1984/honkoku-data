**From the *Tashiki-hen* (多識篇, Encyclopedic Nomenclature)**

　　Black *Shii* (黒芝) *[colloquial names: Kurotake / Kuroshijikutake]*
　　　　　(alternate name: Genshii / 玄芝)

This fungus grows in shaded, damp ground and on rotting wood. It also grew on the water-worn boards at my own home — not on rotting wood, but sprouting through the gaps in the boards.

*(Lower right notation:)*
Two specimens obtained in the rain. Drawn from life in the rear garden on the 5th day of the 7th month of the Kinoto-Hitsuji year (1835), Mizunoe [cyclical day].

**Gosongān (胡孫眼)** — Japanese name: *Saruno koshikake* (Monkey's Bench Fungus) — a specimen with two tiers.

　　　The *Youyang Zazu* (酉陽雑俎) states: In Daibei (代北), there is something called "Jukei" (樹鶏, Tree Chicken), shaped like an inverted cup. It is commonly known as *Gosongān* (胡孫眼, "Monkey's Eyes").

　　　The author (Baien) remarks: "Jukei" refers to fungi that grow on trees and is not limited to *saruno koshikake* alone; it is a general term for all wood-growing fungi.
