### *Kayatake* / *Kawatake* / *Shishitake* / *Kōtake* (茅蕈; *Sarcodon imbricatus* or similar)　[Referenced in the *Xihu Youlan Zhi* (西湖遊覧志)]

The *kō-take* (革茸) is abundant in Arima, Settsu Province, and also found in various other provinces. When eaten raw, its flavor is bitter; but when dried in the shade and then eaten, the flavor is exceedingly fine and sweet. Those from Aki Province are of the finest quality.

---

### *Iwatake* (石耳; *Umbilicaria esculenta*)

It grows on large rocks in the mountains. Those found in high places are gathered by leaning ladders against the rock face and clinging to ropes. It is an especially fine product among all kinds of fungi. Its flavor is excellent and good. When dried, it is even more so.

　　　　　　　　　Both varieties drawn from life on the 7th day of the 9th month of the *Hinoe-saru* year.

---

### Stone Ear (*Iwatake*) and Miraculous Fungus (*Reishi*)

According to the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目; *Honzō Kōmoku*): The *Iwatake* grows on rocky cliffs in various mountains. Its shape resembles wood-ear fungus (*kikurage*; *Auricularia*). Viewed from a distance, it looks like smoke. After harvesting, it is dried in the sun, and the sand and soil are thoroughly washed away. When prepared as a vegetable dish (such as with eggplant), it surpasses wood-ear fungus in quality. If eaten over a long period, it improves one's complexion; even into old age, the complexion does not deteriorate. It also brightens the eyes and replenishes one's vital essence (*sei*).

In our country (Japan), it is found on high mountains throughout the land.
