### *Nezumi-shimeji* (Mouse Shimeji) / Also: *Shiba-take*

There is another mushroom also called *Nezumi-shimeji*. It is also known as *Shiba-take*. It is edible, and its flavor is the same as shimeji.

---

### As the *Jōjuku-ken-shi* (常熟縣志, Gazetteer of Changshu County) states:

**Hóng-gū** (*Benitake* / *Shimokoshi*)　[Poisonous Mushroom]

**Yān-zhī-gū** (*Benitake*)〔*Fumu Fushi* (福母府志) · *Hakkoku Tsūshi* (八国通志)〕— It has a powerful poison. Those who eat it vomit blood that cannot be stopped, and many die.

---

### *Kenneifushi* (建寧府志, Gazetteer of Jianning Prefecture)

**Hóng-jūn** (Red Mushroom)
　Commonly called *Zhū-gū* ("Vermillion Mushroom"). This is the same as *Yān-zhī-gū* above.

### *Yáng-dù-cài* (羊肚菜, *Ikuchi*)

The *Honkō* (*Bencao Gangmu*, Compendium of Materia Medica) states: "The *yáng-dù-cài* has a shape resembling a sheep's stomach (*dù*) and bears a honeycomb-like (*fēng-kē*) surface" — this description refers to precisely this mushroom.

### *Maboku* (馬勃, *Kitsune-no-chabukuro* / *Kitsune-bokuri*, Puffball)

- *Mimitsubushi* — as listed in the *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草, Japanese Materia Medica)
- *Yabu-dama* ("Thicket Ball")
- *Hokori-take* ("Dust Mushroom")
- Listed under the moss (*苔類*) section of the *Bencao Gangmu*. Not a food item.
- Also: *Yabu-dama* 〔*Heian* (平安)〕
- *Ishi-no-wata* ("Stone Cotton") 〔same source〕
- *Tsubo-take* (*Tsumbo-take*) ("Deaf Mushroom") 〔same source〕
- *Hokori-take* 〔same source〕
- The smaller variety is called *Yama-tsuguchi* ("Mountain Pine-mouth") in Tsukushi (Kyushu).
