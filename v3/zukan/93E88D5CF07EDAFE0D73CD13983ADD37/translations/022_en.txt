## Ground Mushrooms (地蕈類, *Jijin-rui*)

### Matsutake (松蕈, *Tricholoma matsutake*)

○ The *Kinpu* (菌譜) states: "Matsutake grows in the shade of pine trees. Everything that comes from the pine is something one cannot help but love. It is effective when eaten to treat *sōdaku fukin* (turbid, uncontrolled urination)."

○ The *Dongui Bogam* (東医宝鑑, Korean medical encyclopedia) states: "Pine mushroom (*Matsutake*): its nature is balanced (*hei*), its flavor is sweet, and it is non-toxic. It is exceedingly fragrant and delicious. Imbued with the essence of pine, it grows beneath old pine trees deep in the mountains, drawing upon the pine's vital essence to live. It is the foremost among all tree mushrooms."

○ Matsutake grows in abundance in the ninth and tenth months (September–October). That its flavor and fragrance are both utterly perfect, making it the finest among all tree mushrooms, is truly so. The produce of Yamashiro Province (*Sanshū*) is the best; it grows there in great abundance. It is said that it will not grow unless the pine is a *me-matsu* (female pine; i.e., the Japanese red pine, *Pinus densiflora*). In the western provinces, where *o-matsu* (male pine; i.e., the Japanese black pine, *Pinus thunbergii*) are plentiful, matsutake is scarce, while *bukuryō* (茯苓, poria mushroom, *Wolfiporia extensa*) is abundant. In the Kinki region, female pines are numerous, and poria is scarce. Dried matsutake also retains good fragrance and flavor. It is preserved in salt and sent to distant places. There is virtually no ordinary cooking in which matsutake is not used.

○ Wherever matsutake grows, a wonderful fragrance invariably envelops those who approach. At the very spot where it grows, an extremely white mold-like substance appears, resembling a white skin. When this is pierced, white powder is found inside, with the mushroom lying horizontal within. Then, gradually, it rises upright and pushes through the ground to emerge. Although it is said that matsutake grows by drawing upon the essence of pine, it emerges from the soil and thus belongs to the category of ground mushrooms (*jijin*), not tree mushrooms (*moku-jin*). It does not exist in the Kantō region but is found abundantly throughout the provinces of the Kansai. In Saga (near Kyoto), four grades are recognized: the finest is called *Fuse-Shaka* ("Reclining Buddha"). Its stem is extremely thick and large, and it juts out laterally, lying low along the ground. Its color is pure white, it has a fine fragrance, and its flavor is superb. The next grade is called *Uzura-take* ("Quail mushroom"), which resembles the scales of a pine and has a mottled skin like a quail. The next is called *Tora-fu* ("Tiger marking"), which has horizontal bands of white, brown, and red intermixed. The last is ordinary matsutake. The produce of Tanba Province is generally dark in color and inferior in flavor.

---

○ When matsutake first emerges, the stem is short; as days pass, the stem grows. The cap is round, and the underside (gills) is not yet visible — it is as if a thin sheet of paper has been stretched across it. Eventually, the cap opens of its own accord, the thin membrane breaks open, and the gills on the underside are revealed. In salted mushrooms, this stage is ultimately difficult to distinguish. The fresh mushroom is taken as the true (unaltered) form.

*gather the lotus flowers on the banks of the Kamo River.*

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　(Teika)

　　　　　　　　　　　Drawn and recorded on the 3rd day of the 9th month, *Hinoe-Saru* year [1836].

The matsutake market beside the Tenma market is considered the foremost among all the provinces. The area from the north end of Tenma Bridge to the north end of Tenjin Bridge in Osaka is known as the "Tenma-ichi district." Large markets for fresh vegetables, dried goods, and the like thrive there day and night. During the matsutake season, the mushrooms are sold in the market in the middle of the night by torchlight. Vast quantities pour in from the mountains of Nose and Katsuo in Settsu Province. In Kyoto, there is a matsutake market along Takakura Street, below Nishiki, which is especially prosperous. Large quantities of matsutake from Fushimi Inari-yama, Takao-yama, Ryōan-ji, and other locations in Kyoto are transported there.
