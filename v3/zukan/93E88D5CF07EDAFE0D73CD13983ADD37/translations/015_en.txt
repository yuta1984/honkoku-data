**[Right Folio, Upper Section]**

From the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目, *Compendium of Materia Medica*) and the *Bamin Tongzhi* (八閩通志, *Comprehensive Gazetteer of Fujian Province*):

***Kōjin* (香蕈 — Shiitake)**　　In common usage, the characters 椎箪 are employed.

The *Bamin Tongzhi* and *Fuzhou Fuzhi* (福州府志, *Fuzhou Prefectural Gazetteer*) state: "The fragrant mushroom (*xiāngxùn*) grows on rotten wood in deep forests; its flavor is supremely delicious."

The *Hengyue Zhi* (衡岳志, *Gazetteer of Mount Heng*) states: "The larger fragrant mushrooms are as large as a plate (*ban*). In spring, the people of the mountains often gather and eat them fresh."

The *Quanzhou Fuzhi* (泉州府志, *Quanzhou Prefectural Gazetteer*) states: "They grow on rotting wood (*kuaia-mu*) and are called 'fragrant rush mushroom' (*xiānggū*); they are also known as 'wood-ear' (*mù'ěr*)."　《Interlinear note: 'Wood-ear' (*mù'ěr*) is the same name as *kikurage* (木クラゲ, jelly ear fungus)》　In the *Bencao* (materia medica), the purple-colored variety is called *xiāngxùn* (香蕈).

"Fragrant mushroom" (*kōkin* / 香菌) is classified as a type of *shii-kin* (椎菌, oak mushroom). However, the passage stating that the purple-colored variety is called *kōkin* likely refers specifically to shiitake mushrooms.

Matsutake and shiitake are the finest among all mushrooms, being perfect in both aroma and flavor. Those currently produced from various provinces are mostly cultivated rather than wild. The variety grown on *shii* (Castanopsis) wood is considered the finest. In the western provinces, it is said that they are often cultivated on *kashi* (oak, *Quercus*) wood. Enoki (*Celtis sinensis*) wood can also be used in the same way, but the aroma and flavor are not as complete. Most are dried and sold as merchandise. Shiitake vary considerably in size, with no uniformity. Extremely small ones are called *tsubu-shiitake* ("grain shiitake").

Dried shiitake soaked in water, with fine rice bran kneaded in, are arranged with their stems set in rows; after passing one night, they become fresh shiitake again. The flavor is supremely fine.

**[Right Folio, Lower Right Section]**

Shiitake with unopened caps (before the cap has spread) are called *tsubu-shiitake* ("grain shiitake") and are sold sun-dried. The large, hand-selected ones are called *eri-shiitake* ("choice shiitake"), while those consisting only of small specimens are simply called *shiitake*. They are particularly prized in Buddhist temple households. Rather than waiting for them to grow naturally, many people cultivate them artificially, causing them to fruit abundantly in two seasons — spring and autumn. The cultivation method is similar to that used for enoki mushrooms (*榎葺き*, enoki-fuki). Shii wood effectively eliminates the smell of straw and bran. Produce from sun-facing (south-facing) growing spots is considered superior.
