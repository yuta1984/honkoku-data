**[Right Folio, Upper Section]**

**As recorded in the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目, *Compendium of Materia Medica*) and the *Bamin Tongzhi* (八閩通志, *General Gazetteer of the Eight Min Regions*):**

***Kōshin* (香蕈, Shiitake)** — In common usage, the characters 椎蕈 (*Shii-take*) are used.

The *Bamin Tongzhi* and *Fuzhou Fuzhi* state: The *xiāng xùn* (香蕈) grows atop rotting wood in deep forests, and its flavor is exceedingly fine.

The *Hengyue Zhi* states: The *xiāng jùn* (香菌), when large, is the size of a plate (*ban*). In springtime, mountain people frequently harvest and eat them fresh.

The *Quanzhou Fuzhi* states: It grows upon rotting wood and is called *xiāng gū* (香菰); it is also known as *mù ěr* (木耳, "wood ear").
　　(Marginal note: *Mù ěr* is the same name as *ki-kurage* / wood ear fungus)
In the *Bencao Gangmu*, those of purple color are designated *xiāng xùn* (香蕈). The *xiāng jùn* is classified as a type of *shii-kin* (椎菌 / *Castanopsis* mushroom). Yet it is also stated that those of purple color are named *xiāng jùn*. This likely refers to shiitake.

Both *matsutake* (pine mushroom) and shiitake rank as the finest among all mushrooms, being perfectly endowed with both fragrance and flavor. They are now produced in various provinces, and the majority are cultivated and harvested by the grower's own effort. Those that grow on the shii tree (*Castanopsis*) are considered the best. In the western provinces (western domains of Japan), it is said that many are cultivated on oak (*kashi*) trees. Hackberry (*enoki*) wood can also be used in the same manner, though the fragrance and flavor are not fully developed. Most are dried and sold as commercial goods. Shiitake vary greatly in size; the extremely small ones are called "*Tsubu-shiitake*" (grain/pellet shiitake).

Dried shiitake, when re-hydrated with water, with the small ones kneaded and the stems arranged and planted, become fresh shiitake after a single night. The flavor is exceedingly fine.

**[Right Folio, Lower Right Section]**

Shiitake in which the cap has not yet opened are called "*Tsubu-shiitake*" (bud shiitake), and these are blanched and dried. Large ones that have been sorted and selected are called "*Eri-shiitake*" (select shiitake), and those consisting only of small ones are simply called "*Shiitake*." They are especially prized in Buddhist temples and monastic households. Many people do not wait for them to grow naturally but cultivate them artificially. They grow in dense clusters mainly in the two seasons of spring and autumn. The cultivation method is the same as that for hackberry (*enoki*) thatching cultivation. Shiitake effectively removes the odors of straw and herbs. Those produced in sunny, sun-facing locations are considered the highest quality.
