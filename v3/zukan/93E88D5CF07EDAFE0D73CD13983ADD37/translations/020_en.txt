**Yanagi-take (柳菌, Willow mushroom)**

This mushroom grows on the partially rotted portions of standing willow trees. It resembles the mushroom that grows on paper mulberry (*kōzo*) trees. When eaten, its flavor is excellent and it is light in nature (easily digestible).

　　Two varieties

　　　　26th day after rain in the 8th month of the *Hinoto-Tori* year (丁酉, 1837),
　　　　collected in the forest and drawn from life.

---

Around the Jizōdō area of Shinshū, river willows (*kawaya-nagi*) are abundant, and mushrooms grow prolifically at their roots. The cap is blackish-gray in color, and the underside is black. The flavor is quite good. However, it was told (by locals) that eating these mushrooms and then drinking sake will invariably cause stomachache. Furthermore, this mushroom differs greatly from the willow mushroom illustrated here.

---

**Nire-take (楡菌, Elm mushroom)**

Grows on rotting elm (*nire*) wood. It resembles the chestnut mushroom (*Kuri-take*) but is fragile when raw. Its flavor is good and it is edible.
