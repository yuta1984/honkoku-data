**[Author's note (Baien's record):]**

The head priest of a temple in Mito (*Suifu*) told me: In Mito, the early variety of matsutake is called *samatsu* (早松). What is called *samatsu* in Edo (*Tōbu*) is there called *Tsuchi-mukuri* ("earth-heaver"). The *samatsu* of Musashino (Edo) grows like a single root of a sweet potato, splits open of its own accord, and sends forth its cap. Because it emerges from the soil with earth clinging to its head like a crown, it has presumably earned the name *Tsuchi-mukuri* ("earth-heaver"). Since there is no matsutake to be found in the nearby mountains of Edo, the *samatsu* is especially prized there.

Subsequently, on the 8th day of the 9th month of the first year of Kaei (1848), I climbed Mount Takao in Tama District, Musashi Province. That very day, *samatsu* were growing on the mountain. As it was raining, I was unable to venture deep into the forest, and in the end I could not gather any. In the mountains there was a single tea house whose proprietor had gathered many *samatsu* to sell. The largest among them had caps reaching nearly one *shaku* (approx. 30 cm) in diameter. The charcoal-maker told me these all came from within this mountain.
