### From the *Honkō* (*Bencao Gangmu*, Compendium of Materia Medica), entry on *Guǐ-gài* (鬼蓋):

**One variety: *Dì-qín* (地芩)**〔*Asa-take* / *Ichinichi-take*, "Morning Mushroom" / "One-Day Mushroom"〕

This mushroom shuns sunlight — it sprouts in the morning and withers by evening. It grows in mountain fields, the corners of residential gardens, and shaded, damp ground. There are a great many unnamed fungal varieties; some are poisonous. Edible ones are few.

### As listed in *Igan-sai Kinpin* (怡顔斎菌品):

***Tsuyu-zaemon*** (梅雨左衛門, "Rainy Season Zaemon")　〔*Yūrei-gusa*, "Ghost Grass"〕

　Among gardeners and horticulturalists, it is called *Ginryūsō* ("Silver Dragon Plant").
　It is also referred to as *Kusa-jūyō* (*Cǎo-cōng-róng*, "Herbal Cistanche"). I have published detailed illustrations and evidence for this in my *Sōmoku Gafu* (草木画譜, Picture Album of Plants and Trees).

---

　　7th month, 29th day of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year [1835] — drawn from life, from specimens grown in my own garden.

---

### *Sasa-take* (笹々, "Bamboo-grass Mushroom")

　This mushroom is edible. It is non-poisonous.

　　5th month, 26th day of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year [1835] — drawn from life.

### From *Kinpin* (菌品):

**Dō-kin / *Tsuchi-take*** (土菌, "Earth Mushroom")　〔Have not heard of anyone actually eating this〕

〔Alternate names: *Dù-xùn* (杜蕈), *Dì-xùn* (地蕈), *Dì-jī* (地鶏), *Gū-zi* (菰子), *Zhāng-tóu* (獐頭)〕

The *Honkō* states: "The *tǔ-jūn* (earth mushroom) grows in the soil. It is commonly said to be formed from the vapors (*qi*) of poisonous insects, and it is toxic. Eating it will kill a person. It is liable to be confused with the *é-gāo-xùn* (鵞膏蕈, goose-fat mushroom) found in the mountains."

---

　　Same year (*Kinoto-Hitsuji*), *Mizunoe* (*Jin*) 7th month, 6th day — drawn from life by the master of the rear garden.

---

### *Hebi-take* (蛇茸, "Snake Mushroom")

A mushroom resembling *Hatsu-take* (初茸, *Lactarius* sp.) but lacking gills on the underside is called *Hebi-take*. It is powerfully poisonous. In general, any mushroom that lacks gills on its underside is entirely poisonous and must not be eaten.

---

### From *Kinpin*　〔Poisonous Mushrooms〕

- **Abura-Ikuchi** (Oil Ikuchi)
- ***Hebi-take*** ("Snake Mushroom")
- ***Uji-take*** ("Maggot Mushroom")　〔common name〕

　　When this mushroom grows old and rots, it dissolves and turns into maggots (*uji*). The manner in which they move is exceedingly strange.
