## *Hiratake* (Oyster Mushroom)

**From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会):**

***Gyoku-jin* (玉蕈; *Abura-shimeji* / *Hahaso-shimeji*)** · ***Hen-jō* (匾茸)**

Its color is a light yellow, resembling the color of oil, and thus the people of the countryside call it "*Abura-shimeji*" (油シメジ, "Oil *Shimeji*"). This is distinct from a summer variety that bears the same name. This mushroom grows abundantly in groves of *hahaso* (柞; oak/konara trees), and when eaten its flavor is excellent — it is considered the finest among all *shimeji* varieties.

**From the *Chōmonjū* (著聞集):**

> *Hiratake* wa / yoki musha ni koso / nitari kere
> Osoroshi nagara / sasuga / mimahoshi

*("The oyster mushroom resembles a fine warrior indeed — fearsome to behold, yet one cannot help but want to look upon it.")*

— *Kujō Daijō-daijin* (九條大政大臣; Grand Minister of State of the Kujō clan)

---

All three varieties are edible, and their flavors are exceptionally fine.
*Daikoku-shimeji* is of the highest flavor. *Abura-shimeji* is also delicious. *Murasaki-jin* (紫蕈) is inferior in flavor.
