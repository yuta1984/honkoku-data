---

#### *Matsutsuyu* / *Shōro* (松露; Truffle-like Fungi) — Alternative name: *Jijin* (地腎, "Earth Kidney")

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Drawn from life on the 7th day of the 9th month of the *Hinoe-saru* year.

They grow abundantly underground in sandy soil in pine groves. Their color is slightly reddish-brown, and in shape some are round while others are flattened.

The *Chuzan Denshinroku* (中山伝信録; *Record of Transmissions from the Ryūkyū Kingdom*) states: "The *shōro* enters the earth and transforms into a fungus. Its shape resembles a taro, and its flavor is delicious."

Furthermore, the *Guangdong Xinyu* (広東新語; *New Discourse on Guangdong*) states: "The *Jijin* (地腎) is likely formed by the vital resinous essence of the pine tree accumulating and steaming within the earth. Alternatively, it is said to form from pine pollen falling to the ground. It is also called *Shōkō* (松黄, 'Pine Yellow'). Its flavor is sweet and delicious, and it is well worth eating."

There is one variety called *kuri-shōro* (栗松露, "Chestnut Truffle"), which is yellow in color and also of excellent flavor. That which is slightly darker in color is called *mugi-shōro* (麦松露, "Wheat Truffle"), and this is the variety depicted in the illustration. The white-colored variety is called *kome-shōro* (米松露, "Rice Truffle"). Those produced in Fujisawa, Sagami Province, are of fine flavor when fresh. They grow abundantly in the third month. When old, they release a cloud of dust-like spores.

> *Shōro* are produced in many places, but among them, the pine groves of Chikuzen and Iki, Miho in Suruga Province, Mihara, and the Sumiyoshi pine groves of Settsu Province are especially well known. They are also found in various locations in Musashi Province, including the area around Inokashira Benzaiten, Fukagawa Sunamura, and the Chōshū domain's estate, where they grow abundantly.

### According to the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目): a variety of *Kikkai* (鬼蓋, "Demon's Cap")

#### *Chigon* (地芩)

#### *Kitsune-take* (狐蕈; Foxes' Mushroom)　[One variety: *Kitsune-take*]

It grows along the paths through the bamboo grove in my own garden. Its shape resembles a fox's parasol (*kitsune-no-kasa*), with a stipe measuring five or six *sun* (approximately 15–18 cm) in length. It is poisonous and must not be eaten.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Collected and drawn from life on the 2nd day of the 10th month of the *Hinoe-saru* year.
