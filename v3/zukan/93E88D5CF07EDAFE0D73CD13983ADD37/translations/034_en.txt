## *Karakasa-take* (Parasol Mushroom) — one variety: *Tengu no kasa* (Tengu's Umbrella)

On the 7th day of the 8th month of the *Tenpō Hinoe-Saru* year (1836), waiting for clear weather, I traveled to Nemura in Itabashi, Bukō (Edo). Matsugoro, a peasant of Nemura whom I had known for many years, agreed to help me collect mushrooms, and he took a day off from his lodging to guide me along the way. We entered the mountains seven or eight *chō* from Matsugoro's lodging in Nemura, making our way toward a place called Nishi-Tsuhara in Nemura, gathering mushrooms as we went along the path. We finally arrived at Nishi-Tsuhara. The pine forest there stretched approximately one *ri* in all directions — east and west indistinguishable — and by the evening hours, ○ (*shimeji*) were abundant. We collected many varieties along the way, and after spending half a day in the mountains, we finally returned home — it was already evening. The following day, the 8th, I drew each specimen from life. The names of the mushrooms illustrated here are recorded in the local dialect of the people of Nemura. Mushrooms are also plentiful at a place called Hikawa-yama, in the vicinity of Nishi-Tsuhara.

---

**Three varieties exist — all highly poisonous and must not be eaten.**

### *Jin-kasa-take* / *Kasa-take* (Cap Mushroom)

[From the *Honzō Kōmoku* (Bencao Gangmu):]

　**Guī gài** (鬼蓋; *Kuikai*) — also known as: *Kitsune no kasa* (Fox's Umbrella), *Oni-kasa* (Demon's Cap), *Ji-gai* (Ground Cover), *Oni-ya* (Demon's House), *Chō-sei* (Morning-born). Commonly called "Fox's Umbrella."

From the same work:

*"Guī gài* is also a type of earth fungus (*tsuchikin*); it grows in clusters in shaded areas near posts and pillars. In summer, when rain falls, it immediately sprouts — born in the morning and dead by evening. Its color is red, the cap is black, and the stalk is red. Those with a yellowish-white color are each called *Ji-qín* (地芩 / *Chigon*)."
