### *Keiso* / *Netsumitake* (鶏㙡) *(yellow variety)*

The *keiso* (*keitei*) has an exceptionally fine flavor, second only to *shimeji* (占地). One may eat it in large quantities without any ill effect — it is non-toxic.

　　　　　　　　　　Collected in the mountains of Nemura, Itabashi, Bukō (Edo region). Drawn from life.

### *Makotake* / *Mabashira* / *Magotake* (蘑菰蕈) *(also called* Niku-take*; colloquially* Mashi-take*)*

According to the *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目, the great Chinese materia medica), the *makotake* is cultivated by burying mulberry, boar-thorn, or other such woods in the ground, watering them with rice-washing water, and then waiting for the mushrooms to emerge before harvesting. At two to three *sun* in size, with a small base, before the cap has fully opened it is white, soft, and hollow within, its form resembling the flower of a *giboshi* (玉簪, hosta) that has not yet opened. It is colloquially called "*keikutsumakotake*" (鶏腿磨菰) because its flavor resembles that of chicken. It is also said to promote longevity. There is a separate variety called the "white *makotake*" (白磨菰蕈), which is identical in form.

### *Shike-take*

This is a variety of *Komusō-take* (虚無僧タケ), but without the "robe" (outer veil/skirt), having only a cap. From the white opening at the top of the cap, the stem is hollow within. Its markings are like threads woven into a net, and its root resembles a taro. It is exceedingly heavy for its size. The color of the cap is black like lacquer, as though the lacquer has dried. When touched with the hand, it sticks like *mochi* (rice cake). It is a poisonous mushroom. Its smell is exceedingly foul.

　　　　　　　　　　Collected on the 2nd day of the 9th month of the *Hinoe-saru* year (丙申, 1836), in the wooded grove of the rear garden.
　　　　　　　　　　Drawn from life.
