　　　　　　　　**Reishi (Lingzhi / Sacred Mushroom)**

According to the *Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica), there are an exceedingly large number of varieties of *zhi* (sacred fungi), including some that bear flowers and fruit. They are classified and named by the Six Zhi: blue-green, red, yellow, white, purple, and black. It is said they are born from the essence of mountains, rivers, rain, clouds, the four seasons, the five phases, yin and yang, and day and night, producing the Five-Colored Sacred Fungi, and they are considered auspicious omens for a sage ruler. They sprout in the sixth month; in spring they are blue-green, in summer purple, in autumn white, and in winter black. Their flavor follows their color — blue-green is sour, yellow is sweet, and so forth. In all, there are several hundred varieties, including Stone Zhi, Wood Zhi, Flesh Zhi, and Fungal Zhi.

【Left Folio】
**Stone Zhi (Sekishi)**　Having the form of stone, it grows at the edges of seaside rocks, stone mountains, and island shores.

**Flesh Zhi (Nikushi)**　It resembles flesh, attaches itself to great rocks, and is a living thing possessing both head and tail. It is red like coral, white like sliced fat, lustrous like *ze-qi* (a plant), blue-green like kingfisher feathers, and yellow like purple gold. All are luminous and translucent, like solid ice. The larger specimens weigh over ten *kin* (Chinese pounds); the smaller, three to four *kin*.

**Fungal Zhi (Kinshi)**　It grows deep in the mountains, atop great trees or beside spring waters. Its form may resemble a palace, a dragon or tiger, a carriage and horse, or a flying bird. Its five colors are not constant.

**Wood Zhi (Mokushi)**　There are several varieties. *Hisetsu-shi* grows on thousand-year-old pine trees and contains resin within the bark, shaped like something in flight. If consumed, it grants long life.

**Mokuyo Zhi (Mokukyo-shi)**　It grows beneath the roots of thousand-year-old *Phellodendron* (cork tree), and has fine, thread-like roots. If consumed, one becomes a *chisen* (earthly immortal).

**Thousand-Year Zhi (Sensei-shi)**　It grows on the roots beneath a dead tree and resembles a seated person. If carved, blood flows from it. If this blood is applied to both feet, one may walk upon water and render oneself invisible. It may also cure illness.

Li Shizhen once expressed his doubt: *zhi* are generated from the residual energy of rot and decay, just as tumors and warts grow upon the human body. And yet, throughout all ages, people have regarded them as auspicious herbs. The claim that consuming them can make one immortal is, he concluded, a complete error.

The *Youyang Zazu* (Miscellaneous Morsels from Youyang) states: Sometimes, without reason, *zhi* will grow on the pillars of a house. White ones foretell bereavement; red ones, bloodshed; black ones, theft; yellow ones, joyous events. Those shaped like a human face foretell loss of wealth; those shaped like an ox or horse foretell conscription to distant labor; those shaped like a turtle or snake foretell a poor silkworm harvest. This is precisely what I wished to say.

Terajima Ryōan writes: There are many varieties of *zhi*, but Stone Zhi and Flesh Zhi are exceedingly rare and extraordinary. The kind most commonly seen resembles a matsutake mushroom but is large, firm, and solid; its underside is white and smooth; it may have a stipe or be without one. It is found among the root stumps of large trees, and is popularly called *Saru-no-koshikake* ("Monkey's Seat"). This is likely what is referred to as a variety of *Mokuyo Zhi*.
