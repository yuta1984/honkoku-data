**As stated in the *Tashiki* (多識篇, *Tashiki-hen*)**

**Saicachi-take (梍莢蕈, *Gleditsia*-wood mushroom)**

This mushroom grows from the rotting wood of the *saicachi* (*Gleditsia japonica*, Japanese honey locust). It does not grow as prolifically as the chestnut mushroom (*kuri-take*). I cultivated it in my back garden by pouring viscous/moist water over it and caused it to grow. Its texture is brittle and fragile, and it cannot be eaten.

---

**Kashi-take (樫菌, Oak mushroom)**

This mushroom grows abundantly on rotting oak (*kashi*) wood. By pouring viscous water over a suitable support structure, one can cause it to grow naturally. It is widely cultivated in the western provinces (western Japan). When dried, some people sell it fraudulently as shiitake. Someone of my acquaintance from Bishū (備州, the collective name for Bizen, Bitchū, and Bingo provinces) told me of this. In its fresh, raw form, it resembles the shiitake grown by the *shii-buki* cultivation method.

Drawn from life in the back garden, acquired on the 29th day of the intercalary 7th month of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year (乙未, 1835).

In the *Mizunoe* (壬) seventh month of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year (乙未, 1835), Mr. Doi produced a specimen that had grown on rotting cedar (*sugi*) wood in the mountains. I requested it and drew it from life. This was fifteen days after it was obtained.
