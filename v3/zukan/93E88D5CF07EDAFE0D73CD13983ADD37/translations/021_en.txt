**Aogiri-take (梧桐菌, Chinese parasol tree mushroom)**

Grows on the rotting wood of the Chinese parasol tree (*Firmiana simplex*). The mushroom resembles *Oshi-take* (a type of oyster mushroom) or *Kashi-take* (oak mushroom). When dried, it looks like *Oshi-take*. It is edible. However, it does not grow in great abundance. There was one Chinese parasol tree in my garden, but it eventually died. I left the wood as it was, and later, after it was used as fence stakes in the back garden, mushrooms grew from it — just as depicted in the illustration.

　　　　　　　　　　On the 4th day of the 9th month of Bunsei 8 (1825), the *Hinoto-Tori* year,
　　　　　　　　　　three mushrooms were obtained from the back garden and drawn from life.

**Take-dake (竹菌, Bamboo mushroom)**
[Alternative names: *Take* (Tashiki-hen), *Joku-jin* (蕈), *Chikudai* (拾遺), *Chikuko* (綱目), *Takeni* (竹蕈)]

Grows on bamboo stumps. It resembles *Babi-take* (a coral/broom mushroom) and is extremely fragile. The variety that grows on bitter bamboo (*Phyllostachys bambusoides*) is highly poisonous.

---

**Egoma-giri-take / Aburagiri-take (罌子桐蕈, Tung oil tree mushroom)**

Grows on the stumps of the tung oil tree (*Vernicia fordii*, 油桐). It resembles *shimeji* mushrooms and also resembles *Nara-take* (oak mushroom / *Armillaria* species). Its flavor is not yet known. It grows in large, dense clusters.
