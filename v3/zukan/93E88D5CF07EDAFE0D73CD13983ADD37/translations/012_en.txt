**Kosongan (胡孫眼)** — One variety

Japanese names: *Kumo-take* (雲タケ, "cloud mushroom")
　　　　　　　　*Mannin-yuge* (マンチンユゲ, "ten-thousand-year mushroom")
　　　　　　　　*Iwai-take* (イワイタケ, "rock mushroom") / *Kuro-koke* (黒コケ, "black moss")

**[Right folio, lower right]**

There is another unrelated species under the same name "ground mushroom" (*jijin* / 地蕈); these are distinct. This mushroom grows mostly on rotting oak (*nara*) wood, rising up as if clouds were billowing forth. It grows abundantly in damp, shaded places. It resembles layers of *saruno-koshikake* (shelf fungus) stacked upon one another. Its cap is thin. It is called "*Mannen-take*" (the ten-thousand-year mushroom), sharing its name with *reishi* (霊芝, *Ganoderma* sp.).

　　　　　　　　　　　　　Drawn from life in my own garden.

**[Left folio, upper right]**

*From the Tashiki (多識)*

**Kikurage (木耳, wood-ear fungus)** 《interlinear note: *Kino-take* / *Kikurage*》 — Also known as: *Mokuju* (木檽), *Mokkin* (木菌), *Mokushō* (木樅), *Jukei* (樹鶏), *Mokuga* (木蛾)

　　The variety growing on elderwood (*niwatoko* / 接骨木) is considered the finest grade. [Continues to the left]

Li Shizhen (李時珍) states: Wood-ear fungus (*mù ěr*) grows upon rotting wood and has neither branches nor leaves. It is born of the residual *qi* of damp heat. Whether it is wholesome or toxic necessarily follows the nature of the tree upon which it grows. The *Bencao* (materia medica) lists five types of wood-ear: those growing on mulberry (*kuwa* / 桑), Chinese scholar tree (*enju* / 槐), elm (*nire* / 楡), willow (*yanagi* / 柳), and paper mulberry (*kōzo* / 楮). All of these — both the soft variety (*kikurage*) and the hard variety (*saruno-koshikake*) — are referred to as "wood-ear" (*mù ěr*). Of these, the wood-ear growing on elderwood (*niwatoko* / 接骨木) is considered the superior grade. Those that change color upon being brought back after harvesting, or that glow when observed at night, are all poisonous. Those who suffer poisoning from them are treated with the juice of winter melon (*tōgan* / 冬瓜) vine as an antidote.
