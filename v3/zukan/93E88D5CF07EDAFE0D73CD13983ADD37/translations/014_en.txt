**From the *Kinpu* (Fungus Register) of Sakamoto Kōzen**

　　***Take no Ishizue*** (Bamboo Stone-Staff / Bracket Fungus of Bamboo)

Found in abundance in the damp, moist areas of bamboo groves, growing clasped around the old roots of dried, dead bamboo where small bamboo grass (*ko-zasa*) grows thickly. It resembles the Hard Wood-Ear (*Sarunokoshire* / bracket fungus) but is soft and contains some moisture. It is said to come in two forms: *Shiramaki* (white-rolled) and *Murasaki-maki* (purple-rolled).

　　　　　　　　　　　　On the twenty-sixth day of the eighth month,
　　　　　　　　　　　　the *Hinoto-Tori* year (丁酉, 1837), I obtained
　　　　　　　　　　　　this in a bamboo grove and drew it from life.

**[Left folio]**

***Risuju-kin* — *Sumomo-take*** (Plum Tree Mushroom)

Grows on the rotting wood of a plum (*sumomo*, *Prunus salicina*) tree in the back garden. It resembles the *Nara-shimeji* (Oak Shimeji) and has a pleasant fragrance. It also resembles the *Kuri-take* (Chestnut Mushroom). It is not poisonous when eaten. Obtained and drawn from life on the last day (*misoka*) of the eighth month of the *Hinoto-Tori* year (丁酉, 1837).
