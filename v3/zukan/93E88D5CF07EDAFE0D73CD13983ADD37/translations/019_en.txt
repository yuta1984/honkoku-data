**Nara-take / Nara-motashi (楢菌, Oak mushroom)**　　　**Kuri-take (栗菌, Chestnut mushroom)**

This mushroom grows abundantly on dead *awa* (millet) wood. I cultivated it in my back garden, watering it morning and evening with a viscous liquid (*nebari-mizu*) to encourage its growth. Its flavor is excellent and it is considered a particular delicacy. Non-toxic.

**Keyaki-take (欅蕈, Zelkova mushroom)**

Nowadays, rather than waiting for *Enoki-take*, *Ryaku-take*, and *Kashi-take* to grow naturally, one can cultivate them artificially. Cut down a large tree, plant the log firmly in the ground, cover the portion of the stump that protrudes above the surface (about five to seven sun [≈15–21 cm]) with a straw mat, pour rice-rinsing water (*kome no nuka-mizu*) over it repeatedly, and after some days have passed, mushrooms will grow abundantly. This method works equally well for various types of trees.

**Namera-susuki-hana (滑煤華, Smooth-stalk mushroom)** *(Reference: Wakan Sansai Zue and Kinpu)*

This mushroom also grows on the enoki (hackberry/nettle) tree. Large specimens have caps of just over one *sun* (≈3 cm), while smaller ones measure three to four *bu* (≈0.9–1.2 cm). They grow in clusters of mixed sizes, displaying a light brownish color. The underside of the cap has fine grooves (*gill-like markings*) and is exceedingly smooth. The underside of the cap (*susu*) is black. When thoroughly washed and boiled while still smooth, it makes an excellent food. "*Name*" (奈女) means smooth; "*susu*" (須々) refers to the sooty color of the stem; and "*ki*" (木) refers to the upper part of the stem, and so forth.
