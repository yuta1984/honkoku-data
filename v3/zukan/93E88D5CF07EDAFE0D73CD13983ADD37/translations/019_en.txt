**Nara-take / Nara-motashi (楢菌, Oak mushroom)** 　　**Kuri-take (栗菌, Chestnut mushroom)**

*(Regarding Kuri-take)*
This mushroom grows abundantly on dead chestnut wood. I cultivated it in my back garden, pouring glutinous water (*neba-mizu*) over it morning and evening to encourage its growth. Its flavor is excellent and it is considered especially rare and prized. It is non-toxic.

Obtained from the back garden on the 29th day of the intercalary 7th month of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year (乙未, 1835), and drawn from life.

*(Regarding Nara-take)*
Grows on rotting oak (*nara*) wood. Like the chestnut mushroom, it tends to grow in large clusters in a single spot. In autumn, after rain, collected locally. Drawn from life on the 7th day of the 8th month of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year (乙未, 1835).

**Keyaki-take (欅蕈, Zelkova mushroom)**

*(Note on cultivating Enoki-take, Kasuri-take, and Kashi-take)*
Nowadays, rather than waiting for these mushrooms — *Enoki-take*, *Kasuri-take*, and *Kashi-take* — to grow naturally, one can cultivate them artificially. Cut a large tree and bury it firmly in the ground, leaving a stump of five to seven sun above the surface, then cover it with straw matting and pour rice-washing water (*kome-togijiru*) over it two or three times. After several days have passed, mushrooms will grow abundantly. This method works for all kinds of wood.

**Nami-susuki (滑煤華, "Smooth-smut flower")** 〔From the *Wakan Sansai Zue*, Mushroom Section〕

This mushroom also grows on the Japanese hackberry (*enoki*) tree. Large specimens have caps about one *sun* across; small ones are about three to four *bu*. They grow in dense clusters of various sizes and are tinged with a light brownish color. The inner surface bears fine markings and is very smooth. The cap is a sooty black color. When thoroughly washed and boiled until smooth, it makes an excellent dish. The element *name* (奈女) means "smooth," while *susu* (須々) refers to the sooty color of the stalk. The character for "tree" (木) is an abbreviated form of another character, and so on.

〔Collected from rotting wood at a bamboo well (竹井) in one's home garden on the 21st day of the 8th month of the *Kinoto-Hitsuji* year (乙未, 1835), and drawn from life.〕
