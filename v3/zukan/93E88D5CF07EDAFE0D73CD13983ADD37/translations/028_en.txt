[According to *Honzō* / Materia Medica:] *Kigai* (鬼蓋) and *Jikei* (地芩) are listed together; they belong to the category of "fox's umbrella" fungi (*Kitsune-no-kasa*), referring to those that produce both stalk and cap. They grow in wet ground and bamboo groves during the summer months and in the continuous autumn rains. Their scent carries a distinctly musty odor. There are three varieties: red, white, and yellow — all highly toxic.

The *Bencao Gangmu* (本草綱目, *Honzō Kōmoku*) states: *Onihude* (鬼筆, *Guĭbĭ*) grows in places of filth and excrement; it resembles a writing brush and is purple in color, emerging in the morning and dying by evening. It also belongs to the category of *Kigai*, and those [lacking a skirt] have red-purple filaments (*hige*), hollow in form like a flower. It is used medicinally to treat carbuncles and ulcers (*yōjo*) as well as venereal sores (*gekan-sō*).

　　**Jipi** (地皮, "Ground Skin")

[From *Kyūkō Yafu* / *Jiuhuang Yepu*, Wild Plants for Famine Relief:]
　　**Jikurage** (地踏菜, *Chitōsai*)
　　　Also known as: *Jikurage* (地耳, "Ground Ear")

　[Japanese regional names:]
　　*Kuroma*
　　*Ushi-no-kawa-take* ("Cow-hide Mushroom") — [Locality:] Iga, Nabari
　　*Kurohachi*
　　*Ja-kui* ("Snake-eater") — [Locality:] Kinshū (Ōmi Province)

It resembles *kikurage* (wood ear mushroom) in shape and grows on the ground in wet areas and bamboo groves. During the summer and particularly during the prolonged autumn rains, it grows sparsely in clusters. Because those growing on wood are called *kikurage* (木耳, "tree ear"), those growing on the ground are called *jikurage* (地耳, "ground ear").
