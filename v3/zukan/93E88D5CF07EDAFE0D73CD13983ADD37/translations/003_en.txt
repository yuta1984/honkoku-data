Preface to the Kinpu (Catalog of Fungi)

The fungi produced in our country are by no means few in number. Fungi, as a rule, are all born of the steaming, sultry vapors that rise from damp, heated earth. Among them, there are those of fine and beautiful quality, and those that are not fine at all. In the most extreme cases, there are those that carry great poison — some have already killed people. One cannot afford to be without the ability to discern them.
Although the *Kinpu* by Chen Renyu of the Song dynasty, the *Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica) by Master Li (Li Shizhen), and various other works cover many varieties, there remain many that are not recorded therein. Therefore, I once set out to gather and compile those remaining varieties,

【Left Folio】
roaming through the fields, plains, and mountain forests of the surrounding countryside, and wherever my feet could reach, I spared no effort in searching and collecting specimens. Whatever I obtained, I invariably recorded in illustrated form so as to capture its true likeness. As for descriptions of the qi (vital nature), taste, and physical form, I fear there may be errors in the names given, and so I leave this to the judgment of gentlemen scholars of later times.

　　　Written in the Sanka-rō (Sanka Pavilion) in the sixth month of late autumn, the year Hinoe-saru (丙申), the seventh year of the Tenpō era (1836).

　　　　　　　　Baien Mōri Motohisa
