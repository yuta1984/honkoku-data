【Miscellaneous Trees (雑木, Zōki)】

---

**From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会, "Illustrated Sino-Japanese Encyclopedia"), section on Shrubs (灌木類):**

　**Taranoki (楤木, Japanese Angelica Tree)** 〔Chinese pronunciation: *cōng* (音: 忽); Tang-period reading: *Tsongmotsu*〕
　Commonly called *Taranoki* (太良乃木).

　　〔Note: The *Wamyōshō* (和名抄) uses the character 桵 (*zui*) and reads it as *tara*, but this is incorrect.
　　The character 桵 refers to 甤核 (*zuikaku*), a different plant entirely.〕

---

**From the *Kyūkō Honzō* (救荒本草, "Materia Medica for Relief of Famine"):**

　**Jaquenot-does-not-perch (鵲不踏, *Que bu cai*)** — also known as *Sōmoku* (楤木) 〔i.e., *Taranoki*〕

---

**From *Yamato Honzō* (大和本草)**
**and *Bukō Sanbutsushi* (武江産物志), it states:**

　**Taranoki (楤木)**

---

　On the 28th day of the intercalary 6th month of the Year Teigai (1827),
　sketched from life in my garden.

　　　　　Listed under the 【Miscellaneous Trees】 section of *Yamato Honzō*.

---

**Matsuoka Joān Gentatsu (松岡恕庵玄達),
in his *Honzō Ikkagen* (本草一家言, "Personal Views on Materia Medica"), states:**

　**Taranoki (楤木)**

　　Called *Tara*, or *Taranoki*, or *Udomodoki* (独活茂登幾, "Udo-lookalike"),
　　with the alternate name *Tori-tomarazu* (鳥不宿, "Where Birds Do Not Perch").
　　Also known as *Kachi-nnoborazu* (鵲不登, "Where Magpies Do Not Climb"),
　　*Tori-tomarazu* (鳥不楼), and *Kachi-fumazu* (鵲不蹈, "Where Magpies Do Not Tread").
　　The *funtō* (吻頭, tip/sprout) is called *Udomodoki*.
　　The *Megi* (小檗, Japanese barberry, *Berberis thunbergii*) is also called
　　"*Kachi-fumazu*" (*Que bu dao*, "Where Magpies Do Not Tread"), but it is an entirely different plant.
　　〔Kotobuki (寿, i.e., the author Mōri Baien) notes:〕
　　　"The *Aritōshi* (蟻通し, *Rhaphiolepis* sp.) is also called *Tori-tomarazu* ('Where Birds Do Not Perch').
　　　 All of these names arise from the fact that the leaves, stems, and trunks of these plants
　　　 are covered with numerous thorns/spines."
