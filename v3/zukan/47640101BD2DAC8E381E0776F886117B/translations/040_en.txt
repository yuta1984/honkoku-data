**From the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (和漢三才図会), Section on Moisture-Loving Herbs (*Shitsusōrui*):**

　*Seikō* (青蒿, *Artemisia annua*)　Alternative names: *Sōkō* (草蒿), *Hōkai* (方潰), *Ken* (菣; pronounced like "Ken/Qian"), *Kōkō* (香蒿), *Jinkō* (𤜢蒿)

　　○ *Kawara-ninjin* (River-bank carrot/wormwood)
　　○ *No-ninjin* (Field carrot/wormwood) — name appearing in the *Shijing* (毛詩)

　　　　Colloquially called "*Kusa-yomogi*" (臭蒿, "stinking mugwort"), and also "*Kusa-ninjin*" (臭胡蘿蔔, "stinking carrot").
　　　　The *Tashikiben* (多識篇) states: "*Kara-yomogi*" (加羅与毛岐)

**I (Baien) say:**

The *Kyūkō Honzō*'s identification of *Seikō* (青蒿) as *Inchin* (茵陳蒿) is incorrect. *Inchin* and *Seikō* are distinct species. *Inchin* has leaves that are blue-white in color and has no fragrance. *Seikō* has green, lush leaves and possesses a faint aroma. The statement in the *Wakan Sansai Zue* that "*Seikō* has no branching" is uncertain and questionable. It is noted in the *Honzō* (本草) that *Seikō* is harvested on the 6th day of the 6th month and used in preparing *shinkyoku* (神麯, a fermented medicinal preparation).
