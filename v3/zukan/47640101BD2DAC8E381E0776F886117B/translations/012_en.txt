The Jiuhuang Bencao (Kyūkō Honzō) states:
　Zéxiè (Saji-omodaka / Arrowhead)　Commonly called "Mizu■-na." Also known as Shuixie, Jǐnxiè (Gifusha),
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Mángyù (Bōu), and Gǔxiè (Kokusha).

The Tashiki-hen, section on moisture-loving grasses, states:
　Zéxiè (Takusha)　Japanese name: Nakebi　Upon reflection:　　Other name: Yǔsūn (Uson)
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Mizunaki
　　Takusha　■ [reading: Yu]

The Jiuhuang Yepu (Kyūkō Yafu) states:
　Jiécǎo (Kokusō)　Japanese name: Sashi-omodaka
　　　　　　　　　　Also called "Ta-jisa" — a local dialect term from Kōtō district, Shinshū (Nagano) province.

　　　　My commentary:
　　　　　In the Jiuhuang Yepu, Jiécǎo (Kokusō) is identified as Sashi-omodaka;
　　　　　however, Jiécǎo has leaves resembling those of Zéxiè but smaller, with bluish-white flowers.
　　　　　It is a distinct and separate species, and its root differs from that of Zéxiè.
　　　　　To equate Zéxiè with Jiécǎo is not appropriate.

　　　　　　The twenty-fourth day of Rinsho (sixth month), Year of Hinoe-inu.
　　　　　　Drew from life, with the aquatic plant placed on my desk.
