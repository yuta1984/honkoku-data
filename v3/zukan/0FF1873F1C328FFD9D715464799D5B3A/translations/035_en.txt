The "Tashiki-hen," Mountain Fruits section, states:
　　　Ginkyo (銀杏, ginkgo)　Japanese name: Ichō　Commonly called　　　Alternative name: Hakka (白果) [as noted in daily use texts]
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Name: Gin'an
　　　　　　　　　Kōkyakushi (鴨脚子)


　　　　In the ginkgo, those whose leaves have deep lobes are male, and they do not bear fruit.
　　　　Fruit with three ridges is male, and fruit with two ridges is female, which bears fruit.
　　　　The female has leaves with slightly fewer lobes, and some have no lobing within the leaf.
　　　　As shown in the illustration of the flowers above, it appears to be a flower but is not truly a flower. Like
　　　　Sichuan pepper berries with no petals, two fruits form a pair; if one looks in the morning beneath the tree,
　　　　fallen flowers are found there daily. The fruit of the stem is of benefit for people's use.
