## 【Junsai / Watershield (*Brasenia schreberi*)】

The *Wakan Sansai Zue* (Illustrated Encyclopedia of Japan and China), under the category of aquatic grasses, states:
　　*Junsai* (蒪) [interlinear note: *Nunaha*]　蓴　[These are] different names for the same plant.
　　[Interlinear note: Tang Chinese pronunciation: *Chien*]　Same as above. Details omitted below.

---

The *Bencao Gangmu* (Compendium of Materia Medica) states: The *chun* (蒪) plant, being by its nature slippery (*namerakana*) following the water, is therefore named *chuncai* (蓴菜). The young stems in spring and summer, before they have become uniformly slippery [interlinear note: *numeri*], are called "*yacai*" (雅菜). Those whose leaf tips have stretched and extended are called "*sijunsai*" (絲蒪, "thread watershield"). When they have aged into autumn [past their prime], they are called "*kuijun*" (葵蒪).

---

The *Tashikiden* (Encyclopedia of Identifications), under the category of aquatic grasses, states:
　　蒪 (*sen*)　Japanese name: *Nunaha* (奴那波)
　　　　　　　Alternative names: *Suiki* (水葵, water mallow) [source: a poetry work ■], *Batei-so* (馬蹄草, horsehoof herb)
　　　　　　　*Mao* (茆, *riu*) [interlinear note: two readings — *u* and *ryu*], *Rokki* (露葵) [source: *Bencao Gangmu*]

---

The *Wamyō Ruijushō* (Japanese Names for Things, Classified) states:
　　蒪 (*Nunaha*): Yewang [i.e., the compiler Minamoto no Shitagō] notes that the character 蒪 has the *fanqie* (reverse cutting) reading of *shi-lun*. The Japanese name is *Nunahа* (沼奈波). It is an aquatic herb.

　　Su Jing's commentary on the *Bencao* (Materia Medica) states: From the third and fourth months through to the seventh and eighth months, it is generally called "*sijun*" (絲蒪, "thread watershield"). From the Frost's Descent (霜降, around late October) through to the second month, it is called "*kanjun*" (環蒪, "ring watershield").

The *Kyūkō Yafu* (Wild Provisions for Famine Relief) includes an entry on *Junsai* (*Nunaha*).

---

The *Yamato Honzō* states: 蒪 [interlinear note: *Nunaha* / *Junsai*]. The Japanese name is *Nunaha*. It grows in swamps (*numa*), and its stem is long and resembles rope (*nawa*). The name presumably carries the meaning of "swamp rope" (*numa-nawa* → *nunaha*). Nowadays in common speech, the sound has shifted and it is called *Junsai* (蒪菜).
