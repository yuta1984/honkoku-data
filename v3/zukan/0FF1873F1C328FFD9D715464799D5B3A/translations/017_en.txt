【Natsu-tsubaki (Summer Camellia / *Stewartia pseudocamellia*)】

In the *Wakan Sansai Zue* (Illustrated Encyclopedia of Japan and China), under the category of tall trees, it is recorded as "Sala Twin Trees" (Sāla dvandva).

The *Fanyi Mingyi Ji* (Collection of Translated Buddhist Terms) states the following: "Sāla" (娑羅) is rendered in this land [China] as "firmness" (堅固), because the tree does not shed its leaves in either winter or summer; it is therefore named "firm and steadfast." In the phonetic glossary of the *Avatamsaka Sutra*, it is translated as "lofty and far-reaching" (高遠). After the Buddha entered nirvana, the twin trees in the four directions all drooped and covered the Tathāgata, and those trees turned utterly pale and white in their sorrow.

　[My note:] "Sāla" is the original name, and "twin trees" (双樹) refers to a grove. In common usage, the combined form "Sāla twin trees" (娑羅双樹) has come to be used. The *Youyang Zazu* (Miscellaneous Morsels from Youyang) states that "the flowers of the Sāla tree resemble those of the lotus," which differs from this plant (Natsu-tsubaki). There is also one specimen at Hasedera Temple in Yamato Province.

The *Jikinsho* states:
　Sāla Twin Trees　　○ Yabu-tsubaki (also called Yabu-tsubaki)
　　　　　　　　　　　　This name does not appear in any of the commonly known texts.

The *Yamato Honzō* states:
　Sāla Tree
　　　This flower opens in the morning and wilts in the evening. This account is also recorded in the *Jikinsho*. The leaves are said to resemble those of the persimmon tree, which corresponds well [with what is observed here]. Whether this is truly the Sāla tree is uncertain. The *Senkan Ruisho* also includes an entry on the Sāla tree.
