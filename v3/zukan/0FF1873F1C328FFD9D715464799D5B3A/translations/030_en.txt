The "Wakan Sansai Zue" (Japanese-Chinese Illustrated Encyclopedia of the Three Realms) in the "Moisture-loving Grasses" category states:
　　　Sedge (suge)　Pronounced "kan"
　　　　　Japanese name:
　　　　　　　Suge

　　In the sixth month, the leaves are harvested and sewn into conical hats (kasa).
　　Commonly called "suge-gasa" (sedge hats). In Ōmi and
　　Ise provinces, they are extensively planted in paddies
　　for profit.



　　　　　　　　　　　　　　The "Mōgyū" (Méng Qiú), in Zhu Gong's Supplementary Commentary, states:
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　To peer at the sky through a stalk of sedge, to observe
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　the sea through a snail shell — to know but little



　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　The "Yamato Honzō"
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　records this in the Water Grass category

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Hinoe-Inu year, early summer, 9th day — True copy


The "Wamyō Ruijushō" (Japanese Name Classified Collection) states:
　　　Sedge (suge)　The "Tang Rhymes" says: kan 《Pronounced "kan"; the character is sometimes written as ■》 【■, in the original manuscript written as "grass radical + 間"】
　　　　　　Japanese name: Suke — a name for a grass


The "Imyō Ruijushō" (Classified Collection of Alternative Names) states:
　　　Mishiwa




　　　　Kotobuki [the author, Baien] states:
　　　　　Although sedge hats are produced in abundance throughout various provinces, those of Kaga Province are considered the finest. The color of Kaga sedge
　　　　　is pure white, and the stitching is fine and delicate, making them well-proportioned. The sedge hats of Naniwa in Settsu Province are also considered a famous local product.


　　　　　　　"Man'yōshū" (Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves):
　　　　　　　　　Oshi-teru ya　Naniwa no suge-gasa　yoki furushi
　　　　　　　　　　Nochi nite tare ga kin　kasa naranaku ni


　　　　　Furthermore, it states: In the "Engishiki" (Procedures of the Engi Era), there is a reference to the "Kasamuji clan of Settsu Province." Today, the villagers of Fukae village, east of Tamazukuri in Osaka, make their livelihood exclusively by sewing sedge hats. This is likely a tradition passed down from antiquity.
