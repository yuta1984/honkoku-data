The "Wakan Sansai Zue" (Japanese-Chinese Illustrated Encyclopedia of the Three Worlds) in the "Wetland Grasses Category" states:
　　　Suge (sedge grass)　Pronounced "kan"
　　　　　Japanese name:
　　　　　　　Suge (須介)

　　In the sixth month, the leaves are harvested and used to sew sedge hats (sugegasa).
　　In Ōmi and Ise, it is widely cultivated in rice paddies for profit.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　In the continued commentary by Zhu Gong on the "Mōgyū" (Mengqiu):
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Like peering at the sky through a sedge tube
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　or gazing at the sea through a narrow gap — one knows only a little

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　"Yamato Honzō"
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Records this in the [Aquatic Plants] category

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　Hinoe-Inu year, early summer, 9th day — True copy

The "Wamyō Ruijushō" (Japanese Names Classified Encyclopedia) states:
　　　Suge (sedge)　The "Tang Rhymes" (Tōin) says: Kan 《pronounced "kan"; the character is sometimes written as "kan" 簡》
　　　　　　Japanese name: Suke — a name for a grass

The "Imyō Ruijushō" (Encyclopedia of Alternative Names) states:
　　　Mitsushiba

　　　Kotobuki [the author] says:
　　　　Sedge hats (sugegasa) are produced in many provinces, but those from Kaga Province are considered the finest. The color of the sedge
　　　　is pure white, and the thread-sewing is fine and excellent. The sedge hats of Namba in Settsu Province are also renowned as a local specialty.

　　　　　　Man'yōshū (Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves):
　　　　　　　　Oshi-teru ya / Naniwa no sugegasa / yoku furushi
　　　　　　　　　Nochi ni te ta ga kin-kasa / naranaku ni
　　　　　　　　(The radiant Naniwa sedge hat, well worn —
　　　　　　　　　Who later would make it into a brocade hat?)

　　　　Furthermore, he says: What is referred to as the "Kasanui clan of Settsu Province" in the "Engishiki" corresponds to the
　　　　present-day villagers of Fukae village to the east of Tamazukuri in Osaka, who specialize in sewing sedge hats as their family trade. This must be a tradition passed down from ancient times.
