【Right Page】
An insect closely resembling a *suzumushi* (bell cricket). The back is flat and forms ridges on the sides. Copied on the 6th day of the 9th month, Ding-Chou year.

The head is narrow, and the beak is long, curved downward like a hook, with two short antennae. The entire body is dull earth-black in color with no luster, and the abdomen is dark brownish-black. The wings are small and folded, carried on the back. The legs are light brown with black spots. The front legs are black, extremely thin at the base, then suddenly swelling, and tapering again toward the tips.

【Left Page】
*Gunbai-mushi* (军配虫; Fan-shaped Bug). Commonly called *gunbai* [military fan]. Named so because of its resemblance to a *gunbai uchiwa* (a war fan used by military commanders). During the latter part of mid-summer in the Kō-jutsu year, upon inquiry from the Western Citadel, this name was submitted along with a rough sketch in reply. No Chinese name exists.

The entire body is transparent like flakes of mica. All the veins of the wings form hexagonal tortoiseshell patterns. The small wings below the top project vertically upward. Although I have attempted to copy it faithfully, the most intricate and detailed portions are exceedingly difficult to render in a drawing.

It is a rare small winged insect appearing between summer and autumn, smaller in form than a sesame seed. When observed under a microscope, small wings emerge in three directions from its neck, as if it were wearing a gauze cap. The white-spotted areas form fine vein patterns resembling a white mesh, creating a tortoiseshell (*kikkou*) pattern. It is truly a marvel of nature, beyond what brush and ink can fully capture. I have here attempted a rough hand-copy of its general form.

(In vermilion ink) This is an aphid (*aburамushi*) that clings to the rolled leaves and stems of lotus plants cultivated in ornamental pots. Its size is approximately that of a perilla (*egoma*) seed. Copied using a microscope.
