Found near the Eastern Capital (Edo),
in rivers and the sea.
The texture is soft,
and the shape is not unlike
a human tongue. It has minute
soft spines.
It is light pink in color. Fishermen
sometimes find it caught on
the hooks of their longlines.
Called "Sumara."

【Left Page】
△Sea Swallow (Ophiuroid/brittle star) — Rare specimen. Local dialect of Hyōgo Port, Sesshū: Tentsuru-montsuru. Also known as Tentsu.
　　Kumon-tsuku. Also known as Tetsuru-mozuru. All of these are corruptions of "Tentsuru-montsuru." The people of Sesshū
　　roast and dry it, grind it into powder, and take it with warm sake. It treats acute pain in the chest, asthma, shortness of breath, difficulty breathing, and those who cannot speak. A tiny amount (ittōkei) provides immediate relief.
　　It can also be decocted and taken as a drink to treat unbearable shan pain (hernia/colic) surging toward the heart. In the capital (Kyoto), it is often sold as a wonderful medicine. It also treats bruises and
　　injuries with stagnant blood (blood stasis): burn it to black ash, grind into powder, mix with warm sake, and take orally. Using it unburned is said to be even more effective. Fishermen of Gotō Islands in Hizen
　　grind the dried, preserved creature into powder, make it into a paste, and apply it to treat bruises, injuries, and pain. Hence it bears the name "Bone-setter" (konetsugi).
　　In Chikuzen, it is also called "Honetsugi" (bone-setter) and used as a wonderful remedy for bruising. The full form is illustrated here. The upper surface has five branching arms,
　　and on the underside there is a ten-petal chrysanthemum pattern in the center. There is also a plum blossom pattern.
　　In the very center is a small round hole, which is the mouth. Although it performs remarkable medicinal feats,
　　an effective dose is only two to three bu. One must never exceed this dose. Too much may cause numbness and loss of sensation (maboku-funin). Take heed, take heed.」

○ Found in the sandy sea floor from Kanagawa to Kanazawa and Kamakura. Resembles the shape of a gourd (hyōtan) or a hair pin (kōgai).
　The Chinese name is Shacho (沙箸, "sand chopsticks"), also known as Etsuo-yosan (越王餘算). The western regional dialect calls it "Umi-yanagi" (sea willow). Length is over two shaku,
　and inside there are white bones
　like willow
　branches.
