The variety known as "fine-leaved *Senbyō* (Kinbai-zasa)" is of Japanese origin. It grows in sunny spots within the mountains near the seashore of warm regions. Its leaves resemble those of *Cyperus* (*Kayatsurigusa*, nutsedge) and are soft; it opens yellow flowers. It fruits from summer through autumn. The fruit forms seed pods beneath the flower. When ripe, the pods contain small black seeds inside. It must not be confused with the Chinese variety.

　　　　　[For reference, the *Bencao Gangmu* (*Pen-ts'ao Kang-mu*), in its "Collected Commentaries" (*Jijie*) section, states: Li Xun (*Li Xún*) says: The leaves are green and grass-like (*mao*), yet soft and somewhat broader, with longitudinal striations on the surface. They also resemble the seedlings of a young palm (*Trachycarpus*) just beginning to grow. The plant reaches about one *chi* (approx. 30 cm) in height. It withers completely in winter and sprouts anew at the beginning of spring. In the third month it bears flowers resembling those of the gardenia (*Gardenia jasminoides*), yellow in color, and does not produce fruit. Its root has a single, straight stem, about the thickness of a little finger; beneath it are short, slender subsidiary roots attached to it. The outer skin is slightly coarse and brown; the inner flesh is yellow-white. The root is harvested in the second and eighth months, sun-dried, and used medicinally.]
