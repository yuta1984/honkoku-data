In one's leisure hours, to follow where the heart leads, to delight the senses, and to nourish the spirit — this is in no way contrary to the Way. The calligraphy of Yan Zhenqing (*Shinkei*) and the paintings of Wang Wei (*Makitsu*) are surely instances of precisely this.

Kiyohara Shigemi (*Jūkyo*) of Owari domain has a profound love for plants and trees; his ability to distinguish and identify them is clear and penetrating, and he has composed this work. It may well be that his intentions lie in just this spirit. He has assessed the suitability of the soil, carefully examined local products, and recorded everything in minute detail without omission, such that even specialists and renowned authorities could not surpass him.

[Left Page]
Not only does he find joy in pursuing his own aspirations, but his work also brings great benefit to others. However, one who conducts themselves at a level surpassing the common world must invariably bear the burden of censure from ordinary society. Some may criticize this as overstepping one's proper bounds. For this reason, I offer these words in explanation, and present them as a preface.

The twelfth month of Bunsei 8 [1825]
