**Koji-sō (Yukinoshita / Tiger's-ear herb, *Saxifraga stolonifera*)** [From the *Bencao Gangmu*]

It grows in shaded, damp ground and in the crevices of rocks and stones. Its leaves do not wither even through winter. In spring it sends out young leaves. Both the leaves and their stalks are covered with fine hairs. Its manner of growth is such that leaves emerge facing each other in pairs, both front-to-back and side-to-side (opposite arrangement). In summer, a reddish stem rises from the center of the plant, bearing a small flower with two white petals crowning the top and three petals below, opening from the top downward. Some people say that they have never seen this flower open and fall — but this is mistaken. There is no flower that does not eventually shed its petals. From summer through autumn, red thread-like runners (*stolons*) extend from among the leaf stalks, producing small plantlets at their tips and thereby propagating themselves. Those that propagate by producing root-runners (*rhizomes*) and offsets are listed below.

**Tamashida** [Chinese name: not yet determined]

Its leaves resemble those of *Kanshū* (*Hosobakijino-o* / *Cyrtomium fortunei* or similar fern) and are long, not withering through winter. It is rather

sensitive to cold. Its roots bear perfectly round tubers (*rhizomes*), varying in size. From the base of the leaf stalks it produces root-runners, sending forth young shoots, and thereby propagates itself by division. Other similar examples are omitted here.

　　　　　　[For reference, the *Bencao Gangmu* (*Pen-ts'ao Kang-mu*), in its "Collected Commentaries" (*Jijie*) section, states: Li Shizhen (*Shizhen*) says: Tiger's-ear (*Huěr*) grows in shaded, damp places. People also cultivate it on rocky hillsides. The stem reaches five or six *cun* (approx. 15–18 cm) in height, and has fine hairs. Each stem bears a single leaf, shaped like the canopy of a lotus leaf. People call it "Stone Lotus Leaf" (*Shí Hé Yè*). The leaf is about the size of a coin, and in shape resembles both the small young leaves of the mallow (*Malva*) and the shape of a tiger's ear. In summer it opens small flowers of pale red color.]
