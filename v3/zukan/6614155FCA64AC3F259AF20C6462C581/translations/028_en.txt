**Suisōsō (Wood Sorrel / Oxalis corniculata)**　[Bencao Gangmu (Compendium of Materia Medica)]

It self-propagates in many places and spreads easily. It also produces roots at the nodes of its stems, with each node giving rise to one stem bearing three leaves. The leaves are light green in color and do not wither through winter. From spring to summer, it sends up slender stems from the base of the leaf stalks and opens small five-petaled yellow flowers with fine stamens. After flowering, it forms seed pods. At dusk, the leaves droop downward and all the leaf undersides fold together; the following morning they open again and fold together once more, repeating as before. The variety whose leaves and stems are both red-purple in color is called "Sekisonshi" [Tujing Bencao]. There is also a variety called "Yamakatabamisō" [Chinese name unknown], which is one of its kinds and likewise folds its leaves at night (nyctinastic). The following two or three kinds that exhibit this nyctinastic leaf-folding behavior are listed below:

- Gappō (Aeschynomene indica / Indian jointvetch)　[Huatuo Zhongzangjing]
- Shitsuri (Tribulus terrestris / Puncturevine)　[Bencao Gangmu]
- Ketsumei (Senna obtusifolia / Sicklepod)　[Same as above]
- Gōkansō (Lespedeza cuneata / Chinese bush clover)　[Shiyi ji]
- Sanpendzu (Chamaecrista nomame / Dwarf partridge pea)　[Jiuhuang Bencao]
- Koshi (Lespedeza / Bush clover)
