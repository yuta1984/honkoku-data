**Yōnyū (Tsuru-ninjin / *Codonopsis lanceolata*)** [from the Appendix to *Sha-shen* in the *Bencao Gangmu*]

In general, climbing plants (*manso*; vines) include left-twining (*sa-ten*) and right-twining (*u-ten*) varieties. Those that rotate in the direction east→south→west→north are left-twining; those that rotate west→south→east→north are right-twining. This *yōnyū* (tsuru-ninjin) has the characteristic of twining in both left and right directions. In the mountains and in shaded spots in villages, it sends out a parent vine (*oya-zuru*) from old perennial roots in spring, which twines to the left; four leaves grow in opposite pairs (*taisei*) and are pale green. From the center of this, a child vine (*ko-zuru*) grows, which twines to the right. However, the direction of twining—left or right—is not necessarily the same every year. It produces three-lobed leaves, and in autumn, purple tubular flowers open at the tips of the child vines. Their shape resembles a bell. The rhizomes, flowers, and leaves all exude a white milky sap and have a distinctive odor.

Right-twining (*u-sen*) examples are omitted here; two or three species of left-twining (*sa-sen*) plants are listed below.

**Gomishi (*Schisandra chinensis*)** [from the *Bencao Gangmu*] — Its vine twines to the left. There are southern and northern varieties. The northern *gomishi* (朝鮮五味子; Korean *Schisandra*) is of Korean origin (*kanshu*).

Its leaves drop in winter (*chōraku*), and new leaves emerge in spring. In summer, white flowers open on the old vines and fruits are produced. When ripe, the fruits become deep red. The shape of each cluster is elongated. The Japanese-grown variety is sometimes called "*matsufusa*." The vine twines to the left.

The southern *gomishi* (南五味子; *Kadsura japonica*, *binankadzura*) grows abundantly in dense woodland thickets in many places. Its leaves do not wither through the winter; new leaves emerge in spring, white flowers open among the leaves in summer, and fruits are produced. When ripe, the fruits turn red. The shape of each cluster is round.

**Shitō (Fuji; Wisteria)** [from the *Bencao Gangmu*] — It is native to the mountains. Today it is cultivated throughout various regions, twining to the left around trees and bamboo. White wisteria (*shiro-fuji*) is frequently right-twining. Furthermore, from summer through autumn, some plants produce right-twining vines; the vines, stems, and leaves all bear soft hair (*mōjō*), and they open pale pink flowers that do not hang downward (*kasui*). This is a variety of white wisteria, which occasionally opens white flowers in spring. It is a remarkably rare and unusual variety.
