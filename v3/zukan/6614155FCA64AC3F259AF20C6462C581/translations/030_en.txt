【Right folio】

**Mokuzoku / Tokusa** (木賊, *Equisetum hyemale*, rough horsetail) [*Bencao Gangmu*]

This plant grows in the ravines of deep mountains. In summer it produces new stems, which bear neither leaves nor branches — only nodes. The tips of these stems often wither and die, so flowering is rare. Of the new stems produced in the current year, those whose tips do not wither will grow somewhat taller by the following year, and in autumn they produce flowers at the tops of the stems. The shape of the flower closely resembles the tip of *tsukushi* (土筆 / horsetail shoots, *Equisetum arvense* sporophytes), forming a pattern like the shell of a turtle's back. It bears no fruit. One or two similar species are listed on the left.

**Monkei / Sugina** (問荊, *Equisetum arvense*, field horsetail) [Appendix to Mokuzoku]

This plant grows abundantly in fields and on embankments. In spring it produces *tsukushi* (筆頭菜, spore-bearing shoots) from its overwintering roots [*Qingyang Fuzhi*]. These shoots are the plant's flowers. Afterward it produces vegetative shoots that branch out, with node connecting to node in succession — resembling the nodes of *tokusa*.

【Left folio】

**Setsusetsu-sō / Inu-tokusa** (節々草, *Equisetum ramosissimum*, branched horsetail) [*Bencao Yuanshi*]

This is an aquatic plant. It also grows in dense clusters in moist ground. It has nodes but no leaves; it produces branches below each node and flowers at the tip. Its flowers resemble those of *tokusa* (horsetail). It bears no fruit. It is an error to identify this plant as *maō* (麻黄, *Ephedra sinica*, Chinese ephedra / "katsunegusa"). *Maō* does not grow natively in Japan.

[Classical Chinese citation: *Bencao Gangmu*, "Explanation of Names" section]

> Li Shizhen states: This plant has nodes, and its surface is rough and abrasive. Those who work and polish wood or bone use it to rub and scrape, after which [the surface] becomes smooth and lustrous — hence it is called, as it were, "the enemy (*zoku*) of wood."

[Same work, "Collected Explanations" section]

> Zhang Yuxi states: The *mùzéi* (horsetail) plant grows about one *chi* tall in dense clumps. Each root produces a single stem, with no flowers or leaves. There are nodes at every *cun* interval; the color is green, and it does not wither through winter. It is gathered in the fourth month.
