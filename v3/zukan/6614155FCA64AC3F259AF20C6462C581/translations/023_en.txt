**Shisumatsuri (*Oshiroibana*, Four-o'clock flower / *Mirabilis jalapa*)** [from the *Hiden Kakyō*] — It bears flowers in summer, opening at the *shinji* (around 4:00 PM, the Hour of the Monkey) and wilting at the *tatsudoki* (around 8:00 AM, the Hour of the Dragon).

**Karo (*Kikarasuurui*, Chinese cucumber / *Trichosanthes kirilowii*)** [from the *Bencao Gangmu*], **Ōka (*Karasuurui*, crow gourd / *Trichosanthes cucumeroides*)** [same source], **Gekkakō (*Naftori*, tuberose / *Polianthes tuberosa*)** [from the *Zhangzhou Fuzhi*], **Ōhosuge** [Chinese name unknown], and **Tama-no-kanzashi** [same source; one variety of the *gyokusō* / *Hosta*] and the like — these open their flowers in the evening and wilt in the morning. There are also several varieties that open in the morning and wilt in the evening, but these shall be omitted here.

[Quotation from the ***Bencao Gangmu* (Honzō Kōmoku), "Collected Explanations"**]: Shizhen states: The *ken* (芡, Gordon Euryale / *Euryale ferox*) produces leaves in the third month, lying flat upon the water. They are larger than lotus leaves, wrinkled like thin silk gauze (*koku*), puckered and rippled as if boiling, green on the upper surface and purple on the underside. Both stems and leaves bear thorns. The stems can grow to a length of over one *jō* (approx. 3 meters); they also have hollow channels within, and contain fibrous threads. The tender ones can be eaten after peeling. In the fifth and sixth months, purple flowers appear; the flowers open facing the sun, forming a bract. On the outside are green thorns in the shape of a hedgehog's spines or a chestnut burr. The flower sits atop the bract, also resembling the beak of a chicken or the snout of a hedgehog. When opened, inside is a mottled, soft flesh; the seeds within are strung together like beads. The white kernel inside the shell is shaped like a fish's eye.

[Quotation from the ***Hokkorokku* (*Beihu Lu*, Records of the Northern Household)**]: The water lily (*suiren*) resembles the *awa* (*Nymphoides*, floating heart) but is larger, floating on the water's surface. Its flowers bloom on several stems and come in five colors. In summer they open on their own; at night they contract and sink to the bottom of the water; by day they emerge again.
