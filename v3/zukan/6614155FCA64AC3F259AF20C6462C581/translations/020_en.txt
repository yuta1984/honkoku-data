**Right page:**

**Natsufuji** [Chinese name unknown] — A variety of purple wisteria (*shitō*; *Wisteria*), left-twining, which opens pale whitish flowers in summer that hang downward.

**Nindō (Suikazura; Honeysuckle, *Lonicera japonica*)** [from the *Bencao Gangmu*] — Abundant in dense woodland thickets in the mountains. The vine twines to the left. In summer, flowers open between the paired leaves. At first they are white, then later change to yellow, whereupon they promptly fall. For this reason the flower is called "*kinginка*" (金銀花, "gold and silver flower") [from the *Nindō* *Jikkai* section of the *Bencao Gangmu*].

**Ōdoku (Kashiuimo; Air Potato, *Dioscorea bulbifera*)** [from the *Gunbu Fushi* (*Junwu Fuzhi*)] — The tubers are planted in spring. Its vine twines to the left, and the leaves resemble those of *joyō* (nagaimo; *Dioscorea opposita*) and *san'yaku* (yamanoimo; Chinese yam). From summer through autumn, small tubers (*ko-imo*) form in the axils of the leaves, resembling *mukago* (aerial bulbils). Flowers also grow from the leaf axils, hanging downward like the flowers of the *joyō*. It produces fruit. The old tuber (*oya-imo*; the parent corm) has many fibrous roots (*hige-ne*). Its form is illustrated in the appendix.

**Left page:**

**Josei (Yaitōbana; *Paederia foetida*, or possibly *Metaplexis japonica*)** [from the *Bencao Gangmu*] — Abundant in fields and bamboo groves. The vine twines to the left; the leaves grow in opposite pairs (*ryōtai*), and flowers open between the leaves in summer.

Beyond these, there are several other species that are left-twining or that differ in character; these are omitted here.

[Classical Chinese passage:]
The *Jikkai* (集解; Collected Commentaries) on *Sha-shen* in the *Bencao Gangmu* states: Zàng Qì (*Chén Cánggì*) says: The root of *yōnyū* (羊乳) resembles that of *sainei* (*Adenophora triphylla*) but is round, roughly the size of a fist. There are angular nodes on top; when broken, a white sap flows out. People take the root as a substitute for *sainei*. The sprout forms a vine; when broken, it exudes white sap.
