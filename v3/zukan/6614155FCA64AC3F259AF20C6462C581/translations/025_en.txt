**Senbyō (Kinbai-zasa)** [*Curculigo orchioides*; from the *Bencao Gangmu* (Pen-ts'ao Kang-mu)]

It is of Chinese origin (a variety introduced from the continent). In spring, leaves emerge from old perennial roots. They are light green with longitudinal striations. From summer through autumn, a spathe (bract-sheath) emerges from beside the leaf stalk, from which a slender white stem extends — barely one *sun* (approximately 3 cm) in length — covered with fine hairs. At the tip of this stem, a single deep-yellow flower with six petals opens. From the center of the flower, six stamens/pistils extend. There is no receptacle, and the flower does not fall off; rather, when it withers and shrivels, another spathe emerges and a new flower opens, just as before. A spathe is produced for each flower. After flowering, the stem withers, and within the spathe beneath the soil, a three-ridged seed pod forms. When it ripens, it splits open from the top, and within the white pericarp (inner flesh) there are small black seeds. This process repeats for each flower. Thus, the flower appears above ground while the seeds remain underground, meaning the flower and seeds exist in different locations. Various theories exist, but none of them explain the presence of seeds within this underground spathe.
