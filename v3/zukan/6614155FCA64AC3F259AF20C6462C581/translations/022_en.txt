As for the water lily (*suiren*, hitsujigusa), it grows in ponds and marshes in mountain areas. Its stems are long, resembling the strings of a koto. According to the depth of the water, a single leaf grows at the tip of each stem, floating on the water's surface; it is lustrous and green, or sometimes tinged with purple. The leaf veins resemble those of the lotus leaf, and the inside of the stem resembles the hollow channels of a lotus root. In summer it produces long stems, and at the tips of these stems it opens flowers of four petals, four layers, totaling sixteen petals. The form resembles the flower of the Gordon Euryale (*ken*, *Euryale ferox*). The outermost petals are white on the upper surface and green on the underside; further inward they are pure white. At the *hitsujidoki* (around 2:00 PM, the Hour of the Sheep), the flowers open on the water's surface; toward evening they immediately wilt, and the following day the same occurs again.

In general, the opening and falling of flowers — their flourishing and fading — each plant has its own appointed time, and it does not deviate from this. And yet, there are those who go against the plant's nature and force the morning glory (*kengyūka*, *asagao*) to bloom in the middle of the night. This is something that brings shame upon flowering plants, and it is

a great error. Those flowers that have a fixed time of opening — I shall list a mere two or three examples below.

**Yarakukinsen (*Gojika*)** [Called *Gojika* (午時花, "Noon Flower") in the *Hiden Kakyō*; "Wakan Tsūmei" in the *Ōshi Iien*] — Seeds are sown in spring. The leaves that first appear have no lobes; at four or five leaves, lobes develop; by the tip they cease again. All leaves have serrated margins (*henshī*). In autumn, flowers of vermilion-orange (*endanshoku*) open among the leaves. They have five petals; the stamens are also vermilion and carry yellow pollen. At the center are white stamens. They open at the *goji* (around noon) and wilt at the *shiji* (around midnight). Hence, it is also called by one name "Shigoka" (子午花, "Midnight-Noon Flower") [per the *Nōho Rokusho*].

**Yaseikwabyo (*Chōrosō*)** [from the *Jiuhuang Bencao* (*Kyūkō Honzō*)] — It bears flowers in summer, opening at the *shiji* (around 10:00 AM, the Hour of the Snake) and wilting at the *goji* (around noon, the Hour of the Horse).

**Sekiryūtan (*Fudesō*)** [from the *Bencao Huiyan* (*Honzō Igen*)] — It bears flowers in spring; it opens when the sun reaches its zenith, and wilts when the sun moves to the west.

**Chōkōkabyo (*Chōjinasubi*)** [from the *Jiuhuang Bencao* (*Kyūkō Honzō*)] — It bears flowers in summer, opening at the *biji* (around 2:00 PM, the Hour of the Sheep) and wilting at the *jūtsuji* (around 8:00 PM, the Hour of the Dog).
