**Rakkasei (落花生 / Peanut; also called Tōjin-mame [唐人豆, "Chinese person's bean"])** 　[*Zhifu Quanshu* (致富全書 / *Complete Book of Enrichment*)]

Seeds are sown in spring. The seedlings grow at a slant; each stem bears four leaves, which are covered with fine hairs. Toward dusk, the leaf surfaces fold together, and in the following morning they open again. In summer, yellow flowers bloom between the leaves at the Hour of the Snake (around 10 a.m.), and by the Hour of the Horse (around noon) they wither and shrivel — yet they do not fall off and scatter. When one flower has finished, another opens in the same manner as before. Afterwards, from the base of the flower stems between the leaves, white roots emerge and enter the soil, where they form pods containing two or three seeds, or sometimes one. This is an example of a plant whose seeds develop in a different location from where its flowers bloom. Below are listed other plants that likewise form pods in the soil.
