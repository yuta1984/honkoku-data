　　　　　　　　　The *Bichuan Huajing* (秘伝花鏡 / *Secret Traditions of the Flower Mirror*) states: The Senninshō (仙人掌 / cactus) originates from Min and Yue (Fujian and Guangdong provinces). It is neither herb nor tree, nor is it fruit or vegetable. It has no branches, no leaves, and absolutely no flowers. From the soil a single flat pad thrusts upward, indistinguishable from the palm of a hand. Its skin is blue-green, lustrous and bright, and pleasant to behold. On the surface of the pad, fine millet-colored dots are produced. Each year only one leaf grows from the top; in the current year it grows to the left, in the following year to the right, accumulating layer upon layer as it grows upward. If planted within the home, it can suppress fire disasters. If one wishes to propagate it, take one of the pads, cut it into three or four pieces, and plant them in rich soil — a complete pad will grow naturally of its own accord.

　　　　　　　　　The *Zunsheng Bajian* (遵生八牋 / *Eight Discourses on the Art of Living*) states: It resembles a palm; the color is emerald green, with many millet-like dots on its surface, and the daughter pads grow one after another from the top. It may well be called a remarkable tree.

　　　　　　　　　The *Guihai Yuheng Zhi* (桂海虞衡志 / *Record of the Customs and Products of Guihai*) states: Ryōkotsuboku (龍骨木 / Dragon-Bone Tree) is emerald-blue in color, and in form resembles dried bones.
