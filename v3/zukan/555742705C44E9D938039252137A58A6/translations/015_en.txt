Red, purple, and yellow are considered the natural base colors. The species of the dosegai (burden-bearing shell) group, the shijimi (freshwater clam) group, and tansu (mussels) have black as their base color. Among those whose base color is green, besides the matsuba-gai (pine needle shell), those bearing a blue-green color are the dosegai. All others bearing green color are the result of rust (discoloration) caused by seawater. The ao-nishi (blue turban shell), when its outer skin is peeled, reveals blue — this is its natural base color.

○Regarding clams (hamaguri), freshwater mussels (bō), and oysters (kaki), names are established to serve as a convenient means of distinguishing their forms, patterns, spots, and textures. Broadly, all shells of ten thousand varieties are assigned to the following ten shapes. Those that do not fit are listed in the section on unusual forms.

○Generally, the growth of shells among all clam species occurs as secretions are sent from the adductor muscle (column), and after passing through many years, they become fully mature and aged.

○Regarding how bivalve species reproduce, there should be both oviparous (egg-laying) and viviparous (live-bearing) types. This has not yet been confirmed in detail.
In general, when distinguishing size among the hamaguri and bō types, measurement is applied to the longest dimension regardless of whether it is vertical or horizontal, and the rest can be understood accordingly. #1
