【Right folio, upper section】 (On astrological compatibility)

Concerning astrological compatibility (*aishō*): Wood and Fire, Fire and Earth, Earth and Metal, Metal and Water, Water and Wood — these combinations are greatly auspicious. When Wood meets Wood, or Water meets Water, this is called *hiwa* (a state of mutual accord), and in general, matching elements of the same type are considered auspicious.

【Right folio, lower section】 (Ise Monogatari)

[Section Seventeen]
A person who had not sent word for many years came to view the cherry blossoms at their peak. The host composed:

*Ada nari to na ni koso tateri sakurabana / toshi ni mare naru hito mo machi kere*
(You cherry blossoms — known by reputation as fickle and fleeting — yet even you, it seems, were waiting for this person who comes so rarely in a year.)

Reply:

*Kyō kozuba asu wa yuki to zo furi namashi / kienu wa ari tomo hana to mi mashi ya*
(Had you not come today, by tomorrow it would surely have fallen like snow. And even had it not scattered — could you have seen it truly as blossoms?)

[Section Eighteen]
Long ago, there was a woman who was somewhat flirtatious in her heart. (She appears to have been a woman who served in attendance near Narihira, living in his neighbourhood.) Knowing that (Narihira) was a man who composed poetry, she (hoping to draw out Narihira's heart) broke off a faded chrysanthemum blossom (a wilted white chrysanthemum) and sent it to the man (Narihira).

*Kurenai ni niou wa izura shira-giku no / eda mo tawawa ni furu ka to mo miyu*
(Which of these is the white chrysanthemum branch, glowing as it does with crimson? It appears as though (the crimson) falls thickly, bending the branches low.)

The man (Narihira), feigning ignorance, composed in return with seeming nonchalance:

*Kurenai ni niou ga ue no shira-giku wa / orikeru hito no sode ka to mo miyu*
(The white chrysanthemum resting atop the crimson glow — it looks as though it might be the sleeve of the very person who broke it off.)

[Section Nineteen]
Long ago, a man (Narihira) had become mutually intimate in heart and spirit with a lady-in-waiting (*kodachi*) serving in the household of a woman in palace service. Before long, the two had grown entirely estranged. Since they were in the same place (the western quarters of the Somedono Palace), the woman could see the man before her eyes — yet the man conducted himself as though she were not there at all. The woman composed (in the manner of a *Kokinshū* poem):

*Ama-gumo no yoso ni mo hito no nari yuku ka / sasuga ni me ni wa mieru mono kara*
(Have you become, like a cloud in the sky, someone utterly distant and apart? And yet, despite everything, you are there before my very eyes.)

At which the man replied—

【Left folio, upper section】 (Procedures for a marriage proposal)

Now, once the discussion (of a marriage proposal) has reached its conclusion, one should first send a congratulatory gift (*shūgi*) as a formal offer. Since it is sent from the man's side, one must select an auspicious day according to the man's own (astrological) reckoning. The manner of the congratulatory gift should be appropriate to one's station — whether upper, middle, or lower. For those of the humblest rank, it is said that even a knot of dust will suffice — for it must serve as a token of one's sincerity and trust.

【Left folio, lower section】 (Ise Monogatari, Section Nineteen, continued)

(Composed in the manner of a *Kokinshū* poem): "Remaining only in the distant sky like a cloud, the passing of months and days is because the wind in the mountain I have entered blows so fiercely" — so he composed, yet it was said that there was in fact another man (that is, the woman had transferred her affections to someone else).

[Section Twenty]
Long ago, a man (Narihira) saw a woman who lived in Yamato (the old capital of Nara) and came to meet her secretly. After some time had passed, being a person in palace service, on his way back (from court duty), around the third month (*yayoi*), he broke off a branch of maple (*kaede*) whose leaves had turned in a remarkably beautiful manner and, from along the road, sent it to the woman with a poem:

*Kimi ga tame taoreru eda wa haru nagara / kaku koso aki no momiji shi ni kere*
(This branch I broke for your sake — though it is still spring, it has taken on the colours of autumn's crimson leaves.)

This was sent off, but the reply did not arrive until after (Narihira) had reached the capital, brought back by a messenger:

*Itsu no ma ni utsurou iro no tsuki nu ran / kimi ga sato ni wa haru nakaruran*
(When did its colour change and fade away entirely? Perhaps in the place where you dwell there is no spring at all.)

[Section Twenty-One]
Long ago, a man and a woman were deeply, mutually devoted to one another — a bond with no straying of the heart toward another. Yet somehow (the man could have had no idea of the reason), over some trivial matter, the woman came to find the world unbearable, and thinking she would leave and be gone, she composed this poem and wrote it upon something (in the room):

*Idete ina ba kokoro karoshi to ii ya sen / yo no ari sama wo hito no shiraneба*
(If I go away and leave, will they say my heart was light and fickle? Since you know nothing of the way things truly are in this world...)
