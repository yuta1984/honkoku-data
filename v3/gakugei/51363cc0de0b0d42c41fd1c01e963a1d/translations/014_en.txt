**[Section 8]**

Long ago, there was a man. Perhaps finding life in the capital too difficult to bear, he set off eastward, taking one or two friends with him, to seek a place to settle. Upon seeing smoke rising from Mount Asama (*Asama no Take*) in Shinano Province, (he composed this poem):

**[Cited from the *Shin-Kokinshū*:]**
> *Shinano naru / Asama no take ni / tatsu kemuri*
> *Ochikochi hito no / miya wa togamen*

*(The smoke that rises from Mount Asama in Shinano — would those near and far really find fault with it? — No, just as no one condemns the smoke, neither can anyone censure my burning love.)*

**[Section 9]**

Long ago, there was a man. That man, having come to regard himself as utterly useless and superfluous in the world, resolved: "I shall not stay in the capital. I will go east and find a province in which to live." And so he set out. He took with him one or two friends he had long kept company with. There was no one who knew the road, and they wandered on their way. They arrived at a place in the Province of Mikawa called Yatsuhashi ("Eight Bridges"). The reason this place was called Yatsuhashi was that the river waters there branched out like a spider's legs in eight directions, and eight bridges had been laid across them — hence the name Eight Bridges. They dismounted in the shade of a tree by the marsh there and ate their dried provisions (*kareii*). In the marsh, irises (*kakitsubata*) were blooming in extraordinary beauty.

---

**[Left folio, lower section]**

*(Continued)* — Seeing this, one of the party spoke up: "Compose a poem with the word '*kakitsubata*' *(note: this means to compose an acrostic poem [*oriку*], placing each syllable of the word at the head of each successive verse-phrase)*," he said, "placing those five syllables at the head of each phrase, and expressing the heart of this journey." And so (the man) composed:

> *Karagoromo / kitsutsu narenishi / tsuma shi areba*
> *Harubaru kinuru / tabi wo shi zo omou*

*(note: The syllable "shi" is a kakari-musubi filler syllable used to regulate the rhythm)*

*("Because I have a wife in the capital, as familiar and dear to me as a well-worn Chinese robe, I think with deep feeling of this journey, which has carried me so far away.")*

When this was recited, all present let their tears fall upon their dried provisions, until the provisions were quite sodden *(note: "hotobini keri" means they became swollen and soft with moisture)*.

They continued on their way and arrived in Suruga Province. Coming to Mount Utsu, the path they were to take into it was exceedingly dark and overgrown *(note: "kurohosoki" means thickly overgrown as a mountain in summer)*, tangled with vines and maple branches, and as he was thinking what a lonely and unexpected misfortune it was to find himself in such a place, he encountered a wandering ascetic (*shugenja*). "*(note: "kakaru michi wa" and the following words are spoken by the ascetic)* How do you come to be travelling such a road?" the ascetic said. Looking at him, (the man) saw that it was someone he had known before. Wishing to send word to a certain person remaining in the capital, he wrote a letter and entrusted it to the ascetic.

**[Cited from the *Shin-Kokinshū*:]**
> *Suruga naru / Utsu no yamabe no / utsutsu ni mo*
> *Yume ni mo hito ni / awanu nari keri*

*("On the path beside Mount Utsu in Suruga — even in waking reality [utsutsu], even in my dreams, I cannot meet that person.")*

Looking upon Mount Fuji, he saw that though it was already the end of the Fifth Month, snow was falling brilliantly white upon it.

**[Cited from the *Shin-Kokinshū*:]**
> *Toki shiranu / yama wa Fuji no ne / itsu tote ka*
> *Kanoko madara ni / yuki no furu ran*

*("O Mount Fuji, peak that knows no seasons — on what occasion, for what reason, do you scatter snow in fawn-spotted patterns?")*

As for that mountain, if one were to compare it to something here in the capital, it would be as though Mount Hiei were stacked twenty-fold upon itself... *(continues on the following page)*
