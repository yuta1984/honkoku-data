**[Right Folio, Upper Section]**

*Man Fire × Woman Fire:* Greatly inauspicious. Children may be born, but clothing and food are insufficient; quarrels are frequent. However, worshipping the fire deity brings good fortune.
　Poem: *The longing that burns like fire only grows stronger — / how vexing it is to meet at such a merry gathering.*

*Man Fire × Woman Earth:* Greatly auspicious. Three or nine children. Wealthy and long-lived. Should worship the fire deity. Three of these children should be established as heirs.
　Poem: *The heart that first fell in love is deeply bound — / how unforgettable is your kindness.*

*Man Fire × Woman Wood:* Auspicious. Two or eight children. Wealthy and fortunate. Auspicious if one worships the clan deity. A connection with cattle and horses.
　Poem: *I think things may pass by hopefully like this — / yet yearning for the afterlife is a forlorn feeling.*

*Man Fire × Woman Metal:* Greatly inauspicious, though half-auspicious. Two or three children. Both clothing and food are scarce. However, they may become known to others.
　Poem: *Without even a moment to forget one's thoughts of them, / to be lost in brooding is such a sorrowful thing.*

*Man Fire × Woman Water:* Greatly inauspicious. Children may be born but poverty prevails. Both clothing and food are scarce, and quarrels are numerous. Of three children, one turns out badly.
　Poem: *Though neither of us has a heart inclined to meeting, / a child born of my body alone there cannot be.*

**[Right Folio, Lower Section]**
**○ Ume ga E (Plum Branch) section**

○ It is on the plum branch that the warbler should make its nest — but what shall become of it when the wind blows? O warbler who lodges in the blossoms.

○ The loneliness of the village where blossoms fall. The faint, intermittent sounds of the koto. The scent of tachibana orange blossoms on one's sleeve. The mountain cuckoo (hototogisu) pays a visit.

○ At the pillow of a love-dream in the moment of drowsy sleep, pledging vows in the hour before dawn — waking, the original sorrow returns, and there is nothing but tears.

○ O plover crying as the night deepens — what do you brood over through the night? Taking the floating world as the grief of Suma, how close you draw "Minamoto" (Hikaru Genji) to me.

○ Like the white-bark bow-wood (shira-mayumi) that ought to spring back, an eighty-year-old elder bends his back forward in a fit of lovesickness.

○ The pine wind of Miho blows and ceases; the offshore waves, too, must have calmed. Together with the moon reflected on the water, one composes verses gazing at Mount Fuji.

**○ Kokoro Zukushi (Heart's Devotion) section — also called Okogama no Kyoku**

○ In the autumn wind that consumes the heart, with the waves of Suma Bay as one's pillow, lying alone with one's robe spread beneath, unable to weave even a dream — night after night.

○ Far, far away from one's hometown, here by the clear river (Sumida / Suminoe). Let me ask the birds of the capital — do you dwell there, or not?

**[Left Folio, Upper Section]**

*Man Earth × Woman Earth:* Half-auspicious. Good at first, bad later. Three children, but in all things one lacks the strength to act as one wishes. Prone to various illnesses.
　Poem: *Though I crush my body and scorch my breast with flames, / my life is one in which living seems without purpose.*

*Man Earth × Woman Metal:* Greatly auspicious. Nine children. Blessed through farmland. Should always worship the deity of the hearth. All things go as one's heart desires — truly fortunate.
　Poem: *Building a storehouse together between east and west — / it is as though a spring of sake wells up.*

*Man Earth × Woman Water:* Greatly inauspicious. Nothing goes as one wishes. No children. Like dropping and losing something, there is no strength. Should worship the clan deity.
　Poem: *I have heard that earth and water are the seeds of plants — / then why are we without a child born to us?*

*Man Earth × Woman Wood:* Half-auspicious. Poor at first, good later. Five children. A samurai should hold a domain; a merchant should hold gold and silver.
　Poem: *Though earth and wood make a pairing prone to loss, / if one prays, the blessings of the gods are bestowed.*

*Man Earth × Woman Fire:* Greatly auspicious. Five children. Both clothing and food are plentiful. All things go as one's heart desires; free from illness and long-lived.
　Poem: *Ah, what joy — how increasingly happy my own life is! / For in the ten thousand things, I am not wanting.*

**[Left Folio, Lower Section]**

(…let me ask the bird — are you there, or not?)

○ At the break of a summer night, the cuckoo (hototogisu) that wakes one from sleep — what appears white is the deutzia blossoms gleaming in the moonlight.

○ A small ox-cart standing in the mist, drawn up discreetly — a cart kept waiting in secret. Accepting vows sworn in secrecy from others' eyes, the path to the bedchamber.

○ Drawing the waters of the upper reaches of the Asuka River into the inkstone's well, the words I write will never be exhausted — for as long as I have this life to spend this day.

○ In the dusk of the evening when our vow was pledged, guided by the deep incense burning in the night air, I open the lespedeza-reed gate of the place I have come to visit — and there is the scent that has transferred to my sleeve.

**○ Taihei (Great Peace) section — also called Taiheiraku, and also known as Hinatsuchi no Kyoku**

○ The pine wind of an age that has long settled into heavenly peace. The young crane lives a thousand years; in the valley stream, the tortoise plays.

○ A secret pledge is never a shallow thing. I try to conceal my longing thoughts, but that they show in the color of violet — how fleeting and frail.

○ How ephemeral — yet how could I bring myself to resent a moon without a single cloud? And yet, somehow, the tears on my sleeve will not stop — in this evening dusk.
