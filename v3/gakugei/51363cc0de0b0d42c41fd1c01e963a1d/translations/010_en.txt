**[Right folio, upper section]**

This refers to the matter of Amaterasu Ōmikami (the Great Divinity of Heaven-Illumination). Although she manifests in female form, she is revered as the progenitor of the earthly deities (chijin). For this reason, woman is the origin of humankind. That said, as the world declines into its latter age, hearts become less honest and upright. It is for this reason that even the *Tsurezuregusa* states that "the nature of women is, in all cases, crooked"—

**[Right folio, lower section]**

*(Continued from the previous page)* — not yet settled, there was a woman in the western quarter of the capital. This woman surpassed the people of the world. In her case, her heart surpassed even her appearance. It seems she was not exclusively one man's wife *(note: meaning she was a woman who already had a master/husband)*. A sincere and devoted man *(note: referring to Narihira)* spent time conversing with her *(note: meaning they talked together at length)*, and upon returning home, what did he think? It was the first day of the third month; he sent this poem in the gentle rain:

**[Cited from the Kokinshū:]**
> *Oki mo sezu / ne mo sede yoru wo / akashite wa*
> *Haru no mono tote / nagame kurashitsu*

*(Neither rising nor sleeping, I passed the night through,*
*and as befits the spring, I spent the day lost in longing gaze.)*

**[Section 3]**

Long ago, there was a man. Wishing to send to a woman he was secretly devoted to *(note: meaning to place one's thoughts upon someone)* a thing called "hijiki-mo" *(note: a type of seaweed, also written as "鹿尾藻," hijiki)*, he sent this poem:

> *Omoi araba / mugura no yado ni / ne mo shinan*
> *Hijiki mono ni wa / sode wo shikitsutsu mo*

*(If you truly feel for me, I would lie down to sleep even in a weed-choked dwelling,*
*spreading out my sleeve as a mat of hijiki seaweed.)*

This concerns the time when the Nijō Consort had not yet entered the service of the Emperor *(note: the Emperor Seiwa)* and was still living as a private noble lady *(note: "tadabito" here denotes a person of high rank living outside imperial service)*.

**[Section 4]**

Long ago, at the Eastern Fifth Avenue (Higashi no Gojō), there resided the Empress Dowager *(note: this refers to the Somedono Consort, the mother of Emperor Seiwa)*. In the western wing of that residence lived a woman *(note: referring to the Nijō Consort)*. Unable to approach her as he wished *(note: meaning things did not go as he desired)*, with a heart full of yearning—
