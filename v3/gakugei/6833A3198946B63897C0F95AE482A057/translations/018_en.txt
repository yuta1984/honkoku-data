The folk tradition has erroneously transmitted this, and it may have been corrupted into the pronunciation "karameki."

**Site of Ichikawa Castle**　The exact location is no longer known. According to the *Gikeiki* (Chronicle of Yoshitsune): "On the eleventh day of the ninth month of Jishō 4 (1180), [Yoshitsune] arrived at a place called Ichikawa in Matsudo-no-shō, on the border of Musashi and Shimōsa." It appears that in former times, "Matsudo" was referred to as a shō (manor). In the *Kamakura Ōzōshi*, it is recorded that Uesugi Noritada established and supported the two sons of Nakatsukasa Nyūdō Ryōshin—Sanetane and Yoritane—and had them take refuge in Ichikawa Castle in Shimōsa Province. In the first month of Kōshō 2 (1456), Nan Zusho, Yanada Dewa-no-kami, and many others were dispatched. After numerous battles, on the nineteenth day of the same month, the castle was finally taken by assault. Yanada Kawachi-no-kami had sortied from Sekiyado, seized more than half of Adachi District in Musashi Province, and captured Ichikawa Castle (and so forth). For further details, see the earlier section on Ishibama Castle in Volume Six.

**Nemoto Bridge**　This bridge spans a small river between the Ichikawa ferry crossing and Sōneiji Temple. It takes its name from the fact that this area is called Nemoto Village. The water flowing beneath the bridge originates from the former site of the Mama inlet.

**Ankokuzan Sōneiji Temple**　Located on a hill to the north of Ichikawa post station, alongside the flow of the Tone River. It is a Zen temple of the Sōtō sect and one of the three temples that constitute the Sōrokuji (Bureau of Monks) of the Kantō region—the other two being Tomita Daichūji in Hitachi and Ogose Ryūonji in Musashi. The principal object of worship is Śākyamuni Buddha, and the founding abbot was known as Tsūyō Oshō. The temple was originally located in Ōmi Province, but in the third year of Tenshō (1575), Hōjō Ujimasa moved it to the site of Sekiyado in this province (Shimōsa). However, owing to repeated flooding, it was finally relocated to its present site during the Kanbun era (1661–1673). Inside the main gate, on the right, there is a shrine to Shiogama Rokushya Myōjin (the Six Shrines of Shiogama), said to be modeled after the Shiogama Shrine in Mutsu Province. An elm tree said to have been planted by Ōta Dōkan stands to the right of the avenue of trees along the main gate road, facing the stone marker inscribed "Geba" (Dismount from Horse). There is also an aged plum tree beside the guest hall, also said to have been planted by Dōkan himself. This temple distributes the "Gedokugan" (antidote pills) of Dōshōan of Kyoto.

**Kokufu-no-dai (Government Office Platform)**　The hills stretching from the vicinity of Sōneiji Temple to the area around Mama are collectively referred to by this name. The *Hōjō Godaiki* states: "In old documents, it is written variously as 'Kokufudai,' 'Kofunodai,' and 'Kōnodai,' while those who live there today say it is written 'Kōnodai.'" Upon reflection, "Kōnodai" (鴻台) refers to a place to the west of Kuribashi in Musashi Province, and is not the same as this location. The *Wamyō Ruijushō* records that the provincial government office (kokufu) of Shimōsa was in Katsushika District. Based on this, since this hill is near the kokufu, it came to be called "Kokufu-no-dai." Someone has said that the provincial seat of Katsushika in Shimōsa was in former times principally situated in the area of Kasai, and Kasai was once part of Shimōsa Province. In the travel diary *Azuma Miyage* by Sōchō from Eishō 6 (1509), it is written: "We spent about half a day pushing through reeds and rushes within the seat of Shimōsa Province in Kasai, and stopped at Jōkōji Temple at the Imai ferry, which had been a Pure Land gate." This should be taken as evidence.
