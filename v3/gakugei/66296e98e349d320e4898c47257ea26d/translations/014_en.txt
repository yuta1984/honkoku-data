【Right Page】
《Classical maxim》　"The words of a person of virtue are few." (from the *Yijing* / *Book of Changes*, "Xici xia" / Appended Judgments, Part II)

　The hototogisu —
　　as if to say
　　　that one whose words
　　　　overflow and multiply
　　　　　is wanting in refinement —
　cries out
　　but once.

(The hototogisu cries only a single cry, as if to admonish us that a person whose words are many is lacking in grace and dignity. Just as the cuckoo speaks but once, so too does the person of true virtue say only what is necessary — for it is in restraint of speech that true refinement resides.)

(Those of deep wisdom speak little. As the hototogisu cries but a single note, so the truly refined person does not waste words. To speak too much is to diminish one's own dignity — and so the cuckoo cries once, and once only, as if to remind us of this truth.)

【Left Page】
《The Monk Jichin (Jien)》　"To extend one's own heart to others — this is called *jo* (恕 / benevolent empathy)."

　Only when one
　　imagines it
　　　upon one's own body
　　　　does the pain of another
　　　　　become truly known —

　Do you not know
　　that life
　　　is something
　　　　precious
　　　　　and dear?

(Only by placing ourselves in another's position — feeling their suffering as if it were our own — can we truly understand what it means to be in pain. Do you not know how dearly precious life is to every living soul?)

(True empathy — *jo* — means projecting one's own feelings onto others, imagining their suffering as one's own. When we do so, we come to understand at last how another's pain truly feels. And with that understanding comes the realization: life is something every person holds precious and dear. How can one forget this?)
