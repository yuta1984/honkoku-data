【Right Page】
《Same author (The Monk Jichin / Jien)》　　　[Virtue] Constancy of Character (*setsusō*)

　Would that there were
　　some way
　　　to fashion
　　　　the human heart
　　after the *shiishiba* oak
　　　of the mountain —
　　　　whose leaves
　　　　　never change,
　　　　　　no matter the season.

(Just as the evergreen *shiishiba* shrub on the mountain keeps its leaves unchanged through every season, how I wish that the hearts of people could likewise hold to an unwavering, constant integrity.)

(The *shiishiba* is an evergreen that does not shed its leaves or change its color with the turning of the seasons. If only human hearts could possess that same steadfast, unshakable constancy — this poem is a meditation on the virtue of *setsusō*: moral integrity and fidelity to one's principles, regardless of circumstance.)

【Left Page】
《□□□□ (Author name illegible)》　"Do not commit evil, even if it be a small thing."
(Classical maxim — cf. *Sanguo Zhi* and similar texts)

　Telling oneself
　　that "this much
　　　is merely
　　　　the way of the floating world" —
　permitting it
　　within one's heart —
　　　it is in just this way
　　　　that transgressions
　　　　　so carelessly
　　　　　　come to be.

(By saying to oneself, "this is just how the world works — a thing this small is surely permissible," one allows the heart to excuse wrongdoing. And it is precisely in such casual, unguarded self-permission that sins, one by one, accumulate so lightly and thoughtlessly.)

(The most dangerous moment is not when we knowingly choose evil, but when we tell ourselves that a small wrong is no wrong at all — that it is merely "the custom of this fleeting world." It is in that quiet permission granted within the heart that transgressions take root. No evil is too small to refuse.)
