【Right Page】
《Lord Minamoto no Yorinobu》　"Rashness and haste never bring achievement."

　Had I not hurried so,
　　　I need not have been drenched —
　　　　　for behind the traveler
　　　the shower that swept
　　　　across the field path
　　　　　is already clearing.

(If only one had not rushed out in haste, one would never have been caught in the rain — for just as the passing shower over the wild road clears up in the wake of the traveler who hurried through it, so too does rash and hasty action lead only to needless hardship. Patience, not haste, is what brings true success.)

【Left Page】
《Lord Kōjun》　"If others can do it in one attempt, I shall attempt it a hundred times." (from the *Zhongyong* / *Doctrine of the Mean*)

【Upper verse】
　If one walks on
　　　without flagging,
　even the ends
　　　of a thousand leagues
　　　　shall come into sight.

【Lower verse】
　Even at the pace
　　　of an ox —
　　　　slow though it may be.

(No matter how slow the pace — even if it be as unhurried as an ox — if one walks on without ever growing idle or weary, then even the far reaches of a thousand leagues shall at last be reached.)

(Where others may achieve something in a single try, one must be willing to repeat the effort a hundred times over. Yet with such perseverance — plodding on like an ox, unhurried but unceasing — one can travel a thousand leagues and reach what once seemed impossibly distant.)
