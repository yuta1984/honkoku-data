【Right Page】
《[Author name illegible]》　"Not losing the heart of the newborn child" (Mencius)

　As a young child
　　　　gains wisdom
　　　　　　one thing
　　　　　　　　at a time,
　how sad it is
　　to see them drift
　　　　ever further
　　　　　　from the Buddha.

(The "heart of the newborn child" — the pure, uncorrupted mind that Mencius speaks of — is the mind we are all born with. Yet as a child grows and acquires knowledge and worldly cleverness one by one, they paradoxically stray further and further from the original pure mind, the mind of the Buddha. This is a cause for deep grief.)

【Left Page】
《"Not to teach one's child is the father's fault"》

　I, who have been so negligent,
　　　　may barely deserve
　　　　　　to be called human—
　and yet, how earnestly I wish
　　to make my child
　　　　into a true human being.
　Where, I wonder,
　　　　will this child's path lead?

(I myself, having been lazy and neglectful in my own learning and moral cultivation, can scarcely claim to be a proper human being. And yet, precisely because of this, I pray all the more fervently that my child will become a person of true virtue. But I am filled with anxious uncertainty about what path lies ahead for this child.)
