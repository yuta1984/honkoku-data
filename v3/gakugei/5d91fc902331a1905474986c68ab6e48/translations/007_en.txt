[Right folio]

[Upper section]

The "seven emotions" — joy, anger, sorrow, pleasure, love, hatred, and desire — are a path (an aspect of human nature) from which even sages and the wise cannot depart. How, then, could anyone entirely avoid feeling anger or resentment? To be angry at things worthy of anger, and to rejoice at things worthy of rejoicing, is the constant manner of the sage. Nevertheless, even when there is cause for anger, "that belongs to the other party," and even when there is cause for joy, "that too belongs to the other party" — why should one carry it within one's own heart? This is the very reason why Yan Hui (Gankai) did not transfer his anger onto others. The phrase "do not scatter it so recklessly" (*sa no mi wa chirashi so*) also expresses this same spirit. The word *sa no mi* means "recklessly" or "excessively."

[Lower section]

In this world,
　　morning and evening
one's belly
　　rises like Mount Tatsuta —
do not scatter
　　that flushed face
　　　of autumn leaves
　so recklessly.

(*In this world, there are things each morning and evening that stir one to anger. Yet do not scatter abroad — as if scattering the scarlet maple leaves of Mount Tatsuta — that reddened face of rage.*)

[Left folio]

[Upper section]

The phrase "atsukahi-gusa" (lit. "the grass of dealings/managing affairs") refers to things that require careful attention and management — that is, the troublesome matters and social obligations one encounters in the world. As long as one lives and mingles in this world, one cannot get along without knowing how to handle such "atsukahi-gusa" (the ways of the world, the art of managing affairs). Those who pay not the slightest attention to their own conduct, yet presume to speak with an air of cleverness, and who do not understand the shortcomings of others, will find misfortune descending upon them at once. It is just like the saying "when the lips are lost, the teeth feel cold" (*kuchibiru ochite ha samuku nari* — i.e., the loss of one's neighbor brings hardship to oneself), or like a quarrel next door that becomes a noisy intrusion upon one's own ears. If something ill befalls another person, one should quickly take the matter in hand and do what one can to help.

[Lower section]

One who does not know
　　even a drop
　　　of the dew
of the world's
　　*atsukahi-gusa* —
　　the art of managing affairs —
is truly
　　a sorry
　　　soul indeed.
