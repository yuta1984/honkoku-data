[Right folio]

[Upper section]

There are historical examples of borrowing roads (territories or highways) from neighboring lords in order to strike an enemy, and so such cases may indeed arise. However, if such requests are repeated too often, anyone would find them tiresome. In that case, one ought to exercise proper consideration and thoughtfulness. The preceding poem said that "one should generally not borrow things," and now this poem says that "borrowing and lending are the ways of this fleeting world," while further expressing the sentiment that when such requests become too frequent, they are a nuisance. One should savor and reflect deeply on this meaning.

[Lower section]

In this world,
　　telling others
　　　of one's needs
　　is common custom —
but when it is
　　repeated
　　　again and again,

how tiresome
　it must become!

(*It is the way of the world to ask others for favors, but when such requests pile up repeatedly, they will surely come to be regarded as a nuisance.*)

[Left folio]

[Upper section]

"Is it only the cherry blossoms at their peak and the moon perfectly unclouded that one should admire?" — surely one should not deeply lament the scattering of blossoms or the declining of the moon. For these are all the natural conditions of this fleeting world. Here, by invoking the moon and the flowers, all manner of things are encompassed, and the nature of the ephemeral world is expressed. Where the text says *ikuhodo mo naki* ("not lasting very long"), one should also read into it the sense of "not even enough time to live out one's life." In this way, within the brief span of a life as short as a single joint of reed — a span no more than a dream — to spend one's mornings and evenings consumed in resentment and complaint over all manner of good and bad happenings, over the various affairs of this world: such is the state of mind that is truly pitiable. One should simply uphold the way of harmony (*wa no michi*).

[Lower section]

In this world
　that lasts
　　not long —
　　　over
　　moon
　　　and flowers,
to harbor
　resentment
　　and complaint
is how
　　tasteless
　　　a thing!

(*In a world that does not last long, to waste one's time in resentment and grumbling over the moon and flowers — over all the transient things of life — is truly a hollow and futile thing.*)
