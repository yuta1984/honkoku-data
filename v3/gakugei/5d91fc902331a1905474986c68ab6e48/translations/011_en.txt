[Right folio]

[Upper section]

This is the spirit expressed in poems from the *Kokin Wakashū* and other collections — the sense captured in the phrase *ōsa kirusa* ("if you raise one side, the other falls"), the back-and-forth of "say this and you get that response." Among the many people of this world, there are surely those who carry this kind of disposition, as one can see from even a general survey of human society. This all arises from a twisted heart that does not know the way of harmony (*wa no michi*) and insists only on asserting itself. One should exercise self-restraint and reflect carefully, so as not to be utterly abandoned by one's companions and friends. A person who, even after death, is remembered and longed for as someone who was "graceful and gentle" — how admirable and worthy of emulation such a person would be!

[Lower section]

　How thoroughly
　　tired of them
　　　people have
　　become —
those in this world
　who say this
　　and say that,
making themselves
　so difficult
　　and trying:
　　　even those around them
　　　　have grown weary.

(*Those in this world who constantly complain and quibble, making themselves difficult to get along with — even the people around them will eventually grow completely tired of them.*)

[Left folio]

[Upper section]

Du Mu (杜牧, *Toboku*) of China was a man of incomparable literary genius, and he wrote the "Rhapsody on the Epang Palace" (*A'fang gong fu*, 阿房宮賦) with such vivid mastery that it seemed as though he had seen it with his own eyes, a thousand years after the fact. And yet, as he himself argued, it could in no way compare to what Li Si (李斯) and Zhao Gao (趙高) — people who were actually present at the time — witnessed firsthand. The present poem should be understood in a similar vein. There are countless examples throughout history of rulers who accepted the slanderous words of treacherous ministers as the truth and thereby brought calamity upon their kingdoms; of men who severed the bonds of marriage and shunned the fellowship of friends; and of cases where a stepmother's slander caused a father to neglect his own true children. One should savor and truly understand the meaning of this poem.

[Lower section]

In this world,
　make what
　　you see
　　　with your own eyes
your truth.

　　　　What you merely
　　　　　hear told

　　　　　　　　is a thing
　　　　　　　　　　far away.

(*In this world, you should trust what you yourself have witnessed with your own eyes as the truth. That which you have only heard from others is something distant from reality.*)
