Even if one were to govern the realm, what task could there be that one could not accomplish? This is precisely why such a person is dependable in all things — and it is the great guiding principle of the world that everyone should be this way.

Regarding the phrase "brothers" (*ani ototo*): one should read the character *uyamahi* (reverence/respect) as meaning "to revere one's elder brother," attaching it to the character for *otōto* (younger brother); and likewise, one should read the character *hagukumu* (to nurture/cherish) as meaning "to cherish one's younger brother," attaching it to the character for *ani* (elder brother). This is a matter of literary grammar (rhetorical convention). The intent of the commentary is already made clear by the preceding poem.

[Lower section]

O brothers —
　　to show
　　　reverence
　and to cherish one another:
　this is how
　　　all should
　　　　surely
　be in
　　this world.

[Upper section]

The phrase "without negligence" (*tōkan naki*) means a sincere and attentive heart. Among ordinary people, it is common that as one grows intimate with another, the feeling of respect gradually fades. Yan Pingzhong (*Anpei-chū*) was praised by Confucius himself for being skilled in forming friendships and maintaining respect even after long acquaintance. This accords with the saying in the *Quli* (*Kyokurei*) chapter of the *Book of Rites*: "The worthy person, even when familiar, still shows reverence" (*nare te kei su*). In the *Tsurezuregusa* (Essays in Idleness), there is a tale of a monk from Ninnaji temple who, in his eagerness to enliven a drinking party, placed a three-legged cauldron (*ashikanae*) on his head, only to find that it could not be removed, resulting in a prolonged ordeal. Incidents of this kind seem to arise from the pitfalls of excessive familiarity (going astray). There is surely also a proper manner in which to conduct friendships, and there ought to be an appropriate path to follow.

[Lower section]

In this world,
　without
　　negligence,
　one ought to be
　　　courteous
　　and sincere —
　　　　this is how
it should
　rightly be.
