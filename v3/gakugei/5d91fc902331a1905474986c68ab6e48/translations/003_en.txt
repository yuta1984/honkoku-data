His verses were likened to gold and jade, and there were many that were passed down and spoken on the lips of people throughout the world. For this reason, those who practiced *haikai* revered him as the founder of their way. In particular, during the Daiei era, he composed one hundred *waka* verses in a single night, bestowed them upon his disciples, and made them a guide for moral instruction (*teikin*). The clarity and accessibility of his words must have been born of his intention that even foolish children and those of low station might recite them aloud, savor them in their hearts, readily understand them, and take them as lessons for daily life.

[Left folio]

Furthermore, at the very beginning of the collection he makes known that filial piety (*kō*) is the foundation of all virtuous conduct (*hyakkō*), and he expounds above all on the way of the Five Constant Virtues (*gojō*). Placing the two characters for "the world" (*yononaka*) at the head of each verse, he titled the work *Yononaka Hyakushu* ("One Hundred Verses on the World"). When one reflects on this, is it not entirely fitting that it has come to be called "the *Analects* of Ise" (*Ise Rongo*) among people of the world? The great teachings of this land — this sacred land of the gods — surely need not yield even to the profound precepts of the Duke of Zhou and Confucius from those distant foreign shores.
