[Right folio]

[Upper section]

It is the way of the world that the person you care for does not necessarily care for you in return. That Qu Yuan (屈原) drowned himself in the Miluo River (汨羅), and that Jia Yi (賈誼) was sent away to Changsha (長沙) — both of these misfortunes arose from the mistaken assumption that others would think and feel just as they themselves did. In the same way, even among friends, if one voices one's opinions too forcefully, it may well lead to estrangement. The *Analects* (*Lunyu*) say: "When friends remonstrate too often, they will be distanced." It is perhaps best to let things pass quietly, without voicing everything one thinks. The poem that says "there is no one who feels exactly as I do" speaks a most reasonable truth. For a retainer serving a lord, it is said that if one has remonstrated three times and still gone unheeded, one should depart.

[Lower section]

(*In this world, if you assume that others share your own feelings and intentions, you will inevitably find that there are differences and misunderstandings.*)

[Left folio]

[Upper section]

In the "Quli" (*Proper Conduct*) chapter of the *Book of Rites* (*Liji*), it is written: "The essence of propriety is to humble oneself and honor others. Even toward a peddler carrying his wares on his back, there is always something worthy of respect — how much more so toward those of wealth and high rank!" The phrase "those who carry and peddle" (*fuhan no mono*) refers to the sort of lowly people who carry loads on their backs and travel about selling goods — humble folk of the mountains and byways. Just as a lean horse may be mistaken for a poor one, or a man of poverty mistaken for one lacking in wisdom, one should reflect carefully on the nature of human judgment in this world. One must never harbor an attitude of contempt or disregard toward those one considers below oneself, even in passing. And how much more does this apply to those who stand above oneself!

[Lower section]

you must

(*Even toward those you consider to be of low and humble station — people of the mountains and fields — you must never permit yourself to look down upon them with contempt.*)
