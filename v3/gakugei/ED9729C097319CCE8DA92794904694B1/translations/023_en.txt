**Hitoyo-zuka (One-Night Mound)**　Located in the fields to the southwest of the same area. This place is called "Maeno." According to tradition, during the time of the Hōjō clan of Odawara, when their forces were attempting to seize the castle of the Chiba clan, the attacking soldiers constructed a gun emplacement (artillery platform) at this site in a single night, fired a great cannon from atop this mound toward the castle, and ultimately set the castle garrison ablaze in a fire attack.

**Saitaisan Enpukuji**　Located approximately two chō to the southwest of Kumano Gongen Shrine, in Nishidai village. It is a Zen temple of the Sōtō school, affiliated with Seishōji temple at the foot of Atago hill in Shiba. The principal image is a seated statue of Shaka Nyorai (Śākyamuni Buddha) in the "Nenge" (Flower-Holding) pose, measuring one shaku four or five sun in height, and is said to be the work of the monk Gyōki Bosatsu. Some say, however, that only the head was made by Gyōki, while the rest of the body is the work of a later craftsman. The temple was founded by Ōta Dōkan, the lay monk, and its first abbot (kaisan) was the Venerable Unkō Shuntoku, the fifth generation head of Ryūonji temple in Ogose. 《Marginal note: This is one of eighteen temples founded by Unkō.》 It is said that when this temple was formerly located in Kawagoe, it was a sub-temple of Ryūonji. At present, it is endowed with a temple stipend of twenty koku of rice. The temple possesses an old document from the Eishō era. Its text reads as follows:

　　　Shimura Nishidai no uchi, Obara Saburō's current residence and the fields on both sides thereof,
