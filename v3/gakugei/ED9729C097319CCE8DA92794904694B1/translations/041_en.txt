> *Wakakusa no / tsuma mo komoaranu / fuyu sare ni / yakate mo karuru / Nobidomé no tsuka*
>
> (In the winter when not even a young wife hides in the young grass, the Nobidomé Mound too shall soon wither away in the burning fields.)
>
> — *Dōkō Jugo* [the Retired Archbishop Dōkō]

*(Interlinear note: Passing this place, there is a market held in the village called Hizaori. [The rest is omitted.]*

*(Interlinear note: It may be noted that in ancient times, the practice of setting fire to open fields, burning the grass to use as fertilizer, and then sowing seeds was called "yakihata" (slash-and-burn agriculture), or "hita" (fire-field). The practice known today in Chichibu district and Shinshū province as "yakikari soba" [buckwheat grown by slash-and-burn method] is precisely this. However, because there was danger of such fires, when they burned strongly, spreading to people's dwellings, embankments and mounds were built to intercept and stop the wild fires — and it is from this practice that the name "Nobidomé" [fire-stop] likely derives. The mounds within the precincts of Heirinzi today, referred to by names such as "Tsukumo-zuka" and "Narihira-zuka," are probably of the same kind. It may be that such names were assigned by later generations under the influence of the Tales of Ise.)*

## Kinhōzan Heirin Zen Temple

Also known as Yōshin'in. It is located approximately eight chō [~870 meters] to the east of the Nobidomé highway. It is a Zen temple belonging to the Myōshinji school of Kyoto.

*(Interlinear note: It is said that in ancient times it belonged to the Daitokuji school.)*

The founding abbot was the Great Monk Sekishitsu Zenkyū.

*(Interlinear note: A stone monument records: "Dharma heir of the Monk Kobayashi [Korin Mō] of Hōning [Baoning], Tang China; passed away on the twenty-fifth day of the ninth month of Kōō 1 [1389].")*

The restorer of the temple was known as the Great Monk Settō.

*(Interlinear note: He passed away on the second day of the ninth month of Genna 9 [1623].)*

The temple was originally located in Iwatsuki, in Adachi district of the same province [Musashi], but was relocated to its present site in Kanbun 3 [1663].

*(Interlinear note: At the time when the monk Sekiin was serving as abbot.)*

*(Interlinear note: The former site is in Iwatsuki, which today corresponds to the areas called "Kanashige Village" and "Heirinzi Village.")*

There are four subsidiary buildings within the temple precinct.

**Buddha Hall (Butsuden)** — The principal image of worship is Śākyamuni Tathāgata.

*(Interlinear note: This Buddha Hall is said to have been moved and rebuilt from Iwatsuki.)*

**Mountain Gate (Sanmon)** —

*(Interlinear note: Located in front of the Buddha Hall. Wooden images of the Sixteen Arhats are enshrined in the upper story. This mountain gate is said to have been moved as it was from Iwatsuki and rebuilt here.)*
