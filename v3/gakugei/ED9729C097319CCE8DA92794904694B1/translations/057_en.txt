## Kotesashi Plain (小手指原)

The area spanning approximately thirteen to fourteen *chō* (roughly 1.5 km) to the northwest of Kitano Shrine, encompassing the regions around Kawagoe and the Iruma River, is collectively known as Kotesashi Plain.

[Interlinear note] It is said by local people that the old site of Kotesashi Plain remains in Shimo-Nerima Village, Toshima District, though this is difficult to verify. Scholar Arai Hakuseki stated that Kotesashi Plain refers to the land stretching six or seven *ri* (roughly 24–28 km) in all directions to the northwest of Kitano Mononobe Tenjin Shrine. He noted that it has since been cultivated and reduced to a mere seven hundred-odd *koku* of land. And so on.

[From the *Shin'yōshū* poetry anthology]
*On crossing into Musashi Province and encamping at a place called Kotesashi Plain, while assigning positions to the troops, and commanding the warriors to be courageous, the following poem was composed:*

*Kimi no tame yo no tame nani ka oshikaran / sutete shi nochi aru inochi nari se ba*
("What is there to regret, for the sake of my lord and for the sake of the world? / If only this life, cast aside, might yet endure afterward.")
— His Imperial Highness Prince Muneyoshi (Munenaga), Minister of Central Affairs

[As recorded in the *Taiheiki*] On the twentieth day of the intercalary second month of Shōhei 7 (corresponding to the first year of Bunna in the Northern Court calendar), at the Hour of the Dragon, the forces descended upon Kotesashi Plain in Musashino. One commanding general, Nitta Musashi-no-kami Yoshimune, divided over fifty thousand cavalry into five contingents. Under another commander, Nitta Sahyōe-no-suke Yoshioki, a force of over twenty thousand cavalry was deployed, holding positions across a six-*ri* radius. A further force of over twenty thousand cavalry under Wakiya Saemon-no-suke Yoshiharu was arrayed at five separate positions. Upon hearing that the enemy was at Kotesashi Plain, the Shōgun (Ashikaga Takauji) divided his one hundred thousand cavalry into five contingents and advanced along the central road. Thus, the combined forces of the Nitta and Ashikaga clans — two hundred thousand cavalry in all — descended upon Kotesashi Plain. When the enemy raised three battle cries, the allied forces responded with three cries of their own. The sound seemed to reverberate as high as the thirty-three heavens and as deep as the foundation of the earth — so tremendous was the uproar.

[Passage omitted]

Aeba no Myōtsurumaru, eighteen years of age, a youth of incomparable beauty, served as the commander of the *Hana Ikki* (Flower Band) that day. Adorned especially with flowers as befitting his role, he charged out at the very vanguard of the Hana Ikki. He fought fiercely against the seven thousand cavalry of the Kodama clan, was pressed hard, and was driven back without being able to turn the tide even once. The Shōgun's one hundred thousand cavalry retreated in a straight line without looking back. Nitta Yoshimune advanced ahead of his own standard, declaring: "He is a rebel against the imperial court and the enemy who slew my father. If I do not take Takauji's head this very moment and display it before the army gate, when shall I ever have my chance?" Paying no heed whatsoever to the other enemy troops retreating to the north and south, he pursued only the great banner bearing the two-line *hikiryō* crest wherever it fled. As the fleeing forces whipped their horses and the pursuers drove their fastest steeds, the forty-six *ri* (roughly 180 km) of the Bandō road from Kotesashi Plain to Ishihama was covered in an instant. When the Shōgun crossed over Ishihama — [passage omitted] — some twenty-odd of his close attendants wheeled around to meet the pursuing enemy as they began to ford the river, grappling with them one after another until they fell in battle. During this interval, the Shōgun barely managed to escape his desperate situation and galloped up onto the opposite bank.
