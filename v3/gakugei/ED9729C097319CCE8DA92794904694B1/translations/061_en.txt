**The "Eōgō Kajisui" (Holy Manifestation Sacred Water)**
Located in the West Valley. [Inserted note: In ancient times, when the Great Master Kōbō Daishi conducted one thousand sessions of *goma* (fire ritual) practice over the course of a summer retreat, through an auspicious sign from a divine dragon, he sought and found a clear spring at that spot. From that time to the present, a thousand years later, it is said that this sacred spring never runs dry even during drought. On one occasion, a devout person looked into the water and beheld the venerable form of Daihi (the Great Compassionate One), and it is said that from this event the spring came to be called "Eōgō" (Divine Manifestation). It is reported that not a few people have drunk this well water and been cured of illness.]

**The Shrine of Biwa-jima Benzaiten**
[Inserted note: Located on a small island in the pond at the eastern entrance of Senjuya Valley. It is so named because the island is shaped like a *biwa* (lute). In the summer of the *kimō* year of the Hōreki era, the Venerable Ryōsei of this temple had workers dredge the pond, and from the mud they recovered two stone monuments. Inscribed on the face of the monuments were the characters for Benzaiten along with dates: one marked the 5th year of Bun'an (己巳, 1448), and the other the 2nd year of Jōji (癸卯, 1363).]

**The Plaque of the Niō-mon (Two Kings Gate)**
[Inserted note: "Goan-zan"] Written by the eighty-eight-year-old elder, Provisional Senior Prelate Kōsei, of Tsukubazan-saki Goji-in Temple.

**The temple's *engi* (founding legend) states:**

In ancient times, in accordance with the votive wish of Emperor Shōmu, the Great Bodhisattva Gyōki, while traveling through various provinces to teach the Buddhist teachings, arrived at this place to find that the sun had already set. He therefore hung his staff (shakujo) beneath the trees and spent the night in sutra-chanting and Zen meditation. As deep night approached, a voice chanting the *Senju Dhāraṇī* (Thousand-Armed Dhāraṇī) was heard from within the forest. The Great Master, filled with a sense of wonder, searched the surrounding area, whereupon a wondrous fragrance drifted through the air, a sacred light shone brilliantly from atop a tree, and the holy form of Senju Daihi (the Thousand-Armed Bodhisattva of Great Compassion) appeared suddenly in a divine manifestation. He then waited for dawn, felled the sacred tree, carved a likeness of the venerable image he had beheld, and enshrined it here permanently for the salvation of all sentient beings. [Inserted note: It is for this reason that this place is called "Senjuya" (Thousand-Armed Valley).]

Later, during the Kōnin era, when Kōbō Daishi was on his way to Yudonosan in Ushū (Dewa Province),

he stopped at this place along the road. Thereupon an old man came and said: "In this mountain there is a sacred image of Daihi made by the Great Bodhisattva Gyōki, yet people do not know of its sacred power. I have been awaiting the arrival of the Great Virtue (Kōbō Daishi) at this place. Quickly establish a temple hall here." After saying this, the whereabouts of the old man became unknown. [Inserted note: This old man is venerated as the Chishu Gongen (Local Deity of the Land), and even now remains in the mountain.] Thereupon the Great Master entered the mountain in search, and indeed found as the old man had told: the image of Senju Daihi, along with the attendant images of Tamon (Bishamonten) and Fudō and two other venerable figures. He then built a simple grass-thatched hall on the site. [Inserted note: This is the founding of the present temple.]

Thereafter, during the Kōan era, a great epidemic spread throughout the land, and not a few people died. At this time, an old monk went from house to house and proclaimed: "In my hermitage resides a venerable sacred image of Daihi. Those who come and pray shall be freed from all illness. If you wish to find my hermitage, go to the place in the mountain that glows with light deep in the night." The villagers followed his instructions, guided by the light in the night to this place, and worshipped the sacred image, praying for relief from illness. They received great miraculous benefit and rejoiced with jubilation, and people came without cease day and night. [Inserted note: Based on the old monk's words, the mountain was named "Goan" (My Hermitage), and because the place where the Great Master had practiced Zen meditation was called "Kongōjō-kutsu" (Vajrayāna Cave), the temple came to be known as "Konjō-in" (院 of the Diamond Vehicle). The place that emitted the sacred light is the Senju-dō (Thousand-Armed Hall) at Yamaguchi.]
