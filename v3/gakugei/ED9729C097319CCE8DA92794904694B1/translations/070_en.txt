　　　　　…a mountain ascetic (*yamabushi*) named Fukusen, who at Kannon-ji temple produced a bamboo grass cutting, whereupon he noticed that something called *yamaimo* (mountain yam) was unexpectedly present there, and composed a *haikai* verse:

　　　*Noyasobi no / sakana ni yama no / imo soete / hori motometaru / tokorozawa kana*
　　　(Seeking to dig up mountain yam to accompany our field-feast — ah, Tokorozawa!)
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　— Dōkō Jugo

　　　[Note: As for the mountain ascetic named Fukusen mentioned above, the site has now fallen into ruin, surviving only barely in the name of a field-plot in this area, known as "Fukusenbō-zuka." Consequently, even among the local inhabitants, those who know the name have become rare.]

**Yūseki-zan Shinkō-ji Temple** — also known as *Kannonin*. Located in the same district, to the west, at the entrance to the post-station, in a place called Kawara-juku. It belongs to the Shingi (New Rite) branch of Shingon Buddhism, and is affiliated with Anraku-ji Temple at Nariki. The principal image is a standing figure of *Shō Kannon* (the Correct-Form Avalokiteśvara), measuring over one *shaku* in height, said to be the work of the Great Bodhisattva Gyōki. According to tradition, during the Kenkyū era, when Lord Yoritomo went hunting at Nasuno in Shimotsuke Province and also at Mihara, the land on which a temporary lodging (*kariya*) had been built for the occasion was subsequently donated to be attached to the principal image of this temple. [This was the land equivalent to six *koku* in yield.] As the years went by, however, during a period of military strife the land was seized by others. Then, in the Genkō era, when Lord Nitta Yoshisada launched his punitive campaign against Hōjō Takatoki, he came to this temple and offered prayers before the principal image. He then launched his assault on Kamakura and destroyed Takatoki. Revering this as the divine protection of the sacred image, after his triumphant return he once again donated the six-*koku* plot of land that had previously been seized, and from that time onward, the temple continued without interruption…
