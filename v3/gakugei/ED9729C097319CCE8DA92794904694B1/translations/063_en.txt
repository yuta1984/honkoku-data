Having raised a righteous army from Kōzuke Province (present-day Gunma Prefecture) and unfurled his banner on the Musashino plain, Nitta Yoshisada engaged the Kamakura forces in battle at Fuchū and Bunbai-kawara. His army was defeated, and he withdrew to the Kume River, establishing his encampment on the eastern peak of this mountain. At that time, Lord Yoshisada presented a written prayer to Kannon (Avalokiteśvara), beseeching divine military merit for the subjugation of enemies of the Imperial Court. That night, in Yoshisada's dream, the Thousand-Armed Kannon of Great Compassion appeared on horseback, personally bestowing upon him a bow and arrows from her own hands. Upon waking from this dream, his gratitude knew no bounds. He fashioned a whip from a branch of a cherry tree in the garden, made a solemn vow, and set forth for the camp at Sekido. Soon thereafter, tens of thousands of troops joined from the direction of Echigo and Shinano Provinces, and in no time the entire Kamakura clan was destroyed. *(The place where the banner was planted is called "Shōgunzuka" (General's Mound) to this day, and the cherry tree known as "Yoshisada's Vow Cherry" is said to still flourish there.)*

Long afterward, during the Tenshō era (1573–1592), the temple was graciously favored with reverence by the reigning house (the Tokugawa), and upon the bestowal of the vermilion-sealed temple domain, its prosperity increased a hundredfold compared to ancient times, and it became a sacred site protected by both humans and heavenly beings.

**Shinni-zan Butsuzō-in Shōraku-ji Temple** — Located approximately twelve *chō* (about 1.3 km) to the west of the Yamaguchi Kannon-dō, in the village of Shōrakuji. It belongs to the Shingi Shingon sect and is affiliated with Shinfuku-ji in Nakato. The founder of the temple's revival (*chūkō kaisan*) is known as the Venerable Shinkei. *(He passed away on the fourteenth day of the first month of Genna 9 [1623].)*  The principal object of worship (honzon) was recently lost in a fire, and newly
