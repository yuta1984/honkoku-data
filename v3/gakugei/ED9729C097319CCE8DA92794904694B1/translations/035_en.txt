### The Historic Site of Tachino (立野)

Although there are several places referred to as "Tachino" today, there is a place called Tatemura (舘村), belonging to Niikura District and neighboring the south of Hikimata Village, which is likely to be the original site.
*[Interlinear note: However, the lands of former sites all tend to follow the names of the villages and hamlets that replaced them, and the names denoting open fields and moorlands naturally lose their ancient names. By the medieval period, "Tachi" (多知) had imperceptibly shifted in reading to "Tate" (多天), and the written form has now likewise been changed to "Tatemura" (舘村).]*

The local people say that all the land from either side of the Kuromakawa River to the area of the Yanasegawa River and Shiroko was in ancient times the site of a horse pasture (*makino*). Considering this claim, the land is indeed close to the water's edge and the terrain is most convenient for grazing horses; the local people's account would seem to have considerable foundation.
*[Interlinear note: Places with the same name are also found in Adachi, Kami, and Tsuzuki districts.]*

Furthermore, to the west of Edo, there are many place names connected to horse pastures: Nerima, Chikumasawa, Uchinomaki, Kuromakawa, Hikimata (Hikimata) *[Interlinear note: nowadays "mata" (馬多) is written simply as "mata" (又)]*, Umahikisawa, Komabayashi, Nomaki *[Interlinear note: now written "Nomaki" (野馬木)]*, and others — all of which serve as evidence of their connection to the ancient horse pastures.

**From the *Gosen(waka)shū* (後撰集):**

*(Preface)* When Kanesuké Ason was serving as a lesser captain of the Left Palace Guards (*Sakon no Shōshō*), on the day he was to depart to receive the horses of Musashino, an unexpected matter arose, and in his place another lesser captain of the same office went to receive them; from Ōsaka Barrier he sent back the attendants (*zuishin*) and sent this poem (to Kanesuké):

> *Aki-giri no / Tachino no koma wo / hiku toki wa / kokoro ni norite / kimi zo koishiki*
> (When leading the colts of Tachino through the autumn mist, my heart rides forth, and how I long for you.)
> — Fujiwara no Tadafusa

**From the *Shin Chokusenshū* (新勅撰集):**

> *Hi wo hete wa / akikaze samushi / saoshika no / Tachino no mayumi / momiji shi ni keri*
