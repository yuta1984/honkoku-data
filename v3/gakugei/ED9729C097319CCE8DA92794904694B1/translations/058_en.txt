*(Continuation from the Taiheiki)*

The fleeing enemy numbered over thirty thousand cavalry; the pursuing allied forces numbered a mere five hundred. The far bank of the river rose sheer as a standing folding screen, and tens of thousands of enemy cavalry wheeled around and held their ground there as a last stand. The sun had already passed well into the Hour of the Rooster, and it was no longer possible to distinguish the deep pools from the shallows in the river. Nitta Musashi-no-kami Yoshimune could not order his men to ford the river, and no allied forces followed from behind. Grinding his teeth in frustration — "What a bitter thing this is!" — he turned back toward the main camp. Yoshimune said, "We have failed to cut down the Shōgun. Today the sun has already set, so let us gather our forces and attack Ishihama tomorrow." And so he turned back to Kotesashi Plain.

"Where is His Lordship the Sahyōe-no-suke (Yoshioki) holding position?" he asked of the soldiers he encountered on the way. They answered, "His Lordship the Sahyōe-no-suke and His Lordship Wakiya were holding position together and were well for a time, but they were defeated by Lord Nikiand have retreated to the east." He then asked, "And those watch-fires visible over there — are they enemy or allied?" They replied, "There should not be a single allied horseman in this area. Those are likely watch-fires lit by the forces of the Niki brothers or by men of the White-Banner League (*Shirohata Ikki*). It would seem unwise to remain in this area with so few men. We suggest you slip away under cover of night, cross over toward Usui Pass (*Fuefuki Tōge*), await the massing of forces from Echigo and Shinano, and give battle once again." Upon hearing this, Yoshimune

pondered for a moment and said, "Indeed, that is the right course of action," then asked "Where is Usui Pass?" and departed under cover of night. And so on. *(The above is a summary of the essential points.)*

[Interlinear note] The passage in the *Taiheiki* stating that "Nitta Yoshimune pursued Shōgun Takauji and covered the forty-six *ri* of the Bandō road from Kotesashi Plain to Ishihama in an instant" does not refer to Ishihama on the Sumida River, but rather, according to local tradition, to the place called Ushihama (*Cow Shore*), located upstream along the Tama River from the landing at Hino (*Hino no Tsu*). Upon reflection, across the Tama River to the southwest of Ushihama lies land called "Ninomiya." It forms a sheer cliff, which accords well with the description in the *Taiheiki* that "the far bank of the river rose as high as a standing folding screen." Furthermore, the same text refers to "Fuefuki Pass" (also written *Saofuki Tōge*, read as *Usui*, and described as the border between Kōzuke and Shinano provinces) — this is likely an error by the author. In the present province (Musashino), there is a place called Shōgunzawa Village in Hiki District, which is the old site where General Tamura (*Tamura Shōgun*) established his camp during his eastern campaign. The local people have enclosed this land, built a small shrine, and venerate it as the Shōgun Daigongen. That place lies on the road from the area of the Iruma River to Kōzuke Province, to the northwest of the post-town of Imaichi (*Imaichi-juku*), and facing Shōgunzawa there is a mountain slope called Fuefuki Pass. The account that Yoshisada retreated after his defeat at the Battle of Bunbai, moved his forces to the Kume River, withdrew again to the Iruma River, and then descended toward Fuefuki Pass that same night — this cannot refer to the border between Kōzuke and Shinano provinces. The journey to the Kōzuke-Shinano border exceeds twenty *ri*, and it would be quite impossible to reach it in a single night. From the Iruma River to this pass is just over four *ri*, and it seems plausible that one could indeed reach it within the night.

## Yamaguchi Kannon Hall (山口観音堂)

Located in Niihori Village, approximately half a *michi* (roughly 2 km) to the southwest of Kitano Village. [The general area to the north of Sayama, adjoining Iruma District, is collectively called "Yamaguchi." Local custom holds that this place was the site of a castle belonging to a man called Yamaguchi Heinai Saemon.] The temple is known as Goan-zan (*Goan Mountain*) Kōnjōin (*Golden Ascension Cloister*) Shinkōji (*True Light Temple*), of the Shingon sect, affiliated with Gokokuji Temple in Ōtsuka, Edo. Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai) is venerated as its founding patriarch.

The principal image enshrined in the main hall is a standing statue of the Thousand-Armed Kannon (*Senju Kannon*), two *shaku* and three *sun* in height (approximately 70 cm), attributed to the great master Gyōki. The flanking attendant images are Tamonten (Vaiśravaṇa) and Fudō Myōō (Acalanātha), each approximately three *shaku* (roughly 90 cm) in height, said to be works of the great master Gyōki, or alternatively described as sacred images received through his spiritual attainment (*kantoku*).
