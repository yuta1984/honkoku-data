　The Right General, Lord Yoritomo, heard of this and pressed his request most insistently. Although for Hidehira the image was a treasured sacred icon kept in strict seclusion, he could not silently refuse the Shogun's stern command, and so he promptly consented. He prepared a lotus palanquin (*renyо̄*) and dispatched several tens of warriors to escort the sacred image to Kamakura. However, when the procession reached the vicinity of Fuchū in Musashi Province, the lotus palanquin became as immovable as a great boulder and would not advance at all. The Right *Bakka* (Lord Yoritomo) was informed of this, and declared, "Kamakura must not be a place with karmic affinity for this principal image," and ordered that it be returned to Ōshū. From this event, that place came to be called "Kurumakaeshi Village" (the Village of the Turned-Back Carriage). [Kurumakaeshi Village is situated near present-day Fuchū.] It is said that afterward, in accordance with a divine revelation, the image was enshrined here in Horinuchi Village. [At the gate-entrance of this temple there stands an ancient stone monument dated to Kenchō 8 (1256). In former times it was no more than a modest grass-thatched hermitage, but in Tenshō 10 (1582), under the imperial-titled Zen Master Tentoku — the Venerable Eishi Junfu — it was established as a proper temple. Thus Zen Master Tentoku is revered as the founding abbot (*kaisan*).]

**Tokorozawa** [also written as *Tokorosawa* 野老沢]

　This place is a post-station (*ekisha*) on the Chichibu Highway, falling within Iruma District (*kōri*). Markets are held on days with the numbers three and eight in the month, and the town is lively and bustling. From the Yotsuya Ōkido (Great Gate at Yotsuya) in Edo to this place is more than seven *ri* in the westerly direction. [It is said to be four *ri* to Kawagoe, and five *ri* to Ōme.]
