**Atago Gongen Shrine**
Located on a wooded hill to the southwest of the same location (Shakujii). It is considered the *suijaku* (manifestation in the form of a Shinto deity) of the principal Buddha image of Sanpō-ji Temple. The grounds measure approximately 150 paces east to west and over 100 paces north to south. According to tradition, this is the site of a castle belonging to Ōta Dōkan, and the local people call this area *Shiroyama* (Castle Hill). Before it embraces the Sekikawa River, and behind it backs onto the Osono-i (a well). To the north there is a small hill from which Mount Fuji can be seen. Several hundred paces to the south there is an embankment known as the "Dōkan Embankment." The local people say it served as a shortcut to *Kōjō* (Edo Castle).

**Hikawa Myōjin Shrine**
This serves as the guardian shrine (*chinju*) of five villages combined: the upper and lower villages of Shakujii, Tanaka, Seki, and Yahara. The annual festival (*reisai*) is held on the nineteenth day of the ninth month. Shrine attendants (*shajin*) and shrine maidens (*miko*) come from the Shinmei-gū Shrine in Shiba, Edo, to perform *kagura* (sacred music and dance). This is the long-established custom. Sacred rites are also performed on the twentieth day of the same month.

**Sanpō-ji Pond**
Located at the same place. The circumference is approximately 530-odd paces. In the middle there is a small islet, upon which a shrine to the Pond-Spirit Benzaiten has been erected. The waters of this pond are warm in winter and cool in summer. It does not overflow in floods, nor does it dry up in droughts. It flows abundantly and irrigates the farmland of several dozen villages; the downstream flow winds around the area of Itabashi and Ōji, and eventually merges with the Arakawa River.
*[Interlinear note: According to village elders, among the many fish in this pond, there are some bearing the imprint of a torii (shrine gate) upon them. It has been said from ancient times that to catch such fish will bring divine punishment.]*

## [Left Page]

**Teruhi Mound (Teruhi-zuka)**
*[Interlinear note: Located at the same place. According to the tradition passed down by village elders, the founder (kaisan) of this temple, once during his time in the capital, sat in attendance outside the seats of the nobility on the night of the fifteenth day of the eighth month, and offered this hokku (opening verse): "There is no moon — just as the sun shines, such is this night." The court nobles and attendants praised it greatly; it was presented for imperial inspection, and His Majesty was deeply moved, bestowing upon the monk the title "Teruhi Shōnin" (Saint Teruhi). This mound is likely the grave of Teruhi Shōnin, though this is not entirely certain.]*

**Site of Shakujii Castle (Shakuji no Shiro-ato)**
Located beside the Sanpō-ji Pond. The northern side of its grounds is bordered by the pond waters. The area called the *ōte* (main gate approach) has become paddy fields, but the shapes of the dry moats (*karabori*) on both sides still remain to this day. It is said that during the Bunmei era (1469–1487), the Toshima clan inhabited this castle.
*[Interlinear note: According to one person, the Toshima family genealogy states that Toshima Saburōhyōe Nyūdō Yasukage — son of Toshima Saburōhyōe Yasutomo — resided in this castle. After Yasukage's death, as his son was still young, his younger brother Sakon-no-tayū Kagemura succeeded to his elder brother's position, ruling over five districts of Musashi Province — Adachi, Tama, Niikura, and Toshima — and resided in Shakujii Castle. Based on this account, it appears that from Kagemura down to Kageyū-zaemon, successive generations held this place as their castle seat; however, as detailed below in the entry on Nerima Castle, on the eighteenth day of the fourth month of Bunmei 9 (1477), the castle was attacked and overthrown by Ōta Dōkan, after which it fell into ruin. During the Eiroku era (1558–1570), it is recorded in the Hōjō clan's *bungenchō* (register of retainers' stipends) that Ōta Shinrokurou, a retainer of the Odawara Hōjō clan, held the land of Shakujii.]*
*[Editorial note: Upon reflection, there is a temple in Shakujii called "Toshimayama Dōjō-ji," and the local people say that this too is the site of an ancient castle. Based on this, one might think it could have been a castle of the Toshima clan. The temple's mountain name (*sangō*) also seems to suggest a connection. This warrants further investigation.]*

**Shakujii Myōjin Shrine**
Located in Shakujii Village. It is maintained and worshipped by Sanpō-in. The divine object (*shintai*) of worship is a single sacred stone, which, according to tradition, was discovered in the ground when a well was being dug long ago.
*[Interlinear note: The stone is extremely hard like iron, and bears a faint bluish tint. It measures over two shaku in length, and about one shaku in circumference at its widest point. It is what is known as a "stone sword" (sekken), belonging to the category of ancient artifacts from antiquity called "thunder hammers" (raidzui) and the like.]*
Thus it is said that the place name "Shakujii" originated from this stone.

**Site of Nerima Castle (Nerima no Shiro-ato)**
Located beside Aizen-in in Kami-Nerima Village. It was the castle seat of a certain member of the Toshima clan.
