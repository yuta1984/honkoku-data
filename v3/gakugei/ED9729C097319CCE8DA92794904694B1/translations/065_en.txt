*(Upon reflection: In the mortuary register (*kakochō*) of Matsugaoka [Engaku-ji] in Kamakura, Sagami Province, the following entry appears: "Seventh year of Tenbun [1538], tenth month, seventh day — Ōimi Gosho Sahyōe-no-kami Yoshiaki, Hasshō-in Kūzen Dōtetsu." Perhaps the two characters "Kūtetsu" were divided and rendered as "Kūzen Dōtetsu." If so, this ancient document would be one that Yoshiaki presented to Futami Shōken. Examining the monogram (*kaō* [cursive seal]), it appears to resemble the character for "Yoshi" (義). However, since Yoshiaki's monogram is omitted from reference works such as the* Kaōyabu, *the* Zoku Kaōyabu, *and the* Ko-Ōyabu, *there is no means of verification. This matter should be corrected at a later date.)*

**Hako-no-Ike (Box Pond)**　This pond is located several hundred paces to the northwest of the post station (*ekisha*) at Hakonegasaki. *(This area lies along the route from Hachiōji to Nikkō-san in Shimotsuke Province (Yashū), and during the sixth and seventh months, more than half of the pilgrims traveling from the provinces of Ōu and elsewhere to climb Mount Ōyama in Sagami Province pass through here, making it a very busy thoroughfare at that season. Whether the area came to be called "Hakonegasaki" because Hakone Gongen was enshrined here as the tutelary deity, or whether this deity was invited here because the place was already called "Hakonegasaki,"*

*can no longer be determined.)* In ancient times the circumference of the pond was said to be over thirty *chō* (approximately 3.3 kilometers), but now it has been entirely reclaimed and converted into cultivated fields or transformed into woodland, leaving only a slight depression overgrown with grass, where the sound of wind through the pines serves as a substitute for the sound of waves—thus the pond barely preserves its former semblance. *(At present, within a small pond of about forty to fifty paces in circumference, there is a small islet upon which a little shrine to Benzaiten has been constructed. Water shield (*junsai*, *Brasenia schreberi*) is a local product of this area and is said to be of particularly fine flavor.)* The single peak that rises from the northeastern shore of this site marks the beginning of the Sayama hills, which extend eastward for approximately three *ri* (about 12 kilometers) *(the vicinity of Shōgunzuka being taken as the eastern terminus of these hills)*. For this reason, local people also call this pond "**Sayama-no-Ike** (Sayama Pond)."

> *Fuyu fukami / Hako no ike-be wo / asa yukeba / kōri no kagami / minu hito zo naki*
> (In the depths of winter, walking along the shores of Hako Pond at dawn, there is no one who fails to behold the mirror of ice.)　　— *Tomochika*

**Horikane-no-I (The Horikane Well)**　This is located in Horikane Village, over two *ri* (approximately 8 kilometers) to the south of Kawagoe. Since it lies beside the Sengen Shrine (*Asama-no-miya*), it is designated "Asama Horikane." *(The area in front of this shrine was the ancient Kamakura Highway (*Kamakura kaidō*), the road leading to Kōzuke and Shinano Provinces. The current shrine building was erected during the Keian era [1648–1652] by Lord Matsudaira Zushū. The administrative temple (*bettō*) is called Jiunan and belongs to the management of Kōrin-in in Kawagoe.)* To the left of the Sengen Shrine there is a depression in the ground, within which a stone well-frame (*igeta*) measuring approximately six *shaku* (about 1.8 meters) on each side, half-buried in the earth, is called the "**Horikane-no-I**" (the Horikane Well). Beside it stands a stone monument erected in former times by one Iwata, a retainer of Lord Akimoto of Kawagoe, measuring over five *shaku* (about 1.5 meters) in height.
