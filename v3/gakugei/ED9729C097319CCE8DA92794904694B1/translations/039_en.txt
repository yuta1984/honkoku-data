*(Interlinear note: In the travel record "Kaikoku Zakki," it is written: "Passing through Sasai, I proceeded to the site of Jūtama in Ōtsuka, Musashi Province, where the mountains and rivers changed many times over. That night at my lodging, [I composed the following poem].")*

> Scaling steep mountains, crossing turbulent seas,
> Everywhere the road ahead is fraught with hardship.
> In a sparse dwelling through the night, beneath wind and snow,

*(Interlinear note: It is noted that the "Sasai" mentioned in this travel record likely refers to Sasai in Koma District. The temple possesses six ancient documents.

*(Interlinear note: The first is dated the twenty-seventh day of the seventh month of Bunmei 12 [1480], concerning the position of annual ritual officiant (nenjū gyōji-shoku) at Kiyoto; it states that, in accordance with the petition submitted, the appointment was duly made, and that there must be no deviation from the stipulated conduct regarding the parishioners (montochū), the patrons of Kumano pilgrimage, and all those under the jurisdiction. Another document is a certificate dated the eighteenth day of the first month of Bunmei 19 [1487], with content essentially identical to the above. **Former Residence of Nanbata Danjō** — This refers to the land of Jūgyokuin.

*(Interlinear note: "Nanbata" is today written as "Nanbata [南波田]" or sometimes "Nanbata [南畑]." This land is flanked by the two rivers, the Arakawa and the Shinkawa, and has suffered frequent flood damage since ancient times. It is said that eventually, having obtained permission from the authorities, the written characters of the name were changed.)*

Nanbata Danjō-no-Chū was a retainer of Ōgigayatsu Uesugi Shuri-no-Taifu Tomooki, and guarded the castle of Matsuyama in the same province. On the twentieth day of the fourth month of Tenbun 15 [1546], at the night battle of Kawagoe, he fell into an old well at Tōmyōji-guchi and died an accidental death.

*(Interlinear note: The "Odawara-ki" and other records write this as "Tōmyōji [灯明寺].")*

*(Interlinear note: Today, within the town of Kawagoe, there is an ancient temple founded by Shinkōbō, the second-generation holy man of the Yūgyō sect, which is called "Tōmyōji [東明寺]" — this is said to be the former site. Whether "Tō [灯, lantern-light]" should be read as "Tō [東, east]" is worth further investigation.)*

What is said to be the grave of Danjō-no-Chū currently exists at a temple called Nichirenji in Mizuko Village.

*(Interlinear note: The gravestone bears the inscription "Nichiren Daikoji," with the date recorded as the sixteenth day of the fourth month of Tenshō 5, Hinoto-Ushi year [1577]. The date of death recorded thus greatly contradicts [the date of the battle]. This may perhaps be the grave of one of his kinsmen.)*

**Saizōin** — Located approximately four chō [~440 meters] to the west of Jūgyokuin in the same area. It is a Honzan-ha shugendo establishment, called Tōkaku-san Kannonji. The principal image, Shō-Kannon (Āryāvalokiteśvara), is a seated figure measuring five sun eight bu [~17.6 cm] in height, and is said to have been carved by Kōbō Daishi. According to tradition, when the Great Master visited this place in ancient times, he carved this image in response to a divine dream.

*(Interlinear note: The hall structure is built in the chōna-zukuri [adze-cut timber] style, differing from present-day architectural methods.)*
