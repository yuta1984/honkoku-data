[Side note: According to local legend, ever since the Founding Abbot sealed away the frogs, saying they were a disturbance to Buddhist study (*kangaku*), the frogs of this temple have had no voice.]

**From the Biography of the Founding Abbot (*Kaisanden*):**

The monk Shōkei (釈聖冏), whose religious name was *Yūrensha Ryōyo* (酉蓮社了誉), was of the Minamoto clan (*Genji*). He was the son of Shiraki Shimanokamiyoshimitsu, a retainer of the Satake clan — lords of Iwase in Kuji District, Hitachi Province (modern Ibaraki Prefecture), and a branch of the clan.

[Side note: "Mitsu" (満) is written as "Mitsu" (光) in some versions. His parents prayed at Iwase Myōjin Shrine, and he was born on the twenty-fifth day of the first month of Ryakuō 4 (1341), the year of the Metal Ox (*kanoto-mi*). Later, the eighteenth abbot of Jōfuku-ji temple in Furitura, the Venerable Shin'yo, opened a grass-thatched hall at the site of his birth and named it Tanjō-ji (Temple of Birth).]

At the age of five, his father Yoshimitsu died in battle. Their domain (*saiyu*) was seized by enemies, and their property was plundered by brigands. Mother and child therefore took refuge in the mountains, living in poverty through heat and cold for three full years. Afterwards, his mother entrusted this child to the Venerable Ryōjitsu of Jōfuku-ji on Mount Sōchi in Furitura, for the sake of his late father's salvation, and had him enter the priesthood.

[Side note: He was eight years old at the time. He took the religious name Shōkei.]

By nature he was exceptionally gifted and wise — hearing one thing, he would comprehend ten. At the age of ten, he first applied himself to study and mastered it with remarkable speed. By eleven, he had independently read widely across all schools of learning, both Buddhist and secular. He once had an audience with Master Renshō, from whom he received the oral and spiritual transmission of the profound lineage of Pure Land Buddhism transmitted through the three countries (India, China, and Japan). He also visited the residence of the Venerable Jōe of Kuwahara in Sagami Province (modern Kanagawa Prefecture), lodging outside the study hall to pursue his learning. Ultimately, he received complete transmission and ordination in the doctrines of the Shirahataichiha (White Flag) school, and spent four or five years spreading the teachings.

[Side note: "Shirahatа" (White Flag) takes its name from the place where the Venerable Jaku-e resided, and this became the name of the school.]

A great many people — both clergy and laity — received his teachings. The master

[Side note: at the age of forty-six]

returned to Jōyō (Hitachi Province). At that time, his teacher Ryōjitsu was already past eighty in age (*hachijun*), and so he appointed Master Shōkei as the head priest of Jōfuku-ji.

[Side note: at the age of seventy-five]

Furthermore, in the twenty-second year of the Ōei era (1415), the year of *kinoto-hitsuji*, he selected a quiet piece of land on the bank of Koishikawa in Musashi Province and built a hall there.

[Side note: This is the origin of the present-day Dentsu-in temple.]

Beside it was a clear spring.

[Side note: This is the present-day Gokuraku-sui ("Pure Land Water") at Sōkei-ji temple.]

Following the example of the holy founder's (Hōnen's) sacred sites, he named that water *Yoshimizu* ("Auspicious Water"). The master dwelt at Muryōzan for a mere six years. One evening he fell mildly ill. Calmly he bathed, put on clean robes, and wrote a death poem (*jisei no ge*), saying:

> *Hōkō hashū — mitsu hachijūnen — sokkon tanteki*
> *Chi fu shiki nichi — kagayaku Tōzan — tsuki Seiten ni*
>
> *(Freely going forth and returning — fully eighty years — right now, directly:*
> *Known or unknown, the sun — shines upon the eastern mountains — the moon over the western sky.)*

Having finished writing, he sat upright, pressed his palms together, chanted the sacred name (*Namu Amida Butsu*) with his lips, faced west, and passed away serenely. This was on the twenty-seventh day of the ninth month of the twenty-seventh year of Ōei (1420), the year of *kanoe-ne*.

[Side note: Worldly age: eighty; years since ordination (*rō*): seventy-three.]

Whenever the master sat in meditation, there was a radiance upon his face and crown, just like a half-moon; and when he faced a book, his shadow shone distinctly against it.

[Side note: For this reason, he was known in the world as the "Crescent Moon Abbot" (*Mikazuki Shōnin*).]

The books he compiled throughout his life were so numerous as to make an ox sweat (a classical Chinese expression meaning an enormous quantity of writings); his literary talent was resplendent, penetrating the most subtle points and reaching the highest profundity. The world unanimously held that the master possessed the "ten virtues" (*jittoku*). He also received transmission of Japanese court poetry (*waka*) from the priest Tonna, and composed ten volumes of commentary on the prefaces to the *Kokinshū* (the Imperial Anthology of Japanese Poetry).
