**Kōun-zan Jōren-ji Temple** — also known as Keigaku-in. Located more than three chō (approximately 330 meters) before the bridge in the same area, on the left side of the road. It belongs to the Jōdo (Pure Land) sect and is affiliated with Enzan (Zōjō-ji Temple). The principal image of Amida Nyorai (Amitābha Buddha) was crafted by the Buddhist sculptor Kasuga. The temple was founded by the Venerable Shinyo of the Eiren-sha, known by the priestly name Ryōken Muteki.

The temple was established during the Ōei era (1394–1428), and serves as the bodaiji (memorial temple) of Itabashi Shinano-no-Kami Tadayasu, the local lord of this land. During the residence of the fifth abbot, the Venerable Myōyo, known as Ryūtaku Oshō, in the Tenshō nineteenth year, Kanoto-U (1591), the temple was granted a vermilion seal of immunity from interference by the Tokugawa house. Furthermore, in the summer of Kanhō third year, Mizoto-I (1743), when the Taikiju (the Shōgun) came hunting in this area and rested at the temple, this became an established and permanent precedent.

**Aioi Sugi (Twin-Growth Cedar)** — *Located in the rear garden of the temple. Long ago, during a hunting excursion in this area, the lord inquired after the name of this cedar. The resident monk replied that it was called "Aioi" (Growing Together). For this reason, it is still called the Aioi Cedar — the Cedar of Matrimonial Ties.*

**Meo-no Matsu (Male-and-Female Pine)** — *Located in front of the main hall. It is said to have been planted with the intent of praying for peace throughout the land.*

**Tomb of Itabashi Tadayasu** — *Located within the same temple precinct. The face of the gravestone reads: "Honjuin-dono, former Shinshū Kūzan Yūken Zenjōmon, died on the twenty-first day of the eleventh month of Bunroku 2, Mizoto-Mi year (1593)." According to one account, the genealogy of the Itabashi clan records that Itabashi Shinano-no-Kami Tadayasu was originally of the Toshima clan, served Hōjō Ujinao, settled in this land, and took the name Itabashi. Another account states that the Toshima clan genealogy records that the younger brother of Takinogawa Nagato-no-Kami was named Itabashi Jirō Toyokiyo. It may be that Toyokiyo was the first to settle in this land and adopted Itabashi as his clan name. If so, Tadayasu would also be a descendant of Toyokiyo. *the Odawara-ki records that on the thirteenth day of the first month of Taiei 4 (1524), during the battle between Hōjō Ujitsuna and Uesugi Tomooki, Tomooki was defeated and retreated toward Itabashi, at which time a certain pair of brothers of the Itabashi clan and others died in battle — these too were likely members of the same clan. The same record also states that in the late tenth month of Tenshō 1 (1573), when Yanada Nakatsukasa-no-Taifu, lord of Sekiyado Castle in Shimōsa Province, conspired with the Satake clan in an act of treachery, Ujimasa deployed from Odawara and besieged Sekiyado. At that time, Chiba Jirō, lord of Ishihama Castle in Musashi Province on the Odawara side, fell in battle against Kikuma Zushō, a unit commander of the castle garrison. Since only daughters remained in the Chiba family of Ishihama, by order of Ujimasa, the third son of Hōjō Hitachi-no-suke Ujishige was adopted as son-in-law and married to the daughter, continuing the Chiba line. Administration thus passed to Kiuchi Kunai-shōyū. His yoriki (mounted retainers) included Itabashi Higo-no-Kami (lord of Itabashi Castle) and Matsudo Echizen-no-Kami (lord of Akatsuka Castle). This Higo-no-Kami was also likely a member of the same clan.*

**Kinoshita Inari Shrine** — Located at the far end of the same post station (Itabashi-juku), reached by turning left from the highway into a small alley, and enshrined within Chisei-ji, a temple of the Jōdo sect. It is said to be a divine image received through spiritual inspiration by the Venerable Hōyo of the Shinren-sha, known as Rinsō Oshō, who led the temple's revival in Genna 3, Hinoto-Mi year (1617). It is transmitted that when Toyotomi Hideyoshi was still known as Kinoshita Tōkichirō, he devoutly venerated this deity, and subsequently rose to become the supreme military commander of the realm — hence the shrine has come to be reverently called "Kinoshita Shusse Inari" (the Inari of Kinoshita's Rise to Fame). *It is also called Kyara Inari or Tōkichi Inari.*

**Shimizu-zaka (Clear Water Slope)** — Located in Shimura. It is also commonly known as "Jizō-zaka" (Jizō Slope). Its old name was "Okidono-zaka," so called because it was opened by a certain Oki-no-Kami (Governor of Oki). *(Details are found in the following entry on Kumano Shrine.)* The terrain here is steep and rugged, causing great hardship to travelers on the highway.
