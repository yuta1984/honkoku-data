Every year, on the eighth and twelfth days of the fourth month, the inner doors are opened and the public is invited to form a karmic bond (*kechien*) with the enshrined image.

The temple's founding legend (*engi*) states: In the thirteenth year of Tenpyō, the year of *Kanoto-mi* (741 CE), the Bodhisattva Gyōki — then forty-three years of age — set out to guide and convert the multitudes of the eastern provinces. He first undertook a retreat (*sanrō*) at the Kumano Gongen shrine in southern Kii Province. On his return journey, he came upon a great cryptomeria (*sugi*) tree by the roadside. Intending to use it as material for a Buddha image, he felled the tree and made a vow:

"If this endeavor accords with the will of the Buddha, then this wood shall arrive before me at the place with which it has a karmic bond."

With that, he cast the wood into the valley stream at that spot and then proceeded eastward. When he arrived at this very place, that sacred timber (*reiboku*) had already washed ashore in the inlet here.

[Side note: In ancient times, it is said that the entire area from around here to the vicinity of Takada, extending to the inside and outside of Kanda Bridge, was all tidal estuary — rivers and inlets fed by seawater.]

Revering the will of the Buddha, and in honor of the Compassionate Mother (*Jibo*), Gyōki turned to face the east, offered incense and flowers (*kōge*), performed obeisance, and devoted himself entirely to sincere faith. Thereupon, before his very eyes, Yakushi-jo appeared, radiating a golden light. Gyōki then used the base of the aforementioned cryptomeria — the *motoki* (root wood) — to carve this principal image (*honzon*) in likeness of that vision, and built a hall on this site in which to enshrine it. Furthermore, in order to save all sentient beings wandering through the six realms of transmigration (*rokudō*), he used the upper portion of the tree — the *sueki* (tip wood) — to carve six statues of Amida and distributed them to six separate locations.

[Side note: These are what are referred to as the "Six Amida of Edo" (*Edo Roku Amida*).]
