### Bell Inscription (Bonsho Inscription)

**[Original Inscription]**

In the domain of Musashi Province (武州), Sakinishi District, Iwatsuki Estate, at Kinpōzan Heirin Zen Temple: After the turmoil of war, there was no one to sound the morning gruel bell, and the temple's great bell (lit. "whale") fell silent. The principal lay patron of our mountain, a scion of the Kira clan of Hatō District, Mikawa Province (参州)—a man of upright character from the Genkōchi lineage of the Minamoto clan—Hōrin Sōmuisan Shōan Jussō (法林宗無為祥菴寿参) and Yūgaku Sōei Koji (雄嶽宗英居士), moved by filial devotion, commissioned the casting of this bell and donated it to Kinpōzan. His merit piles high as a mountain; his filial piety accumulates lofty as a peak. May the blessings spread, and may the glory of his family endure for tens of thousands of years. The celebratory inscription reads:

*The sense of hearing becomes pure and clear—one hundred and eight [sins] are lifted. When it roars, the earth resounds; when the mallet strikes, the heavens are moved. The morning light shines bright beyond the tower; the moon illuminates the temple gate. The patron's virtue shall not stand alone—may it reach the children and grandchildren for hundreds of billions of years.*

　　　Written on the thirteenth day of the ninth month, Genwa 9 (元和九年, 1623), year of Mizunoto-I (癸亥)
　　　　　　　　Resident monk, Unpō Sōi (雲峰宗怡)

**[Supplementary Inscription (Added at the Time of Recasting)]**

The temple was originally located to the west of Iwatsuki Castle in the same province (Musashi). At the beginning of the Kanbun era (c. 1661), it was relocated to its present site. Details of these events are fully recorded in the temple's own records. I came to oversee the affairs of this temple in the summer of Enpō Kinoe-Tora (延宝甲寅, 1674), and after four years, in the year Hinoe-Tatsu (丙辰, 1676), a small fire broke out outside the main hall, and the bell tower was soon destroyed by flames. The bell's sound when struck still carried, but its resonance was already damaged. Moreover, in the winter of Ten'na Mizunoe-Inu (天和壬戌, 1682), I finally commissioned a founder to recast the bell, with the dimensions and specifications entirely following the old design. As for the wording of the inscription, it was not lightly altered; rather, it is here respectfully re-engraved. It is my prayer that this act may align with the initial vow and aspiration, and that it may forever preserve the foundations of this temple's restoration and prosperity.
