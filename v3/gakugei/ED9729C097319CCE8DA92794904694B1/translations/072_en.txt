**Tōkōzan Yakuōji, known as Jishōin.** Located in the same area (Tokorozawa), reached by turning into the northern side lane and proceeding along the back road to the opposite side. It belongs to the Sōtō sect of Zen Buddhism and is affiliated with Eigenji temple in Kume village. [The founder of the temple's revival is known by the name of the Venerable Taishun of Kōzan.] The principal image of the Yakushidō (Medicine Buddha Hall) is a seated figure of Yakushi Nyorai (Bhaisajyaguru, the Medicine Buddha), approximately three *shaku* (roughly 90 cm) in height, said to have been carved by the great monk Gyōki. [The pedestal is crafted from gold wire and is said to be of extraordinary skill and refinement. However, as it is a *hibutsu* (secret Buddha), the shrine cabinet (*zushi*) is not readily opened.]

According to tradition, during the Genkō era, Nitta Musashi-no-kami Yoshimune engaged in numerous battles, but as the fortunes of his clan declined, he was routed in every engagement, and many of his retainers and followers perished. Consequently, Yoshimune came to this place, entered a small Yakushidō hall, temporarily took Buddhist vows, and spent his years in concealment. **Toda — The Sacred Spring of Haguro**

A sacred spring wells up from the hollow of a *muku* (Japanese hackberry) tree. People who come to worship draw the water and give it to the sick to drink, and as it is said to have miraculous efficacy, the place has become greatly popular and bustling of late. In the *Bencao Gangmu* (*Honzō Kōmoku*, the Chinese Materia Medica), this type of water is listed as *Bantianhe* (半天河, "mid-heaven river water"), with the explanatory name *Shangshuishui* (上池水, "upper pool water").
