**The Shichisha Gongen Shrine (Shrine of the Seven Gongen Deities)** *(Located approximately one hundred paces east of Shōraku-ji, on top of a hill. It is said to enshrine seven of the deities among the twenty-one shrines of the Sannō (Mountain King) pantheon. The shrine holds a stipulated domain. To the east of this temple there is a place called "Torii-ba" (Torii Ground), which is said to be the site where the first torii gate of this shrine once stood in ancient times. A festival is held every year on the nineteenth day of the ninth month.)*

**The Founder's Pagoda (Kaisan-tō)** *(The area behind the Shichisha Gongen is called the "Kaisan-tō" (Founder's Pagoda), but this is only a name, and no actual structure in the form of a pagoda survives. The title of the founder is also unknown, as all old records have been lost. It is perhaps the site where the memorial pagoda of the revered founder Sonkai Shōnin once stood.)*

In ancient times, this temple was a great monastic complex (*daigaran*) and served as the prayer site (*kigan-sho*) for successive generations of the Kamakura shogunal house. At that time, twelve *bō* (sub-temple lodgings) stood in magnificent array, but as times changed and the world transformed, now only their names remain, preserved entirely as place names (*aza*) in the surrounding fields. *(The reason Shōraku-ji is called "Daibō" (Main Temple) appears to be a designation relative to its sub-temples. Moreover, in a place to the southwest of the Daibō called "Dōchi-iri," ancient roof tiles are sometimes unearthed, providing the most conspicuous evidence of the ancient monastic structures. Also, several old stone monuments (*kohi*) from the eras of Bun'ei, Kagen, and Bunshō still exist.)*

*(Upon reflection: in the* Nihon Shoki *(Chronicles of Japan), it is recorded that in the first year of Emperor Bidatsu's reign, the year of *jinshi* (572 CE), in the summer, fourth month, an envoy from Koguryŏ (Koma) arrived and presented a memorial document. This document was written on a raven's feather. Numerous scribes (*fuhito*) were assembled and asked to read it, but even after three days, none were able to do so. There was, however, a man named Ō no Chinni (Wang Chen-er), ancestor of the Fune no Fuhito clan, who steamed the feather over rice steam, pressed polished silk cloth against it to transfer the characters, and was thereby able to read the text in full detail. The fact that this temple's mountain name (*sangō*) is called "Shinni-zan" is most fitting and meaningful. Now that the old records have been lost, we can no longer know its ancient origins, which is a matter of no small regret.)*

**The Former Residence of Niihori Genba** — The person said to have resided in the area of Yamaguchi-Niihori was a retainer of Ōta Dōkan. Originally living in Niihori in Yanaka, Edo, he moved to this location for a time due to certain circumstances, and with the

divine image of Inari (the fox deity) passed down in the Ōta household, he established a shrine through a *kanjō* (ritual invitation of the deity), which now serves as the guardian deity (*gohōshin*) of this temple. *(This is what is called the "Tennyo Inari" [Celestial Maiden Inari].)*  Genba is said to have later returned to live in Niihori in Toshima. It is for this reason that the name "Niihori" also exists in this area.

**The Former Site of Yamasumi Hikosaburō** — This refers to a small hill approximately three *chō* (about 330 meters) to the northeast (the *ushitora* direction) of the Shichisha Gongen Shrine. Local people say it is the site of the fortification (*shirō*) of one Yamasumi Hikosaburō. *(The area referred to is about three to four *chō* in circumference. The matter of Yamasumi Hikosaburō is to be investigated further.)*

In this area there are fourteen or fifteen old established households, among which some possess ancient documents belonging to the Futami family, as well as written orders (*chūmon-gaki*) for battle banners and curtains. *(One Giemon, who works as a blacksmith, is said to be a descendant of a man called Futami Shōken, and these items are kept in his household.)*

Furthermore, at the foot of this hill there is a small shrine to the Suwa deity, with a stone sword (*sekken*) as its sacred object (*shintai*). *(Its material is bluish in color and resembles copper or iron.)*

The ancient document reads as follows:

> It has been reported by reliable witnesses that on the occasion of the recent battle at Kohō-suji, you ran about actively in service, which is most admirable. Accordingly, one sword (*tachi hitokoshi*) is hereby presented to you. You are to continue to render ever greater service henceforward. This letter is as stated.
