The kōji chambers (*kōjimuro*) of Edo are pit-style chambers (*anamuro*), generally about one and a half *ken* wide and approximately ten *ken* long. The four walls are constructed in a manner resembling bamboo fencing, with straw packed into the earthen walls; the ceiling consists of logs laid horizontally, covered with straw mats (*mushiro*), and topped with soil. As for the entrance, it is as depicted in the earlier illustration. Now, in an earthquake such as this one — where even the flat surface of the streets cracked and split from the shaking — how could something dug so deeply into the ground possibly remain undamaged? Thus, among those that were shaken and collapsed, we record here a few of the most severe cases:

○ In Hongō Shinmachi, nine houses collapsed; among them, Mikawaya Hikobei and Chōshichi fell into the interior of their kōji chambers. At Yokonezaka in the same area, seven houses collapsed. ○ In Harukichō 2-chōme of the same area —

— four houses. ○ 3-chōme: one house. 5-chōme: one house. 6-chōme: two houses. Maruyama and Kikuzaka: four houses, among them Iseya Jirobei, whose entire dwelling fell in. Motomachi in the same area: five houses. ○ In front of the Yushima Tenjin gate: four houses. ○ Uekichō in the same area: one house. Mikumichō: three houses. ○ 6-chōme in the same area: two houses (rear tenements). Kasugaichō: one house. ○ Daruma Yokochō in the same area: two houses. ○ Within the precincts of Kanda Myōjin Shrine: two houses.

The above cases were investigated in each respective neighborhood, and the names of those involved are fully known; however, as it is not an auspicious matter, we have deliberately omitted them here.
