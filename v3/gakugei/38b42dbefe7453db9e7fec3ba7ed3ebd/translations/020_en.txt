△ Near Yarai-shita in Ushigome, there lived a man called Tōji, who was possessed by a fox spirit. At around the Hour of the Horse (noon) on the second day of the tenth month, he announced to the people of the neighborhood: "Tonight, there will certainly be a natural disaster. Everyone, take care and flee from danger. I myself shall withdraw to a place of safety." He then shook off and knocked down those who tried to stop him and ask what he meant, broke into a run, and disappeared entirely from view. On account of this warning, some prudent people began gathering up their belongings and making various preparations; but seeing them do so, others laughed and mocked them greatly, saying: "To believe the ravings of a fox-possessed man and start making a commotion now — how utterly ridiculous!" But that very night, the earthquake struck, and there were many who, understanding the words of Tōji for the first time, bitterly regretted their scorn. 

After the earthquake had subsided, as long as the fox-possessed man had not returned to his home, people thought that further aftershocks must be coming; and even those who had only heard the story secondhand felt no peace of mind. Moreover, since fires had broken out in various places, people pulled their belongings and household goods from collapsed or tilted buildings, used sliding doors and paper screens as makeshift walls, and fashioned roofs from wooden shutters, barely managing to keep out the cold. People did not lay their heads on pillows at all, passing the time in a state of half-wakefulness and half-dream, when at last the fox-possessed man returned. The people thought: "Well then, there must be no more danger now," and asked the fox-possessed Tōji: "Where on earth did you run off to?" He merely smiled and said something to the effect that "it is not a matter for ordinary mortals to know." Everyone then felt reassured, patched up their homes, and resumed their livelihoods as before. And it is said that the fox, too, departed from him, and he returned to his former self.
