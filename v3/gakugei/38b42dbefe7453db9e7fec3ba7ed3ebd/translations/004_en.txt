## [Right Folio]

In the five districts of Shin-Yoshiwara, a great many houses collapsed, and in some places fires broke out simultaneously. Not only courtesans but also many visitors lost their lives. Among those present were the blind masseurs who came to the quarter every night — and although their numbers were considerable, it is said that only two of them perished. Remarkable indeed that they, being blind, managed to escape.

Among the brothels, Okamoto-rō in Kyōmachi Icchōme, Matsubaya in Kyōmachi Nichōme, Wakasaya in Kadochō, and Okada and Iseja in Edomachi Nichōme —

## [Left Folio]

— as well as Miuraya and Yoshi▢emon, suffered especially catastrophic destruction, and more than half of the courtesans in their employ burned to death. At Miuraya in particular, the proprietors selected courtesans who were able to walk and sheltered them in the underground storage cellar, intending to save them — but fire entered the cellar, and all of them burned to death, it is said.

An official investigation into the deaths by fire within the quarter put the toll at over 630 people. Only one earthen storehouse survived — having collapsed in the earthquake and then escaped the flames in its ruined state. Of the houses themselves, only two or three remained in the lower part of Kyōmachi Icchōme, and the houses on the western side of the Gojikken-dori road outside the Great Gate also survived. Everything else burned away without a trace — a sight that was truly pitiful to behold.

People said that there could be no survivors from such a catastrophe, yet one after another, they all managed to escape, and temporary shops were soon opened, each doing a lively trade. Looking over these establishments, however, the two houses of Sugata Ebiya and Matsubaya were nowhere to be seen.
