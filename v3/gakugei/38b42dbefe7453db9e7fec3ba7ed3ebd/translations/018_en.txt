*Painting by Ittōsai Yoshitsuna*

○ In Hongō Shinmachi there lived a man by the name of Mikawaya Hikobei, who ran a prosperous miso business. On the night of the second day [of the tenth month], while all members of the household were asleep, his wife Toyo went down into the garden to let the young boy of the house relieve himself. Just at that moment the earthquake struck. Crying out "Anaya!" in shock, she attempted to return to where she had been standing, but the ground shook so violently that the cover of the kōji chamber burst open, and she was swallowed into the pit. Toyo fell in with a single scream, still clutching her infant child. Her husband Hikobei and even the servants were thrown into a panic and tried desperately to rescue her, but as they did so, the earth on all sides came crashing down, the footings of the pillars gave way, the great house collapsed, and all manner of objects fell in upon one another — and so, to their great sorrow,

— there was no means of saving her, and they could only stand by helplessly. As the house lay in ruin all around them, fires broke out on every side, throwing everything into even greater chaos, and their own lives were in grave peril. By the time they had managed to get through the night, dawn had broken and the fires had subsided — yet aftershocks continued without pause. Unable to leave the matter as it was, they cleared away the scattered debris and dug into the earth, where they found Toyo still clutching the child's feet, both mother and child having changed color and breathed their last. Hikobei's grief goes without saying, but even the unfeeling servants were choked with tears of sorrow, and in the end they held a funeral for the deceased. Though it was a natural disaster, it is truly a matter for profound lamentation. From such an account, one may grasp the severity of the chaos that prevailed. As for the place where the kōji chamber collapsed, logs have been laid across it as a bridge, and people now pass over it in their daily comings and goings.
