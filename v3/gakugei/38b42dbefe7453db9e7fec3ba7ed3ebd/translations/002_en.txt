## Ansei Kemmonshi, Volume II (Middle)

Records from various locations, from the northeast to the west

**[Entry 20]** From the area of Nakanomachi in the Yoshiwara pleasure quarter, extending to Fushimichō, Edochō (both blocks), the twelve establishments of Ageyamachi to the south, Nishikagashi and Kadochō to the north, the Mikazuki tenements and seven other longhouse complexes to the south, Kyōmachi (both blocks), and the area commonly known as Shinmachi — the entire pleasure quarter was consumed by fire. Although five storehouses (dozō) survived despite severe damage, the entire area was left looking like an open field.

△ My friend Murayama Genbei was a publisher (hanmoto) for the Nakamura-za theater, and as his house in Setomonomachi had burned down in a fire the previous year, he was now living in Hashiba. Having finished some business in Icchōme and returned home, he sat down beside the fire brazier intending to have some tea, when — strangely — the paper lantern (andō) flew up nearly two feet into the air, and before he could even wonder what was happening, he was slammed to the ground by the collapsing building. He waited quietly for a moment, and catching sight of the front shōji screen through the darkness, he managed to pull his mother and wife to safety through that opening. He then carried out the tatami mats, doors, and shōji screens to prepare against the cold. His mother then said, "How strange — all of a sudden a great river has appeared right in front of us." When Genbei looked around, he too saw what appeared to be a great river before his very eyes. In fact, the row of houses along the eastern side had all collapsed and fallen, forming what looked like an embankment, so that the view was now unobstructed and the Sumida River appeared much closer than before. It was only then that mother and son finally understood, for the first time, just how tremendous the earthquake had truly been.
