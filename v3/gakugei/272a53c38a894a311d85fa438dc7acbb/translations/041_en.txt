**Episode 101**

Long ago, there was a man called Ariwara no Yukihira, who held the rank of *Sahyōe no Kami* (Captain of the Left Imperial Guard). Hearing that there was fine wine at this man's house, he had invited as his guest of honor a man called Fujiwara no Masachika, who served at court as *Sachūben* (Middle Controller of the Left). On that day, Yukihira had prepared hospitality as host. Being a man of refined taste, he had arranged flowers in a jar. Among the flowers was a remarkable wisteria bloom, whose trailing branch measured some three *shaku* and six *sun* in length. Taking this as the subject, they composed poems. Before the composing was finished, a sibling of the host, having heard that the host was entertaining, came and joined the party. Yukihira detained him and asked him to compose a poem as well. Since this person knew nothing of poetry from the start, he declined, but being pressed to compose regardless, he produced this:

　*Saku hana no / shita ni kakururu / hito ōmi* / *arishi ni masaru / fuji no kage kamo*

(So many are the people who take shelter beneath the blossoming flowers — the shade of the wisteria surpasses even what it once was in former times.)

When it was said that he had composed this poem, (people remarked that) he had composed it with the thought that the great Minister (*Ōki Ōtodo*) was at the height of his glory and prosperity, and that the fortunes of the Fujiwara clan (*Fuji-uji*) were flourishing all the more — so it was said.

**Episode 102**

Long ago, there was a man. He did not compose poems, yet he understood the ways of the world well. A highborn woman had taken Buddhist vows and, weary of the world, had come to live in a remote mountain village far from the capital. Since they had been acquainted from before, he composed and sent her this poem:

　*Somuku tote / kumo ni wa noranu / mono naredo* / *yo no uki koto zo / tōku narite fu*

(Though one who turns away from the world does not ride upon the clouds and vanish, still, the painful things of this world are said to have become distant to you.)

So he sent word to her. This woman was a princess (*Miya*) of the Saikū (*Saigū*, the Shrine Princess institution).

**Episode 103**

Long ago, there was a man. He was deeply sincere and tender-hearted, with not a trace of fickleness. He served the Emperor of Fukakusa (*Fukakusa no Mikado*). Whether he had erred in his judgment, he became intimate with a woman who served the Imperial princes. And he composed:

　*Nenu ru yo no / yume o hakanami / madoromeba* / *iya hakanani mo / nari masaru kana*

(Finding the dream I saw on this sleepless night to be fleeting, when I drift off into a light slumber, it becomes all the more fleeting still.)

So he composed and sent it. "What a rough and graceless poem that is!"
