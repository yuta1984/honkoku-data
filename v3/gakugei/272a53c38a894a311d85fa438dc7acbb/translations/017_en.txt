【Section 34】

Long ago, a man composed for a woman who had been cold and indifferent toward him:

*Ieba e ni / iwaneba mune ni / sawagare te / kokoro hitotsu ni / nageku koro kana*
(If I speak my feelings, they become grief; if I stay silent, they churn within my breast — either way, my heart alone is left to grieve in these days.)

He must have been rather forward in speaking his feelings aloud.

【Section 35】

Long ago, a man composed for someone with whom he had — against his own wishes — lost contact:

*Tama no o wo / awa wo ni yorite / musubere ba / taete nochi mo / awan to zo omou*
(Because I twisted and tied the jeweled thread as frail as foam, even after it has broken, I still think we shall meet again.)

【Section 36】

Long ago, a woman kept asking a man whether he had forgotten her. He composed for her:

*Tani sebami / mine made wa heru / tama kazura / taen to hito ni / waga omowa naku ni*
(Like the jeweled vine (kudzu) that, finding the valley too narrow, stretches all the way to the peak — there is no way I could be thinking of cutting these feelings off from you.)

【Section 37】

Long ago, a man met a woman who was given to amorous affairs. Perhaps he felt uneasy about her, and composed:

*Ware narade / shitai mo toku na / asagao no / yūkage matanu / hana wa ari tomo*
(Let no one but me undo your sash — even if there be flowers like the morning glory that do not wait for the evening shade before they wilt and fade.)

Her reply:

*Futari shite / musubi shi himo wo / hitori shite / ai miru made wa / tokaji to zo omou*
(The cord that we two tied together — alone, I intend not to untie it until we meet again.)

【Section 38】

Long ago, a man went to visit Ki no Aritsune, but Aritsune had been out and came back late. The man composed and sent him:

*Kimi ni yori / omoi narai nu / yo no naka no / hito wa kore wo ya / koi to iu ran*
(Through waiting for you, I have grown accustomed to this longing — is this what people in the world call "love"?)

Aritsune's reply:

*Narawane ba / yo no hito goto ni / nani wo ka mo / koi to wa iu to / toishi ware shi mo*
(Because I was unaccustomed to it, I too once asked everyone in the world: what is it that people call "love"? — and now I know.)
