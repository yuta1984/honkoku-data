*Yuku mizu to / suguru yowai to / chiru hana to / izure matete fu / koto wo kikiran*
(Flowing water, the passing years, scattering blossoms — of which of these could one ever hear the word "wait"? None will wait.)

"Adakurabe" (a vain contest, a fleeting rivalry) — this must be the story of a man and woman who, while secretly harboring feelings for each other, carried on their clandestine visits.

---

**Section 51**
Long ago, a man planted chrysanthemums in someone's garden, and composed:

*Ueshi uwe wa / aki naki toki ya / sakazaran / hana koso chirame / ne sae kareme ya*
(Will the chrysanthemum I planted not bloom in a season without autumn? The flowers may scatter, but surely even the roots will not wither away.)

---

**Section 52**
Long ago, there was a man. From someone, he received a decorated *chimaki* (rice dumpling for the Boys' Festival). In reply, he composed:

*Ayame kari / kimi wa numa ni zo / madoikeru / ware wa no ni idete / karu zo wabishi ki*
(Cutting sweet flags — you have lost yourself in the marsh. As for me, I go out to the fields to cut them, and yet how forlorn I am.)

And with this, he also sent a pheasant.

---

**Section 53**
Long ago, a man managed to meet a woman who had been difficult to see, and while they were talking together, a cockerel crowed. He composed:

*Ikade ka wa / tori no nakuran / hito shirezu / omou kokoro wa / mada yofukaki ni*
(Why ever does the cock crow? In my heart that secretly longs for you, the night is still deep.)

---

**Section 54**
Long ago, a man sent this poem to a woman who had been cold toward him:

*Yukiyaranu / yumej i wo tadoru / tamot o ni wa / amatsusora naru / tsuyu ya okularan*
(On the sleeves of one who gropes along the dream path that cannot reach you — does the dew fall from the heavens above? [So wet are they with tears.])

---

**Section 55**
Long ago, a man composed this poem after the woman he had set his heart upon became impossible to obtain:

*Omowazu wa / ari mo suramed o / koto no ha no / orifushi goto ni / fushigoto ni tanomaruru kana*
(If you did not think of me at all, perhaps that would be that — yet at every turn of phrase, at every moment of your words, I find myself given cause for hope.)
