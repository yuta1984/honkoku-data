*Flowing water, the years that pass, and scattering blossoms—which of these, I wonder, has one heard can be "waited upon" (matsu / pine tree)?*

(This section concerns) a mutual rivalry in fickleness (adakurabe)—it must be the tale of a man and woman who had been meeting in secret.

---

**"Section 51"**

Long ago, a man planted chrysanthemums in someone's garden, and composed:

*Could it be that what I planted will not bloom when there is no autumn to come? The flowers may scatter, but surely even the roots will not wither away.*

---

**"Section 52"**

Long ago, there was a man. Someone sent him a decorative chimaki (a festive rice dumpling). In reply,

*You who cut the sweet flag (ayame) seem to have lost your way in the marsh. As for me, I go out to the fields to cut it—and that is a lonely, wretched thing.*

Saying this, he sent back a pheasant chick.

---

**"Section 53"**

Long ago, a man met with a woman who was difficult to see, and while they were conversing, a cock crowed, so he composed:

*How can the bird be crowing already? The heart that secretly longs for you still feels the deep of night.*

---

**"Section 54"**

Long ago, a man sent this poem to a woman who had been cold and indifferent to him:

*On the sleeve that gropes along the dream-path I cannot complete—does the dew that hangs in the far heavens come to rest there?*
(My sleeve is wet with tears shed while wandering a dream I cannot reach.)

---

**"Section 55"**

Long ago, a man composed this after the woman he had set his heart upon had become impossible to meet:

*I might be able to cease thinking of you—and yet, at every turn of phrase and season, I find myself made to place my hope in you once more.*
