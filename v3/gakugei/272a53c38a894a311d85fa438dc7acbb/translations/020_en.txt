(Section 43, continued)

The time was the fifth month. The man sent a reply poem:

*Iori ōki / shide no taosa wa / nao tanomu / waga sumu sato ni / koe taezu ba*
(O hototogisu, they say you have many lodgings — yet I still ask it of you: as long as your voice never ceases in the village where I dwell, please do not forget me.)

【Section 44】

Long ago, a man was going off to a distant province, and a friend decided to hold a farewell banquet (umanoshanamuke) for him. As they were not strangers to one another, the host had his house servants bring out a cup of sake, and he also intended to attach a woman's robe (sōzoku) as a farewell gift. The host composed a poem and had it tied onto the robe when it was passed over:

*Idete yuku / kimi ga tame ni to / nugitsureba / waga sae mo naku / narinubeki kana*
(When I remove this robe as a gift for you as you set out, it seems as though even I myself might cease to exist — so much do I give of myself to you.)

This poem was so moving and beautiful within its occasion that one should not rush to recite it aloud, but rather savor it deeply in one's heart (= it is a poem to be appreciated in quiet contemplation).

【Section 45】

Long ago, there was a man. A woman — the carefully sheltered daughter of a certain person — had always wished she could somehow speak to this man. Perhaps it was too difficult for her to reveal her feelings, for she fell into a sickness of longing and was on the verge of death. At that moment she said, "This is what I have been feeling all along," and her parents, overhearing this, tearfully informed the man. He rushed to her in a panic, but she had already died. He shut himself away in a daze. The time was the last day of the sixth month (Minazuki), in the sweltering heat; in the evening he had been entertaining himself with music and play, and when the night grew late, a somewhat cool breeze began to blow. Fireflies rose high into the air, glimmering brightly. The man lay on his side watching them, and (recited):

《From the Gosen'shū》

*Yuku hotaru / kumo no ue made / inubeku wa / aki kaze fuku to / kari ni tsuge kose*
(O departing firefly — if you can fly all the way to the top of the clouds (to the heavens above), please tell her — even as a pretense — that the autumn wind is blowing.)

*Kure gataki / natsu no higurashi / nagamureba / sono koto to naku / mono zo kanashiki*
(As I gaze upon the long summer day that refuses to end, I feel an ineffable sadness with no particular cause.)

【Left Page】

【Section 46】

Long ago, a man had a very dear friend. They were inseparable and cherished each other greatly, but when the friend went off to a distant province, the man felt deep sorrow as they parted. After months and years had passed, the friend sent a letter saying: "It is truly distressing that I have been unable to meet you, and so much time has passed. Have you perhaps forgotten me?" The friend had written in great anguish: "The hearts of people in this world sometimes drift apart, so I suppose one is bound to be forgotten." In response, the man composed and sent:

*Me garuto mo / omohoe naku ni / wasurururu / toki shi nakereba / omokage ni tatsu*
(It does not even feel as though you have grown distant from me — because there is no time when I forget you, your face always rises before me.)

【Section 47】

Long ago, there was a man who earnestly longed to draw close to a certain woman. However, having heard that this man was a philanderer, the woman grew increasingly cold toward him. She said:

《From the Kokinshū》

*Ōnusa no / hikite amata ni / narinureba / omoedo e koso / tanomazarikere*
(Like the great paper-streamer offerings (ōnusa) that are touched by many hands, you are sought after by so many — and so, though I think of you, I simply cannot bring myself to put my trust in you.)

《Reply, from the man (from the Kokinshū)》:

*Ōnusa to / na ni koso tatere / nagare temo / tsui ni yoru se wa / aru to iu mono wo*
(Yes, I may have the name of an ōnusa, drifting at the will of many — but even a drifting thing finds its final resting place at the riverbank, they say.)

【Section 48】

Long ago, there was a man. He was waiting for someone in order to hold a farewell banquet (umanoshanamuke), but the person never came. So he composed:

《From the Kokinshū》

*Ima zo shiru / kurushiki mono to / hito matan / sato wo ba karete / towubeki arikeru*
(Now I understand — waiting for someone is a painful thing. I should not have left the village (where you were) but stayed to visit you there instead.)
