(Section 24, continued)

"Please open this door," he knocked, but she would not meet him, and instead sent out a poem:

*Ara-tama no / toshi no mi-tose wo / machi wabite / tada koyoi koso / nii-makura sure*
(Through three long jewel-like years I have waited and grown weary of waiting for you — and it is only tonight that I lay my head upon a new pillow, with another.)

When she sent this out, he replied:

*Azusa-yumi / mayumi tsuki-yumi / toshi wo hete / waga seshi ga goto / uruwashimi seyo*
(Azusa-bow, mayumi-bow, tsuki-bow — as I have done through all these long years, love and cherish him well.)

And with that he made to leave. Then the woman called out:

*Azusa-yumi / hikedo hikanedo / mukashi yori / kokoro wa kimi ni / yori ni shi mono wo*
(Whether the azusa-bow is drawn or not drawn — from long ago, my heart has always leaned toward you, and you alone.)

Hearing this, the man turned back. The woman, overcome with grief, stood up and ran after him, but could not catch up, and collapsed where there was a spring of clear water. On a rock there, she wrote with the blood of her fingertip:

*Ai omowade / karenu ru hito wo / todome kane / wa ga mi wa ima zo / kie hate nu meru*
(Unable to detain one who left without a thought for me — my own body now seems to fade away entirely.)

Having written this, she perished there.
