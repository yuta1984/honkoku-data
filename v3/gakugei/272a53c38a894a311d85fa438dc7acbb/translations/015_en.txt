(Section 24, continued)

The man knocked, asking her to open the door, but she would not open it, and instead sent out a poem:

*Aratama no / toshi no mitose wo / machiwabite / tada koyoi koso / nemakura sure*
(Three long years — a span of precious time — I have waited, weary with longing; and it is precisely tonight that I am at last laying down my pillow (beside another).)

To this, the man replied:

*Azusa-yumi / mayumi tsuki-yumi / toshi wo hete / waga seshi ga goto / uruwashimi seyo*
(Like the azusa bow, the mayu bow, the tsuki bow — so too have the years passed by; love and cherish (this new man) just as tenderly as I once cherished you.)

And as he made to leave, the woman called out:

*Azusa-yumi / hikedo hikanedo / mukashi yori / kokoro wa kimi ni / yori ni shi mono wo*
(Whether the azusa bow is drawn or not — from long ago, my heart has always leaned toward you, and you alone.)

So she said, but the man went home all the same. The woman was overcome with grief; she rose and ran after him, but could not catch up, and collapsed at a place where clear water flowed. On a rock that lay there, she scratched out with her fingernail:

*Ai omowade / karenuru hito wo / todome kane / waga mi wa ima zo / kie hatenu meru*
(Unable to hold back the one who left without caring for me in return — my very self seems now on the verge of vanishing altogether.)

Having written this, she faded away to nothing at that very spot.

【Section 25】

Long ago, there was a man. He sent a poem to a woman who, though one might hesitate to call her easy — for she was not one to be trifled with — had not refused him outright either:

*Aki no no ni / sasawakeshi asa no / sode yori mo / awade nuru yo zo / hiji masari keru*
(More than the sleeves wet with dew when I parted the grasses in the autumn fields at dawn — the sleeves I have soaked (with tears) on the nights I spend without seeing you are far more drenched.)
