【Right folio】

(Episode 75 — continued)

(The woman composed:)

　*Iwama yori / ouru mirume shi / tsurenaku wa / shihohi shiomichi / kai mo aranamu*

(If the "mirume" seaweed growing between the rocks is to be so heartless, then there would be no use in the tide going out and coming in at all.)
*Note: "mirume" is a pivot word for both "mirume (seaweed)" and "miru me (the chance to meet and see)"; "shihohi shiomichi" refers to the ebb and flow of the tide.*

(And the man composed again:)

　*Namida ni zo / nuretsutu shioru / yo no hito no / tsuraki kokoro wa / sode no shizuku ka*

(Is it the tears that wet and wilt me — or is it the heartlessness of you, a person of this world, dripping from my sleeve like drops of dew?)

This was a woman with whom meeting in this world was exceedingly difficult.

**Episode 76**

Long ago, when the Nijō Empress was still called the御息所 (Miyasundokoro) of the Crown Prince, she went to worship at the Uji Shrine. An elderly man who served in the Palace Guards, receiving his reward in turn along with the other attendants — a gift given from the imperial carriage — composed a poem and offered it up (to her):

　*Ōhara ya / Oshio no yama mo / kyō koso wa / kamiyo no koto mo / omoiizurare*

(Even Mount Oshio of Ōhara — today of all days, must be calling to mind the age of the gods.)

Whether she found it moving in her heart, or what she thought of it, is not known.

**Episode 77**

Long ago, there was an Emperor known as the Tamura Emperor (Emperor Montoku). The Imperial Consort (nyōgo) of that time, known as Takakiko, was present at court. When she passed away, a memorial service was held at Anshōji temple, and people offered up dedicatory gifts. The offerings collected amounted to some thousand in number. These many offerings were attached to branches of trees and set before the pagoda, so that the whole scene looked as though the mountain itself had moved and appeared before the pagoda. At that time, the man who was then serving as Right Great General (Udaishō) — Fujiwara no Tsuneyuki — was presiding; and as he distributed the branches of incense, he summoned together those who could compose poetry and asked them to offer up poems on the theme of that day's memorial service, with a feeling of spring in the verse. The elderly man who had been serving as the Right Master of the Horse (Muma no Kami), though his sight had dimmed with age, composed:

　*Yama no mina / utsurite kyō ni / au koto wa / haru no wakare wo / tou to naru beshi*

(That all the mountain's colours are reflected here, and that we meet today — this must be to pay our respects to the parting of spring.)

Looking back on it now, it was perhaps not so remarkable a poem — but at the time, people wondered if anything could surpass it, and were deeply moved.

**Episode 78**

Long ago, there was an Imperial Consort known as Takakiko. When she passed away, the forty-ninth day memorial service was held at Anshōji temple. The man then serving as Right Great General, Fujiwara no Tsuneyuki, attended the service. On his return from that service, he called upon the residence of the Prince who had formerly been the Yamashina no Zenji (Governor of Yamashina). At this prince's estate at Yamashina, a waterfall had been set cascading and streams made to flow, and the grounds had been made most beautifully. Arriving there, (Tsuneyuki) said: "I have served you from afar, but have not yet served you from close by. Tonight I shall attend upon you here." The Prince was delighted, and had preparations made for the night's sleeping-chamber.
