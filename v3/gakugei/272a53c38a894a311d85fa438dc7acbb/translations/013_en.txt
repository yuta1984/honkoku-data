(Section 21, continued)

(The woman's poem)

*Ima wa tote / wasururu kusa no / tane wo dani / hito no kokoro ni / makase zu mo ga na*
(Now that it has come to this — even the seeds of the "forgetting grass" you would plant to erase me from your mind, I wish you would not scatter them at your heart's will.)

(Reply)

*Wasuregusa / ubu to dani kiku / mono naraba / omoikeri to wa / shiri mo shi namashi*
(If only I could hear even that you had planted the forgetting grass — then at least I would know that you had thought of me.)

Once more they exchanged poems as though things were as they had been before, and the man composed:

*Wasuraran to / omou kokoro no / utagai ni / arishi yori ge ni / mono zo kanashiki*
(In my anxiety that you might forget me, I find things even sadder now than they were when we were together.)

(Reply)

*Nakazora ni / tachi iru kumo no / ato mo naku / mi no hakanaku mo / nari ni keru kana*
(Like a cloud drifting through the empty sky, leaving no trace — how fleeting and insubstantial my very being has become.)

Though they exchanged these words, since each had gone their separate way, they grew estranged from one another.

【Section 22】

Long ago, though a relationship had ended all too briefly, the woman must still have been unable to forget, for she sent word from her home:

*Uki nagara / hito wo ba e shi mo / wasurene ba / katsu uramitsutsu / naho zo koishiki*
(Though I find it painful, I cannot bring myself to forget you — and so, even as I resent you, I find I love you still.)

To this the man replied, saying "just so," and composed:

*Ai mite wa / kokoro hitotsu wo / Kawashima no / mizu no nagarete / taеji to zo omou*
(When I see you face to face, my heart is singular as ever — like the waters of the river at Kawashima flowing on, I believe this feeling will never cease.)

Yet that very night he departed. They spoke of things past and things to come, and then:

*Aki no yo no / chiyo wo hitoyo ni / nazurae te / yachiyo shi nineba / ya aku toki no aran*
(If we were to liken a thousand years of autumn nights to a single night, and were to spend eight thousand such nights together — could there ever come a time when we grew weary of one another? Surely not.)

(Reply)

*Aki no yo no / chiyo wo hitoyo ni / naseri tomo / kotoba nokori te / tori ya naku nan*
(Even if a thousand years of autumn nights were made into one, our words would still remain unspoken — and the dawn bird would crow before we finished.)
