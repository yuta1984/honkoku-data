【Right folio】

(Episode 69 — continued)

Hearing that a hunting messenger had arrived, (the provincial governor) kept him drinking wine all through the night, so that the man was completely unable to meet with the lady. As dawn broke he was about to depart for Owari Province, and the man wept tears of blood in secret, yet still could not see her. Just as the night was on the verge of turning to dawn, from the lady's side, a poem was written on a sake-cup saucer and sent out to him. He took it and saw:

　*Kachihito no / wataredo nurenu / enishi areba*
　(A traveller on foot crosses and is not wetted — if there is such a bond between us…)

It was written, but the lower verse (shimo no ku) was absent. On that same sake-cup saucer, using charcoal from a pine, the man wrote the completing lower verse:

　*mata Ausaka no / seki wa koenan*
　(…then we shall cross the Barrier of Ausaka once more.)

When dawn broke, he crossed over into Owari Province. The Saigū (Vestal Princess) was, in the reign of Emperor Seiwa (Mizunoo), the daughter of Emperor Montoku and the younger sister of Prince Koretaka — (Princess Teishi).

**Episode 70**

Long ago, a man, returning from his duties as a hunting messenger, took lodging near Oyodo, and called out to the young attendants of the Saigū:

　*Mirume karu / kata ya izuko zo / sao sashite / ware ni oshieyo / ama no tsuribune*

(Where is the place where one cuts the seaweed? Punt your pole and show me the way, O fisherman's boat.)
*Note: "mirume" is a pivot word (kakekotoba) meaning both "seaweed (mirume)" and "a chance to meet and see (miru me)."*

**Episode 71**

Long ago, a man came to the Ise Saigū as an imperial messenger, and spoke words of amorous intent to the palace there. A lady of the household composed privately:

　*Chihayafuru / kami no igaki mo / koenu beshi / ōmiyabito no / mimaku hoshisa ni*

(Even the sacred fence of the mighty gods I feel I could leap over, so great is my longing to see you, O noble one from the Great Palace.)

The man replied:

　*Koishiku ba / kite mo miyo kashi / chihayafuru / kami no isamuru / michi naranaku ni*

(If you long for me, come and see for yourself — this is not a path that the gods forbid.)

**Episode 72**

Long ago, a man had a woman in Ise Province whom he had not yet been able to meet, and when he said he was going to the neighbouring province, she was very resentful. (She composed:)

　*Ōyodo no / matsu wa tsuraku mo / aranaku ni / uramite nomi mo / kaeru nami kana*

(The pine of Ōyodo is not heartless — yet here are the waves, returning full of resentment alone.)
*Note: "matsu (pine)" is a pivot word for "matsu (to wait)."*

---

**Episode 73**

Long ago, though a man knew a certain woman was somewhere nearby, he could not even send a message to her vicinity, and so he composed in longing:

　*Me ni wa mite / te ni wa torarenu / tsuki no uchi no / katsura no gotoki / kimi ni zo arikeru*

(You are like the laurel tree inside the moon — seen by the eye but impossible to reach by hand.)

---

**Episode 74**

Long ago, a man, deeply resentful of a woman, composed:

　*Iwane fumi / kasanaru yama ni / aranedomo / awanu hi ōku / koi wataru kana*

(Though ours is no mountain of piled-up boulders, the days we do not meet are many, and still I go on longing for you.)

---

**Episode 75**

Long ago, a man said he would take a woman to Ise Province to meet her. The woman composed:

　*Ōyodo no / hama ni ou to iu / miru kara ni / kokoro wa naginu / katarawa nedo mo*

(Like the "miru" seaweed said to grow on the shore of Ōyodo — the moment I saw you, my heart grew calm, even though we have not yet spoken.)
*Note: "miru" is a pivot word for both "miru (the seaweed)" and "miru (to see/meet)."*

Having said this, she was even more aloof and cold, so the man composed:

　*Sode nurete / ama no karihosu / watatsu umi no / miru wo au ni te / yaman to ya suru*

(Must I end with only "seeing" — like the sea-weed the fisher-folk cut and dry, sleeves wet from the sea — is this mere glimpse to be all there is of our meeting?)
