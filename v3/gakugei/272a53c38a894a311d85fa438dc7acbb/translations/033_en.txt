(Episode 78 — continued)

(…should I simply remain as I am — no, that would never do.) When the Emperor of Sanjō made his imperial progress, (Tsuneyuki) had presented a most magnificent stone from the beach at Chisato in the province of Ki (Kii). This had been presented after the imperial progress had taken place, and so it had been placed in the drain before the chambers of a certain person; but (Tsuneyuki) said, "I shall present this to the Prince known as Shimako no Miko," and sent stone-handlers and attendants (tonerī) to fetch it. Before long, it was brought. This stone was even more splendid to behold than it had been described. Thinking that to present it just as it was would be too plain and unremarkable, he had the assembled people compose poems. The man who had been serving as Right Master of the Horse (Muma no Kami) shaved green moss (to decorate the stone) and, on a panel of lacquerwork (maki-e), attached this poem and presented it:

　*Akaneto mo / iwa ni zo kawaru / iro mie nu / kokoro wo misen / yoshi no nakereba*

(Though it be tinged with madder-red, it is the rock that changes colour and is seen — having no means by which to show you my invisible heart, I offer this instead.)

So it is said the poem ran.

**Episode 79**

Long ago, a prince was born at Uji. At the lying-in chamber (ubuya), people composed poems. An elderly man who was of the maternal grandfather's line composed:

　*Waga kado ni / chihiro aru kage wo / uetsureba / natsu fuyu tare ka / kakurezaru beki*

(Since I have planted at my gate a tree whose shade extends a thousand fathoms deep, who in summer or in winter could fail to shelter in its shade?)
