**Episode 84**

Long ago, there was a man. Though he was of humble station himself, his mother was of royal birth (a princess). His mother lived in a place called Nagaoka. The son served at court in the capital, and although he wished to go and visit her, he was often unable to do so frequently. As he was her only child, she loved him with the deepest tenderness.

Then, around the time of the Twelfth Month (Shiwasu), an urgent message arrived in the form of a letter. When he opened it and looked, there was a poem (from the *Kokinshū*):

　*Oinureba / saranu wakare no / aru to ieba / iyoiyo mimaku / hoshiki kimi kana*

(Now that I have grown old, they say there is a parting that none can escape — and so I find myself longing more and more to see you, my dear child.)

The son wept bitterly and composed in reply (from the *Kokinshū*):

　*Yo no naka ni / saranu wakare no / naku mo gana / chiyo mo to inoru / hito no ko no tame*

(Would that in this world there were no such thing as an unavoidable parting — for the sake of a child whose mother prays that he might live a thousand years.)

**Episode 85**

Long ago, there was a man. The lord whom he had served since childhood had his hair cut and entered holy orders. Every New Year (the First Month), the man would without fail go to pay his respects. Since he served at the Imperial Court, he was not ordinarily able to go. Nevertheless, he would attend, never having lost his original devotion. On this occasion, those who had formerly served (the lord) had gathered and come in great numbers; and as it was the New Year, a ceremony was held and wine was presented to all.
