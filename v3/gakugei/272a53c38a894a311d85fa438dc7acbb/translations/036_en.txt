**Episode 84**

Long ago, there was a man. Though his own station in life was humble, his mother was of royal (imperial) birth. This mother resided at a place called Nagaoka. Her son served at court in the capital, and though he intended to visit her, he was unable to go frequently. As he was her only child, she cherished him deeply and tenderly.

In such circumstances, around the twelfth month of a certain year, a letter arrived marked as urgent. When he opened it and looked, he found a poem (from the *Kokinshū*):

　*Oinureba / saranu wakare no / aru to ieba / iyoiyo mimaku / hoshiki kimi kana*

(Now that I have grown old, they say there exists a parting that cannot be avoided — and so I find myself longing more than ever to see you, my child.)

The son wept bitterly and composed in reply (from the *Kokinshū*):

　*Yo no naka ni / saranu wakare no / naku mo gana / chiyo mo to inoru / hito no ko no tame*

(Would that in this world there existed no parting that cannot be avoided! — for the sake of a child who prays for a thousand years of life for their parent.)

**Episode 85**

Long ago, there was a man. From his boyhood he had served a certain lord. That lord took holy orders and became a monk. (The man) always came to call upon him in the First Month (Mutsuki, i.e., January). Because he served at the imperial court, he could not come at all times; yet he came to visit without losing the devotion and loyalty he had felt since the old days.

(One year when he visited,) many people who had served the lord in former times had gathered together. As it was the First Month, there were festive ceremonies, and wine was bestowed upon them. Snow fell as if being scattered and poured down, without ceasing the whole day long. Everyone grew drunk, and (someone) remarked that they were snowed in and could not leave — and taking this as a topic, poems were composed. (The illustration on the left folio depicts this scene.)
