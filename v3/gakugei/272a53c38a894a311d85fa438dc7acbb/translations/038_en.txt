【Right folio】

*(Episode 87, conclusion)*

From the woman's side, the seaweed (*miru*) was placed on a tall footed stand (*takatsuki*) and covered with oak leaves (*kashiwa*), and presented to them. Written on the oak leaves was this poem:

　*Wadatsumi no / kazashi ni sasu to / ihafu momo* / *kimi ga tame ni wa / oshimazari keri*

(Even the hundred treasures that the god of the sea holds sacred, said to wear as hair ornaments — for your sake, I would not begrudge them at all.)

For a poem from a country person, this seems perhaps more than sufficient — or could it be wanting in any way? (No, it is quite accomplished enough.)

**Episode 88**

Long ago, a gathering of friends, none of them particularly young, came together to view the moon. Among them, one person composed (*Kokinshū*):

　*Ōkata wa / tsuki wo mo medezi / kore zo kono* / *tsumoreba hito no / oi to naru mono*

(Henceforth I shall no longer so readily admire the moon — for it is precisely this: as the months accumulate one upon another, they become the very thing that ages a person.)

---

**Episode 89**

Long ago, a man who was not of low birth fell in love with a woman of even higher station, and spent years in longing for her.

　*Hito shirezu / ware koi shinaba / ajiki naku* / *izure no kami ni / nakinanowasesen*

(If I were to die of this love, unknown to anyone, it would be a wretched thing — upon which deity would an undeserved ill name then be cast?)

---

**Episode 90**

Long ago, a man had long been trying to find a way to win the heart of a cold and indifferent woman. Perhaps she felt some pity for him, for she sent word: "Very well, then — tomorrow we may at least speak through a screen." The man was overjoyed beyond measure, and yet half-disbelieving too; and so, in a spirit of playful delight, he attached a poem to a branch of cherry blossoms and sent it:

　*Sakura hana / kyō koso kaku mo / niourame* / *anata no mi kata / asu no yo no koto*

(The cherry blossoms bloom so brilliantly today — but all depends on your disposition: what will tomorrow night bring?)

There is that shade of meaning in it as well.

---

**Episode 91**

Long ago, a man who lamented even the passing of the months and days composed this poem near the end of the third month (*Gosenshū*):

【Left folio】

　*Oshimedo mo / haru no kagiri no / kyō no hi no* / *yūgure ni sae / nari ni keru kana*

(Much as I regret it, this day — the very last day of spring — has even come to its evening, and there is nothing to be done.)

---

**Episode 92**

Long ago, a man would go to visit his beloved out of sheer longing, and then return — yet he could not manage to convey even a single message to the woman, and so he composed:

　*Ashibe kogu / tananashi kobune / ikusotabi* / *yuki kaeru ran / shiru hito mo nami*

(Like a small rudderless boat rowing along the reed-beds, how many times have I gone back and forth — and not a soul knows of it.)

---

**Episode 93**

Long ago, a man of low birth had set his heart on a woman of incomparably high station. Perhaps there was some slight reason for hope — he lay thinking of her, rose thinking of her, and worn out with longing, composed:

　*Aunana aunana / omoi wa suteji / nazohe naku* / *takaki iyashiki / kurushikari keri*

(I must meet her, I must meet her — yet I cannot abandon this longing. How painful it is, the love of one so far above and so far below — with no measure to bridge the distance.)

Such things have always, perhaps, been the way of the world.

**Episode 94**

Long ago there was a man. For some reason or other, the woman had ceased to live with him. There was a later husband, but since there was a child between her and the former man, things could not be entirely severed — from time to time, messages were exchanged. Since there was a painter (*e-kaku hito*) on the woman's side, the former man had been sending paintings to her; but the current husband, finding this disagreeable, had caused the painter to send no messenger, not once, not twice. The former man felt this most bitterly, and though he thought to himself: "It is only natural that I have not received any communication about what I have heard — and yet one cannot help but feel resentment toward her." So, choosing his words with great care, he composed a poem and sent it to her. The season was autumn.
