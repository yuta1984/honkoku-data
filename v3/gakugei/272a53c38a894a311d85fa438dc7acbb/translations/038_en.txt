【Right folio】

*(Continuation of Episode 87)*

From the women's side, that gathered seaweed (*ukimiru*) was heaped upon a tall footed stand (*takatsuki*) and covered with oak leaves (*kashiwa*), and presented. Upon the oak leaf a poem had been written:

　*Watatsu umi no / kazashi ni sasu to / iwafu mo mo / kimi ga tame ni wa / oshimazarikeri*

(Even the sacred seaweed that the god of the sea is said to wear as a hair ornament — for your sake, I would not begrudge giving it away.)

One might wonder whether this poem was more than one might expect from a country person, and perhaps that was (the reaction it drew).

**Episode 88**

Long ago, people who were not exactly young anymore gathered together as friends, gazing at the moon. Among them, one person composed (from the *Kokinshū*):

　*Ōkata wa / tsuki wo mo medeji / korezokono / tsumoreba hito no / oi to naru mono*

(Let us no longer admire the moon so freely — for it is precisely the accumulation of these months and their passing that becomes the aging of a person.)

---

**Episode 89**

Long ago, a man of no mean birth had spent years yearning for someone far above his station.

　*Hito shirezu / ware koi shinaba / ajikina ku / izure no kami ni / naki na owase n*

(If I were to die of this love, unknown to anyone, it would be a wretched thing indeed — upon which god would such a baseless ill-reputation be laid?)

---

**Episode 90**

Long ago, a man had continued to think: how might I win over this cold-hearted one? Perhaps out of pity for him, the person sent word: "Very well then — tomorrow, even if through a screen." He was boundlessly overjoyed, and yet also doubtful and uncertain, so he expressed his feelings in playful verse, attaching it to cherry blossoms (in their scattering glory):

　*Sakura hana / kyō koso kaku mo / niōrame / anata no mikata / asu no yo no koto*

(The cherry blossoms must be fragrant and glorious just like this today — but what tomorrow night brings is entirely up to you.)

Such, one supposes, was the spirit behind the poem.

**Episode 91**

Long ago, a man who lamented even the passage of days and months composed this poem near the end of the third month (from the *Gosenshū*):

【Left folio】

　*Oshimedo mo / haru no kagiri no / kyō no hi no / yūgure ni sae / nari ni keru kana*

(Much as I would hold it back, this final day of spring — today of all days — has come to be evening already.)

---

**Episode 92**

Long ago, a man kept going to a woman out of longing, yet kept returning, unable even to send a message to her, and composed:

　*Ashibe kogu / tanashi kobune / iku so tabi / yukikaeru ran / shiru hito mo nami*

(Like a little rudderless boat rowing among the reeds, how many times must I go and come back? — with no one who knows of it.)

---

**Episode 93**

Long ago, a man of lowly station had set his heart on someone utterly beyond his reach. There may have been some small indication that hope was not entirely impossible, for he lay thinking and rose thinking, and worn out with longing, composed:

　*Ōna ōna / omoi wa subeshi / nazohe naku / takaki iyashiki / kurushikarikeri*

(I have poured out all my longing for one I must never approach — the gulf between the high and the low, with nothing to compare it to, is a painful thing indeed.)

Even in ancient times, surely such things were simply the way of the world.

**Episode 94**

Long ago, there was a man (and a woman). What circumstances there had been, one cannot say, but that man had ceased to visit (the woman). Later there was another man, but as there was a child between (the woman and the first man), though not in great detail, she would occasionally send him word. As there was a person skilled in painting on the woman's side, she had a picture painted and sent — but the current man (her new partner) hoarded it away and would not forward it for a day or two. That first man found this most painful, and thought: what I convey to her, I have not received any reply to until now — so I suppose that is to be expected — yet one cannot help but feel resentment toward the other person nonetheless. With this, he carefully composed a poem and sent it off. The season was autumn.
