(Section 25, continued)

*Miru me naki / waga mi wo ura to / shiraneba ya / kare nade ama no / ashi tayuku kuru*
(Perhaps because she does not know that my worthless self is the "shore" she seeks — the diver woman comes dragging her weary feet, without parting from me.)

【Section 26】

Long ago, a man composed a reply to someone lamenting that he could no longer meet a woman who had lived around Gojō:

*Omohoezu / sode ni minato no / sawagu kana / morokoshi fune no / yorishi bakari ni*
(Unexpectedly, a harbor stirs and churns within my sleeve — all because a ship from China came to rest there, just for a moment.)

【Section 27】

Long ago, a man went to a woman's home for just one night and then never went again. The woman had been washing her hands, and a robe he had carelessly thrown aside lay there, its shadow reflected in the water of the basin. She composed on her own:

*Ware bakari / mono omou hito wa / mata mo araji to / omoeba / mizu no shita ni mo ari keri*
(I thought there could be no other person so lost in sorrow as I — and yet, looking down, there is one in the depths of the water too.)

His attendants heard this and told the man, who then composed:

*Minakuchi ni / ware ya miyuran / kawazu sae / mizu no shita nite mo / ro-goe ni naku*
(Does my reflection appear at the water's source? Even the frogs cry out with their oar-like voices from beneath the water's surface.)

【Section 28】

Long ago, a woman who had been given only to amorous affairs left and went away, so the man composed:

*Nadonite kaku / au-go katami ni / nari ni ken / mizu morasaji to / musubi shi mono wo*
(Why have we come to find it so hard to meet each other? We had tied our bond so tightly that not even a drop of water could escape.)

【Section 29】

Long ago, when summoned to compose for the floral celebration held at the chambers of the Crown Prince's Lady of Honor:

*Hana ni akanu / nageki wa itsu mo / seshi kadomo / kyō no koyoi ni / niru toki wa nashi*
(The lament of never having my fill of blossoms is one I have always known — yet there has never been a night quite like this night.)

【Section 30】

Long ago, a man composed for a woman with whom he had grown distant:

【Left Page】

*Au koto wa / tama no o bakari / omohoe de / tsuraki kokoro no / nagaku miyuran*
(Meeting you seems not even as brief as the slender thread that strings the jewels — and yet your cruel, cold heart seems to stretch on endlessly.)

【Section 31】

Long ago, in the palace, as a man passed before the chamber of certain court ladies, perhaps harboring some grievance, a man who had said, "Well then, let us see what becomes of you, like leaves of grass," composed:

*Tsumi mo naki / hito wo ukeba / wasuregusa / onoga ue ni zo / ou to iu naru*
(They say that if one curses an innocent person, the "forgetting grass" will grow upon the head of the one who uttered the curse.)

And there were women who were jealous (and displeased) at this.

【Section 32】

Long ago, after many long years had passed, a man sent a poem to a woman with whom he had once exchanged words of love:

*Inishie no / shizu no odamaki / kurikaeshi / mukashi wo ima ni / nasu yoshi mo ga na*
(Like unwinding again the ancient hemp spool of the lowly folk, if only there were a way to make the past into the present once more.)

But she apparently felt nothing, or so it seemed.

【Section 33】

Long ago, a man had been visiting a woman in Mubara district of Tsu Province. She seemed to be thinking that this time, if he came, she would never let him go. So the man composed:

*Ashibe yori / michikuru shio no / iya mashi ni / kimi ni kokoro wo / omoi masu kana*
(Like the tide that rises ever higher from the reed shores, my feelings for you grow and grow without end.)

Her reply:

*Komori-e ni / omou kokoro wo / ikade ka wa / fune sasu sao no / sashite shirubeki*
(The feelings hidden in my heart, like a hidden inlet — how could the pole that steers a boat ever point to them and make them known?)

As for the ways of country folk — whether good or bad (one can only say, "well, that's how it is").
