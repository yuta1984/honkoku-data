As (the palace attendant) was watching, (the man) took his shoes, threw them into the interior of the room, and climbed up (into the women's quarters). Carrying on in this unseemly manner repeatedly, he felt that his very being was on the verge of ruin, and that he would surely be destroyed in the end. Thinking "What am I to do? Please calm this longing heart of mine," he prayed to the buddhas and gods alike, but his passion only seemed to grow stronger, and still he found it unbearably agonizing to yearn for her. He therefore summoned an *onmyōji* (yin-yang diviner), told him that he had "fallen in love," had him bring the implements for a ritual purification (*harae*), and went to the ceremony. Yet the more the purification rites were performed, the more his sorrow multiplied, and he found himself longing for her even more than before. And so he composed:

*Having performed the misogi purification at the Mitarashi River to be rid of this love — it seems the gods have refused to accept it.*

And with that, he departed.

This Emperor had a handsome countenance, and with deep devotion he recited the names of the buddhas; hearing him chant in his most sacred and reverent voice, the woman wept bitterly. She wept, saying: "That I cannot serve such a sovereign — this is the sorrow and misfortune of my past-life karma. I am bound by this man (and cannot be free)."

In the midst of all this, the Emperor came to hear of the matter, and had the man sent into exile. Thereupon this woman's cousin, the Imperial Consort (*Miyasundokoro*), had the woman bound in place, confined her in a storehouse, and punished her severely. The woman, shut up in the storehouse, wept and wept.
