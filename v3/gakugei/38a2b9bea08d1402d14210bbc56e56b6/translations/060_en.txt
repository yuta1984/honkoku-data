　　A Method for Making Trees Grow Thick

- To make trees grow thicker, score several vertical cuts into the trunk. The tree will grow thick as a result.

　　A Method for Preventing Birds from Picking Up Dried Rice, Grains, Sesame, and Similar Items Left Out to Dry

- When drying dried cooked rice (*hoshiī*), mixed grains, or other items that birds are likely to pick up, place one string of large prayer beads (*juzu*) inside the drying vessel or tray. Birds will be frightened and will not approach.

　　A Remedy for Splinters

- When a splinter of wood or bamboo has become lodged in the skin, grind up fresh green pine shoots and apply them to the wound. When an arrowhead or needle is embedded, mix and mash a mole cricket (*kera*) into cooked rice and apply it — the effect is excellent.

- Day of the Ox (*ushi*): (There will be sorrow or worry.)
- Day of the Tiger (*tora*): (There will be an external matter or outside affair.)

- Day of the Horse (*uma*): (There will be a joyful event.)
- Day of the Sheep (*hitsuji*): (Nothing in particular; ordinary.)
- Day of the Monkey (*saru*): (Peace and calm. [one character illegible])

　　A Method for Warding Off Wind-Driven Fire

- Cut five *shaku* or one *jō* of crimson silk (*momi no kinu*) into the shape of a banner (*hata*), attach it to a bamboo pole, and throw it into the fire. Instantly, the wind will reverse direction and the fire will be extinguished. In urgent situations, throwing in a garment of crimson (*momi*) cloth also works well.
