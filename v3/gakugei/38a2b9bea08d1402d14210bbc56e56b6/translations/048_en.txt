　Method for Making Finely Cut Tofu in the Udon Style

- Press the tofu against two corners of its box, place the palm of your hand against the left side, and using a sufficiently thin-bladed vegetable knife (usuba), slice it finely, removing the slices immediately to the side. Cut it in the manner described above. When boiling, if you add a little kudzu (arrowroot) starch, the pieces will not fall apart no matter how finely they are cut. If kudzu is not available, adding about seven grains of glutinous rice (mochi rice) will serve just as well.

　Method for Removing the Adductor Muscle (Hashira) from Clams

- When boiling clams, if you add seven or eight grains of non-glutinous rice (uruchi rice), the adductor muscle will separate cleanly.

　Method for Simmering Arame (Rough Seaweed)

- When boiling arame seaweed, adding a little vinegar will soften it quickly.

　Method for Correcting Sake That Has Gone Sour with a Mouldy Taste

- Grind the white-roasted shell of an abalone (ishiketsumei / awabi shell) into powder and add it to sake that has turned mouldy and sour — the flavour will be promptly restored.

- Alternatively, wash the fruit of the crow gourd (karasuuri / Trichosanthes cucumeroides) thoroughly, burn it in a fire until it becomes ash, add it to the sake, and stir. The sake will immediately clear and its flavour will be restored.
