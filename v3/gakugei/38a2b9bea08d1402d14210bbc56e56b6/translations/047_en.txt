　Method for Shaping Eggs into Chrysanthemum and Bellflower Forms

- Lay a sheet of hanshi paper inside a morning-glory-shaped sake cup (choku), smoothing it from the bottom so that there are no wrinkles. Crack an egg and pour it directly into the cup. Gather the paper on top into five pleats to create a bellflower (kikyō) shape; gather it into more pleats to create a chrysanthemum (kiku) shape. Tie the pleats with a twisted paper cord (koyori), submerge in boiling water, hold the ends of the koyori and the paper with your fingers, and boil thoroughly, taking care not to let it tilt to one side. Lift it out.

【Left Page, Lower Section】
After it has cooled sufficiently, remove the paper.

　Method for Peeling Chestnuts and Arrowhead Bulbs (Kuwai)

- Whether peeling chestnuts or arrowhead bulbs (kuwai), apply a thin-bladed vegetable knife (usuba) and use it as if rubbing/shaving, rotating the chestnut as you peel. If you cannot manage it neatly, rub [the surface] a little with horsetail (tokusa) and it will come out beautifully, no matter what.

　Method for Making Finely Cut Konjac (Konnyaku)

- It is difficult to slice konjac neatly when trying to shave it thinly. Instead, lay it flat, shave it into thin slices, and then mince it finely — this will produce a suitably fine cut.
