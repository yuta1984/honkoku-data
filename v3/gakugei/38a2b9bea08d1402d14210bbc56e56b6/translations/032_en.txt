**A Formula for Metal Lacquer (*Kana-urushi*)**

- To repair cracked and leaking pots, cauldrons, and other metal vessels, use the following:

　　Iron file shavings (*yasuriko no kezuri-kuzu*)
　　Heated soapstone (*onjaku*)
　　Light lacquer (*kō-urushi*)

Knead these together thoroughly with water and press the mixture into the crack. For large holes, use a camellia leaf

**A Method for Preventing Weevils in Polished Rice (*Tsukigome*)**

- Around summertime, polished rice tends to go bad and become infested with insects. The secret teaching handed down is to store the rice together with its rice bran (*nuka*). Use only as much as needed at a time, sifting it before use.

**A Method for Keeping Insects Out of Books and Clothing**

- To prevent insects from getting into books, place a plant called *asebo* inside the book chest (*shodansu*). This method is also good for clothing.

- For clothing as well, place the seeds of *seikō* (*Artemisia*) in the wardrobe (*tansu*). This is what is commonly called *kawarayo mogi* (riverside mugwort/wormwood). Furthermore, for leather quilts and the like, dry them in the sun, shade-dry the leaves of *kawajisa* (water lettuce / wild chicory), and place them between the layers of the quilt — insects will not eat through them.

**A Method for Making Hair Black and Lustrous**

- To blacken hair and bring out its sheen, boil soybeans in *suimokusa* (wood sorrel / oxalis), take the resulting liquid, and apply it to the hair. Alternatively, add it to sesame oil, let it sit for seven days, and then rub it into the hair.
