　**On the Matter of Illuminating the Bottom of a Well**

- When an object has fallen to the bottom of a well and you wish to see it, lowering a lamp or torch will not allow you to see the bottom clearly. In such a case, hold a single *shu-oshiki* (a lacquered square tray) face-down from above, and the firelight will reflect off it, allowing you to see all the way to the bottom just as you wish — most convenient.

　**A Method to Prevent Insects from Afflicting All Crops in the Field**

- For this method: bury clippings of horse hooves at the four corners of the field or paddy. That insects will not attack the crops is a wondrous thing.

　**On the Method of Mending Cracked or Broken Pots, Jars, and Similar Vessels**

- For a cracked iron pot (*kwansu*): mix wheat flour (*udon* flour) with lime and fiber scraps (*sen-kuzu*), knead with water, and use this to join the broken pieces together. They will not come apart for a long time. For a cracked ceramic jar: mix a little wheat flour into lacquer and apply it to bond the pieces. For a jar that has a crack and is leaking: grate fresh ginger finely and apply it to the inside of the crack, then apply lacquer to the outside to seal it over. The lacquer will gradually seep into the crack from the inside, and the vessel will no longer leak.
