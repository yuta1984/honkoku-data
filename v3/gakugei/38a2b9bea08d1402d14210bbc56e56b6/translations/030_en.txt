**A Secret Method for Preserving Matsutake Mushrooms Without Losing Their Fragrance**

- Take only the caps of matsutake mushrooms and split them into [six pieces if large, four pieces if medium]. Open up an empty salt bale (*shiodawara*) and unfurl it. Lay the caps face-upward in a single layer, sprinkle salt generously over them in good measure, and roll them up inside. Repeat this process as many layers as desired in the same manner. Roll them up, bind the top with rope, tie it up into a bundle like a *tsuto* (a straw-wrapped parcel), and hang it from the eaves.

---

When needed at any time, take it down the day before use, draw out the salt thoroughly, and use the mushrooms in simmered dishes and the like — the fragrance will be just as if they had been freshly gathered. [It is best to use sufficiently fresh mushrooms for pickling.]

**A Method for Boiling Water with a Small Amount of Charcoal in Summer**

- Fill a bucket with approximately one full釜 (*kama*) worth of water, and leave it out in the sunlight. When it becomes warm (*onsui*), transfer it to the cooking pot (*kama*). Light a charcoal fire in a brazier (*hibachi*), place some kind of stand beneath the cooking hearth (*kamado*), position the brazier close to the bottom of the pot, and cover the mouth of the hearth with a lid that allows some air to pass through. Even with only a small amount of charcoal, even a large pot of water will come to a boil.
