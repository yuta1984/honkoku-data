***Taisei Shōji-tsū*** — Summary of newly compiled and expanded contents —

- **Correct Characters** (*seiji*; correcting characters): Rectifies brushstroke forms and distinguishes erroneous characters.
- **Kana Orthography** (*kaji*; kana usage): Thoroughly revises the kana spelling for both on and kun readings.
- **Official Titles and Ranks** (*kanshoku*): Records the corresponding Japanese titles for Chinese-style official designations (*tōmyō*) alongside or below the relevant entries.
- **Pillow Words** (*makurakotoba*): Records the pillow words (*makurakotoba*) used in *waka* poetry beneath each respective entry.
- **Seasonal References** (*kiyose*): Lists and categorizes seasonal topics and phenomena (*keibutsu*) used in linked verse and haiku (*renhai*).
- **Tonal Patterns** (*hyōsoku*; level and oblique tones): Marks the tonal pattern (*hyōsoku*) alongside every character, serving as an aid for composing Chinese-style poetry.
- **Annual Observances** (*nenjū gyōji*): Records without omission the dates of court ceremonies and the annual festivals and religious gatherings at temples and shrines.
- **Key Principles of Character Usage** (*yōji yōketsu*): For characters sharing the same reading but differing in meaning, records the distinctions in meaning and demonstrates that incorrect (*ate-ji*) usage is to be avoided.
- **Set Phrases and Compound Characters** (*seigo jukuji*): Broadly gathers words and compounds used in the classical texts, poetry, prose, and letter-writing manuals (*sekitoku*), appending Japanese readings and explanations.
- **Alternative Names and Sino-Japanese Terminology** (*imyō kanji*): Selects alternative names, elegant appellations (*gashō*), and correct Sino-Japanese designations for things and objects, all verified as to their sources, and appends them for use in composing poetry and prose.

【Right folio, lower section】

***Wakan Sansai Zue*** [Illustrated Sino-Japanese Encyclopedia of the Three Powers]
(Compiled by Master Terajima Ryōan; complete in 81 volumes)

***Hakubutsu Sen*** [A Primer of Universal Knowledge]
(Complete in 7 volumes; combined into 1 volume)

This work records in thorough detail every matter found in heaven and earth—its historical background and origins—arranged in *iroha* order, so that whenever one wishes to consult the deep source of any topic, it can be found immediately and with ease.

This is a work equal to the aforementioned *Wakan Sansai Zue*.

***Myōjutsu Hakubutsu Sen*** [Wondrous Arts: A Primer of Universal Knowledge]
(Complete in 7 volumes; combined into 1 volume)

This work is likewise an extremely valuable household reference. It gathers without omission everything needed for daily life, including secret transmissions, hidden techniques, secret arts, and secret methods of wondrous remedies—all organized in *iroha* order, so that any matter can be looked up immediately. At the end is a section called the "Fire-Suppression Precaution Wheel" (*chinkaYōjin-guruma*), which records vital matters for escaping fire disasters. This work is worth owning for that one section alone. It surpasses the aforementioned *Hakubutsu Sen* by a wide margin.

***Nihon Saijiki*** [Japanese Record of Annual Seasonal Customs]
(Composed by Master Kaibara; complete in 8 volumes, combined edition in 4 volumes)

From the erection of New Year's pine decorations (*kadomatsu*) at the start of the first month, to the bean-throwing ceremony (*setsubun*) at the year's end, this work gathers all seasonal observances without omission, investigating their origins and historical precedents by drawing on both Japanese and Chinese texts. It also thoroughly assembles guidance on seasonal health practices, methods of storing provisions, and even how to cultivate plants and trees throughout the four seasons.

***Jinka Manbō Daiei Jūhō no Sho*** [A Book of the Greatest Principles for Myriad Household Treasures]
Complete in 9 volumes

The first two volumes explain, in thirty-three articles, the conduct of one's person and the proper employment of one's mind—being the foremost of all household treasures, namely: how to attain long life and prosperity, and how to live out one's days in comfort when lord and retainer, parent and child, and siblings are in harmony. These are followed by the seven-volume *Chijutsu Zensho* (Complete Book of Wisdom and Tactics) appended alongside. Truly, this is a book of myriad household treasures.

***Kinnō Chijutsu Zensho*** [Complete Book of Wisdom and Tactics in a Brocade Bag]
(Complete in 7 volumes; also available as 1 volume)

This is a work that sets forth the wondrous secrets and hidden arts by which, using wisdom, one may make weighty matters light and accomplish what seemed impossible with ease of mind. If one employs this book daily in conducting one's affairs, the benefits to one's virtue and prosperity will be vast—it is a book for obtaining myriad treasures—and what this book gathers surpasses the aforementioned *Hakubutsu Sen* manifold.

***Jiten Nenjū Chōhō Sen*** [Selected Annual Household Treasures with Character Dictionary]
(Pocket-size small volume; complete in 1 volume)

This work is an *iroha*-ordered character dictionary and practical household reference (*settsuyōshū*), gathering eighty articles on matters essential to the household. At the end, it collects without omission the annual observances, the festival rites and religious assemblies at shrines and temples throughout the various provinces, and all other matters with fixed calendar dates.

【Left folio, lower section】

***Keiken Iryō Tebiki-gusa*** [Experiential Guide to Medical Treatment]
Complete in 7 volumes

This work gathers secret prescriptions from various traditions that have proven immediately effective, along with fine medicinal preparations from Japanese and Chinese sources. All entries are given full kana readings, and disease names are arranged in *iroha* order with an index. If a household keeps this book and uses it to administer medicines for any illness, the results will be as if divine. Since disease names are arranged in *iroha* order, the remedy for any given illness can be found quickly and easily. Truly, this is a fine book for saving lives and benefiting the world.

***Sanpō Chie no Umi Taizen*** [Complete Sea of Wisdom for Mathematical Methods]
Complete in 1 volume

Though books in the style of *Jinkōki* abound in the world, this work records the innermost secrets of mathematical methods (*sanpō*) and yet is written so as to be easily comprehensible even to beginners—covering topics from Kamei arithmetic (*Kamei-san*) and extraction of square roots (*kaihei*) and cube roots (*kairitsu*) to the *Tenyuanjutsu* (celestial element method), all rendered in *hiragana* to make study remarkably easy. If one studies the abacus (*soroban*) using this book, one will learn from one example and understand ten, and will be able to swiftly grasp and master any kind of calculation.

***Wakan Sangaku Zue*** [Illustrated Compendium of Japanese and Chinese Mathematics]
Complete in 8 volumes

This work supplements what was still omitted from the aforementioned *Chie no Umi*, appending secret oral transmissions on abstruse matters, written in a mixture of *kanji* and *katakana* and accompanied by illustrations—so that, by means of this book, no calculation relating to any thing in the world is left uncovered. Truly, this is the definitive complete compendium of mathematics.
