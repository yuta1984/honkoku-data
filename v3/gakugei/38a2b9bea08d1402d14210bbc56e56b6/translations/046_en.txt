　Secret Method for Dyeing an Imitation Purple (Magaimurасaki)

- Simmer sappanwood (suō) thoroughly until well reduced, dye the cloth with it, and dry it in good weather. Afterward, boil the remaining dregs of the sappanwood again, and with this liquid drip lye (aku) onto [the cloth].

The ash used for the lye should be made from taro stems (imogara). Burn these over a fire to produce lye, and saturate [the cloth] thoroughly and evenly. If the color is too light, apply the lye again. (Marginal note: Sappanwood should be simmered in an earthenware pot. The color will come out remarkably well.)

　Method for Tying Mountain Yam for Use in Cooking

- Whether for clear soup (suimono) or simmered dishes, if you wish to tie mountain yam (yamaimo) into knots before serving, peel the yam well, cut it into thin strips, and soak these in salt water in advance. After removing them and tying them into knots, soak them well in fresh water to remove the saltiness. Then cook and serve.

　Method for Eating Taro Raw Without the Astringent Sting

- Hold the taro in your right hand, hold a small knife in your left hand, and peel the skin. Then, still holding the knife in your left hand, cut the taro and eat it. There will be no astringent sting whatsoever.

　Method for Reconstituting Dried Shiitake Mushrooms to a Fresh State

- Place sand in a vessel, add water to the sand to moisten it thoroughly, plant the dried shiitake mushrooms into the wet sand, and leave them overnight. Even the stems will become soft, as if fresh.
