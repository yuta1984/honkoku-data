　　A Method for Making Rubbings of Carved Stones (*Tōishi-zuri*) or Stone Monuments Without the Text Appearing in Mirror Image
*(This is the method for making stone rubbings [*tōishi-zuri*].)*

- Apply a thin coating of *dōsa* (alum-and-glue sizing solution) to *tōshi* (Chinese paper). Then, whether working from a wooden board or a stone, moisten the object bearing the carved characters with water. When the surface has reached a suitably damp state, lay the prepared Chinese paper over it and use a tapping brush (*tataki-bake*) to carefully press the paper into the carved characters by tapping. Once the paper has dried thoroughly, grind a good-quality ink stick thoroughly on a large inkstone and collect plenty of ink. Then cut an eggplant (*nasu*) at the stem (*heta*), dip the cut surface in the ink, and rub it across the paper. Take great care that the ink does not enter into the recessed areas of the characters themselves.

　　A Charm for Walking at Night

- When going out at night, use the middle finger of either your left or right hand to write the three characters 「我是鬼」(*Ware wa kore oni* — "I am a demon/ogre") on the inside of your palm, then clench your fist tightly and walk on. Naturally, nothing fearful will befall you.

　　The Same Method: A Method for Not Being Bewitched by Foxes

- When going out at night, place a small lump of *shinsha* (cinnabar / vermilion ore) on top of your head. Cinnabar glows at night, and therefore evil or strange beings will not approach you.

　　A Method for Crossing a River Without Difficulty

【Left Page, Lower Section】
- When crossing a river, write the character 「土」(*tsuchi*, meaning "earth/soil") with a brush (on your palm). Writing it with your finger also works. Alternatively, if you carry on your person a piece of paper on which this character has been written in vermilion ink, you will cross without difficulty.

　　A Method for Stopping a Person Who Has Run Away

- Take the clothing or sash (*obi*) of the person who has fled, wrap a magnet in paper, place it inside the clothing or sash, and hang it down into a well. The person will immediately return.
　Alternatively: Nail the sandals (*zōri*) of the person who has run away in front of the cooking stove (*kamado*). The person will be unable to go far and will naturally return home.
