　　Method for Heating *Tororo-jiru* (Grated Yam Soup) in Winter Without It Breaking

- If placed in a *Kōrai-yaki* (Korean-style ceramic) vessel and heated, it will not break (lose its viscosity) at all. Also, if one stalk of *arame* (a type of kelp) is added and heated, no matter how hot it becomes, the viscosity will not be lost.

　　Chapter on Candles and Fire
　　Method for Making Rain-Proof Fire Rope (Fuse Cord)

- Bring *haguro* (tooth-blackening liquid) to a boil and place an ordinary fire rope (*hinawa*) into it. Once well saturated, remove it, dry it in the sun, and light it — even when exposed to rain, the fire will not go out.

　　A Torch for Capturing Distant Light

- Take the shafts of owl feathers, polish the white portions well, fill the hollow centre of each shaft to the brim with the finest quality mercury (*mizukane*), seal the openings tightly, prepare a number of them, and bind them together in a bundle. When this torch is aimed toward a place where there is a fire in the distance, it will bring that light into a dark place. No matter how great the distance, the light transfers well. However, if even a single layer of paper stands between the torch and the fire, the light will not transfer. Furthermore, if this torch is shaken briskly in a dark place, it will also emit light on its own. The more feather shafts used, the brighter the light.
