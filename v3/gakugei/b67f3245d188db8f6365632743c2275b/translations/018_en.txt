[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
Even one who ought
　　to teach others —
　if they themselves
　　are neglectful,
what good is it
　to claim
　　they know
　　　the Way?

*(Commentary)*
To know something means to refine one's own conduct and thereby be fit to teach and guide others. Even if one already "knows," if one does not put that knowledge into practice, it is no different from not knowing at all. As the popular saying goes, "the reader of the *Analects* who does not live by the *Analects*" — what benefit can there be in such knowledge?

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
The one who can clearly discern
　good and ill
　　in others as if from afar —
why is it that
　when it comes
　　to their own self,
　　　they are lost?

*(Commentary)*
Even one who is called perceptive becomes dim when it comes to judging themselves; and even one who is thoroughly foolish can see clearly when judging others. That is to say: even a person of great wisdom and ingenuity tends to be inattentive to their own affairs, while even a lesser person can hit the mark when criticizing others. How is it, then, that a person of learning and intellect, who readily pronounces on the good and ill of others, fails to perceive and understand what lies close at hand — their own self? This is something of which one ought truly to be ashamed and wary.
