[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
Think of today alone
　　as the day
　　　　to serve
　　　your parents —
　do not trust in tomorrow
　　in this world
　　　where nothing
　　　　is certain.

*(Commentary)*
Everyone knows that one ought to be filially devoted to one's parents — and yet, without thinking of the present moment, people tell themselves: "If not today, then tomorrow; if not tomorrow, then the day after," and by thus putting things off, they ultimately pass their entire lives in vain. As the saying goes: "Seeking three years' worth of moxa for a seven-year illness" — while one dithers in this way, one's parents may pass away before one has the chance to act. Or perhaps one's own death may come first — there is no telling. Even if both parent and child survive together, illness or poverty may render one unable to do as one wishes. If that time comes, regret will be of no use whatsoever. The only thing to do is to rouse one's spirit each day with the thought: "Today is a day on which I can serve." It is said: "The filial child begrudges the passing of each day."

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
A woman who is
　　consumed
　　　by jealousy
　　　　beyond all measure —
　one would do well
　　to send her away
　　　without delay.

*(Commentary)*
Jealousy and possessiveness in a wife are common things in this world — nine out of ten women are prone to it in some degree. However, when jealousy runs excessively deep, it becomes the root of calamity. It causes disorder within the household, and from it various misfortunes arise. It is best, upon careful observation, to part from such a woman. "One who is consumed by jealousy should be sent away," Confucius himself is said to have declared.
