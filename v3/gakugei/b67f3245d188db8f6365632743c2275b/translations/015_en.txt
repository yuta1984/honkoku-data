[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
How perilous
　the final end
　　of one who,
　　　mired in folly,
　does not even know
　　their own transgressions
　　　as transgressions.

*(Commentary)*
Among those below the level of a sage, there is no one who is without error. Yet the wise person and the noble person (junzi), when they err, recognize it themselves and swiftly correct themselves, thereby arriving at a state of being without fault. The errors of petty and foolish persons go unrecognized by themselves; and even when recognized, such people hesitate to reform, cover up their wrongs, and allow even small mistakes to grow great — until at last they bring ruin upon themselves and destruction upon their households. The words of the sages teach: "Do not be reluctant to correct your mistakes."

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
In a world where walls have ears
　　and even stones
　　　can speak —
do not do evil
　　on the grounds
　　　that no one will know.

*(Commentary)*
When a person schemes to do evil and commits acts of injustice, thinking to avoid discovery, they may do so deep within a private chamber, or even in the depths of a mountain — yet in the end such things leak out and become known; there is no such thing as something that goes unknown. It is just as the saying goes: "Walls have ears to hear, and stones have mouths to speak." Yet in truth it is not that walls literally listen or stones literally speak: "Heaven knows, Earth knows, others know, I know" — these are called the Four Knowings (Shichi). As occasions arise and events unfold, all things are eventually brought to light; it is also said that "evil deeds travel a thousand leagues." One must be on guard. Zengzi said: "Where ten eyes look, where ten hands point — how solemn and awesome that is!"
