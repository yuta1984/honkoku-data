**Poem:**
*As for the kindness you bestow upon others — set it aside in your heart; but the kindness of others toward you, never forget for long.*

When a person gives even a small thing, they think of it as a great favor, show it on their face, express it in words, and expect something in return. Conversely, when one receives another's kindness, one may feel grateful at the moment, but later forgets it entirely, thinks nothing of it, and in the end there are even those who repay kindness with enmity. In general, to bestow gifts and favors upon others while expecting repayment is nothing more than acting for one's own benefit — it is not true benevolence and compassion. For this reason, when the person who received the favor shows no gratitude and gives nothing in return, one grows angry, resentful, and full of regret, wishing one could take back what was given. The reply of the Great Master Bodhidharma regarding "no merit" also embodies this very spirit of acting without expectation of reward. Furthermore, when one receives another's kindness and compassion, one must never forget it. It is by knowing gratitude and knowing righteousness that one is called a human being. Without this, one is no different from a beast. In the desk inscription of a man called Saishi (Cui Zi), it is written: "When you bestow upon others, take care never to dwell on it; when you receive a bestowment, take care never to forget it."

**Poem:**
*Borrowed things — handle them with even greater care than your own; and before long, return them faithfully.*

When you borrow another person's utensils or tools, pay careful attention from the very beginning, treat them with great care, and once you have finished using them, repair and tidy them up and return them promptly. If you handle them carelessly, keep them for a long time, damage them, or lose them, what will you do? Be careful, be careful.
