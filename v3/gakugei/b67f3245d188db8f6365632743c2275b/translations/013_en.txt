[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
If only my heart
　could be reflected
　　in a mirror —
how very ugly
　its shadow
　　would surely be.

*(Commentary)*
When a person faces a mirror, the good and ill of their appearance becomes clearly visible, and so they are able to correct and improve upon what is wanting. But because the heart has no physical form, it cannot be reflected in a mirror — and therefore one cannot know one's own faults. If the heart were something that could be reflected in a mirror, how very ugly and repulsive its shadow would surely appear; one would be ashamed of it even in one's own eyes. Were that truly so, one ought to reform and correct oneself without delay.

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
Giving rise to anger,
　a fire burns forth
　from within the heart —
　　scorching none other
　　　than one's
　　　　own self.

*(Commentary)*
It is recorded in medical texts that anger injures the liver. Furthermore, among the five visceral organs, anger is associated with the liver; when anger is extreme, the fire of the liver blazes fiercely, scorching and damaging that organ. Moreover, when one gives free rein to anger, one will inevitably harm others. If one harms others, they in turn will harbor resentment and bring harm upon oneself. Is this not the same as wounding oneself with one's own hand? The *Analects of Confucius* states: "To forget oneself in a moment of anger, thereby bringing harm upon one's parents, is delusion." This is something one must guard against most carefully. It is also said: "When gripped by anger, think of the calamity it will bring." When something arises that provokes one's temper, one should call to mind the disaster that will befall — the harm done to others and to oneself — and restrain and check that anger.
