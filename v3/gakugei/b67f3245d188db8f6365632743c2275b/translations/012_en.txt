[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
A wife who is clever
　　　　and has let
　　　　　herself
　　　　　　grow proud —
in the end, she becomes
　an autumn wind
　　to her husband.

*(Commentary)*
In general, it is the proper way of a person to speak little, to be humble, and to have no heart for boasting. A wife, above all, ought to be gentle and restrained, holding back and conducting herself with propriety in all things — for that is what makes her truly kind and trustworthy. If she is sharp-tongued and argumentative, if she looks down on others and exalts herself, conducting herself willfully and as she pleases, then there can be no doubt that she will be forsaken by her husband. Indeed, among the Seven Grounds for Divorce, it is stated that being talkative (*kuchi-mame*) is itself cause for repudiation.

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
Even if
　　your complexion is dark
　　　　and your looks
　　are plain,
　a wife should cultivate
　　clean and proper
　　　　speech.

*(Commentary)*
Even if a woman has a fair complexion and beautiful features, if her heart is twisted and obstinate, she is like dung wrapped in a brocade bag — though she may catch the eye at first glance, she will ultimately be looked down upon. On the other hand, even if a wife is dark-complexioned and plain of face, if her manner of speaking and her conduct are admirable, and her heart is honest and upright, then she is like gold wrapped in coarse cloth: though at first she may not seem to suit a man's taste, in time she will surely come to be regarded as a person of depth and great reliability. As the old saying goes: "Though her form may resemble a barbarian of a distant island, her heart is the flowery capital itself." In the end, a person's worth lies in their heart — the appearance of face and form is but a superficial matter. One must carefully cultivate and discipline one's heart, words, and bearing.
