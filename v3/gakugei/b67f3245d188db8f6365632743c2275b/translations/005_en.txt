I, your humble servant, having long cherished these poems of moral instruction, finally took up my brush in the winter of the sixteenth year of Kyōhō, the year of Kanoto-i (1731).

　　　　Written by Nakamura Sankoshi
　　　　　[Seal] [Seal]

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
If you keep company
　with good people,
evil things
　shall
　　not befall you —
just as the mugwort
growing among hemp
　shows us
　　plainly.

*(Commentary)*
A "good person" is one whose heart is filled with compassion and honesty, who is proper in conduct and etiquette, and who is deeply devoted to filial piety and loyal service. If one draws close to and associates with such a person, one will learn to see and hear good things, and before long one's own character will naturally improve. The mugwort plant is by nature crooked and wandering, yet when it grows among hemp, it is held upright by those stalks and becomes straight of its own accord. Similarly, it is said: mix with vermilion and you turn red; draw near to ink and you turn black. To associate with good people is like entering a room filled with芝蘭 (*shiran* — orchid and thoroughwort); after dwelling there long, one ceases to notice how fragrant it is. To associate with wicked people is like entering a fishmonger's stall; after dwelling there long, one ceases to notice how foul the smell has become — so it is said.
The *Analects of Confucius* states: "For a village, benevolence (*ren*) makes it a fine place. To choose to live where there is no benevolence — how can one attain wisdom?" One must take care to choose one's companions and one's place of dwelling wisely.
