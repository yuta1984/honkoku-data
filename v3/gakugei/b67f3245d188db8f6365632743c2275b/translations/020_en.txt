**[Right Page — Poem]**
Only if you yourself are righteous and others are at fault could such a claim be made;
but in truth, the faults you see in others are your own faults reflected back.

**[Right Page — Prose]**
In general, people do not speak of their own mistakes, and it is foolish to only think others are wrong and to harbor resentment and anger toward them. Both the good and the bad in others all originate from oneself. It is said, "What comes from you returns to you." It is also written: "If you show respect to someone and receive no response, that respect returns to yourself. When you act and do not achieve the desired result, turn and seek the cause within yourself." Furthermore, it is said, "The gentleman is like one shooting a bow — if the arrow misses the mark, he turns and seeks the reason within himself." It is also said, "The gentleman blames himself; the petty person blames others." These sayings can be found in the Analects (*Lunyu*) and the Mencius (*Mengzi*).

**[Left Page — Poem]**
There is no one who, upon seeing such a person, would not feel contempt —
one who hoards their own possessions yet begs for the belongings of others.

**[Left Page — Prose]**
If your own things are too precious to you to share when someone asks, then push that feeling outward and think: others must feel the same way. Do not therefore go about requesting things from others. Conversely, if you receive something from another person and feel glad to have your needs supplemented, use that feeling to gauge the hearts of others, and respond generously when they come to you in need. To extend one's own feelings outward in this way so as to benefit others is called the Way of Benevolence (*jin* / *ren*). In the poem composed by the consort of Lord Ōuchi Yoshitaka, this very sentiment is expressed: "Placing myself in another's position, I come to understand another's pain — if they long for someone, they must long as I long." And yet, how shameful it is for someone to begrudge giving anything of their own, while endlessly and shamelessly asking things of others. Who could fail to despise such a person?
