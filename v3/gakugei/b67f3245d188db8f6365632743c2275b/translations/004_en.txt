It has been newly entitled "The Pond of Pure Water." Even the plain words of common speech, if one becomes attached to them through the appeal of the illustrations, are like filtering through muddy water to reflect the moon in a clear pond — this may well be called a truly delightful pictorial commentary. The publisher, too, inspired by good intentions under the banner of "moral instruction," and wishing to turn children away from such idle pastimes as stone-throwing games toward

[Left folio]
the path of filial piety and fraternal love, carefully examined and corrected the characters worn away and difficult to read, one by one, and asked me to write a preface — which I have duly done. Truly, "goodness" may be an age-old word, yet even in the wakeful moments of spring and autumn nights, it feels refreshingly clear and ever new, and is a matter of which one need not be ashamed before any witness. Therefore, no matter if people call this a work for beginners, or mock it as vulgar and lowly——
