It has been newly titled "The Pond of Pure Water." Even the plain and simple words of common speech, if one is drawn into them by the feeling of the illustrations, will settle in the heart — like searching through muddy waters to find a clear pond where the moon is reflected. This may well be called a rather delightful picture-commentary. The publisher too, moved to goodness by the very idea of moral instruction, and wishing to offer something in place of children's idle games such as stone-throwing,

sought to bring benefit to the way of filial piety and brotherly duty. He carefully examined each faded and worn character, and requested of me (the author) that I write a preface. Truly, "goodness" may be an ancient word, yet even in the waking moments of spring and autumn, it feels refreshingly new and vital; it is something of which one need not be ashamed even in the hidden corners of one's home.
