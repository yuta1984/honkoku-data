[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
To see the person
　who is simply
　　as they are —
that is to see
　a buddha;
　　for the buddha too
　　was once just
　　　an ordinary person, simply as they were.

*(Commentary)*
"Simply as one is" (*tada-ari*) means to be without deception, without pretense — honest, and exactly as one naturally is. By original nature, the human heart is one of utmost sincerity, entirely free of falsehood. Yet it becomes clouded by personal desire and worldly greed, causing one to lose one's true nature and, conversely, to become cunningly wicked, taking deception and pretense as one's strategy. This is the deluded ordinary person (*bonbu*). If one wishes to become a buddha, one must abandon this wicked heart and return to the true mind of one's original nature — the mind of "simply being as one is."

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
Though you dye your robes
　　in ink-black,
　if the heart within
　　remains undyed —
how fleeting
　to wear the robes
　　of one who has crossed the world!

*(Commentary)*
In its original sense, "leaving home" (*shukke*, monastic ordination) means to sever the bonds of fame and profit, and to leave behind the house of worldly attachment. Dyeing one's robes in black is the outward expression of having left behind fame and profit and departed from home. Yet the monks of the present age, though they dye their robes in black, have their hearts turned all the more toward fame and profit. Even if one's outward form is that of a layperson, if one's heart is not bound by fame and profit, that is true renunciation. Such a person is called one who has "dyed the heart." The Venerable Hōnen composed a poem on this theme: "Would that I could dye — the inside of the heart — in ink-black; as for the color of the robe, it matters little either way."
