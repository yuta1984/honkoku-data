[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
Do not insist
　on thinking
　　others wicked —
for in truth,
　one's own self
　　is hardly
　　　good, either.

*(Commentary)*
To reproach only the faults of others while failing to look at oneself is a mistaken way of thinking. Why should it be that only other people are at fault? In most cases, the cause lies in the wickedness of one's own self. There is a poem that says:

*"Like the creature called 'ware kara' that dwells in the seaweed cut by the diving women — crying out 'it is my own doing!' — I shall not blame the world."*

Furthermore, Mencius says: "If you love others and they do not respond with affection, look inward to your own benevolence. If you show courtesy to others and they do not respond in kind, look inward to your own reverence. If your actions do not achieve the results you seek, in every case turn and seek the cause within yourself. When you have rectified yourself, all under Heaven will follow."

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
Recall that from speaking
　about others,
　　transgressions arise —
and guard yourself
　most deeply
　　against it.

*(Commentary)*
It is said: "The mouth is the gate of misfortune; the tongue is the root of calamity." Mencius likewise says: "If one speaks of another's wrongdoing, what can one do about the disaster that follows?" To speak disparagingly of others will, in the end, cause one to be hated and despised oneself. In general, to enumerate the faults of others is not the way of benevolence, and it is the most profound admonition of the sages and worthies.
