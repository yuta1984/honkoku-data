**[Right Page]**

**Poem:**
One who would know the realm and govern their domain must simply hold in their heart a spirit of compassion for the people.

**Prose commentary:**
Those who govern a province or district are like parents to the people. Therefore, they should take to heart the task of extending compassion and loving mercy, and of guiding and saving all beings. From ancient times, there has never been a person who oppressed the people and pursued glory for themselves alone who did not ultimately fall to ruin. Even if the person themselves was fortunate enough to escape destruction, their descendants would surely be wiped out entirely. How could one not fear this? It is said: "The people are the foundation of the state; when the foundation is firm, the state is at peace." It is also said: "What Heaven sees follows what the people see; what Heaven hears follows what the people hear." One who oppresses the people and pushes arrogance to its extreme will first lose the people's trust. When the people's trust is lost, one is abandoned by the Way of Heaven, and in the end, one perishes. One must fear and be prudent.

**[Left Page]**

**Poem:**
Though a person may become both Buddha and god, why would they hold their heart so carelessly and indifferently?

**Prose commentary:**
What is called "Buddha" and what is called "god" are, when all is said and done, nothing separate from ourselves — they are the very mind inherent within every person. This is what is meant by "the Pure Land of the One Mind, the Amida within one's own body." It is also said "the mind itself is Buddha" (*sokushin sokubutsu*), or alternatively, "the original master of the divine" and "the divine dwelling of the gods." When one is deluded, one is a sentient being; when one is enlightened, one becomes a god or Buddha. It all depends on how one holds one's heart. A person's heart of non-desire, non-action, forbearance, and compassion is nothing other than Buddha. The purity and uprightness of that heart is nothing other than the divine. As the poem goes: "Compassion is Buddha, uprightness is the divine, and the crooked person — these are three expressions of the same single heart." It is truly lamentable that people fail to realize this truth, which lies so close at hand, and instead wander lost through the six realms of existence.
