[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
One who would
　　know the land,
　　　know the domain —
such a person
　　must simply
have a heart
　　　that feels
　　　compassion
　　　　　　for the people.

*(Commentary)*
One who governs a province or district is, in effect, the father and mother of its people. Such a person ought to take it to heart to extend compassion and loving care, and to strive to save and guide all living beings. From ancient times, there has never been anyone who tormented the people and pursued glory for themselves alone who did not ultimately come to ruin. Even if such a person were fortunate enough not to perish in their own lifetime, their descendants would certainly be extinguished without fail. How could one not fear and take heed? As it is said: "The people are the foundation of the realm; when the foundation is firm, the realm is at peace." It is also said: "What Heaven sees depends on what the people see; what Heaven hears depends on what the people hear." Those who afflict the people and pursue arrogance to the extreme will first turn the hearts of the people against them. When the hearts of the people turn away, one is abandoned by the Way of Heaven, and in the end, one perishes. This must be feared and guarded against.

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
Into a buddha,
　　　into a god,
　　　　a person
　can become —
　　　so why
　　　　does one keep
one's heart
　　　　so carelessly?

*(Commentary)*
What is called "buddha" and what is called "god" are, when all is said and done, nothing other than what we already are. They are the mind that each and every person is fully endowed with — this is precisely what buddha and god are. This is what is meant by "the Pure Land of one's own mind" and "the Amida Buddha of one's own person"; it is also expressed as "mind itself is buddha," as "the true master of the gods," and as "the divine dwelling of the gods." When one is lost in delusion, one is a sentient being; when one awakens, one is a god or buddha. It all depends on how one holds one's heart. The mind of a person that is free from greed, free from self-serving action, patient, and compassionate — this is buddha. The aspect of that same mind that is pure and upright — this is the gods. As the poem goes: "Compassion is the buddha; uprightness is the god; the crooked person — these three are but expressions of the one same heart." It is truly regrettable that people fail to awaken to this truth, which lies so close at hand, and instead wander lost through the six realms of existence.
