[Right folio]

*(Poem)*
How pitiful indeed
　　are those who slander
　　　their lord and parents,
never once thinking
　　that the fault
　　　may lie
　　　　within themselves.

*(Commentary)*
If one who serves as a subject devotes oneself to loyal service without distinction between what is seen and unseen — rousing one's spirit day and night and giving oneself fully to one's duties — there is no lord who will fail to recognize and value such a person. Likewise, if one who is a child sets aside all selfish desire, truly cherishes and honors one's father and mother, never acts contrary to their wishes, and tends to them with care and diligence, there are no parents who will fail to rejoice at this. And yet, rather than devoting oneself to loyalty and filial devotion, such people indulge their own willful desires; and when their lord or parents treat them in a manner not to their liking, they respond with resentment and slander. This is truly lamentable. When a lord treats someone poorly, or when parents do not deal with a child as they might wish — in the end, the cause of these things lies within oneself. In all matters, one must turn inward and examine oneself, and must not blame others. As the *Analects* say: "The exemplary person (*junzi*) blames himself; the petty person blames others."

[Left folio]

*(Poem)*
How vain it is
　　for one who is
　　　impious toward one's parents
　to pray to the gods
　　and buddhas,
　　　beseeching them:
　　　　"Please,
　　　　　save me."

*(Commentary)*
One who truly fulfills their filial duties toward their parents will receive the protection of the gods and buddhas even without offering prayers. For parents are, to their children, the very Way of Heaven, and furthermore, they are themselves like gods and buddhas. Heaven's Way and the gods and buddhas are the parents of all living beings; and Heaven's Way and the gods and buddhas are, by their very nature, one and the same. It follows, therefore, that to be impious toward one's parents is to act against the will of the gods and buddhas, while to be filially devoted to one's parents is to accord with the will of the gods and buddhas. If one wishes to accord with the will of the gods and buddhas, one must first follow and serve one's parents well. And yet there are those who treat their parents as though they did not exist — neither loving nor honoring them — while making pilgrimages to shrines and temples, bowing their heads in worship, and casting their treasures before the altars as offerings. What benefit can any of this bring? It is said: "The gods do not accept what is offered improperly." And further: "To make offerings to a spirit that is not one's own is mere flattery."
