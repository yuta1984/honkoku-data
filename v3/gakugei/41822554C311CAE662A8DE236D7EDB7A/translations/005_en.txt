【Left Folio】
Onkun Kokujikaku — *Ken* (Upper Volume)
　　　　　　　　　Composed by Takai Bankan Shimei of Bukō (Edo)

The forty-seven characters of the Iroha were created in our country. Because their written form is simple and easy to learn, even men and women of humble station living in mountain villages and rural settlements can acquire them quickly, making them convenient for daily use. For this reason, in ancient times, when young children first began the study of writing, they were given as their models the thirty-one-syllable poem of Mount Asaka and the poem of Naniwa-zu. Yet somewhere along the way, it became the custom to begin with the Iroha as the first model for learners. The forty-seven characters represent the sounds (*on*) of human speech, and by reading them as *kun*, nothing in the universe is left uncovered. The successive combination of Iroha characters to form sounds and readings is called *kanazukai* (kana orthography). Children and women know only that the Iroha is simple and easy to learn, yet they do not know that it contains rules; they fail to perceive the subtle significance contained in the vertical and horizontal arrangement of the fifty sounds of katakana.
