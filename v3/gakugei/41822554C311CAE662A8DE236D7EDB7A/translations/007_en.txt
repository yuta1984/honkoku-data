Characters such as 匂, 遉, and 仮 do appear in Chinese dictionaries, but their meanings are entirely different. When such character forms appear mixed among Chinese characters, they are looked down upon as beans or barley among rice—something out of place—and are not accepted into proper writing. Therefore, the Priest Kūkai created the *iroha* syllabary, and Lord Kibi devised *katakana*, and since that time, our country's people, high and low alike, have used these alongside Chinese characters; this practice continues to the present day, a thousand years later. Truly, the only characters created by our own nation that can be seen today are the *iroha*. Even in the text of *Shotei* (*Shūtei*), I have seen our *iroha* kana written in cursive style with Chinese characters annotating the sounds of each character. From this, one should recognize their antiquity.

A certain Confucian scholar lamented, saying: "Since China is a land of writing, even the common man and woman know their characters. Japan is a land of illiteracy, which is why kana are used. Had there been no kana in our country, all people, high and low, would have been obliged to memorize Chinese characters; but because the *iroha* exists, the common people are kept in ignorance. It is only natural that no sages have appeared in Japan."

To this I (the fool) say: Not so. The transforming and nourishing work of Heaven and Earth shows no partiality to any one direction. Though humans are the most spiritually endowed of all living things, variations in individual capacity for wisdom and folly are no different across ten thousand nations. What sort of capacity did the sons of the great sage Emperor Yao of the ancient past possess? What sort of man was the father of King Yu? China too has its untalented men, and Japan too has its exemplary persons (*kunshi*). To attain the great Way of sages such as Kings Wen and Wu, the Duke of Zhou, and Confucius is not merely because more people know how to write. It is not that the Way exists only in China and not in other countries. Where there is a country, there are people; where there are people, there is a ruler; the ruler governs those below through the Way, and the minister serves those above through the Way. There is no country that lacks the Way proper to itself. Whether noble or humble, those who understand the Way in great numbers are worthy persons and exemplary individuals (*kunshi*). If China had the governance of non-action (*wuwei*) under Tang Yao and Yu Shun, then Japan had the benevolent rule of Emperors Nintoku, Seiwa, and Jinkei. One cannot say that our country has no sages. The term "sage" (*seijin*) is a designation used in foreign lands; since it is not a term of our own country's parlance, even if such a person existed, they would not bear the title of "sage."

There are differences in usage according to the climate and customs (*fūdo*) of each country—whether *on* (Chinese-derived) readings or *kun* (native Japanese) readings are employed. Since our country makes use of *kun* readings, the simplicity of the *iroha* kana is of no small benefit to women and children, and to the common people. China makes use of *on* readings. If *kun* readings are used, characters are few but words are many; if *on* readings are used, words are few but (characters) are many.
