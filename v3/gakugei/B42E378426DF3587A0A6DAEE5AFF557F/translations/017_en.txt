Zuiōzan (Mount Zuiō) Kōmyō-ji Temple　Approximately fourteen chō (about 1.5 km) to the right off the Kanazawa road, located in
　Kōmyō-ji village. It is the fourteenth site on the Bandō Pilgrimage circuit. This temple was
　founded by Kōbō Daishi (Kūkai), and the restorer is called Kōe Ajari.
　It belongs to the Kogi Shingon sect, under Ishikawa Hōshō-ji. Every year on the 10th day of the 7th month and the 18th day of the 12th month,
　a market is held and the area is greatly enlivened.
　The Azuma Kagami states: On the 23rd day of the 1st month of Jishō 5 (1181), at Nagao-dera
　　and Kyūmei-ji, etc. in Musashi Province, it was decreed that the monk Chōei should be placed in charge of their affairs. These are

　The principal image in the main hall is the Eleven-faced Kannon (Ekādaśamukha Avalokiteśvara) 《Small text: Said to have been carved by the Great Monk Gyōki with three bows for every stroke of the chisel; it is a standing image of rough-hewn wood, six shaku (approx. 1.8 m) in height》
　The inscription on the back of the altar cabinet was annotated by the restorer Kōe Ajari.
　　[The inscription reads:] Made of rough timber (araki), the surface is originally carved crosswise; measuring across the ten directions as a standing image that firmly saves all three worlds. Six shaku in height, approximately six jō, the eleven faces reveal the fruition realm; each face shows the profound intent that Gyōki demonstrated.

　Mani-zan (Mount Mani) 《Small text: This refers to the mountain above the Kumano Shrine. Local people call it "Oku-no-in" (Inner Sanctuary).
