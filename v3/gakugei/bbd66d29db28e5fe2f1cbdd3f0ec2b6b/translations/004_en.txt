**On the Origin of Uta Karuta (Poem Cards)**

During the Jōgan era of Emperor Seiwa's reign, Ariwara no Narihira, the Assistant Captain of the Left Imperial Guard, traveled down to the province of Ise as an imperial envoy. There he found Princess Yoshiko (Teishi), who was serving as the High Priestess of Ise (Saigū). The two secretly pledged their love to one another, and it is said that their feelings for each other ran deep. However, mindful of public reputation, Narihira visited the princess quietly to bid her farewell, saying, "Tomorrow I must depart for the province of Owari."

The princess, too, was reluctant to part. As they exchanged cups of sake, the princess wrote the upper verse (*kami no ku*) of a poem on a sake cup and presented it to him:

〽 *Kachihito no / watare to nurenu / enishi areba*
(If we share a bond shallow enough that a traveler on foot could ford it without getting wet...)

Narihira, without a moment's hesitation, took a piece of charcoal from a torch (*taimatsu*) and wrote the lower verse (*shimo no ku*) on the same cup:

〽 *Mata ōsaka no / seki wa koko nan*
(Then let this be our Barrier of Ōsaka, where we shall meet again.)

This episode can be found in *The Tales of Ise (Ise Monogatari)*. It is said that from this occasion, the game of uta karuta was created and enjoyed by people.

※ Note: The word *続松 (taimatsu)*, meaning "torch," can also be read as *tsuto* or *tato*, and by phonetic correspondence is also pronounced *tsuimatsu*.
