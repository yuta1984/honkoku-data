**Peony (Botan)**

The peony is described as the most wealthy and noble of flowers in Zhou Maoshu's essay "On Loving the Lotus" (*Ai Lian Shuo*). It is an exceptionally beautiful flower, and there are many people in both Japan and China who admire and delight in it. Because its bloom lasts for twenty days after first opening, it is alternatively called "Hatsukagusa" (the twenty-day plant). It is also known as "Fukamigusa" (the deep-colored plant).

*From when it blooms until it scatters and fades — watching all this,*
*beneath the spring tree,*
*twenty days have passed.*

*The crimson "Fukamigusa" in full bloom —*
*now that it has flowered, the feeling of reluctance to let it go*
*is far from shallow.*

**Lotus (Hachisu)**

The lotus grows up out of muddy, turbid water, yet is not tainted by that murkiness, remaining pure and clean. For this reason, it has been likened to a *kunshi* (a person of virtue and moral excellence) and celebrated as such. Poets of waka, poets of Chinese verse, and even the Buddha himself take delight in this flower. If people were to keep their hearts as pure as this flower, they would surely be regarded by the world as virtuous persons. Zhou Maoshu loved this flower especially and deeply.

*With a heart that, like the lotus leaf, is not stained by murkiness —*
*why then does it deceive,*
*making the dew appear as jewels?*

*Rising from turbid water,*
*yet purer than the water itself —*
*the white jewel of the lotus dew.*
