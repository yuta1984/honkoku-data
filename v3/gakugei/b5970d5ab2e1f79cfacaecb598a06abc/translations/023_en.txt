(Continued from previous page) Inanimate objects might be preserved within such a cellar, but how could any living, breathing creature survive there even for a short time? Furthermore, charred timbers fell and piled up on top, and the moisture of the earth transformed into intense heat, causing suffering a hundredfold — and death. When faced with sudden crisis, the mind naturally falls into confusion. Nevertheless, one would not normally think of such hazards in such a disaster. It is recorded here, therefore, as a warning to future generations.

▲A certain person from Kyō-machi in the same Yoshiwara district ran to Nihon Tsutsumi with all their might trying to escape the fire. However, the embankment had been split open by the earthquake, with cracks three to five *ken* in length. They stepped into one of these cracks and became unable to move. Meanwhile, fire from the licensed quarter and flames from Tamachi rained down on them, and just as they were wondering what to do, a young man came along. They called out to him and said: "If you save me, I will give you one hundred *ryō* in gold that I have on me." The young man agreed, scraped the earth away, and barely managed to free them. The person then reached into their breast pocket to take out their purse and retrieve the money — whereupon the young man suddenly gave way to greed, snatched the purse, and vanished without a trace. Yet the divine gaze of Brahma and Indra sees all things clearly, and this villain was arrested on the ninth day of the tenth month. Had he accepted the originally promised hundred *ryō*, the later calamity would never have befallen him. If one sees another in peril and rescues them without regard for one's own safety, accepting not even a half-penny in return, that is truly what is called "meritorious virtue." How much more so when one stands to gain a hundred *ryō* — what could there possibly be to complain of? Ah, what a demonstration of the saying that foolish thoughts arise from greed.
