…(lending and borrowing) was to be settled between the parties directly (*aitai-zumi*). ○ In the fourteenth year of the same era (Tenpō 14, 1843), the Shogun made a pilgrimage to the Nikkō shrine. ○ In the seventh month of the third year of Kōka (1846), the year of the Sheep, there was a great flood in Shimōsa Province and the vicinity of Edo: water overflowed as far as the Yoshiwara and the Nihon Embankment, reached up to the neck of the Great Jizō statue at Kozukahara, and the entire area became like a sea. Traffic along the Ryōgoku and Eitai routes came to a halt; in the surrounding villages, houses were swept away and deaths occurred, and so the boat-owners of Edo were ordered to furnish official rescue boats (*yakibune*), which went out and brought the victims to the *Gundai* office at Bakurochō. ○ In the third month of the second year of Kaei (1849), there was a shogunal deer hunt at Kogane. ○ On the third day of the eighth month of the fourth year of the same era (1851), a great thunderstorm raged throughout the night, and lightning struck one hundred and six locations within the city of Edo alone. ○ In the fifth year of the same era (1852), the year of the Rat, a British ship came to Nagasaki and an American ship came to Uraga in Sagami Province. In response, the various domain lords were ordered to strengthen coastal defenses, and a gun battery (*hōdaiba*) was constructed in the sea off Shinagawa. ○ In the seventh year of the same era (1854), a Russian ship came to Osaka. ○ In the first year of Ansei (1854), the development (*kaihatsu*) of Ezo (Hokkaidō) was undertaken. ○ On the second day of the tenth month of the second year of the same era (1855), the great earthquake described in this very account occurred.

Having been born in an age of enduring peace, one naturally delights in the sight of things novel and rare. Among such wonders, one witnesses foreigners from distant lands; sees Western firearms and even steamships being built; watches men clad in armor walking the streets; and sees great cannon hauled through the neighborhoods on wheeled carriages — all of it extraordinary spectacles unbeknownst to men of former times, yet visible before our very eyes today. If one reflects that this, too, is a kind of joy found within suffering, then those of us living now may be counted more fortunate than those of earlier generations. It is with the wish to convey to people of later ages the appearance of both the pleasures and the hardships of our time that I have set my brush to paper and set down these humble records.

【Left folio】

Ueno Kuromonchou — Stationed at the Kumanogōo Shrine — Maidservant of Kakusen'in — **Kane**

The aforementioned person: on the night of the second day of the tenth month last, the earthquake caused the collapse of her dwelling, and she herself was caught beneath the rubble. She sustained wounds in more than ten places across her body and was in tremendous and unrelenting pain; yet, consumed with anxiety for the safety of her mistress, and despite being a woman of little strength, she thrust aside boards and timbers, receiving yet more grazing wounds in the process, and at last managed to crawl her way out from beneath the wreckage. At that moment, a fire broke out in the neighboring house, and greatly alarmed, she began removing wood and roof tiles in order to rescue her mistress. Just then, Sukeemon, the *nanushi* (headman) of the same Kuromonchou district, happened to pass by; she called out to him in a loud voice and begged him to lend his assistance. Although Sukeemon was himself worried about his own home, seeing the pitiable situation before his eyes, he lent his help and set about clearing the debris. While they were doing so, Kihei of Shitaya Chōjamachi, and Shōbei of Yushima Osōji-machi together with his housemate Kichigoro, happened to pass by. She called out and stopped them, entreating them both for assistance. Together, all of them pooled their strength and cleared away the wreckage, succeeding in rescuing three people: her mistress Kakusen'in, Kakusen'in's mother Nao, and her daughter Toku. However, all three had sustained injuries, and while everyone was at a loss as to what should be done, the fire grew ever more fierce, and sparks rained down like a shower of rain.
