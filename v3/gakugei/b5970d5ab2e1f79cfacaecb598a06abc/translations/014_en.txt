In the third year of Meireki (1657), the year of the Rooster, on the eighteenth and nineteenth days of the first month, a great fire devastated Edo, resulting in the deaths of 108,000 people by burning. In response to this, Shoshūzan Muen-ji Ekō-in temple was founded in Honjo to conduct memorial services for the victims. This was long regarded as a disaster of immense scale; yet now there are those who say that the number of dead from the present calamity (the Ansei earthquake) exceeds even that figure. When asked how they arrived at such a number, they replied that within this city (Edo),

【Left folio】

there are many thousands of temples of various Buddhist sects, and when one adds to these their sub-temples and branch establishments, if each temple buried as few as five persons, the total would come to more than 200,000. This reasoning seemed plausible, and upon reflecting on it more deeply, one comes to realize that the death toll was indeed far greater than that of the Meireki fire — and it is only then that one is truly struck with astonishment.

After the earthquake and fires subsided, the sight of countless corpses wrapped in straw mats, or placed in four-to barrels (approximately 72-liter casks) and loaded onto carts to be sent to their respective family temples (bodaiji) was a scene of grief beyond all words. Since people in other provinces are unaware of these events, illustrations have been drawn here so that the conditions of that time may be witnessed by all. As I myself witnessed these things with my own eyes, I believe the estimates I have given of the overall numbers to be unlikely to be far from the truth — and so I write.
