【Right folio】

My friend Mr. Koizumi had been staying in the Kazusa region on business when he heard rumors of the great earthquake and fires in Edo. Abandoning his errand, he hurried back along the road. He had reached as far as Samoto Hachiman when, on the night of the fourth day of the tenth month, at the hour of the Ox (around 2 a.m.), he encountered at the outskirts of the village a woman of about twenty-four or twenty-five years of age, running at speed while holding a child of about two years old. He raised his lantern to get a better look; though her appearance was not that of a low-born person, she ran like an arrow, and he could not make her out clearly. After proceeding a further five chō, five young men appeared carrying a lantern marked with the sign of a circle enclosing the character "Sa." They came upon Koizumi and asked whether he had seen anyone carrying a small child. He told them about the woman he had seen, whereupon they said, "That must be her. It would be impossible to catch up with her now. Let us turn back," and returned the way they had come. Koizumi asked them to explain the matter fully, and they told him: a woman named Yume, wife of a man named Left-■rō in that village, had been killed when a beam fell on her during the earthquake. Although they wished to find someone to care for her two-year-old child, in the midst of such chaos no one could be found to take the child in, and it seemed pitiable to leave the baby to die. They had arranged for the child to receive wet-nursing from another woman, but this was not going as hoped, and the father was beside himself with grief. Yet every night, the child was being taken out, given a full feeding of milk, and returned to some unidentified place within the house. It was a most mysterious thing, they said. Koizumi listened to various accounts from those who had been near the woman, and concluded that this must be the ghost of the mother, drawn by her deep attachment to her child, who was going out to obtain milk from elsewhere and then bringing the child back. And indeed, so it proved to be.

【Left folio】

*Illustration: The western torii gate of Kameido Tenjin Shrine* (Note: there is approximately four shaku and eight sun below the double-tiered base stones / as shown in the left pillar, half of it is buried in the ground)

This torii stands at the western entrance of the shrine. The teahouses and other establishments in this area were all destroyed, losing their vitality in an instant. As illustrated here, the left pillar has broken and fallen entirely from the crossbeam (kasagi) downward, while the right pillar stands like a sword — as if it had been driven down below the double-tiered foundation stones and then wrenched back out again. Generally speaking, houses and storehouses are assembled structures, and it is perhaps understandable that they might crumble and collapse. But this torii had its foundations set firm in the earth as described, and yet it was uprooted and toppled — something entirely beyond the power of human hands to accomplish. From this one may appreciate the tremendous force of the tremors and the awesome mystery of the earthquake.
