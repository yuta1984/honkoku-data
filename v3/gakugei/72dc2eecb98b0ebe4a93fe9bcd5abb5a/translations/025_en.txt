[Item 18] To the west, Hōon-ji Temple's gate-front Demura-chō and both sides of the surrounding streets burned down. △ Mimeguri Inari Shrine, Shirahige Shrine precincts, Kibō-ji Temple, the Umewaka Burial Mound, and the entire Mukōjima area were △ (unscathed). The portion facing the Senju post-town and the great bridge to the west of the Sumida River is omitted. To the south, private residences and lodging houses (hatagoya) suffered major damage, and there were collapsed houses in the middle of the area.

[Item 19] Both sides of Kozukappara-chō burned. The shaking in this area was particularly severe, and not a single earthen storehouse (dozō) remained standing. To the south, Nakamura-chō suffered major damage, with many collapsed houses, leaving it in a state equivalent to those areas destroyed by fire. Further south, Sanya and Asakusahara-chō 2-chōme; and further south still, Motoyoshi-chō, Shin-Toriyoe-chō 4-chōme, Tōzen-ji Temple, Shunkei-ji Temple, Dōrin-ji Temple; on the eastern side of the same area, Kyōden-ji Temple, Fukuju-in, Sōrin-ji Temple, Senden-ji Temple, Kōshō-ji Temple, and Genshō-ji Temple; three temples in 3-chōme; Daishū-ji Temple and Yōhaku-ji Temple in 2-chōme; and on the eastern side of the same area, Genju-ji Temple, Zuisen-ji Temple, Ansei-ji Temple, and Henshō-ji Temple — all the temples of various Buddhist sects from this area extending as far as the Kagami-no-ike pond suffered complete damage to their main halls (hondō), monks' quarters (sōbō), stone monuments, and stone lanterns. The full extent of the destruction is too great to record in its entirety.
