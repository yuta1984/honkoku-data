That natural disasters are difficult to escape is made clear by the fact that even during the reigns of the sage rulers Yao and Shun, there were nine years of flooding, and during the time of King Tang, there was said to have been a great drought lasting seven years. One should understand that extraordinary natural disasters are difficult to avoid even in the reign of the most enlightened and wise rulers. This being the case, at this very moment, as all people bask in the benevolent grace of this age of great peace, we had heard of various natural disasters occurring since ancient times, causing people to suffer calamity and meet untimely deaths — yet we had thought of these as merely events recorded in books, or nothing more than the idle talk of elderly people over tea. However, in recent years, disasters of earthquakes and tsunamis have struck the area from the Kinai region through the Tōkaidō road to the vicinity of Sagami, and many people reportedly perished.
