△ At Fujimi Bridge in Fukagawa, there lived a man named Zengoro, a fireman's laborer (*tobi-ninsoku*) of the Second Company. When the earthquake struck, his own house collapsed, but he managed to pull his wife and children out to safety without injury. Then, hearing a great commotion from next door, he grabbed his fireman's hook (*tobi*) and rushed over, only to find that the entire mansion of Lord Ogasawara Sakyō — save for the entrance hall — had completely collapsed. From within the rubble came the continuous sound of weeping and screaming. Exerting all his strength, Zengoro threw aside great timbers, boards, and roof tiles, rescuing nine ladies-in-waiting (*koshimoto*), and then pulling out twelve more women from the wreckage. An elderly woman cried out, "Please, quickly, save Lady Myōgetsu-in!" *(Note: the mother of the domain lord)* Understanding at once, Zengoro crawled urgently into the collapsed structure, and after a painstaking search, found the nun-princess and brought her to safety, having her attendants escort her to a seat in the entrance hall. He then extinguished fires burning in three locations — the tea room and elsewhere — and rescued a further eleven people. Of these, however, five had already died instantly.

At this time, a fast horse arrived from the upper residence with congratulations on Lady Myōgetsu-in's safety, and it was reported that Zengoro had been the one to rescue her. On the fifth day of the same month, Zengoro was summoned to the upper residence, where he was rewarded on the spot with approximately one hundred *ryō* in gold, and was appointed permanently to the post of head of the construction works office (*sakuji-kata tōdori*), with a stipend of fifteen persons' worth of rations (*fuchi*) for life.

This disaster was a universal hardship — who among us could be said to have benefited? And yet this Zengoro, whose motives were wholly sincere and straightforward — what one might call "a man of ready wit and good sense" — had he not appeared, fires would have broken out from three separate locations, and what catastrophe might have followed is beyond reckoning. Good deeds bring good rewards; evil deeds bring evil retribution. The watchful eye of Heaven and Earth is indeed clear and bright. One may truly say that a person's rise or fall in this world lies entirely within their own heart.

**[Left Page]**

△ In the vicinity of Ōgi Bridge: the lower residences of Lord Iga, Lord Akimoto, and Lord Oki; the middle residences of Lord Nagai and Lord Echigo; and the lower residences of Lord Ōkuroda — in this area, both samurai residences and townspeople's houses suffered widespread collapses, too numerous to record in full.

△ In the vicinity of Uwabashi: the storehouse residence of the Sendai domain, the middle residence of Lord Kuze, and numerous townspeople's houses suffered heavy damage. Scarcely a single stone wall along the riverbanks remained intact. In the same area, the middle residence of Lord Makino Bizen, as well as those of Lord Akita, Lord Kuchiki, and Lord Honda Hyūga, all suffered serious damage.

△ To the north of the same area, near Mannen Bridge, there was extensive destruction and many collapses. Similarly, the stone wall of the One-Eye Bridge (*Hitotsumeido*) in Honjo collapsed, and for a time the crossing was served by ferry. △ The Benten Shrine in the same area was heavily damaged, and numerous stone monuments (*shohi*) were completely destroyed.

㊅ East of Shinhashi (New Bridge): the shogunal boat storehouse (*o-funakura*) sustained no damage. A fire broke out from the town in front of the same area, burning down the boat officials' residence (*funa-yaku yashiki*), the Hachiman temporary shrine (*tabidokoro*), the Saiko-ji Temple in the same shrine's approach town, and several small residences in the southern part, including Yōshin-ji Temple. Rokkenhorichō 3-chōme and Yanagawa-chō 2-chōme also burned. The shogunal rice storehouse (*o-momigura*) in the same area was half-destroyed by fire, one section completely collapsed. The Hagami Shrine survived amid the flames. Several small residences burned. Nearly all structures in this area collapsed.

㊆ On the opposite (east) bank of the same river: Kita-Morishita-chō 3-chōme burned, the fire stopping at Chōkei-ji Temple in the same area. To the south: Kita-Rokkenhorichō, Minami-Rokkenhorichō 4-chōme, and Minami-Morishita-chō all burned. The Shinmei Shrine survived amid the flames. The lower residences of Lord Ogasawara Sado and Lord Inoue Kawachi also burned. Fukagawa Moto-chō and Tokiwa-chō 3-chōme burned, as did the residence of Lord Ōta Settsu in the same area.
