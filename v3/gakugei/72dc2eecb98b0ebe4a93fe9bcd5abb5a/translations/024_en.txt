[Item 13] In the same area, two blocks of Nakanogō Ishiwara-chō burned down. From the vicinity of Minami-Warishitamizu, small samurai residences in various locations collapsed, and everything from townhouses to temple buildings was damaged, with not a single earthen storehouse (dozō) left unscathed.

[Item 14] To the north, two blocks of Ishiwara-chō and Arai-chō also burned.

[Item 15] To the north, one block of Kita-Warishitamizu Kawara-chō burned. In the same area, the middle residence (naka-yashiki) of Lord Matsudaira Suō, the lower residence (shimo-yashiki) of the Lord of Echizen, and the middle residence of Lord Hosokawa Noto all burned. A restaurant and the establishment known as Ogura-bō also burned. △ To the east, the Yanagishima Myōken-dō hall survived unscathed, but everything else around it collapsed. In this area, the residences along the moat belonging to duty officers (kinban-shi) and the numerous small samurai residences and townspeople's houses in all directions suffered massive collapses. At Jōsen-ji Temple in Koume Village, the main hall (hondō) and the Soshi-dō hall survived, but the temple grounds suffered extensive damage.

[Item 16] To the southeast, near the Honjo Itsutsu-me ferry crossing, more than half a block of Gokyō-chō burned. The damage and destruction by fire in this area was extreme, comparable to the situations described previously. The stone embankments along the riverside all the way to the Ōkawa (Sumida River) crumbled in a great many places, and storage sheds, firewood, and other materials along the riverbank fell into the river, causing a state of chaos and debris-strewn disorder beyond description.

[Item 17] The Kameido Tenjin Shrine survived intact, but its grounds suffered major damage. One block of the temple's gate-front town (monzen-chō) burned. Furthermore, a fire broke out from the neighborhood watch post (jishinban-sho) at the corner of the same area, and small fires occurred here and there nearby. Moreover, there were many collapsed and crumbled houses among the small samurai residences and townspeople's homes in the vicinity, with some areas left in a state no different from those that had burned entirely.
