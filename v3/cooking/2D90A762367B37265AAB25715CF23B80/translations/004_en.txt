Upon taking their seats, the "Ō-hikiwatashi" (grand presentation dish) of the "kusa" (informal/grass style) is to be served. Each guest offers congratulatory words, and mochi (rice cakes) in the shape of tori-ko (bird-egg form) are placed in a single layer on a sanpō (ceremonial stand) freshly lined with paper, decorated with ornamental noshi (dried abalone strips), and distributed to each person individually. Additionally, a soup with fin (hire no suimono), earthenware sake cups (kawarake), and tin vessels are brought out, and each guest should participate in the sake-drinking ceremony (sakazuki-goto).
At that time, the hikime and no (ceremonial arrow) attendants perform the ritual raising, and the elder female attendants (rōjo) are also to be given congratulatory gifts, and sake is served to them. After this, zōni (a ceremonial soup with mochi) is served.
The ceremonial meal settings follow either the "shichigosan" (7-5-3, i.e., three tiers of courses) or "gogo-san" (5-5-3) format, and the celebratory meal is to be served according to each guest's rank and means.

Supplementary note: For the Ō-hikiwatashi, a large sanpō stand is lined with fresh paper, and long noshi (dried abalone), kelp (konbu), pounded chestnuts (kachiguri), Japanese pepper (sanshō), and rice are arranged together, and one serving is presented to the entire assembled company for communal celebration. The celebratory meal settings are generally in the shichigosan or gogo-san format, and the dishes need not match those of an ordinary everyday meal; rather, they should be in the style of the second and third courses (ni-no-zen and san-no-zen).
