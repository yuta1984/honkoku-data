**Method for Yuzu Chidori (Yuzu "Plover")**

Finely chop only the yuzu peel and grind it thoroughly in a mortar, then add white miso. For every one *gō* of miso, add approximately ten large yuzu peels' worth. Also add five *shaku* of non-glutinous rice flour, and five *shaku* of glutinous rice flour (the dark variety). The glutinous rice flour should be lightly toasted and ground into a fine powder. These two types of flour combined should equal one *gō* in total. Grind this mixture together well with the yuzu miso, then roll it out to the thickness of a rice cracker using a thin rolling pin, and cut it into pieces approximately the size of a poem card (*uta karuta*) cut in half. Steam in a steamer basket, then dry and place in a serving dish to be used as-is. Uses include: accompaniment dish (*tori-zakana*), tiered lacquer box (*sage-jū*) contents, and tea sweets (*cha-gashi*).

**Yuzu Kayaku Tofu Mushi (Steamed Tofu in a Yuzu Vessel with Garnishes)**

Take a large *mochi yuzu* and use it as a yuzu pot (*yuzu-gama*), carefully hollowing out the inside. The garnishes (*kayaku*) consist of: roasted chestnuts, deep-fried tofu (*abura-age*), flat wheat gluten (*hira-fu*) cut into small strips and seasoned, wood ear mushrooms finely chopped, shiitake mushrooms cut into thin strips and seasoned, burdock root (*gobō*) cut into small rounds then julienned (also seasoned), and ginkgo nuts cut in half. Place an appropriate amount of these ingredients inside. Squeeze the excess water from crumbled tofu (*oboro tōfu*), mix in a little arrowroot starch (*kuzu*), place the garnishes into the yuzu pot, add the tofu, and steam carefully so it does not overflow. For serving as a *chawan* dish (tea-bowl dish), grind high-quality white miso thoroughly, thin it with sake, warm it over charcoal, and place the yuzu pot in a tea bowl [to serve].
