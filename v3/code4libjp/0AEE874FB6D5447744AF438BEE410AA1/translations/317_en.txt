Revering the Emperor, they employed the secret stratagem of suppressing the tail (reducing the power of large domains). It is understood that by the time of Taiyū-kō (Tokugawa Iemitsu), the intention of restoring the ancient system of prefectures had already arisen. They prohibited the construction of large ships and castle-building, established checkpoints at key locations, and kept wives and children together as hostages near the shogun's seat, gradually using these secret stratagems to bring the realm under control. For the several hundred years since then, because no foreign threats came, internal troubles did not arise either. However, in the current situation where foreign threats demand full attention, since the prohibitions on large ships and the hostage-wife system have already relaxed, the checkpoints and fortifications serve no purpose. Once having suffered humiliation from foreign barbarians with no time to redress the shame, both the hostage-wife decree and the tail-suppression strategy are, in my estimation, effectively impossible to implement in the Kantō. From now on, the best course is to thoroughly uphold the Emperor, turn the imperial radiance outward, distribute appropriate stipends according to the principle of "holding the jewel as lord and killing with men," bring all these parties under control, make the jewel (Emperor) sacred, make the sacred a protector of the shogun himself, issue decrees to the domains concerning hostage requirements and land surrenders pointing to shrines, make one-third of the realm shrine domain, distribute the remaining two-thirds among the domains, establish firm sacred precincts in strategically deep and vast locations, have the shogun become the permanent chief administrator, designate hereditary retainers and others as shrine priests and shrine families, designate the Three Domains and other large domains as heads for each province. Then the great principles being thus established, and with all minor matters also under the Lord (Emperor), the whole of the imperial nation will be mutually complete, the tail will also be appropriately positioned, and I believe the natural principles of reason and circumstances described in the foregoing text will be achieved. In general, governing the interior cannot be understood by remaining inside alone — it is only after going outside that one can understand how to govern the interior and what is best from without. In ancient times, because the Fujiwara clan fought for power, it ultimately became the age of warriors. Now, even if Satsuma and Chōshū fight against the Matsudaira (Tokugawa) house, it will ultimately become the age of foreign barbarians. As if the arteries and veins connecting hands, feet, head, and abdomen of a single body achieve proper circulation, only then can the full strength reach the entire imperial nation and the body function as one. At that point, armies to punish crimes and forces to chastise wrongdoers, with just one sweep in the direction of intention, may perhaps extinguish both domestic troubles and foreign threats.

Question: To begin with, is the imperial nation the nation of the Court or of the bakufu? Are the domains subjects of the Court or of the bakufu? It is said that the shogun's direct territories and the landholdings of the various domains should be surrendered to the Court and that a decree making the domains royal subjects might be issued — what do you say?
Answer: The imperial nation is the nation of the Court, and the domains are subjects of the bakufu. If there is a decree for land surrender and royal subjects, then it follows the path of establishing the national foundation as described in the foregoing text. Even if surrendered once, there is good reason to have it returned again. Even if they become royal subjects, there is no hardship in following the bakufu's intentions and not losing the bonds of loyalty between lord and subject.
