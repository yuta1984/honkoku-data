…What a commander must never forget is tenri (the principle of Heaven, natural order) and ōsei (benevolent kingship, righteous governance).

《Box: Six》 To govern one's country poorly, to be despised by the ten thousand people, and yet to claim that one will never lose when fighting against an enemy state — this cannot be guaranteed. If one is to suffer immediate defeat regardless, why not simply submit to the enemy state? Battle is something one enters only when one knows one can win. A benevolent person (jinsha) is like one who draws a bow. One who draws a bow first straightens his own posture, and only then releases the arrow. If his form does not waver, the arrow will surely find its mark without fail. No matter how desperately one wishes to strike the target, if one's form is not correct, missing the mark is plain for all

【Left Page】

to see. In exactly the same way, no matter how fervently one wishes to win in battle, if the governance of one's own country is poor, the ten thousand people will not give their hearts to the cause. With troops who repeatedly turn away in defiance, if one's own formations crumble, how can one possibly win a war? First and foremost, one must govern one's own country thoroughly and well, gather many capable people, discipline oneself and conquer one's own weaknesses, cultivate deep prudence, bring stability to the nation, love the ten thousand people, and — even toward those who are favored companions of the commander — bestow rewards and recognition upon those who possess hearts of loyalty and righteousness, correct the moral order of the realm, and (be wary of) the rise to prominence of those who are merely the commander's favorites and close companions…
