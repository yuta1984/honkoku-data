(Continuing from the previous page) Cut and gather bamboo and timber, and lay them side by side to the width of the road. Then collect straw scraps, miscanthus grass (kaya), and pampas grass (susuki) in whatever quantities are at hand, spread them over the bamboo and timber, and tread them down firmly so that the feet of men and horses do not sink in. Only after this should the troops advance. Likewise, when there is a trench-cut (horikiri) at any point along a mountain road, the same signal is used to open the passage: again halt the troops, throw in kaya grass and various other materials, and heap soil on top. Furthermore, if each soldier carries just a small amount of earth or stones in his hands and tosses it in as he passes, even a shallow trench-cut will be filled in and passable as-is.

《Section Sixteen》 On the Commander's Conduct on Mountain Slopes

▲ At difficult mountain passes, the commander too should dismount and proceed on foot. Because all the mounted men will dismount as well, this provides the horses with a much-needed rest above all else. Moreover, if even the foot soldiers see that the commander himself is on foot, even the most exhausted among them will take heart and press forward with renewed vigor. It also serves as an act of respect on the part of the commander toward his men. This is the most important thing to bear in mind.
