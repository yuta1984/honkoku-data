　Four: Should a war arise, and if one who delights in disorder comes attacking our land, it is because there is no other recourse. In this circumstance, with the harmony of the people within, the ten thousand people who have been won over to loyalty, together with their lord, shall stand firm in readiness even unto death, solidifying their unity without any divided loyalties; and moreover, by making use of the advantages of terrain, building castles, and devoting themselves fully to preparations for defense — when the time for battle comes,

　there shall be no defeat. The commentary (*hyō*) states: "Unless the governance of a country is in disorder and the country is in a precarious state, an enemy general will not make his move." The foolish generals of this world do not understand the harmony of men's hearts at all. In times of peace, they utterly forget the coming of disorder, they do not maintain a sufficient number of men, and when a sudden crisis arrives, they panic and flounder as though cold water had poured into their sleeping ears, and try their hand at battle, thinking that perhaps they might win. This is nothing more than trying to make flowers bloom and fruit ripen on a tree without roots.
