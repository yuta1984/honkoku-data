《Section Twenty-Two》 On the Difficulty of Advancing Through Snow

▲ In snow of five to six sun (approximately 15–18 cm), one should advance as in normal conditions. When the snow has accumulated to nine sun or one shaku (approximately 27–30 cm), the cavalry should be sent ahead to roughly trample through and break a path in the snow, after which the infantry should be made to advance along the trail thus created. When the snow has accumulated to one shaku four or five sun (approximately 42–45 cm), it is most advisable to assess the terrain and establish a firm encampment. On snowy roads, the troops will be utterly exhausted and will be scarcely capable of fighting effectively when the moment comes. Regardless of how urgent the situation may seem—even fearing the possibility of a surprise attack—it is not appropriate to force the army to advance recklessly.

《Section Twenty-Three》 Also, On Heavy Snow

【Left Page】

▲ In campaigns along the Tōkaidō, the Gokinai (Five Home Provinces), the Nankaidō, the Saikaidō, and the Sanyōdō, marching through snow should not present particularly great hardship, as these are regions where heavy snowfall is rare. Even if one shaku five or six sun (approximately 45–48 cm) of snow falls, it will soon melt once the weather clears. What should one think about deploying troops toward the Hokuriku region or the San'in region during the winter season? The people of snow country do not suffer from snow and are well-practiced and skilled in it—to attack such experienced lands from a country unfamiliar with heavy snow would be most disadvantageous. Likewise, what should one think about a snow-country force launching an offensive toward a warmer region during the winter months? In any case, it will not do if the routes of communication and supply from one's own territory are not functioning adequately.
