Knowing the route of advance is of paramount importance. When the aforementioned scouts return and report on conditions ahead — such as mountain passes and slopes having been cut and blocked, the soil of the straight roads (nawate-michi) having been dug up and turned to make footing treacherous, bridges over rivers having been destroyed, ferry crossings eliminated, dense thickets along the mountain edges, and the likelihood of ambushes — all of this must be heard and confirmed in detail. A Construction Magistrate (fushin-bugyō) should be dispatched along with foot laborers (kobito) and black-spade workers (kurokwa), to repair the roads as fully as possible, tear down any neighboring structures as needed, lash together rafts, and make preparations for crossing the river.

Now, at such times, there may be obstructing ambushes that prevent the road from being

repaired. In such cases, one or two mounted warriors (kiba) should be assigned with forty or fifty foot soldiers (ashigaru), and these should be suppressed with firearms (teppō), while the roads and river crossings are put in order. Furthermore, if the thickets along the mountain edges cannot be cleared by cutting, they should be burned away. Having attended to every such consideration with full preparedness, it is most proper to advance the troops. Furthermore, it goes without saying that one should know in advance all possible routes through enemy territory and advance along the most favorable one.
