【Right Page】

The most important thing of all is to know the route along which the forces should advance. When the aforementioned scouts return and report on conditions ahead—that mountain paths and slopes have been cut away, that the soil of the raised roads (nawatemichi) has been dug up and turned over to impede footing, that bridges over rivers have been destroyed and no ferry crossings remain, that there are thickets at the foot of the mountains, and that ambushes may be concealed there—all of this must be heard and confirmed in detail. A Construction Magistrate (fushin bugyō) should be dispatched, accompanied by laborers (kobito) and black-mattock workers (kurokwa), and they should duly repair the roads, demolish any nearby structures that obstruct the way if necessary, lash together rafts, and make preparations for crossing the rivers. Now, in such situations, there may well be harassing ambushes that prevent the repair of roads.

【Left Page】

In such a case, one or two mounted warriors should be assigned forty or fifty foot soldiers (ashigaru), and these should hold off the enemy with firearms (teppō) while the road and river are repaired. Furthermore, if the thickets along the mountain's edge cannot be thinned out, they should be cleared entirely. By attending to all such matters with thorough understanding and preparation, it is supremely important to advance the troops. Moreover, it goes without saying that one should know in advance how many routes run through enemy territory, and advance along whichever direction is easiest.
