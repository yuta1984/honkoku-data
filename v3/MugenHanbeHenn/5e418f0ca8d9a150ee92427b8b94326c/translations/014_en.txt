One round of the conch consists of three blows repeated three times, totaling nine blows. Three rounds consist of nine blows repeated three times, totaling three times nine, or twenty-seven blows. In all cases, the conch must be made ready before it is needed, and there must be no negligence.

《Section Five》 On Advancing in Two Columns

▲ A small force may advance in a single column. A large army must advance in two columns; otherwise, the rear will stretch out endlessly with no end in sight. Now, when marching in two columns and encountering a single-plank bridge or a narrow road, one person from the left and one from the right should alternate and merge together; and when the road widens again, they should return to two columns. Anyone who has business to attend to and must ride out to the side

《Section Six》 On the Advance of the Supply Train (Konida)

▲ The supply train of the vanguard is to advance closely behind the vanguard; that of the central formation (nakasonae) and the bannermen (hatamoto) is to follow closely behind their respective formations. Identifying markers (konida-jirushi) are to be attached to the supply trains, and one or two mounted warriors are to be assigned to accompany the supply train of each formation, with suitable servants also assigned by each retainer.
