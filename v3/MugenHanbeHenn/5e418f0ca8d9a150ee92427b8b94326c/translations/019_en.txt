The story of the old horse (who knows the way home) is a trivial matter known to everyone. When it comes to quietly retaining men in service, even placing one person from each of the sixty-odd provinces amounts to only a small number. If one were to place men of the rank of foot soldiers (ashigaru), keeping two per province would still total only around one hundred twenty or thirty men. In such cases, one should have the retainer from that particular province serve as a guide.

《Section Eighteen》 On Ambushes (Nobushi)

▲ At worrying locations such as the shade of mountains and dense forests, scouts (monomi) should be sent ahead to assess the situation. Either drive off any hidden enemies by opening fire with firearms, or until the entire force has passed through, station two or three mounted warriors with thirty-some archers and

《Section Nineteen》 On Advancing Through Difficult Passes and Narrow Roads with Rearguard Vigilance

▲ Five or six foot soldiers should be posted to each of the surrounding hills as lookouts for a distant view, and the army should advance while remaining vigilant against ambushes. Additionally, mounted warriors should be interspersed among the archers and gunners as the column moves forward. Ambushing forces will invariably let the archers and gunners pass and then strike from the rear. This is a precaution against being caught off guard in such circumstances.
