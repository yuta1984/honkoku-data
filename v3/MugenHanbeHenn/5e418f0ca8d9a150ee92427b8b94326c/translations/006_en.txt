　How lamentable! The *Tradition* (*Den*) says: "If the myriad lands bear guilt, let it rest upon my own person." When the people of the realm fall into pain, suffering, and innocent misery, it is not something brought about by the people themselves — it is the fault of the ruler alone. And so, a wise and virtuous ruler takes the practice of blaming himself most earnestly, while keeping the blame he places upon others to a minimum. It is also said: "If the four seas fall into dire poverty, Heaven's stipend shall come to a permanent end." Unless one corrects the situation in which the people of all four quarters of the realm decline and reach the depths of sorrow, one acts against the Mandate of Heaven; thus Heaven's blessings will fade, the ruler's allotted fortune will come to a lasting end, and the realm and state shall be lost — this admonition is without limit.

　Three: Should a war arise, and if the enemy nation's general delights in evil, oppresses the ten thousand people of his land, indulges in the way of greed and desire, devotes himself to his own solitary glory, spares no extravagance, exploits the people, imposes all manner of extraordinary levies (*kayaku*) upon the farmers, sets the crop inspections and land surveys unreasonably high, demands harsh collection even when the standing crop falls short, and as a result countless people suffer hunger and cold, many die without guilt, all are worn to exhaustion under swift and severe punishments, and the people lament and grieve, crying out, "When will this day ever end?" — in such circumstances, one should destroy that wicked general, and then——
